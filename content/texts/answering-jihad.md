[[nabeel-qureshi]]'s short 2016 book on Islam and violence, written in the months after the December 2015 San Bernardino attack. Three argumentative moves: (i) reconstructing [[muhammad]]'s military trajectory from Mecca to Medina via the [[sira]] and [[sahih-al-bukhari|hadith]] to argue that classical jihad is doctrinally rooted in primary sources, not a modern distortion; (ii) deploying [[naskh-abrogation]] to argue that Medinan sword verses (paradigmatically Q 9:5) override earlier Meccan tolerance verses on the classical Sunni reading; (iii) typologising contemporary Muslims into three groups — those who treat sharia as a totalising program, those formed by [[sufism]] and folk traditions, and post-Enlightenment secular Muslims — to argue that the question "is Islam violent?" is not univocal.

The book's pastoral aim is to refuse both the secular-progressive denial ("nothing to do with Islam") and the anti-Muslim weaponization of the same data; the title's "better way forward" is Christian neighbor-love framed against jihadist enmity.
