The Roman baptismal interrogation-creed of the second and third centuries — extant in Greek in the letter of Marcellus of Ancyra to Pope Julius I (340) and in Latin in [[rufinus|Rufinus]]'s commentary (c. 404) — and the immediate ancestor of every later Western symbol. Structurally tripartite (belief in God the Father almighty; in Christ Jesus his only Son, born of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary, crucified under Pontius Pilate, buried, raised on the third day, ascended, seated at the Father's right, coming to judge; in the Holy Spirit, the holy Church, forgiveness of sins, resurrection of the flesh), R is structured by the threefold baptismal question of [[claim-matt-28-19-trinitarian-baptismal-formula|Matt 28:19]] and articulated by the same [[regula-fidei|rule of faith]] that [[irenaeus]] and [[tertullian]] cite as the apostolic deposit.

R lacks several phrases the developed [[apostles-creed]] will later add — "creator of heaven and earth," "suffered," "descended into hell," "the communion of saints," "life everlasting," "catholic" before "Church" — and so represents the simpler stratum from which the Latin tradition grew. It carries no anti-Arian or anti-Christological technical vocabulary (no *homoousios*, no two-natures terminology); its work is baptismal and catechetical, not conciliar. Eastern symbols of the same period (Caesarea, Jerusalem, Antioch) parallel R structurally but with regional variations of phrase; the [[nicene-creed]] of 325 is dogmatically a Caesarean-type local creed retooled by [[nicaea-i|Nicaea]] for explicit anti-[[arianism|Arian]] use.
