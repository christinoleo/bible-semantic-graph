Second book of the [[torah]]. The Mosaic-Yahwistic charter text: liberation of Israel from Egypt, Sinai theophany, Decalogue, Covenant Code, and tabernacle instructions.

Source-critically a composite — J, E, and P all densely represented per the [[documentary-hypothesis]]; P dominates the tabernacle cycle (Ex 25-31, 35-40), J/E the plague narrative and Sinai pericopes. Pentateuchal redaction places final form in the Persian period; historicity of the Exodus event itself remains contested (no extra-biblical attestation, archaeological silence at Egyptian and Sinai sites, late-date 13th-c. vs early-date 15th-c. proposals).

Structure: prologue and call of [[moses]] (Ex 1-6) — Hebrew slavery, burning bush, divine-name revelation (Ex 3:14, "ehyeh asher ehyeh"); plague cycle and Passover (Ex 7-13); deliverance at the sea (Ex 14-15, including the Song of the Sea, often dated as among the oldest Hebrew poetry); wilderness journey to Sinai (Ex 16-18); Sinai pericope with Decalogue and Covenant Code (Ex 19-24); tabernacle instructions, golden calf apostasy, renewed covenant, and tabernacle construction (Ex 25-40).

Themes: liberation, covenant, divine presence, law as constitutive of the people. The divine name [[yhwh]] is here disclosed to Moses; the Sinai covenant becomes the template against which prophetic critique later measures Israel's fidelity. Tabernacle theology (P) develops the trope of mobile divine presence later transferred to the Solomonic temple in [[1-kings]].

Major figures: [[moses]], [[aaron]], [[miriam]], [[zipporah]], [[jethro]], [[pharaoh-of-exodus]], [[yhwh]].

Reception: paradigmatic liberation narrative — central to Passover Haggadah, patristic typology (Red Sea/baptism, Passover lamb/Christ), Black liberation theology, Latin American liberation theology, and modern Jewish self-understanding. The Decalogue holds privileged status in both Jewish and Christian ethics.
