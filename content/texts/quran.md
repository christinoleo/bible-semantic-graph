The primary scripture of Islam: 114 sūras of varying length received, on the Islamic account, by [[muhammad]] in Mecca and Medina between 610 and 632 CE through the angel Jibril ([[gabriel-archangel]]) and codified under the third caliph ʿUthmān (mid-7th century). Believed to be the uncreated speech of Allah preserved verbatim in Arabic — a doctrine of textual perfection ([[ijaz]]) that makes the [[quran]] the touchstone against which all prior revelations ([[torah]], [[psalms]], [[injil]]) are judged corrupted ([[tahrif]]) wherever they diverge.

The Quran retells many biblical narratives — [[abraham]], [[moses]], [[jesus]] — with striking christological divergence: [[jesus]] is a virgin-born prophet (Q 19) who performed miracles (Q 3:49), ascended to Allah (Q 4:158), and prophesied a successor named Aḥmad (Q 61:6), but neither died on the cross (Q 4:157) nor shares divinity (Q 5:72-73, 9:30). Critical-historical scholarship on the Quran's composition, sources, and textual history ([[john-wansbrough]], [[patricia-crone]], the Corpus Coranicum project) operates largely outside confessional Islamic categories and is itself contested within the [[islamic-apologetic]] tradition.
