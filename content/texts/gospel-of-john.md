Fourth canonical Gospel, sharply distinct from the synoptics in vocabulary, structure, chronology, and Christology. Lengthy farewell discourses replace parables; "kingdom of God" recedes ([[jesus]] speaks of it only at 3:3-5); "I am" (ἐγώ εἰμι) sayings (bread of life, light, door, good shepherd, resurrection, way/truth/life, true vine) and seven signs (Cana, official's son, lame man, feeding, walking on water, blind man, [[lazarus]]) structure self-revelation. Three Passovers structure the ministry (~3 years) versus the synoptic single Passover.

Authorship: traditional ascription to [[john-apostle]] son of Zebedee, identified with the unnamed "disciple whom Jesus loved" (13:23; 19:26; 20:2; 21:7, 20) — defended by Irenaeus (citing Polycarp). Critical scholarship since the 19th century has problematized this: the redactional comment at 21:24 ("we know his testimony is true") implies post-mortem editorial framing; theological development presupposes decades of reflection. Mainstream view: composed within a "Johannine community" or school over an extended period, reaching final form ca. 90-110 CE. Brown's reconstruction (1979) traces stages from a low-Christological Jewish community through expulsion from synagogue (cf. ἀποσυνάγωγος 9:22; 12:42; 16:2) to high-Christological consolidation and internal schism (visible in [[1-john]]).

Sources: a hypothetical "Signs Source" (Bultmann; later Fortna) is the most-discussed pre-Johannine layer; relation to synoptics ranges from independence (mainstream) to literary dependence on Mark/Luke (Neirynck). The prologue (1:1-18) is widely treated as a redacted hymn.

Christology: highest in the NT. Pre-existent Logos identified with God (1:1, 14, 18); equality and unity of Father and Son ("I and the Father are one" 10:30; "before Abraham was, I am" 8:58); explicit deity confession at 20:28 ("My Lord and my God"). Sacramental references (water and blood from the pierced side, 19:34; "true bread from heaven", 6:32-58) are theologically dense; reading them as eucharistic/baptismal is contested.

Major figures: [[jesus]], [[john-the-baptist]] (subordinated polemically — "He must increase, I must decrease" 3:30), [[peter]] (paired and often contrasted with the Beloved Disciple), [[lazarus]] (raised in ch. 11 — climactic seventh sign, absent from synoptics), [[mary-magdalene]] (first resurrection witness, 20:11-18), [[mary-of-bethany]], [[martha]] ("I am the resurrection and the life" addressed to her, 11:25-27), [[mary-mother-of-jesus]] (Cana, the cross), [[pontius-pilate]] (extended trial dialogue, 18-19), [[judas-iscariot]].

Anti-Jewish rhetoric: characteristic Johannine usage of "the Jews" (οἱ Ἰουδαῖοι) as a hostile collective (esp. 8:44, "you are of your father the devil"). Critical scholarship reads this as intra-Jewish polemic of a community recently expelled from synagogue, but its reception history weaponized the text in Christian antisemitism — a major problem for modern Johannine interpretation.

Reception: foundational for Nicene and Chalcedonian Christology; the Logos prologue is the Christmas-day Gospel reading in Western liturgy. Patristic favorite ([[john-apostle]] "the theologian"). Bultmann's existentialist reading (1941) and the discovery of the Qumran scrolls (recalibrating "light/darkness" dualism as Jewish, not Hellenistic) reshape 20th-c. Johannine studies.
