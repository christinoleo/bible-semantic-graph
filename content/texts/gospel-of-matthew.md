First Gospel in canonical order, though almost certainly not the earliest written. Traditional ascription to [[matthew]] the tax-collector apostle (Papias, early 2nd c.: "Matthew composed the logia in the Hebrew dialect") is rejected by modern critical consensus, which treats the text as anonymous Greek composition dependent on [[gospel-of-mark]] — incompatible with apostolic eyewitness authorship. Dating: ca. 80-90 CE, post-70 (the destruction of the Temple appears refracted in 22:7).

The Synoptic Problem: Matthew shares ~90% of Mark's content, with verbal agreement in shared pericopes too high for independent eyewitness recall. Two-source hypothesis (still mainstream) holds Matthew used [[gospel-of-mark]] plus a sayings source Q (shared with [[gospel-of-luke]]) plus material unique to Matthew (M). Farrer hypothesis dispenses with Q, treating Luke as dependent on Matthew. Griesbach (Matthean priority) survives in conservative circles but is minoritarian.

Community setting: most likely a Jewish-Christian community in Syria (Antioch is the standard candidate), in the aftermath of 70 CE and amid emerging Pharisaic-rabbinic consolidation at Yavneh. The Gospel's posture toward "the Pharisees" (cf. ch. 23 woes) reflects an intra-Jewish polemic between two competing post-Temple movements rather than a Gentile-Christian outsider's critique.

Literary structure: birth narrative (1-2), five great discourses bracketed by narrative ("And when Jesus had finished these sayings…", 7:28; 11:1; 13:53; 19:1; 26:1) — Sermon on the Mount (5-7), missionary discourse (10), parables of the kingdom (13), community discipline (18), eschatological discourse (24-25). The five-discourse pattern is widely read as Matthew structuring [[jesus]] as new Moses giving new Torah, paralleling the Pentateuch.

Theological emphases: fulfillment of Hebrew scripture (the formula "this happened to fulfill what was spoken through the prophet…" recurs ~12x), [[jesus]] as Davidic messiah and new Moses, kingdom of heaven (circumlocution avoiding the divine name), ecclesiology (only Gospel using ἐκκλησία, 16:18; 18:17), Great Commission to the nations (28:16-20). The Sermon on the Mount (Beatitudes, antitheses, Lord's Prayer, Golden Rule) is the most influential ethical text in Christian history.

Major figures: [[jesus]], [[mary-mother-of-jesus]], [[joseph-of-nazareth]], [[john-the-baptist]], [[peter]] (commissioned as rock, 16:18), [[matthew]] (the tax-collector called in 9:9), [[judas-iscariot]], [[pontius-pilate]], [[herod-antipas]].

Reception: dominant Gospel in early church liturgy and catechesis (the Didache leans heavily on Matthew); Lord's Prayer and Great Commission shaped baptismal and Eucharistic practice; Sermon on the Mount foundational for Anabaptist, Tolstoyan, and modern pacifist readings. Patristic ordering places Matthew first on assumption of apostolic authorship, fixing canonical sequence.
