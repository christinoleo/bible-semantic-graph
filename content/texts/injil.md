The *Injīl* — Arabic transliteration of Greek *euangelion* — is, in Islamic doctrine, the original divinely-revealed scripture given to [[jesus]] (Q 5:46, Q 57:27), counted alongside the [[torah]] of [[moses]] and the [[psalms|Zabur]] of [[david]] as a true prior revelation. The [[quran]] addresses Jews and Christians as the *Ahl al-Kitāb* — People of the Book — and treats the Injil they possess in the 7th century as binding on them: Q 5:47 commands the people of the Injil to "judge by what Allah has revealed in it," Q 5:68 declares they "stand on nothing" until they observe the Torah and the Injil, and Q 7:157 / Q 10:94 send Muhammad's questioners to that same scripture to verify his prophethood.

Islamic tradition distinguishes the Injil as Allah revealed it to Jesus from the four canonical Gospels ([[gospel-of-matthew]], [[gospel-of-mark]], [[gospel-of-luke]], [[gospel-of-john]]) — held, on the strong-[[tahrif]] reading, to be corrupt human compilations that displaced the original — yet the Quranic appeal to the Injil in Q 5:47 and Q 7:157 is to scripture *contemporaneously available* to its 7th-century audience, not to a lost archetype. That double commitment — the Injil's contemporary authority AND its supposed corruption — is precisely the contradiction the [[islamic-dilemma]] formalizes: see also [[claim-quran-defers-to-torah-and-gospel]] and the Islamic counter-reading at [[claim-quranic-references-are-to-uncorrupted-originals]].
