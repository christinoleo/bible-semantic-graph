Sixth book of the Former Prophets. From the ascent of [[elijah]] in a chariot of fire through the parallel reigns of Israel and Judah to the fall of Samaria (722 BCE) and the destruction of Jerusalem and exile to Babylon (586 BCE).

Climactic close of the Deuteronomistic History. The exilic redaction frames the catastrophe theologically: both kingdoms fall because of covenant infidelity, with the northern kingdom condemned uniformly and the southern kingdom evaluated against the Deuteronomic centralization standard. The Josianic stratum, by contrast, treats [[josiah]]'s 622 BCE reform (2 Kgs 22-23) — keyed to the discovered "book of the law", widely identified with an early form of [[deuteronomy]] — as a renewal of pure covenant religion.

Structure: [[elisha]] cycle, including the [[naaman]] healing and prophetic interventions in court politics (2 Kgs 1-8); [[jehu]]'s coup and extermination of the Omrides (2 Kgs 9-10); parallel reigns of Israel and Judah down to the Assyrian destruction of Samaria (2 Kgs 11-17), with a sustained theological obituary for the northern kingdom; sole survival of Judah, [[hezekiah]]'s reign and the Sennacherib crisis (2 Kgs 18-20, paralleled in [[book-of-isaiah]]); [[manasseh]]'s long apostate reign (2 Kgs 21); Josiah's reform (2 Kgs 22-23); decline through Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, [[zedekiah]] to the Babylonian destruction (2 Kgs 24-25); coda on Jehoiachin's release in exile, hinting at residual hope for the Davidic line.

Themes: prophetic-royal interaction, retributive theology, the Deuteronomic centralization principle as criterion for evaluating every king, the catastrophe as just judgment that nevertheless leaves a thread of Davidic survival. The Naaman pericope is a striking early voice for inclusion of the foreigner.

Reception: rabbinic and patristic traditions treat the fall of Jerusalem as paradigmatic of divine judgment; Christian reception draws on the Naaman story (Lk 4:27) to thematize Gentile inclusion. Modern critical-historical work uses 2 Kings as the primary biblical witness against Assyrian and Babylonian sources to reconstruct the Iron Age Levant.
