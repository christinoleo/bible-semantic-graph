Fourth book of the [[torah]]. Wilderness wandering between Sinai and the plains of Moab: census, rebellions, oracles of Balaam, transition toward conquest.

Hebrew title *Bemidbar* ("in the wilderness") is descriptively accurate; the Greek-derived "Numbers" reflects the two census frames (Num 1, Num 26). Source-critically mixed — predominantly P (especially the censuses, cultic legislation, and itinerary lists), with substantial JE material in the rebellion and Balaam narratives. The [[documentary-hypothesis]] treats Numbers as a redactional patchwork; later scholarship (Knohl, Milgrom) refines the P/H distinction here.

Structure: preparations at Sinai with first census (Num 1-10:10); departure and wilderness rebellions (Num 10:11-21:35) — the spies' report and the generation condemned to die in the desert, the Korah revolt, Miriam and Aaron's challenge to [[moses]], the death of [[miriam]] and [[aaron]], Moses' striking of the rock; arrival on the plains of Moab with the Balaam oracles (Num 22-24), Baal of Peor apostasy, second census (Num 26), and final preparations for conquest under [[joshua]] and [[caleb]] (Num 27-36).

Themes: divine discipline of a disobedient generation, intercessory leadership, holiness violated and restored, the failure of the exodus generation as a theological problem. The Balaam pericope is one of the few Pentateuchal passages with possible extra-biblical attestation (the Deir Alla inscription names a Balaam son of Beor).

Reception: rabbinic tradition derived extensive aggadah on the Korah rebellion and the brazen serpent (Num 21); patristic typology read the bronze serpent christologically (Jn 3:14). The Priestly Blessing (Num 6:24-26) is among the oldest attested biblical texts (Ketef Hinnom silver scrolls, 7th c. BCE) and remains in synagogue and Christian liturgy.
