Undisputed Pauline letter (or composite of letters), written ca. 55-57 CE between [[1-corinthians]] and [[romans]]. The most personally raw and rhetorically agitated of the undisputed letters; [[paul]]'s sustained apologia for his apostleship against rival missionaries he ironically calls "super-apostles" (ὑπερλίαν ἀπόστολοι, 11:5; 12:11) who have impressed the Corinthians with rhetorical polish, ecstatic credentials, and demands for letters of recommendation.

Partition theories: the letter's tonal and topical discontinuities — abrupt break at 2:13 / 7:5; severe tone of 10-13 against conciliatory tone of 1-9; the integrity of 6:14-7:1 (a paragraph with vocabulary and theology atypical of Paul, sometimes assigned to Qumran-style anti-pagan paraenesis) — have generated multiple partition hypotheses. Common reconstructions identify:
- the "previous letter" mentioned in [[1-corinthians]] 5:9 (lost);
- [[1-corinthians]];
- a "tearful" or "severe" letter (alluded to in 2 Cor 2:3-4; 7:8-12) sometimes identified with chs. 10-13;
- a "letter of reconciliation" (chs. 1-9 or 1-7);
- a separate collection appeal (chs. 8 and/or 9, possibly originally distinct).

Bornkamm and others see 2 Cor as a composite of as many as five fragments. Unity defenders (Hafemann, Harris) argue chs. 10-13 address a continuing minority opposition the earlier chapters did not. The debate remains open; many critical commentaries adopt a two- or three-letter partition.

Structure (canonical form): defense of Paul's integrity and ministry (1-7), collection for Jerusalem saints (8-9), polemical defense against opponents and the "fool's speech" (10-13).

Key theological material: the "treasure in clay jars" (4:7) and the theology of cruciform ministry; new covenant ministry with Moses' veil typology (3, reading [[exodus]] 34); reconciliation through the one "who knew no sin became sin" (5:21); collection for Jerusalem as concrete expression of the gospel (8-9); apostleship validated by weakness, not strength (11:23-30, the catalog of sufferings; 12:7-10, "thorn in the flesh" and "power made perfect in weakness"); Paul's "ascent to the third heaven" (12:1-4).

Opponents: identity of the "super-apostles" much debated — Judaizing missionaries (analogous to those in [[galatians]]), Hellenistic-Jewish thaumaturges, pneumatic enthusiasts, or some combination. They appear in chs. 10-13 demanding credentials Paul refuses to compete for, instead boasting in weakness.

Major figures: [[paul]], [[timothy]] (co-sender, 1:1), [[titus-companion]] (Paul's emissary to Corinth, 2:13; 7:6-16; 8:6, 16-24 — pivotal figure in the reconciliation), an unnamed brother (8:18; 12:18).

Reception: the cruciform-apostleship theme (esp. 4:7-12; 12:9-10) is central to Pauline spirituality and 20th-c. theologies of suffering; the collection material (8-9) underwrites Christian theologies of generosity; the new covenant and veil typology (3) shapes patristic and Reformation hermeneutics of OT-NT relations.
