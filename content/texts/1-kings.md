Fifth book of the Former Prophets; with [[2-kings]] originally a single scroll. From the death of [[david]] and accession of [[solomon]] through the divided monarchy down to the death of [[ahab]] and the threshold of [[elisha]].

Core of the Deuteronomistic History. Source materials referenced internally include the Book of the Acts of Solomon, the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Judah, and the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel. Critical scholarship distinguishes (following Cross and Smend) at least two Deuteronomistic editions — a Josianic edition optimistic about Davidic restoration and an exilic redaction explaining the catastrophe — with the framework formula judging each king by fidelity to YHWH and to the centralization principle of [[deuteronomy]].

Structure: Solomonic succession (the Court History climaxes here, 1 Kgs 1-2); Solomon's reign — wisdom, temple construction in Jerusalem, palace, dedication prayer, wealth, foreign queens and apostasy (1 Kgs 3-11); schism under [[rehoboam]] and [[jeroboam]] with the golden calves at Bethel and Dan (1 Kgs 12-14); parallel northern and southern reigns (1 Kgs 15-16); the Elijah cycle — drought, Mount Carmel contest with the prophets of Baal, theophany at Horeb, anointing of [[elisha]], Naboth's vineyard, Ahab's death (1 Kgs 17-22), set against the reign of [[ahab]] and [[jezebel]].

Themes: temple as locus of divine presence (and its theological tension with the prophetic mobility of YHWH), the dynastic ideal vs the prophetic critique, syncretism as the besetting sin of the monarchy, the Elijah cycle as restoration of pure Yahwism against Baal cult. Centralization theology of Deuteronomy directly governs the evaluation of every king.

Reception: rabbinic tradition mines the Solomonic narratives for wisdom motifs; Elijah ascends in the canon (Mal 4) and in later Jewish expectation as forerunner of the messianic age, picked up in Christian reception via [[john-the-baptist]]. The Solomonic temple becomes the durable symbolic referent of biblical religion.
