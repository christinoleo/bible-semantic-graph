The doctrinal letter (Epistula 28) sent by Pope [[leo-i|Leo I]] to Patriarch Flavian of Constantinople on 13 June 449, in response to Flavian's appeal during the trial of the archimandrite [[eutyches]], who had taught a "one nature after the union" Christology absorbing Christ's humanity into his divinity. The Tome is the definitive Latin statement of the two-natures doctrine: "the proper character of each nature being preserved, and coming together in one Person, lowliness was assumed by majesty, weakness by strength, mortality by eternity... each nature does what is proper to it in communion with the other — the Word working what is the Word's, the flesh accomplishing what is the flesh's; the one shines forth in miracles, the other succumbs to injuries." The famous Leonine cadence — *agit enim utraque forma cum alterius communione quod proprium est* — fixes the grammar of the *communicatio idiomatum* (sharing of properties) without confusion of the natures.

The Tome was first rejected at the "Robber Council" of [[ephesus]] II (449), where [[dioscorus-of-alexandria|Dioscorus]] refused to allow its reading and restored Eutyches; that rejection is the direct cause of Emperor Marcian's convocation of [[chalcedon]] in 451. There the Tome was received with the celebrated acclamation *Petrus per Leonem locutus est* — "Peter has spoken through Leo" — examined for consistency with [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]]'s second and third letters to [[nestorius]], and ratified together with them as the conciliar authority shaping the [[chalcedonian-definition|Definition of Chalcedon]]. The Tome is thus the bridge between the Western Augustinian-Tertullian Christological tradition and the dogmatic settlement of the universal church; its rejection by the non-Chalcedonian (Oriental Orthodox) churches mirrors their rejection of the Definition that incorporates it.
