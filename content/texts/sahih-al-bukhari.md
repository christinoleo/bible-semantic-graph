The first of the two *Ṣaḥīḥ* collections and, in mainstream Sunni reception, the most authoritative book after the [[quran]]. Compiled by [[muhammad-al-bukhari]] (810–870 CE / 194–256 AH) in Bukhara and Nishapur over roughly sixteen years from a working pool reportedly exceeding 600,000 reports, of which al-Bukhārī admitted ~7,275 (with repetitions; ~2,600 unique) under his stringent authentication criteria for the *isnād*.

Organized by legal-topical books (*kitāb*) and chapters (*bāb*) — faith, knowledge, ablution, prayer, zakāt, ḥajj, fasting, sales, marriage, jihād, *adab*, etc. — the structure presents the [[hadith]] as a working corpus for jurisprudence (*fiqh*) rather than a biographical sequence. Together with [[sahih-muslim]] the *Ṣaḥīḥayn* form the canonical bedrock of Sunni hadith literature.
