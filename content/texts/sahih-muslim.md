The second *Ṣaḥīḥ*, compiled by [[muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj]] al-Naysābūrī (817–875 CE / 204–261 AH), a student and contemporary of [[muhammad-al-bukhari]]. Contains ~7,500 reports (with repetitions; ~3,000 unique) admitted under authentication criteria similar to al-Bukhārī's, but organized differently: Muslim groups variants of a single report together by chain (*isnād*), which makes the collection more useful for chain-of-transmission analysis than Bukhārī's topical arrangement.

Together with [[sahih-al-bukhari]] forms the *Ṣaḥīḥayn*. Some Sunni scholars (notably the Maghrebi tradition) have ranked Muslim above Bukhārī on technical grounds of *isnād* presentation, but mainstream consensus places Bukhārī first.
