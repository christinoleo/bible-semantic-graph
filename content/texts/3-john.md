Shortest letter in the NT (depending on edition, ~219 Greek words). Personal letter from "the elder" (ὁ πρεσβύτερος) to "the beloved Gaius" (Γαΐῳ τῷ ἀγαπητῷ), apparently a leader in a local congregation within the Johannine network. The most personal and least theological of the Johannine epistles.

Authorship and dating: the same "elder" of [[2-john]] (and most likely [[1-john]]); ca. 90-110 CE; the three Johannine epistles travel together in canonical and manuscript tradition.

Content: commendation of Gaius for hospitality to traveling brothers and the truth (vv. 1-8); complaint about Diotrephes, who "loves to be first", refuses to receive the elder or his emissaries, slanders the elder "with malicious nonsense", refuses to welcome the brothers, and excommunicates those who do (vv. 9-10); commendation of Demetrius (vv. 11-12); travel intentions and closing (vv. 13-15).

The Diotrephes affair: a window onto a real intra-Johannine power struggle. Diotrephes is the earliest named local leader in the NT actively resisting an external apostolic-style authority. Interpretations range:
- power struggle between charismatic itinerant authority (the elder) and consolidating local episcopal authority (Diotrephes) — Käsemann's classic reading;
- theological dispute, with Diotrephes possibly on the secessionist side of the [[1-john]] schism (a minority view);
- personal/political conflict without clear ideological substance;
- a clash over emerging "monarchical episcopate" — Diotrephes prefiguring the Ignatian model just decades later.

The letter does not name a theological accusation against Diotrephes, only refusal of hospitality and authority. The elder's planned response — "I will bring up what he is doing, talking wicked nonsense against us" (v. 10) — implies an unresolved standoff at the time of writing.

Theological content is minimal: God is love, walking in the truth, hospitality as participation in the apostolic mission ("we ought to support such people, that we may be fellow workers for the truth", v. 8) — applying to the itinerant missionary movement that was a structural feature of early Christianity.

Reception: 3 John is a key historical witness to the institutional crisis of the early sub-apostolic period — the transition from charismatic-network to localized-episcopal authority. Often used in ecclesiology debates about apostolic versus local authority; valued in church-conflict counseling literature for its honest portrayal of inter-Christian rivalry; the Diotrephes warning ("loves to be first", φιλοπρωτεύων, a hapax legomenon) is a standard pastoral text on ambition in ministry.

Major figures: the elder (author), Gaius (recipient — note: a common name; identification with the Gaius of [[romans]] 16:23 or [[acts]] 19:29 / 20:4 is speculative), Diotrephes (the antagonist), Demetrius (commended; possibly the letter-carrier).
