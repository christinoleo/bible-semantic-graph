The thirteen letters attributed to [[paul|Paul]] in the [[new-testament|New Testament]]: [[romans|Romans]], 1–2 [[1-corinthians|Corinthians]], [[galatians|Galatians]], [[ephesians|Ephesians]], [[philippians|Philippians]], [[colossians|Colossians]], 1–2 [[1-thessalonians|Thessalonians]], 1–2 [[1-timothy|Timothy]], [[titus|Titus]], and [[philemon|Philemon]]. Modern critical scholarship divides them into seven "undisputed" letters (Rom, 1-2 Cor, Gal, Phil, 1 Thess, Phlm) — authentically Pauline, written 50–60 CE — and the disputed "deutero-Pauline" letters (Eph, Col, 2 Thess, the three Pastorals) whose direct Pauline authorship is contested on stylistic and theological grounds.
