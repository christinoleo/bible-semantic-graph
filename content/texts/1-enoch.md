Composite Jewish apocalyptic work of five separately-composed booklets transmitted as a single book in Ge'ez (Classical Ethiopic): the Book of the Watchers (chs. 1-36), the Book of the Parables / Similitudes (chs. 37-71), the Astronomical Book / Book of the Heavenly Luminaries (chs. 72-82), the Book of Dream Visions (chs. 83-90, including the Animal Apocalypse), and the Epistle of Enoch (chs. 91-108, including the Apocalypse of Weeks). Pseudepigraphically attributed to the antediluvian [[enoch]] of Genesis 5:18-24, the seventh from Adam who "walked with God and was not, for God took him." Canonical only in Ethiopian Tewahedo; received as scripture by the Beta Israel; absent from all other canons.

Composition spans the 3rd c. BCE through the 1st c. CE in Aramaic (the Book of the Watchers and Astronomical Book are the earliest, with [[dead-sea-scrolls]] Aramaic fragments from at least eleven manuscripts at Qumran Caves 1, 4, and 6). The Book of Parables (Similitudes) is notably absent from Qumran, suggesting a 1st c. CE date and either Jewish or Jewish-Christian provenance; its developed "Son of Man" Christology / messianology is the most theologically dense in the corpus and a key text for understanding the background of the Gospel "Son of Man" sayings.

The Book of the Watchers (chs. 1-36) develops the cryptic [[genesis]] 6:1-4 passage on "sons of God" mating with daughters of men. The fallen Watchers — Shemihazah, Asael / Azazel, and their company — descend, take human wives, teach forbidden technologies (metallurgy, weaponry, cosmetics, magic), and beget the giant Nephilim. The flood becomes the divine response to this corruption rather than to general human wickedness. The Watchers tradition becomes the dominant Second-Temple etiology of evil, displacing or supplementing the Adamic-fall framework.

The Astronomical Book (chs. 72-82) presents a solar 364-day calendar with detailed luminary movements, polemically aligned against the lunar calendar of the late Second-Temple Jerusalem establishment. The same calendar appears in [[jubilees]] and in the Qumran scrolls (4QMMT, 4QEnastr-a-d), indicating a shared sectarian dispute. The Animal Apocalypse (chs. 85-90) is a symbolic history of Israel from creation through the Maccabean revolt with humans as animals (a white bull = Adam, white sheep = Israel, wolves = Egyptians, eagles/vultures = Hellenistic and Roman powers).

Direct citation: [[jude]] 14-15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 ("Behold, the Lord came with his holy myriads to execute judgment…") as prophecy of [[enoch]], "the seventh from Adam" — the only undisputed New Testament direct quotation of a non-canonical text introduced as prophetic. [[2-peter]] 2:4 and Jude 6 allude to the imprisoned-Watchers tradition. [[tertullian]] argued 1 Enoch should be canonical precisely because of Jude's citation; [[augustine]] disagreed; the eventual mainstream Christian rejection (4th-5th c.) coexisted with continued Ethiopian acceptance.

Reception and rediscovery: known to early church fathers ([[clement-of-alexandria]], Tertullian, Origen, ps.-Cyprian), the Greek Enoch was lost after the 8th c. in the West and East. James Bruce returned three Ethiopic copies from Ethiopia in 1773, triggering the modern rediscovery. The Qumran Aramaic fragments (Milik 1976) confirmed the work's antiquity and clarified the composite structure. The Book of Parables remains a flashpoint: those who date it pre-Christian see in its "Son of Man" / "Chosen One" / "Elect One" / "Righteous One" titles a direct background for the Synoptic Jesus-tradition; those who date it 1st c. CE Christian or syncretistic see Christian influence flowing the other direction.
