Shortest book in the NT alongside [[3-john]] (245 Greek words; some count 3 John shorter at 219 — exact ranking depends on edition). True letter form (unlike [[1-john]]): standard Hellenistic salutation, body, and closing. Authored by "the elder" (ὁ πρεσβύτερος, v. 1), addressed "to the elect lady and her children" (ἐκλεκτῇ κυρίᾳ καὶ τοῖς τέκνοις αὐτῆς).

Identity of the "elect lady": three options. (1) A particular Christian woman with her family (the most natural surface reading); (2) personification of a particular local church and its members (corporate metaphor, paralleling [[1-peter]] 5:13's "she who is in Babylon"); (3) "Kuria" as a personal name. Mainstream reading favors option 2: a local congregation in the Johannine network, addressed by the elder as a sister congregation ("the children of your elect sister greet you", v. 13).

Authorship: anonymous "elder" — the same figure who writes [[3-john]] and (most plausibly) [[1-john]], part of the Johannine community/school. Traditional ascription to [[john-apostle]] follows from ecclesiastical association. Critical scholarship treats the elder as a community leader of uncertain biographical identity, possibly the same "John the Elder" Papias distinguishes from John the Apostle (Eusebius, HE 3.39).

Content: brief recapitulation of the [[1-john]] situation — affirmation of love, warning against deceivers who "do not confess Jesus Christ coming in the flesh" (v. 7), instruction to refuse hospitality to itinerant teachers who do not bring this confession ("do not receive him into your house or give him any greeting, for whoever greets him takes part in his wicked works", vv. 10-11).

Historical context: traveling teachers were a normal feature of early Christian network life, dependent on local hospitality (cf. [[3-john]] for the positive case; Didache 11-12 for procedures distinguishing true and false itinerants). 2 John raises the stakes: hospitality functions as endorsement, so the household-church is to act as gatekeeper against doctrinally deviant traveling missionaries.

Reception: the "do not greet" injunction (v. 10) is a recurring proof-text in disputes over ecclesiastical discipline, shunning practices (Anabaptist Meidung), and ecumenical engagement with theologically deviant teachers. Brevity and overlap with [[1-john]] mean 2 John is relatively rarely treated independently in academic and homiletical settings.

Major figures: the elder (author), the elect lady (recipient — corporate or personal), the children of her elect sister (the sister-congregation conveying greetings).
