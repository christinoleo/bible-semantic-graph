First of the [[twelve|Book of the Twelve]] (Trei Asar) in the Hebrew canon. Fourteen chapters of dense, textually difficult Hebrew — Hosea is among the most linguistically corrupt books in the MT, with frequent recourse to LXX, Targum, and Syriac for emendation.

[[hosea]] son of Beeri, active in the northern kingdom of Israel during the final decades before its fall to Assyria (722 BCE) — ministry overlaps the reigns of Jeroboam II, the rapid king-turnover after his death, and the Syro-Ephraimite crisis. The book's superscription (1:1) anachronistically synchronizes him with Judahite kings (Uzziah-Hezekiah), reflecting later Judahite redaction.

Structure: marriage narrative and oracles (1-3) — YHWH commands Hosea to marry [[hosea|Gomer]] bat Diblaim, "a wife of harlotry," and to name their children Jezreel ("God scatters"), Lo-Ruhamah ("not pitied"), and Lo-Ammi ("not my people"); the marriage enacts YHWH's relationship with apostate Israel; oracles of judgment and appeals (4-14), including the climactic restoration oracle (ch. 14).

The marriage metaphor — Israel as faithless bride, YHWH as wounded husband — is Hosea's distinctive contribution to OT theology. Critical scholarship debates whether Gomer was an actual cult prostitute, simply a promiscuous woman, or a literary construct; whether ch. 3 ("Go again, love a woman who is loved by another and is an adulteress") refers to Gomer or a second woman; and to what extent the metaphor authorizes or critiques patriarchal norms (feminist critique: Setel, Weems, Sherwood).

Themes: covenant infidelity as ḥesed-violation, the syncretism with [[baal]] worship as adultery, divine pathos (Heschel made Hosea central to his theology of the prophets), tension between judgment and inextinguishable love (most pointed in Hos 11, where YHWH oscillates between wrath and tenderness). Knowledge of God (daʿat ʾĕlōhîm) as the missing virtue (4:1, 6; 6:6).

Reception: Hos 6:6 ("I desire ḥesed, not sacrifice") is cited twice in [[gospel-of-matthew]] (9:13; 12:7) to authorize [[jesus]]'s table fellowship and Sabbath practice. Hos 11:1 ("out of Egypt I called my son") is reapplied in Matt 2:15 to the holy family's flight. [[romans]] 9:25-26 cites Hos 1-2 (Lo-Ammi → Ammi) for Gentile inclusion. Rabbinic tradition treats Hosea as the chief of the [[twelve]] (b. Bava Batra 14b).
