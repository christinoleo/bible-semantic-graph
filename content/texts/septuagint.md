The Greek translation of the [[hebrew-bible|Hebrew Bible]] produced in Alexandria between the 3rd and 1st centuries BCE, traditionally by seventy-two Jewish scholars (hence *Septuaginta*, "the Seventy") under royal commission of Ptolemy II Philadelphus. The standard scriptural text of Greek-speaking Second-Temple Judaism and of the early Christian church — the version most often quoted in the [[new-testament|New Testament]]. Diverges from the Masoretic Text in numerous readings, several of which are confirmed in the [[qumran|Dead Sea Scrolls]] as Hebrew variants the LXX faithfully translates.
