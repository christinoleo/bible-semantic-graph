[[norman-geisler]] and [[frank-turek]]'s 2004 popular-level apologetic, structured as a [[twelve-points-of-christianity-argument-framework|twelve-step]] chain from "truth is knowable" to "the Bible is the Word of God." Each step is built to be defensible against the next — if step *n* fails, step *n+1* never reaches its premises. The book's persuasive register is that *atheism itself requires more faith* than theism, because each step of the chain is positively supported by independent evidence (cosmology, design, morality, history, manuscript transmission, fulfilled prophecy, the resurrection).

Two distinctive Geisler-Turek mnemonics carry their evidential moves: [[surge-new-testament-reliability-framework|SURGE]] for the historical reliability of the New Testament documents, and [[feat-resurrection-evidence-framework|F.E.A.T.]] for the historical evidence for the resurrection. The book popularized the [[kalam-cosmological-argument]] (via [[william-lane-craig]]) and the [[claim-lewis-trilemma-lord-liar-lunatic|Lord-Liar-Lunatic trilemma]] (via [[c-s-lewis]]) for a wide evangelical readership.
