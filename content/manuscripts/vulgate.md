Latin Bible produced ~382-405 CE by [[jerome]] (Eusebius Sophronius Hieronymus) at the commission of Pope Damasus I. Not a unified work: revision of the [[vetus-latina]] Gospels (~383-384) followed by a fresh translation of the OT *iuxta Hebraeos* — from the Hebrew rather than the [[septuagint]] — completed in Bethlehem ~390-405. Jerome's preference for the *Hebraica veritas* over the Greek was controversial in his own time; Augustine objected, fearing Latin congregations would lose continuity with the Greek-based liturgical tradition.

Books not personally retranslated by Jerome — most notably the deuterocanonicals — retained their [[vetus-latina]] form in the eventual canonical Vulgate. Psalter situation messy: Jerome produced three versions (the *Romanum* from old Latin, the *Gallicanum* corrected against Origen's Hexapla, and the *iuxta Hebraeos*); the *Gallicanum* prevailed liturgically and is the Psalter of the medieval Vulgate, while the *iuxta Hebraeos* was philologically Jerome's best work but liturgically displaced.

Standard Western Christian Bible for ~1100 years. Council of [[trent]] (Session IV, 1546) declared it *authentica* — authoritative for "public lectures, disputations, sermons, and expositions" — though this did not preclude scholarly recourse to Hebrew and Greek. Authoritative editions: Clementine Vulgate (1592), Stuttgart Vulgate (Weber-Gryson, critical, 1969/1994), Nova Vulgata (1979, the contemporary Catholic liturgical Latin text, revised against modern critical editions).

Source for: Old Latin/Romance translation lineages, Wycliffe's English Bible, Douay-Rheims, every medieval Western theological vocabulary (*sacramentum*, *iustificatio*, *gratia*, *praedestinatio*) that traveled into vernacular dogmatics. Witnesses textually to Hebrew (Jerome's now-lost Hebrew exemplars, sometimes earlier than MT) and Greek (his NT base) of the late 4th c.
