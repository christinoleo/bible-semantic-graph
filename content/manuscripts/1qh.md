Collection of sectarian hymns from Cave 1, [[qumran]], 1947. Hebrew, ~75-1 BCE on paleography. Cave 4 fragments (4QH^a-f, 4Q427-432) extend the corpus and aid reconstruction of column order, which Sukenik's original edition got wrong.

Hymns formally modeled on biblical [[psalms]] — opening with "I thank you, Lord" (אודכה אדוני, hence the name *Hodayot*) — but theologically distinct. Two subgroups commonly distinguished: "Teacher Hymns" (cols. 10-17 in current numbering), first-person hymns plausibly attributed to the [[teacher-of-righteousness]] or composed in his voice, marked by isolation, persecution, and revelation of mysteries; and "Community Hymns" (cols. 1-8, 18-end), more communal, anthropologically pessimistic ("creature of clay", "vessel of dust"), emphasizing election by grace alone.

Significant for [[new-testament]] backgrounds: the *Hodayot* anthropology — radical human corruption + unmerited divine election — anticipates Paul on grace and depravity in [[romans]] 7-8 and [[ephesians]] 2, and the language of "knowledge of the mysteries" prefigures Johannine and Pauline epistemology. Direct dependence is improbable; common Second-Temple matrix is the standard hypothesis.

Attribution of the Teacher Hymns to a historical Teacher of Righteousness is plausible but contested (cf. Stegemann, Newsom). Shrine of the Book, Jerusalem.
