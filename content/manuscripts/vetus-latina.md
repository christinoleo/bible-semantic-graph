Pre-[[vulgate]] Latin Bible tradition. Not a single translation but a heterogeneous corpus of independent Latin renderings produced from the late 2nd c. CE onward in North Africa and Italy, before [[jerome]]'s revision project began ~383 CE. Augustine (*De Doctrina Christiana* 2.11) complained that "in the early days of the faith anyone who got hold of a Greek codex and thought he had some ability in both languages dared to translate it" — yielding the diversity Augustine then catalogued.

OT Vetus Latina was translated *from* the [[septuagint]], not from Hebrew — making it a daughter version of the LXX and an indirect witness to Greek text-types. NT Vetus Latina rests on early Greek manuscripts of the "Western" text-type, more divergent from the eventual Alexandrian standard than any other ancient version. Direct manuscript survival is patchy — partial copies like the Codex Vercellensis (4th c.), Codex Veronensis (5th c.), Codex Palatinus, Codex Bobiensis — supplemented by extensive patristic citation (Tertullian, Cyprian, Lucifer of Cagliari, Augustine — the latter sometimes preserving Old Latin even after he knew Jerome's work).

Text-critical importance disproportionate to its survival. Several Vetus Latina readings appear to preserve early Greek text-types now otherwise lost; Cyprian's citations are among our earliest Latin witnesses to NT books. For Old Testament textual criticism, the OL preserves a pre-Hexaplaric LXX in numerous passages where the post-Hexaplaric Greek has been contaminated by Origen's recension. Hence its central role in modern LXX text-criticism (Göttingen edition apparatus, Beuron *Vetus Latina* edition).

Vetus Latina readings were preserved in the canonical Vulgate for the deuterocanonical books [[jerome]] did not personally retranslate ([[sirach]], [[wisdom-of-solomon]], [[1-maccabees]], [[2-maccabees]], [[book-of-baruch]], [[letter-of-jeremiah]]) — meaning anyone reading those books in the Latin Vulgate is reading Vetus Latina text. Beuron's *Vetus Latina* edition (ongoing since 1949) is the standard critical reconstruction.
