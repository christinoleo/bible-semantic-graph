Greek codex containing the only substantial early-papyrus witness to both [[gospel-of-luke]] and [[gospel-of-john]] in sequence. Originally ~144 pages, ~102 surviving with extensive text. Paleographically ~175-225 CE, narrowed in some studies to ~200-225 CE.

Found ~1952 in the Bodmer trove from Pabau, Egypt. Held at the Fondation Martin Bodmer, Cologny, until 2007, when an anonymous donor (the Solidarity Association of the Order of Malta, via Frank Hanna III) purchased the codex and donated it to the Vatican Apostolic Library — where it now sits adjacent to [[codex-vaticanus]] in the same collection, a deeply appropriate pairing given the next point.

Decisive evidence in the Hort/Westcott textual program: P75 is textually almost identical to [[codex-vaticanus]] in Luke and John, differing in only ~5% of variation units. Hort had posited that Vaticanus's Alexandrian text reflected a "Neutral" 2nd-century recension; P75 confirmed empirically that such a recension existed by ~200 CE — Vaticanus is not a 4th-century editorial composition but a faithful transmission of an earlier text-type that already existed essentially intact a century-and-a-half before. This more than any other single discovery rehabilitated the Alexandrian text after the criticisms of von Soden and others. (Cf. Carlo Maria Martini's pioneering 1966 study comparing P75 and B for the Lukan text.)

Texts preserved: Luke 3:18-24:53 (with gaps), then John 1:1-15:8 (with gaps). Editio princeps: Martin and Kasser (1961).
