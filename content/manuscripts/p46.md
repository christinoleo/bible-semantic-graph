Earliest substantial collection of the Pauline corpus. Single-quire papyrus codex, originally ~104 leaves, 86 surviving. ~200 CE on paleography (with proposals as early as ~80-100 from Young Kyu Kim 1988 — widely rejected — and as late as mid-3rd c.). Split holdings: 56 leaves at the Chester Beatty Library, Dublin; 30 at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor (P. Mich. Inv. 6238).

Contents in this order: [[romans]] (from 5:17), [[hebrews]], [[1-corinthians]], [[2-corinthians]], [[ephesians]], [[galatians]], [[philippians]], [[colossians]], [[1-thessalonians]] (breaks off at 5:28). The placement of [[hebrews]] *between Romans and 1 Corinthians* is critical: P46 attests a very early scribal-canonical decision treating Hebrews as Pauline and placing it among the lengthier Pauline letters by descending size. The codex never contained the Pastorals ([[1-timothy]], [[2-timothy]], [[titus]]) or [[philemon]] — page-counting confirms the missing leaves at the end could not have held them — direct manuscript evidence that the Pastorals were absent from this textual stream at ~200 CE. Used as evidence by those who treat the Pastorals as later pseudepigrapha.

Textually Alexandrian with significant freedoms — scribally active, with idiosyncratic readings against [[codex-vaticanus]] and [[codex-sinaiticus]] that are nonetheless sometimes adopted in modern critical editions (NA28, UBS5). Notable readings: a "20% better" claim by Zuntz (1953); minor but striking variants in Romans 5, Ephesians 1, Hebrews 1.

Editio princeps: Kenyon, *Chester Beatty Biblical Papyri III* (1936-37). One of the foundational manuscripts that prompted Aland's reorganization of NT papyrus categories.
