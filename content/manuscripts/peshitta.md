Standard Bible of the Syriac churches — name from *pšīṭtā* "simple" (so styled to distinguish it from the later Syro-Hexapla and Harklean versions). Composite production: the OT translated directly from Hebrew in stages by Jewish or Jewish-Christian translators in [[edessa]] / [[adiabene]] from the late 1st through the 2nd-3rd c. CE; the NT formed by the 5th c. as a deliberate ecclesiastical replacement for the older Old Syriac (Curetonian, Sinaitic) and the [[diatessaron]] of Tatian.

NT canon distinctive and reduced: 22 books only. Excludes [[2-peter]], [[2-john]], [[3-john]], [[jude]], and [[revelation]] from the original Peshitta NT — the Antilegomena that the Syriac tradition resisted longest. Those five were later added in the 6th-c. Philoxenian and Harklean revisions but were not part of the original Peshitta canon and remain liturgically marginal in the Church of the East to the present day.

OT Peshitta witnesses, against the [[septuagint]], to an independent Hebrew translation tradition. Variants frequently agree with the [[targumim]] (especially Targum [[targum-onkelos]] for the Torah) and occasionally with non-MT Hebrew text-types preserved at [[qumran]]. Includes [[psalm-151]] (from Greek/Hebrew), Psalms 154-155 (otherwise extant only in [[11qpsa]]), and the Syriac Apocalypse of Baruch ([[2-baruch]]).

Multiple recensional histories — Mosul edition (Eastern Church), West Syriac (Mosul vs. United Bible Societies vs. Leiden critical). Foundational for the Aramaic-speaking churches: Syrian Orthodox, Chaldean Catholic, Maronite, Assyrian Church of the East, Indian Mar Thoma. Editio: Leiden Peshitta Institute (1972-ongoing), the standard critical edition.
