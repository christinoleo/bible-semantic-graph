Largest of three Samuel manuscripts from Cave 4 at [[qumran]] (4QSam^a, 4QSam^b, 4QSam^c). Hebrew, ~50-25 BCE on paleography. Highly fragmentary — only ~10% of original text preserved — but spanning roughly all of [[1-samuel]] and [[2-samuel]].

Text-type emphatically *not* proto-Masoretic. Hundreds of variants from MT, the majority agreeing with the [[septuagint]] (especially the Lucianic recension and [[codex-vaticanus]]) against MT. Many variants are plausibly superior: e.g., the Nahash the Ammonite atrocity narrative preceding 1 Sam 11 (preserved only in 4QSam^a and Josephus, absent from MT and LXX), or numerical figures in 1 Sam 13:1 (which in MT is famously garbled — "Saul was X years old"). Frank Moore Cross and Eugene Ulrich treated 4QSam^a as evidence that the LXX of Samuel rests on a genuinely different and often earlier Hebrew *Vorlage* than the Masoretic, not on translator caprice.

Confirms the textual-critical principle that for Samuel, MT is the demonstrably worst-preserved book in the Hebrew Bible — short, haplographic, and corrupt in numerous passages. The Hebrew University Bible Project and NRSV/NABRE/CEB committee notes consistently restore from 4QSam^a where MT is short or unintelligible.

Editio princeps: Cross, Parry, Saley, Ulrich, DJD 17 (2005). The Chronicler used a Samuel *Vorlage* closer to 4QSam^a than to MT — bearing on synoptic comparisons with [[1-chronicles]] and [[2-chronicles]].
