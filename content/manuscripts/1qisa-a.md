One of the seven original scrolls recovered from Cave 1 at [[qumran]] in 1946-47. Hebrew, ~125 BCE on paleographic grounds (radiocarbon dating from 1990 yielded a range of 335-122 BCE, narrowed to ~150-100 BCE by AMS in 1995). Substantially complete: 54 columns on 17 sheets of leather, preserving all 66 chapters of [[book-of-isaiah]] with only minor lacunae. The oldest substantially-complete copy of any biblical book by roughly a millennium prior to the [[aleppo-codex]].

Text-type closer to a "vulgar" Second-Temple Hebrew than to the proto-Masoretic 1QIsa^b. Hundreds of orthographic variants (plene spellings, matres lectionis added) plus some 250 substantive variants from the Masoretic Text. Most are minor; the famous [[isaiah-53]] reading at 53:11 — "out of the travail of his soul he shall see *light*" (אור) — supports the [[septuagint]] against MT and was adopted by NRSV, NIV. Scribal corrections (supralinear additions, dots over deleted letters) visible throughout, evidence of an active copyist tradition.

Theologically charged for both Jewish and Christian readers: it brackets the period when proto-rabbinic and proto-Christian readings of the [[suffering-servant]] crystallized. The Qumran community read Isaiah eschatologically; pesharim on Isaiah (4QpIsa^a-e) survive separately. The scroll's existence falsifies any claim that Christian copyists corrupted Isaiah's messianic passages — the Hebrew of 7:14, 9:6, 53 stood essentially as MT preserves it, with the limited variants noted.

Provenance debated only at the margins (was Cave 1 a Qumran-community library, a wider Judean refuge cache, or both?). Currently displayed at the Shrine of the Book, Israel Museum, Jerusalem. Foundational reference for textual criticism of Isaiah; cited in every modern critical apparatus (BHS, BHQ, HUBP).
