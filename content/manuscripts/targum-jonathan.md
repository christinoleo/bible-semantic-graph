Companion to [[targum-onkelos]]: official Aramaic translation of the Former Prophets ([[book-of-joshua]] through [[2-kings]]) and Latter Prophets ([[book-of-isaiah]], [[book-of-jeremiah]], [[book-of-ezekiel]], the [[twelve-minor-prophets]]) in the Babylonian rabbinic tradition. b. Megillah 3a ascribes it to "Jonathan ben Uzziel, disciple of [[hillel-the-elder]]". As with Onkelos, the attribution telescopes a longer compositional history: a Palestinian protograph (1st-2nd c. CE) redacted to its received form in Babylonia by ~the 4th-5th c.

Stylistically more expansive than Onkelos. Where Onkelos hews close to Hebrew word order, Targum Jonathan freely paraphrases, glosses, and incorporates aggadic expansions — Isaiah 53 famously rewrites the suffering servant as the triumphant [[messiah]] who exalts Israel while leaving Israel's enemies to suffer (a Jewish-polemical reading against Christian christological appropriations of the chapter). Calendar of judges is glossed with regnal-year computations; obscure prophetic oracles get historical referents; ANE deities are demoted to idols.

Vehicles a remarkable Second-Temple-to-rabbinic transition theology. The Glory ([[shekinah]]), the Word ([[memra]]), and the Holy Spirit appear repeatedly as mediating hypostases between YHWH and creation — important parallel material for [[gospel-of-john]] and [[hebrews]] backgrounds. Eschatological readings of the prophets are pervasive: virtually every prophetic peroration is angled toward a messianic future and the gathering of Israel.

Editions: Sperber, *The Bible in Aramaic* II-III (1959-62); Aramaic Bible series (Liturgical Press / Glazier); ongoing CAL (Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon) digital editions.
