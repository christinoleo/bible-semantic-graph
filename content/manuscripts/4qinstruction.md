The largest Qumran sapiential text, reconstructed from eight fragmentary manuscripts (1Q26, 4Q415-418, 4Q418a, 4Q423). Hebrew, composition ~mid-2nd c. BCE, copies mostly Herodian. Substantially longer than [[proverbs]] or [[sirach]] in original extent.

Addressed throughout to the *mevin* — the "understanding one" — instructing him on practical and theological wisdom: financial conduct, family relations, marriage, agricultural responsibility, dealings with creditors, eschatological judgment. Distinctive in fusing traditional Israelite wisdom genre with apocalyptic worldview: the *raz nihyeh* ("mystery that is to be / mystery of existence", רז נהיה) is a central revealed mystery to be meditated on day and night, intersecting [[1-enoch]]-style cosmic determinism with sapiential parenesis.

Not a sectarian text in the narrow sense — lacks the dualistic vocabulary of [[1qs]] and lacks reference to the [[teacher-of-righteousness]]. Likely pre-Qumran, possibly Hasidic or early Essene milieu, then received and copied at Qumran. Foundational for understanding the [[wisdom-and-apocalyptic]] synthesis that frames [[new-testament]] wisdom traditions (esp. [[james]], [[gospel-of-matthew]] 11:25-30).

Editio princeps: Strugnell, Harrington, Elgvin, DJD 34 (1999). Compositional unity vs. multiple-source debates active in current scholarship (Goff, Tigchelaar, Macaskill).
