Greek pandect (Old and New Testament in one volume), ~325-350 CE, most likely Alexandria though Caesarea and Rome have been argued. Three scribes on 759 surviving leaves of fine vellum, three columns per page, narrow margins. Sigla: **B** in NT criticism, **03** in Gregory-Aland.

Old Testament substantially complete in [[septuagint]] form (with deuterocanonicals — [[wisdom-of-solomon]], [[sirach]], [[book-of-judith]], [[book-of-tobit]], [[1-maccabees]], [[2-maccabees]], [[book-of-baruch]], [[letter-of-jeremiah]]; lacks 1-4 Maccabees and Prayer of Manasseh). NT lacks: 1-2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon, [[revelation]], and the end of [[hebrews]] (breaks off mid-Heb 9:14). The omissions are physical (lost leaves) plus arguably canonical-period choices for [[revelation]].

Held in the Vatican Apostolic Library since at least 1475 (the first Vatican catalogue lists it). Erasmus knew of it; Vaticanus's readings were withheld from Western scholarship for centuries by various papal regimes — Constantin von Tischendorf was denied full access in the 1840s-60s, a circumstance that drove him to St. Catherine's where he discovered [[codex-sinaiticus]]. Full collation only available from the 1860s; full photographic facsimile 1889-1890. Currently digitized in full by the Vatican.

Textual significance: with [[codex-sinaiticus]] and [[p75]], the chief witness to the "Alexandrian" text-type — judged by Westcott-Hort, Nestle-Aland, and most modern critics as the earliest recoverable form of the NT. Famously omits the longer ending of [[gospel-of-mark]] (16:9-20) and the Pericope Adulterae ([[gospel-of-john]] 7:53-8:11). In OT criticism, anchors the Greek text of the Major Prophets and Psalms. Vatican signature: Vat. gr. 1209.
