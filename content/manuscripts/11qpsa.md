Largest of the Psalms scrolls from [[qumran]], recovered from Cave 11 in 1956. Hebrew, ~1-50 CE on paleography. Twenty-eight columns preserve 49 compositions: 39 canonical [[psalms]] from books IV-V of the MT Psalter (largely Psalm 101 onward) plus 10 non-canonical or differently-canonical pieces.

Non-MT order: psalms appear in a sequence radically different from the Masoretic Psalter (e.g., 105 → 146 → 148 → 121 → 122 → ...). Includes [[psalm-151]] (known previously only from the [[septuagint]] and [[peshitta]]), Psalm 154 and Psalm 155 (preserved otherwise only in Syriac), the "Plea for Deliverance" (11QPs^a XIX), "Apostrophe to Zion", "Hymn to the Creator", a prose "David's Compositions" passage claiming David composed 4,050 songs, and a Hebrew of [[sirach]] 51.

Sanders's editio princeps (DJD 4, 1965) argued it represents an alternative *liturgical* Psalter, not just a private anthology — and that book V of the canonical Psalter was still fluid at Qumran. Talmon and Skehan countered that 11QPs^a is a derivative liturgical compilation, not a competing scriptural Psalter. The debate is unresolved; current consensus tilts toward Sanders, complicating any tidy account of when "the Psalter" was canonically closed.

Major implications for the canon debate: if the Psalter's tail was still open in the 1st c. CE, fixed canonical closure of the Ketuvim postdates Qumran. Witnesses to a wider Davidic-Solomonic compositional tradition than the MT preserves. Shrine of the Book.
