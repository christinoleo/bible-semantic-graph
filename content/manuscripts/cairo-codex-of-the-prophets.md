Earliest dated Masoretic codex. Colophon: completed 895 CE by Moshe ben Asher in Tiberias — father of [[aaron-ben-asher]] of the [[aleppo-codex]]. Covers the Former Prophets ([[book-of-joshua]] through [[2-kings]]) and the Latter Prophets ([[book-of-isaiah]], [[book-of-jeremiah]], [[book-of-ezekiel]], and the [[twelve-minor-prophets]]). Torah and Writings not included — never were; this is a Prophets-only codex.

Provenance: long held by the Karaite community of Cairo, hence the popular name. Karaite custodianship is theologically significant: medieval Karaites prized Masoretic accuracy more than Rabbanite copyists for some textual purposes, and a number of important Masoretic codices come through Karaite chains of transmission (cf. the [[leningrad-codex]] provenance, [[firkovich]]).

Textual authority disputed. The dating colophon was once questioned; the colophon stating ben Asher composition is also questioned by some who detect ben Naftali features in the vocalization (Yeivin, Goshen-Gottstein). On the dominant view it is genuinely 895 CE Moshe ben Asher work but with a Tiberian masorah closer to the slightly later ben Naftali school than to the [[aleppo-codex]]. Either way: an unambiguous 9th-c. terminus ante quem for the Tiberian Masoretic system.

Now held by the Karaite community in Abbasiyya, Cairo (with claims and counter-claims of ownership across the 20th century). Used in critical editions including BHS and BHQ for the Prophets. Editio: Pérez Castro, *El Códice de Profetas de El Cairo* (1979-92).
