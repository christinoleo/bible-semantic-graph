Authoritative Aramaic translation of the [[torah]] in the Babylonian rabbinic tradition. Composition layered: a core 1st-2nd c. CE translation, redacted into its present form in Babylonia by ~the 4th-5th c. b. Megillah 3a attributes the work to "Onkelos the proselyte" and links him via a Talmudic confusion with [[aquila-of-sinope]], the 2nd-c. literal Greek translator — the two are now treated as historically distinct (or the same translator's work in different language traditions), with "Onkelos" as a Babylonian honorific for what may have been a Palestinian protograph.

Style: relatively literal compared to other [[targumim]] (Pseudo-Jonathan, Neofiti, Fragment-Targum). Word-for-word rendering of the Hebrew predominates, but characteristic targumic moves are present: avoidance of anthropomorphism (e.g., "the hand of YHWH" → "the strength of YHWH"; "YHWH descended" → "the Glory [Yeqara] of YHWH was revealed"), use of *Memra* (Word) as a circumlocution for direct divine action, and substitution of *Shekinah* and *Yeqara* for direct theophany language. These mediating terms have been read as background for the [[logos]] theology of [[gospel-of-john]] 1 by scholars from Moore through Boyarin.

Recited verse-by-verse alongside the Hebrew Torah in Babylonian and (until modern times) Yemenite synagogue practice — *shnayim mikra ve-echad targum*, "twice scripture and once targum", a halakhic norm. Printed in all standard rabbinic Bibles (Mikra'ot Gedolot) since the Bomberg editions of 1517 and 1525.

Witnesses textually to a Hebrew Pentateuch substantially identical to MT, but with diagnostic interpretive choices that map the late-Second-Temple / early-rabbinic theological grammar.
