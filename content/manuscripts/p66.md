Substantial Greek codex of [[gospel-of-john]] — among the most complete early witnesses to any NT book. ~150 leaves originally, ~78 mostly intact pages preserved, covering John 1:1-6:11, 6:35-14:26, and fragments through 21:9. Single-quire codex, papyrus. ~200 CE on paleographic grounds (with proposals as early as ~125-150 CE by Brent Nongbri 2014 — based on closer paleographic comparanda and codicological features — which would make P66 contemporary with or earlier than [[p52]]).

Found 1952 as part of the Bodmer Papyri (provenance Egypt, most likely Pabau or Dishna near the Pachomian monasteries). Most pages now at the Fondation Martin Bodmer, Cologny, Geneva. Editio princeps: Martin (1956, supplement 1958, Barns appendix 1962).

Distinctive feature: heavy scribal self-correction. ~450 corrections by the original scribe (and possibly a *diorthotēs*), creating an unusually visible window into how a 2nd/3rd-c. scribe negotiated his exemplar against an additional control text. Text-type often classed as "free" or "Alexandrian with Western affinities" — closer to [[codex-sinaiticus]] in some passages, to [[codex-vaticanus]] (and [[p75]]) in others, with idiosyncratic readings elsewhere. Some scholars (Comfort, Royse) read the corrections as moving toward a more Alexandrian standard.

Notably *omits* the Pericope Adulterae (John 7:53-8:11) consistent with [[codex-vaticanus]], [[codex-sinaiticus]], and [[p75]] — the cumulative early-manuscript case for that pericope's secondary status. Foundational for 20th-century Johannine text-critical work, especially via Gordon Fee's *Papyrus Bodmer II (P66)* (1968).
