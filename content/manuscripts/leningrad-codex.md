Oldest *complete* manuscript of the Hebrew Bible. Colophon dates it 1008 (or 1009) CE, copied in Cairo by Samuel ben Jacob from exemplars of the [[aaron-ben-asher]] school — colophons identify Ben Asher manuscripts as the source, making the Leningrad Codex effectively a sister of the [[aleppo-codex]] one generation removed.

Acquired by Avraham Firkovich in the 1830s (provenance trail murky — Firkovich's collection methods are notoriously suspect), entered the Russian Imperial Library at St. Petersburg, currently held by the National Library of Russia (catalog: Firkovich B19A). The city name change drove the manuscript name shifts; "Leningrad Codex" remains conventional in biblical-studies literature.

Base text of every modern critical Hebrew Bible: Kittel's BHK^3 (1937), BHS (1977), and the ongoing [[biblia-hebraica-quinta]] (BHQ). The Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC) and Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible digital editions reproduce it. Where the [[aleppo-codex]] has lacunae (most of the Torah, much of the Writings), the Leningrad is the default fallback.

Three vocalization-and-masorah layers visible: a fairly clean Tiberian Ben Asher text, with sporadic corrections; the masorah parva in margins, masorah magna at top/bottom. Modern critical apparatus marks dozens of cases where Leningrad reads against the broader Tiberian tradition; cf. Dotan's edition (BHQ, BHS Reader's Edition).
