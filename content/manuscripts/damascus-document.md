Sectarian legal-narrative text in two parts. First recovered from the Cairo Geniza in 1896-97 by Solomon Schechter (two medieval manuscripts: CD-A 10th c. CE, CD-B 12th c. CE — initially called "Zadokite Fragments"). Decades later, fragments of the same composition turned up in [[qumran]] Caves 4 (4Q266-273), 5 (5Q12), and 6 (6Q15), confirming its Second-Temple origin and establishing a continuous transmission from the [[qumran-community]] to medieval Karaite-adjacent circles.

Two literary halves: the *Admonition* (CD I-VIII, XIX-XX) — a historical-theological exhortation invoking a "remnant" delivered from Israel's apostasy, with cryptic references to the [[teacher-of-righteousness]], the "Wicked Priest", the "Liar", and migration to the "land of Damascus" (allegorical? geographic? unresolved) — and the *Laws* (CD IX-XVI, plus extensive 4QD additions) — purity, Sabbath, oaths, vows, agriculture, witnesses, the camp/city distinction.

Composition ~100-75 BCE for the Admonition; the Laws may aggregate earlier and later material. Relationship to [[1qs]]: parallel and partly overlapping rule-traditions for what appears to be the same broader movement, but for different settlement contexts — CD presupposes married members living in "camps" across Judea, 1QS a celibate communal yahad. The two-rule problem has driven a generation of debate on Essene/Qumran diversity (Davies, Hempel, Collins).

Standard editions: Charlesworth (PTSDSSP 2); Baumgarten et al., DJD 18 (4QD). Foundational for reconstructing pre-rabbinic Jewish legal hermeneutics.
