Partial Isaiah scroll from Cave 1 at [[qumran]], recovered alongside the [[1qisa-a]] in 1947 but in fragmentary condition. ~50-25 BCE on paleography. Preserves portions of [[book-of-isaiah]] chapters 38-66, with the best-preserved sections in 41-59 and 61-66.

Text-type proto-Masoretic — closer to the consonantal text underlying the [[leningrad-codex]] than 1QIsa^a is. Far fewer orthographic and substantive variants from MT than its more famous companion. Demonstrates that the proto-MT recension was already circulating in Second Temple Judaism, not a late rabbinic standardization.

Together with [[1qisa-a]], grounds the standard textual-critical claim that multiple Hebrew text-types of Isaiah coexisted at Qumran. The contrast is methodologically important: same book, same site, two different recensional families. Cited in BHS and BHQ apparatus. Held by the Hebrew University of Jerusalem (hence the popular sobriquet "Hebrew University Scroll").
