The Masoretic codex of record. Written in Tiberias ~930 CE; consonantal text by Shlomo ben Buya'a, vocalization, accents (te'amim), and masorah by [[aaron-ben-asher]] himself — the last and most authoritative master of the ben Asher Tiberian Masoretic tradition. [[maimonides]] (Hilkhot Sefer Torah 8:4) explicitly consulted "the codex in Egypt" — almost certainly this manuscript — to fix the open/closed parashot of the Torah.

Held in Aleppo, Syria, for ~600 years (hence the name; *Aram Tzova* is the rabbinic name for the Aleppo region), guarded as the community's most precious possession. Damaged in the anti-Jewish riots of December 1947 following the UN partition vote; the synagogue housing it was burned. Smuggled to Jerusalem in 1958. ~40% lost — including most of the [[torah]] (Genesis 1:1 through Deuteronomy 28:17 is gone), Song of Songs, Ecclesiastes, Lamentations, Esther, Daniel, Ezra-Nehemiah. Fate of the missing pages debated (some claim they survive in private hands; periodic single-page recoveries since the 1980s have intensified speculation).

Despite the losses, what survives — most of Prophets and Writings — anchors the Hebrew University Bible Project (HUBP) critical edition and the Mossad HaRav Kook editions. Generally judged superior to the [[leningrad-codex]] in the surviving sections (more consistent masorah, fewer scribal errors), but unavailable in complete form for any single book.

Currently at the Shrine of the Book, Israel Museum, alongside [[1qisa-a]]. Digital facsimile via the Ben-Zvi Institute. Cf. Goshen-Gottstein, *The Aleppo Codex* (1976); Tawil and Schneider, *Crown of Aleppo* (2010).
