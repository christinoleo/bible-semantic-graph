Composite halakhic text reconstructed from six fragmentary manuscripts in Cave 4, [[qumran]] (4Q394-399). Hebrew, paleographically ~75-50 BCE for the extant copies; the composition itself plausibly mid-2nd c. BCE, near the foundation of the [[qumran-community]].

Three sections: a calendrical preamble (a 364-day solar calendar against the lunar calendar of the Jerusalem priesthood), a list of ~22 halakhic disputes — purity rules, sacrificial procedure, intermarriage with gentiles, the status of the deaf and blind — and a hortatory conclusion appealing to a royal or priestly addressee to "examine these matters" and turn from error. The disputes consistently align with positions later attributed to the [[sadducees]] against those later attributed to the [[pharisees]] (m. Yadayim 4:6-7 attests the same disputes).

Pivotal phrase: *ma'aśe ha-Torah* ("works of the Torah", מעשי התורה), the same expression Paul opposes to faith in [[galatians]] 2:16 and [[romans]] 3:20, 28. 4QMMT supplied the long-missing Second-Temple Jewish referent for Paul's term: not general moral effort but boundary-marker halakha. Reframes the [[new-perspective-on-paul]] (Sanders, Dunn, Wright) debate.

Often read as the foundational schism document — the [[teacher-of-righteousness]] separating from the Jerusalem priesthood over halakhic disputes — though the letter's tone (deferential, persuasive) does not prove definitive rupture. Strugnell-Qimron editio princeps 1994, DJD 10.
