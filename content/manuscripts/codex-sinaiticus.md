Greek pandect, ~330-360 CE, with the only complete NT among the great uncials. Sigla: **ℵ** (aleph) in NT criticism, **01** in Gregory-Aland. ~400 leaves survive (out of an estimated original ~730), parchment, four columns per page — the only quadruple-column NT manuscript of this caliber, suggesting design as a high-status commissioned codex (some have associated it with the fifty Bibles Eusebius reports Constantine commissioning for Constantinople, though direct evidence is lacking).

Discovered by Constantin von [[tischendorf]] at St. Catherine's monastery, Mount Sinai, in three campaigns (1844, 1853, 1859). The story is contested: Tischendorf claimed he rescued leaves from a basket destined for fire; the monastery has long maintained the leaves were never in danger and that Tischendorf's permanent removal of the manuscript to Russia was effectively theft under cover of a loan. The codex now divided among four institutions: British Library (347 leaves, the largest portion), Leipzig University Library (43 leaves), the National Library of Russia in St. Petersburg (fragments), and St. Catherine's itself (further fragments discovered in 1975). Digitally reunified by the [[codex-sinaiticus-project]] (2008).

Contents extraordinary: complete Greek [[septuagint]] OT (including [[1-maccabees]], [[4-maccabees]], [[book-of-tobit]], [[book-of-judith]]); complete NT; plus the [[epistle-of-barnabas]] and a substantial portion of the [[shepherd-of-hermas]] — both treated as Scripture by some Eastern communities into the 4th century. (Direct witness to a wider canon-fluid 4th-century church.)

Textually, with [[codex-vaticanus]], the chief Alexandrian witness. Like B, omits the longer ending of [[gospel-of-mark]] and the Pericope Adulterae. Differs from B in [[revelation]], where Sinaiticus is the earliest substantial witness. Multiple correctors (designated ℵ^a, ℵ^b, ℵ^c — partly identified with Caesarean scribes per a colophon at the end of Esther naming a "Pamphilus" exemplar) have left thousands of corrections, an unusually rich window into early text-critical practice.
