Tiny papyrus fragment — ~9 × 6 cm, both sides written — preserving on recto [[gospel-of-john]] 18:31-33 and on verso 18:37-38. From a codex (writing on both sides), not a scroll. Held by the John Rylands Library, Manchester (P. Ryl. Gr. 457).

Dating debated. Initial paleographic dating by C.H. Roberts (1935) gave ~125 CE, with a range of 100-150. Roberts's narrow date made P52 the oldest known NT manuscript by a substantial margin and became a fixed point in scholarly arguments for an early date of [[gospel-of-john]] composition. Subsequent reassessments (Brent Nongbri 2005; Pasquale Orsini and Willy Clarysse 2012) have argued the paleographic comparanda Roberts used cluster more comfortably in the late 2nd or even early 3rd century — a range of ~125-225 is now the cautious consensus. The textual significance is unchanged; the *terminus ante quem* argument for Johannine composition is weaker than mid-20th-century scholarship assumed.

Text differs from later witnesses in no substantive way in the extant verses — important for the cumulative case that the Fourth Gospel's text was stable from a very early date. Provenance: purchased by Bernard Grenfell in Egypt in 1920, identified by Roberts among Rylands holdings in 1934.

Fragmentary in the extreme — about a hundred letters, far short of any meaningful textual comparison apparatus. Its weight in NT scholarship is symbolic and chronological, not text-critical. Frequently invoked in popular apologetics to far stronger conclusions than the data support.
