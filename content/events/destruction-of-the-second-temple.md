The Roman siege and sacking of [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]] by Titus (son of emperor Vespasian) in August 70 CE during the First Jewish-Roman War — recorded extensively in [[josephus|Josephus]]'s *The Jewish War* — culminating in the burning of the Herodian-expanded [[temple|Second Temple]], the destruction of the priesthood as an institution, and the end of the Jerusalem-centered sacrificial system. The event reshaped Judaism into the rabbinic tradition centered on synagogue and Torah-study, and is central to [[preterism|preterist]] readings of the New Testament's eschatological discourses (Mt 24; Mk 13; Lk 21; [[revelation|Revelation]]).
