The split within the [[catholic|Roman Catholic Church]] (1378–1417) during which two — and later three — rival popes simultaneously claimed the See of Peter: in Rome (Urban VI line), in Avignon (Clement VII line), and after 1409 in Pisa (Alexander V line). The crisis arose from the disputed election following the return of the papacy from Avignon and was resolved only by the Council of Constance (1414-1418), which deposed all three claimants and elected Martin V. The episode profoundly weakened the medieval papacy and laid intellectual groundwork for conciliarism and later Reformation challenges.
