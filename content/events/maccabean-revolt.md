The Jewish armed revolt (167–160 BCE) led by the priest Mattathias and his sons — most notably Judah Maccabee — against the hellenization program of the Seleucid king [[antiochus-iv-epiphanes|Antiochus IV Epiphanes]]. Triggered by Antiochus's outlawing of Torah observance and his profanation of the [[temple|Jerusalem Temple]] in 167 BCE, the revolt achieved the rededication of the Temple in 164 BCE (commemorated in Hanukkah) and led to the establishment of the Hasmonean dynasty. The events are narrated in [[1-maccabees|1]] and [[2-maccabees|2 Maccabees]] (deuterocanonical) and are the historical-critical key to the late chapters of [[book-of-daniel|Daniel]] (esp. Dan 7-8, 11).
