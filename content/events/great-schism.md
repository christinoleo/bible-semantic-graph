The formal break of communion between the [[catholic|Roman Church of the West]] and the [[orthodox-eastern|Eastern Christian patriarchates]] dated symbolically to mutual excommunications in 1054 between Cardinal Humbert (representing Pope Leo IX, who had already died) and Patriarch Michael Cerularius of Constantinople. The substantive issues — papal universal jurisdiction, the *Filioque* clause's addition to the Nicene Creed, leavened versus unleavened Eucharistic bread, clerical celibacy — had been accumulating since the fourth century. The break was not fully irreversible until the Sack of Constantinople in 1204 by the Fourth Crusade.
