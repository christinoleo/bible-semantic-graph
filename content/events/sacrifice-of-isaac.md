The Akedah (Hebrew "binding") narrates [[yhwh]]'s command to [[abraham]] to offer his son [[isaac]] as a burnt offering on [[moriah]], the command being rescinded at the last moment by a divine messenger.

The passage is one of the most-interpreted in the Hebrew Bible. Major readings include: a test of [[abraham]]'s faith ([[binding-of-isaac-as-test]]); a polemic against child sacrifice in the surrounding cultures; an etiological narrative for ram-substitution sacrifice; and (in Christian exegesis) a prefiguration of the crucifixion.

It is the climactic episode of the [[abrahamic-covenant]] cycle and is repeatedly invoked elsewhere in Scripture as the paradigmatic act of obedience.
