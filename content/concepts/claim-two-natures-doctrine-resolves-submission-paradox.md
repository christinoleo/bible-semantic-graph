Per [[chalcedonian-christology]], the one person of Christ subsists in two natures, divine and human, "without confusion, without change, without division, without separation"; the divine nature is impassible and does not pray or prostrate, while the human nature does both really and truly. Submission to the Father is therefore located in the assumed humanity — predicated of the one person *qua* human — and is not a denial of the consubstantial divinity.
