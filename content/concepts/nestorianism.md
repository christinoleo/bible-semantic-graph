The Christological position — named for [[nestorius]] of Constantinople (d. c. 451) and rooted in radical Antiochene exegesis — that in [[jesus]] the divine Logos and the man Jesus are two distinct *prosōpa* (persons) joined in a conjunction of will and grace rather than in one *hypostasis*. The diagnostic test was Mariological: Nestorius rejected calling Mary [[theotokos]] ("God-bearer") and preferred *Christotokos*, since (he argued) the eternal Logos cannot have a human mother — a verbal predication [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril of Alexandria]] read as splitting Christ into two subjects. Condemned at [[ephesus]] (431) and again at [[chalcedon]] (451), the position survives liturgically in the [[church-of-the-east]] (the so-called Assyrian "Nestorians," whose actual Christology modern ecumenical dialogue has substantially rehabilitated as orthodox-by-other-words). The doctrinal force of the condemnation is that salvation requires one subject of both natures: it is *God* who suffers in the flesh, not a human partner of God.
