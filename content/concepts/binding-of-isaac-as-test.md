The standard rabbinic and traditional Christian reading of the [[sacrifice-of-isaac]]: that [[yhwh]]'s command was never a genuine divine demand for human sacrifice, but a test (נִסָּה, Gen 22:1) of [[abraham]]'s faithfulness, with the outcome — the ram caught in the thicket — being the always-intended ending.

**Supporting evidence:** the explicit framing in Gen 22:1 ("God tested Abraham"); the broader Pentateuchal polemic against child sacrifice (Lev 18:21, 20:2-5; Deut 12:31); the narrative's careful framing of the divine messenger's intervention as the resolution rather than a surprise.

**Tensions in this reading:** the text never has [[abraham]] *know* he is being tested — his obedience is portrayed as unconditional. Kierkegaard in *Fear and Trembling* famously pushed back against the "test" reading, arguing that flattening the Akedah into a moral exercise empties it of its existential horror.
