If Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are each fully God and yet distinct from one another, common Islamic apologetic reasoning concludes there must be three gods — making the [[trinity|Trinity]] indistinguishable from polytheism ([[shirk]]). [[quran|Q]]5:116 reinforces the suspicion by depicting [[jesus|Isa]] denying that he and [[mary-mother-of-jesus|his mother]] were taken as gods alongside [[allah]] — frequently read as targeting a perceived Christian trinity of Father, Jesus, and Mary, evidencing that the Quran encountered (or constructed) a tritheistic version of Christian doctrine that it categorically rejects.
