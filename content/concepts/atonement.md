The structured means by which a holy God reconciles sinful humans to himself — in the Hebrew Bible through cultic sacrifice (Lev 16, Yom Kippur) and the maxim "the life is in the blood" (Lev 17:11), and in the New Testament centred on [[crucifixion-of-jesus|the cross]] as the once-for-all sacrifice (Heb 9:22-28; Rom 3:25). The Christian tradition has elaborated competing theories — penal substitution, Christus Victor, satisfaction (Anselm), moral influence — each cashing out the same scriptural data differently. Islamic theology rejects vicarious atonement outright (Q 6:164: "no soul earns evil but on itself"); the cross is therefore not only a Christology dispute but also a hamartiology dispute about whether sin requires substitutionary penalty or only repentant submission.
