A lament psalm in [[psalms]] opening "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" — quoted by [[jesus]] from the cross (Matt 27:46; Mark 15:34) and woven through the Synoptic passion narratives via its imagery of pierced extremities, dividing garments, and casting lots. Christian readers since the Patristic era have treated it as a direct messianic prophecy of the crucifixion; [[jewish-anti-missionary]] readers ([[tovia-singer]]) read it as Davidic lament with no messianic referent. The debate sharpens at Ps 22:17 (Heb. 22:17 / Eng. 22:16), where the Masoretic *kaʾari* (כָּאֲרִי, "like a lion") and the variant *kaʾaru* (כָּארוּ, "they pierced / dug") attested in some Dead Sea Scrolls fragments and the Septuagint (ὤρυξαν) yield radically different translations of the same consonantal frame. The textual variant itself is its own contested sub-question.
