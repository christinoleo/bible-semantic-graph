"*nəʾum YHWH lăʾadōnî šēb lîmînî* — YHWH said to my Adonai, sit at my right hand" (Ps 110:1) names two distinct figures both addressed by David as "Lord" — YHWH and "my Adonai" — and [[jesus]] himself presses the puzzle on the Pharisees (Matt 22:41-46, Mark 12:35-37): if Messiah is David's son, how does David call him *Lord*? Christian apologetic reads the verse as an Old Testament disclosure of a plurality of persons within the one divine name, the same Father–Son distinction the doctrine of the [[trinity]] later articulates.
