The Islamic apologetic claim — and the Christian counter-claim — concerning whether [[jesus]] prophesied [[muhammad]] (or, under the Christian reading, the Holy Spirit) in the Paraclete passages of [[gospel-of-john]] 14-16, with the Quranic prooftext [[quran]] 61:6: "And [mention] when Jesus, son of Mary, said: 'O children of Israel, I am the messenger of Allah to you, confirming what came before me of the Torah and giving good tidings of a messenger to come after me whose name is **Aḥmad**.'"

The Islamic case turns on a **philological substitution**: that the Greek παράκλητος (*paraklētos*, "Advocate / Comforter," translating Aramaic *menaḥema* in Syriac tradition) in John 14:16-26 was originally περικλυτός (*periklytos*, "renowned / praised"), which is semantically equivalent to the Arabic *Aḥmad* (أحمد, "most-praised," from root ḥ-m-d, the same root as *Muḥammad*). On this reading, Jesus literally names Muhammad in the Gospel — but Christian transmitters substituted the visually similar παράκλητος for περικλυτός.

The Christian case has three components:
- **Manuscript evidence:** every Greek manuscript of John reads παράκλητος. No manuscript has περικλυτός. The variant the Islamic argument requires has zero textual attestation.
- **Internal Johannine semantics:** Jesus describes the Paraclete as the *Spirit of truth* who "will dwell in you" (John 14:17), "will teach you all things" (14:26), and "proceeds from the Father" (15:26) — language that describes the Holy Spirit, not a future human prophet who would arrive five centuries later.
- **Philology:** *Aḥmad* and *Muḥammad* share the root ḥ-m-d but are not interchangeable forms. The Quranic passage could equally name any praiseworthy figure; identifying Aḥmad with the historical Muhammad is a back-projection.

A historical wrinkle worth flagging: the Quranic "Aḥmad" is not always read in Islamic tradition itself as a proper name. Some classical tafsir treat *aḥmad* in Q 61:6 as a common adjective ("more praiseworthy") rather than a name. The strong reading "this is the name *Muhammad*" is a later interpretive consolidation.

See [[ahmad-prophecy-islamic-reading]] and [[ahmad-prophecy-christian-reading]] for partisan treatments. The neutral textual fact is that Q 61:6 exists, the Paraclete passages exist, and there is no manuscript bridge connecting them.
