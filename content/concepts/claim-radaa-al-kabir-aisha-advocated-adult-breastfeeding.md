*[[sahih-muslim|Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim]]* 1453 records [[aisha|ʿĀʾisha]] narrating that [[muhammad|Muhammad]] instructed Sahla bint Suhayl to breastfeed her grown adopted son Salim to make him *maḥram* — and ʿĀʾisha thereafter endorsed the principle generally, while the other wives of Muhammad rejected it as a one-off concession (per *Muwaṭṭaʾ* and parallel hadith). The episode binds three problems together for Islamic apologetics: a hadith-attested instruction by Muhammad to a strange and contested practice, ʿĀʾisha's role as both narrator and generalizing advocate, and the foundational status of the text — all of which trigger [[islamic-dilemma|the dilemma]] over selective acceptance of the very canon Islamic apologetics elsewhere invokes against the Bible.
