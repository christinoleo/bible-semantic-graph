The argument that the existence of objective moral values and duties is best explained by a transcendent moral lawgiver. Classically formulated by Kant as a postulate of practical reason and reframed by [[william-lane-craig|Craig]] as a deductive case: if God does not exist, objective moral values do not exist; objective moral values do exist; therefore God exists. The argument turns on the contested second premise — whether moral realism is defensible — and on the contested first premise's challenge from secular meta-ethics (constructivism, error theory, evolutionary debunking).
