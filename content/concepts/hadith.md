The corpus of reports (*aḥādīth*) of the sayings, actions, and tacit approvals of [[muhammad]], transmitted with an *isnād* (chain of narrators) and a *matn* (content), and collected through the 8th–10th centuries. Hadith are the second textual source of Islamic doctrine and law after the [[quran]] and the primary witness to the [[sunnah]] — the normative example of the Prophet.

The Sunni tradition recognizes six canonical collections (*Kutub al-Sittah*): [[sahih-al-bukhari]] and [[sahih-muslim]] (the two *Ṣaḥīḥān*, regarded as most rigorously authenticated), [[sunan-abu-dawud]], [[jami-al-tirmidhi]], [[sunan-al-nasai]], and [[sunan-ibn-majah]]. The Shia tradition follows a different corpus, centered on al-Kulayni's *Al-Kāfī* and the *Four Books* (*al-Kutub al-Arbaʿah*). The discipline of hadith criticism (*ʿilm al-rijāl*, *muṣṭalaḥ al-ḥadīth*) grades each report's chain (*ṣaḥīḥ*, *ḥasan*, *ḍaʿīf*) — yet Western historical-critical scholarship since [[ignaz-goldziher]] and [[joseph-schacht]] has argued that many *isnāds* are retrojections from later legal debate, a thesis sharply contested by Islamic scholarship.
