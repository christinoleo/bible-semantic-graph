A contingency-form cosmological argument: (i) everything that exists has an explanation of its existence, either in the necessity of its own nature or in an external cause (principle of sufficient reason); (ii) if the universe has an explanation, that explanation is a transcendent personal cause (since the universe is contingent — it could have failed to exist); (iii) the universe exists; therefore (iv) the universe has an explanation, which is a transcendent personal cause — identified by classical theology as God. Distinguished from the [[kalam-cosmological-argument|Kalam]] in not requiring the universe to have begun to exist; the contingency form goes through even on an eternally past universe.
