The doctrine that the one God of the [[shema-deut-6-4|Shema]] is **one in substance and three in persons** — *una substantia, tres personae* (Tertullian) / *mia ousia, treis hypostaseis* (the Cappadocians) — Father, Son ([[jesus]]), and Holy Spirit, co-equal, co-eternal, distinct in their relations of origin yet numerically one in essence. Formulated against [[arianism]] at [[nicaea-i]] (325) with the term *homoousios* ("of the same substance") and extended at [[constantinople-i]] (381) against the *pneumatomachoi* to affirm the Spirit's full divinity, the doctrine is presupposed by [[chalcedonian-christology]] and remains the defining mark of catholic Christianity over against the unitarian theologies of [[islamic-christology]], [[jehovahs-witness]] doctrine, and modern biblical unitarianism. Patristic exegesis grounds it in the triadic patterns of the New Testament — the baptismal formula of [[claim-matt-28-19-trinitarian-baptismal-formula|Matt 28:19]], the Father–Son–Spirit [[claim-matt-3-16-17-trinitarian-theophany-at-baptism|theophany at Jesus's baptism]], the Johannine [[claim-john-10-30-and-father-are-one|"I and the Father are one"]] — read as the unfolding of a monotheism already plural in [[deuteronomy]]'s *eḥad*.
