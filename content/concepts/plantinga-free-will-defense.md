[[alvin-plantinga|Plantinga]]'s response (*God, Freedom, and Evil*, 1974): for [[mackie-logical-problem-of-evil|Mackie's argument]] to succeed, it must be impossible for a world to contain both God and evil. But it is *possibly* true that every creaturely essence suffers from "transworld depravity" — that in every world where such an essence exists with significant freedom, it commits some moral evil. If so, even an omnipotent God could not actualize a world containing free creatures and no moral evil. Therefore the existence of evil is *logically consistent* with the existence of God. Plantinga frames the move as a defense, not a theodicy — it shows consistency, not actual divine reasons. Widely (including by Mackie himself in print) regarded as having decisively refuted the *logical* problem of evil — though the *evidential* form ([[rowe-evidential-problem-of-evil|Rowe]]) remains live.
