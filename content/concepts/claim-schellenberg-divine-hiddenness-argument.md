Schellenberg's formal argument: (1) If a perfectly loving God exists, no finite person capable of meaningful relationship with God who is *non-resistant* (not actively rejecting Him) is in a state of nonbelief in God's existence; (2) nonresistant nonbelievers do exist; (3) therefore a perfectly loving God does not exist. Premise (1) follows from the analysis of perfect love as always-open-to-relationship; premise (2) is conceded by virtually all philosophers — sincere atheists exist. The argument's force comes from its tighter structure than the [[problem-of-evil|PoE]]: no judgment about *amounts* of evil is required. Standard Christian responses: epistemic distance enables genuine faith; greater goods require initial hiddenness; nonresistant nonbelievers may not exist as defined.
