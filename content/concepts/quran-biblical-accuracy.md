The comparative question of whether the [[quran]]'s retelling of biblical persons and events matches the underlying biblical record — and if not, what the discrepancies imply. Notable cases include Q3:35–37 / Q19:28 calling [[mary-mother-of-jesus|Mary]] "daughter of Imran" and "sister of Aaron" (apparently conflating her with Miriam sister of Moses, who lived ~1300 years earlier); Q28 / Q40 placing Haman as Pharaoh's vizier (Haman is a Persian-court figure in Esther, ~1000 years later); and Q18:83–98 narrating Dhul-Qarnayn (commonly identified with Alexander the Great) as a believing servant of Allah. Christian apologetics treats these as systematic evidence that the Quran retells biblical material from oral hearsay rather than from direct textual or divine access.
