David Chalmers's distinction (*The Conscious Mind*, 1996) between the "easy" problems of consciousness (explaining cognitive functions in terms of neural mechanism) and the "hard" problem — explaining why there is *subjective experience at all*, why neural processing is accompanied by qualia and "what-it-is-likeness" (Nagel, 1974) rather than proceeding "in the dark." The hard problem opens an explanatory gap that no functional or physical account closes: even a complete neuroscience leaves the phenomenology unexplained. The gap grounds the [[moreland-argument-from-consciousness|argument from consciousness]] by showing physicalist accounts of mind are structurally incomplete.
