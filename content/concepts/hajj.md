The annual pilgrimage to the Kaʿba in [[mecca]] during the month of Dhū al-Ḥijja, the fifth of the five Pillars of [[islam]] and obligatory once in a lifetime on every able Muslim with the means (Q 3:97). Rites include *iḥrām* (the consecrated state and white garment), *ṭawāf* (sevenfold circumambulation of the Kaʿba), *saʿy* (running between Ṣafā and Marwa, commemorating Hagar's search for water), the standing at ʿArafa, the stoning of the *jamarāt* at Mina, and the festival sacrifice (*ʿīd al-aḍḥā*) linked to [[abraham]]'s offering. Islamic tradition traces the rites to Abraham and Ishmael's building of the Kaʿba; the [[quran]] reframes them as monotheistic acts purged of the pre-Islamic Quraysh accretions. Christian apologists note that the rite is absent from [[jesus]]'s teaching — a fact invoked by Muslim defenders as a paradigm case of [[progressive-sharia]] (a legitimate later-prophet addition, not a contradiction of the earlier message).
