Q 9:5, from the late-Medinan *Sūrat at-Tawba*, commands the killing of polytheists once the sacred months have passed; classical Sunni *tafsīr* (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr) treats it as abrogating up to 124 earlier verses of patience and forbearance — including the famously cited Q 2:256 "no compulsion in religion." The argument in [[answering-jihad]]: on the [[naskh-abrogation]] principle of "the later overrides the earlier," the Quran's final position on relations with non-Muslims is the sword verse, and the tolerance verses cited by modern apologists are doctrinally voided in the classical reading.
