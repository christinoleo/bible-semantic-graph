The dilemma articulated in Plato's *Euthyphro* 10a: either (a) acts are good *because* God commands them — making morality arbitrary (had God commanded cruelty, cruelty would be good); or (b) God commands them *because* they are good — making the standard of goodness independent of God, and thereby [[claim-no-grounding-objective-morality-without-god|undermining]] the [[moral-argument|moral argument]]'s first premise. Either way, divine command theory fails to provide a foundation for morality that is both binding and non-arbitrary.
