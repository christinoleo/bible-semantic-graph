[[william-lane-craig|Craig]]'s position: God exists *timelessly* sans creation but *temporally* subsequent to creation. Two arguments support this: (i) given an A-theory (tensed) of time, an omniscient God must know tensed facts ("the universe is now 13.8 billion years old"), which entails God's temporal location; (ii) God's causal interactions with temporal events — answering prayer, becoming incarnate — require Him to be temporally related to those events. The view rejects both pure-timeless eternalism (Boethius, Aquinas) and pure-temporal eternalism (process theism), and is distinctive within evangelical analytic theology.
