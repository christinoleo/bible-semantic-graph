An apologetic strategy developed by [[gary-habermas]] and [[michael-licona]] (and popularized in a Muslim-Christian register by [[nabeel-qureshi]]) which defends the bodily [[resurrection-of-jesus]] by appealing only to historical data conceded by the broad consensus of critical New Testament scholarship — including skeptics — so that the conclusion does not depend on biblical inerrancy. The standard six "facts": (i) Jesus's death by crucifixion; (ii) the disciples' belief that he had appeared to them risen; (iii) [[paul]]'s conversion from persecutor; (iv) [[james-brother-of-jesus|James]]'s conversion from skeptic; (v) the empty tomb; (vi) the early dating of the [[claim-1-cor-15-creed-earliest-witness|1 Cor 15 creed]]. The argument runs as inference to best explanation: of all hypotheses (legend, hallucination, swoon, fraud), bodily resurrection accounts for the data most economically.
