Reads Deut 6:4's *ʾeḥād* ("one") as numerical / absolute unity — denying any plurality-of-persons in the one God of Israel — and reads the plural form *ʾĕlōhîm* as a plural of majesty grammatically governing a strictly singular referent (cf. the singular verb *yhwh ʾĕlōhênû* takes throughout the Torah). The argument's polemical force, classically formulated by Maimonides (*Yesodei haTorah* 1:7), is that the [[claim-deut-6-4-echad-compound-unity-christian-reading|Christian compound-unity reading]] is post-hoc back-projection of a [[trinity|Trinitarian]] grammar onto a text whose entire Pentateuchal and prophetic context refuses any pluralization of the divine essence — structurally the same critique [[tawhid|Islamic *tawḥīd*]] makes from a different scriptural base.
