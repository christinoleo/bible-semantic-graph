**Tawḥīd** (توحيد) — the absolute, indivisible unicity of [[allah]] — is the central, non-negotiable axiom of Islamic theology, confessed in the [[shahada]] ("there is no god but Allah") and concentrated in Surah al-Ikhlas ([[quran]] 112): *"He is Allah, the One; the Eternal Refuge; He neither begets nor is begotten; and there is none comparable to Him."* It forbids any partition, hypostasis, or filial relation within the Godhead, rendering [[trinity|Trinitarian]] and [[chalcedonian-christology|incarnational]] Christology categorically inadmissible as [[shirk]] — the gravest of sins. Classical [[classical-kalam|kalam]] developed it philosophically across the [[ashariyya|Ashʿarī]], [[maturidiyya|Māturīdī]], and [[mutazila|Muʿtazilī]] schools through the [[divine-attributes-debate|debate on divine attributes]], while modern [[islamic-apologetic]] mobilizes it as the governing premise of every [[islamic-critique-of-christianity|critique of Christian doctrine]].
