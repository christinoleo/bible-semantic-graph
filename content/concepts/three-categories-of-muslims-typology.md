[[nabeel-qureshi]]'s tripartite typology of contemporary Muslims in [[answering-jihad]]: (i) Sharia-oriented Muslims who treat the Quran-hadith-sira corpus as a totalising legal and political program — the segment from which jihadist movements recruit; (ii) Sufi and folk-cultural Muslims whose practice is mediated by mysticism, saint-veneration, and inherited custom rather than systematic jurisprudence — historically the demographic majority; (iii) post-Enlightenment secular Muslims whose religious identity is ethnic and ceremonial rather than doctrinal. The typology's apologetic point is that "is Islam violent?" cannot be answered univocally because the three populations relate to the source texts differently — but that *the texts themselves* (read with classical [[naskh-abrogation|naskh]]) read toward the first category, not the second or third.
