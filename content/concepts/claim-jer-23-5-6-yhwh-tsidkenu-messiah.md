Jeremiah promises a "righteous Branch" raised up for David who "shall be called *YHWH ṣidqēnû* — YHWH our Righteousness" (Jer 23:5-6). The Christian apologetic reading — pressed against rabbinic and unitarian glosses that treat the name as honorific or referring to Jerusalem — takes the divine Tetragrammaton, ordinarily reserved for [[yhwh]] alone, as predicated of the coming Davidic king himself, so that the messianic figure shares the very name of God: a prophetic disclosure of the plurality-in-unity the doctrine of the [[trinity]] later articulates.
