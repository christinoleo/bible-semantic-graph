If [[claim-tahrif-post-muhammad|the Bible was corrupted after Muhammad]], we should be able to detect that corruption by comparing pre-Muhammad manuscript witnesses to the text Christians and Jews read today. The comparison is empirically straightforward, and the evidence pre-dates the [[muhammad|7th-century]] hijra by centuries.

**Old Testament**: the [[1qisa-a|Great Isaiah Scroll]] (c. 125 BCE) preserves the entirety of Isaiah — *including* Isa 53's [[suffering-servant]] passage and the Christological texts the Quran's Christology must reject — in a form textually continuous with the Masoretic and Septuagint traditions Christians use today. **New Testament**: [[p52|P52]] (c. 125 CE) preserves John 18; [[p46|P46]] (c. 200 CE) preserves nearly all of Paul; [[p66|P66]] (c. 200 CE) preserves most of John; [[codex-sinaiticus|Codex Sinaiticus]] and [[codex-vaticanus|Codex Vaticanus]] (mid-4th c. CE) preserve the entire NT. By [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s lifetime, the [[bible]] had been textually stable for centuries across thousands of manuscripts in multiple language traditions (Greek, Latin, Syriac, Coptic, Ethiopic).

Post-Muhammad corruption is therefore empirically falsifiable: it predicts that the Bibles read after 632 CE should differ from these earlier witnesses. They do not — to within the well-catalogued range of minor scribal variants studied by [[historical-critical-scholarship|textual criticism]], the pre-Muhammad text *is* the modern critical text. The second horn of the [[islamic-dilemma]] is closed.

The Muslim apologist who preserves Quranic authority on the prior scriptures (rejecting [[claim-tahrif-pre-muhammad]]) and accepts the manuscript record cannot also hold that those scriptures contradict the Quran on Jesus's deity, crucifixion, and prophethood. The horns are closed at both ends.
