The revealed legal and moral order of [[islam]], derived primarily from the [[quran]] and the prophetic *sunna* of [[muhammad]] and elaborated through *fiqh* (jurisprudence) across the four Sunni legal schools (Ḥanafī, Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī) and the Jaʿfarī Shīʿī school. Distinguished within Islamic thought from *ʿaqīda* (creed) — the invariant content shared by all prophets ([[tawhid]], prophethood, judgement) — by being community-specific and historically given, which underwrites the [[progressive-sharia]] doctrine that each prophet from [[abraham]] through [[moses]] and [[jesus]] to Muhammad received his own *sharīʿa* (Q 5:48). Covers ritual ([[hajj]], prayer, fasting), personal status, commercial dealings, criminal penalties (*ḥudūd*), and statecraft.
