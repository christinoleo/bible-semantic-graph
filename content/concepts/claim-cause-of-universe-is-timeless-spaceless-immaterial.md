If the cause produces space, time, and matter, it cannot itself be spatial, temporal, or material — a cause of X cannot itself be X without precondition (otherwise it would need a cause). The cosmic cause must therefore be timeless (eternal in the relevant sense), spaceless, immaterial, and unimaginably powerful. These attributes converge on the classical theistic conception of God, a convergence the [[kalam-cosmological-argument|Kalām]] exploits to identify the cause with [[divine-creation|God the Creator]].
