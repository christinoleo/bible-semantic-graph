The [[quran]] itself exists in multiple canonical Arabic textual traditions — the *qirāʾāt* (canonical readings) — most prominently Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim (dominant globally) and Warsh ʿan Nāfiʿ (dominant in North Africa), which feature differences in consonants, vowels, and occasionally entire words. The Uthmanic recension itself was a standardization that destroyed competing codices (notably Ibn Masʿūd's). The popular claim that the Quran is verbatim-uniform across history is itself a textually unsupported claim by Islamic critical scholarship's own standards.
