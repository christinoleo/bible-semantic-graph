Sabbatarian Christians (Seventh-Day Adventists, Seventh Day Baptists, some Messianic congregations) hold that the fourth commandment is moral, not ceremonial, and binding on Christians on Saturday as God created it. [[jesus]] kept it (Luke 4:16) and confirmed lordship *over* it, not abolition *of* it (Mark 2:28); *sabbatismos* remains for the people of God (Heb 4:9). The Constantinian shift to Sunday is, on this reading, a man-made innovation.

This position **empirically agrees** with the Islamic-apologetic descriptive claim — most Christians do not keep [[jesus]]'s Sabbath — but draws the opposite normative conclusion: the response is for Christians to return to Saturday-keeping, not to convert to Islam. The argument is internally christological (lordship of Christ over the Sabbath grounds its perpetuity, not its abolition) and does not concede any point about Muhammad or the Quran.

A subtle apologetic effect: Sabbatarian Christians remove the easiest leverage in the Islamic argument — but at the cost of conceding that mainstream Christianity is in error on this point. Most Christian apologists therefore answer the Islamic claim with `claim-sunday-replaces-sabbath` or `claim-sunday-worship-saturday-still-sabbath`, not with this position.
