To accuse [[paul]] of corrupting [[jesus]]'s message presupposes an independent, uncorrupted record of Jesus's actual teaching against which Paul can be measured — but the Muslim who rejects the [[gospel]] as Jesus's words has no such baseline, so the corruption charge collapses into something the speaker explicitly cannot know. The argument is presuppositional: the Muslim either accepts the extant Gospel as evidence of Jesus's teaching (and then must answer why Paul is more "deviant" than Q's contradictions with it) or denies it, in which case "Paul corrupted Jesus" is a claim made from no epistemic ground at all.
