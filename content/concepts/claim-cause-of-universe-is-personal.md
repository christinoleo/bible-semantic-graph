The cause of the universe must be timeless, immaterial, immensely powerful, and personal — argued by elimination, since impersonal physical causes presuppose the spacetime they would have to produce, and by the agency-of-time-initiation argument, since only a free agent can instantiate a temporal effect from a changeless state. This is what classical theists identify with [[divine-creation]] and the God of [[william-lane-craig]]'s natural theology.
