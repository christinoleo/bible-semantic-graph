The comparative theological problem: Islamic and Christian eschatologies share a remarkable inventory of figures and events — a false messiah ([[dajjal|al-Masīḥ ad-Dajjāl]] vs the [[antichrist]]), a returning Christ ([[returning-isa-islamic-eschatology|Nuzūl ʿĪsā]] vs the [[second-coming-of-christ|Second Coming]]), a global end-times government, a final battle, a thousand-year reign — but with the *roles inverted* on key points. The Islamic returning Jesus breaks the cross, abolishes Christianity, and forces all to submit to Islam ([[claim-returning-isa-of-islam-denies-deity-fits-antichrist-spirit|Q4:159, Bukhari 2222]]); the [[mahdi|Mahdi]] establishes a global caliphate. Christian apologetics (notably Joel Richardson) argues this inversion is theologically diagnostic: an "end-times Jesus" who denies his own deity matches the spirit of the biblical antichrist (1 Jn 4:3), and a [[claim-islamic-mahdi-fits-biblical-antichrist-profile|Middle-Eastern global ruler]] who beheads dissenters maps onto the Beast of Revelation.
