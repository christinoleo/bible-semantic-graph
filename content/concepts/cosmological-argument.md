The family of arguments inferring God's existence from features of the cosmos as a whole — that it exists, that it began, or that it is contingent. Major variants include the [[kalam-cosmological-argument|Kalam]] (the universe began to exist, things that begin have causes, so the universe has a cause), the Leibnizian / contingency form (the universe could have failed to exist, so it requires an external sufficient reason), and the Thomistic first-cause form (Aquinas's Second Way). All share the strategy of treating the cosmos itself as evidence of a being beyond it.
