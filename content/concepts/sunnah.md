The normative example — sayings, actions, and tacit approvals — of [[muhammad]], regarded in Sunni Islam as a binding source of religious and legal authority second only to the [[quran]]. The Quran's repeated command to "obey Allah and obey the Messenger" (Q 4:59, 4:80, 33:21) is the textual ground for *sunnah*'s normative status; the [[hadith]] corpus is its textual witness.

Distinct in concept from *ḥadīth* though often conflated: a single report (*ḥadīth*) is a transmitted text, while the *sunnah* is the underlying normative pattern that the reports collectively preserve. The classical jurists (*ʿulamāʾ*) derived ritual practice, family law, criminal law, and political theory from the sunnah via *fiqh*; modern reform movements ([[muhammad-abduh]], the Salafi turn, modernist Quranism) contest the scope of sunnah's authority and the reliability of its hadith witnesses.
