Michael Rea responds to [[claim-schellenberg-divine-hiddenness-argument|Schellenberg's hiddenness argument]] by denying its underlying picture of [[divine-love]]: silence and felt absence are normal modes of presence within genuine personal relationships — as in [[parent-child-relationship|parent-adolescent dynamics]] or the [[contemplative-prayer|dark night traditions]] of Christian mysticism — and respect rather than violate the addressee's [[human-personhood|personhood]]. The demand for *constant overt evidence* mistakes the relational form of God's love, projecting onto [[existence-of-god|the God of theism]] a [[divine-personality|personality]] He need not have.
