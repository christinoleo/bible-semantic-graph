The Islamic legal-theological controversy over *raḍāʿ al-kabīr* (suckling of the adult), originating in a hadith preserved in *[[sahih-muslim|Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim]]* 1453: when Sahla bint Suhayl asked [[muhammad|Muhammad]] how to handle her adult adopted son Salim — left a non-*mahram* after the Quranic abrogation of adoption — Muhammad instructed her to breastfeed him to establish milk-kinship. [[aisha|ʿĀʾisha]] uniquely among Muhammad's wives endorsed this as a general principle and reportedly used it to permit specific men into her quarters; the other wives rejected it as a one-time concession. The hadith resurfaced in 2007 when an Egyptian scholar (Ezzat Atiyya) issued a fatwa applying it to mixed workplaces, prompting widespread Muslim condemnation — itself a [[claim-cultural-defense-self-undermining-modern-rejection|tell that modern Muslims reject the foundational practice]].
