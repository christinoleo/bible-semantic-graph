The fourth and longest of the [[book-of-isaiah]] Servant Songs (Isa 52:13–53:12), portraying a figure "despised and rejected," "wounded for our transgressions," and "led like a lamb to the slaughter," whose suffering somehow atones for the iniquity of "the many." It is the single most contested prophetic passage in modern Jewish–Christian polemic and the central proof-text marshalled by [[christian-apologetic]] writers ([[michael-brown]]) for a pre-Christian expectation of a vicariously suffering Messiah, and by [[jewish-anti-missionary]] writers ([[tovia-singer]]) for a corporate-Israel referent rooted in the surrounding chapters of Isaiah. The dispute turns on the identity of the servant, the function of the suffering, and whether the chapter was read messianically in pre-Christian Judaism. Partisan readings live in their own Argument Nodes; this Concept names the passage neutrally.
