[[alvin-plantinga|Plantinga]]'s central thesis: belief in God is *properly basic* — it can be warrant-conferring without inferential support from prior premises — in the same epistemic class as belief in other minds, the past, or the external world. The thesis draws on Calvin's *sensus divinitatis* (a faculty by which humans naturally form beliefs about God under appropriate conditions) and Thomas Reid's common-sense epistemology against the [[evidentialism|evidentialist]] requirement of inferential justification. The "Great Pumpkin objection" (any belief could be claimed properly basic) is answered by appeal to [[claim-proper-functionalism-warrant-theory|proper functionalism]]: a basic belief is warranted only if produced by a cognitive faculty functioning properly in its design environment.
