The second premise of [[craig-moral-argument|Craig's moral argument]]: moral realism is true — there really are mind-independent moral facts. Defended by direct moral intuition (the wrongness of torturing innocents for fun is not a cultural convention) and by the universality and convergence of core moral judgments across cultures. The premise is granted by both naturalist moral realists (Wielenberg, Shafer-Landau) and theists; the dispute is over the *grounding* — making the [[claim-no-grounding-objective-morality-without-god|first premise]] the decisive battleground.
