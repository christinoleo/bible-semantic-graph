The [[catholic|Roman Catholic]] doctrine — articulated using Aristotelian *substance / accidents* metaphysics by [[thomas-aquinas|Thomas Aquinas]] and dogmatized at the Fourth Lateran Council (1215) and the Council of Trent (1551) — that in the Eucharistic consecration the entire *substance* of bread and wine is converted into the *substance* of the Body and Blood of [[jesus|Christ]] while only the *accidents* (appearance, taste, weight) of bread and wine remain. Rejected by [[protestant|Protestants]] in favor of various positions (Lutheran sacramental union, Reformed spiritual presence, Zwinglian memorialism); [[orthodox-eastern|Orthodox]] affirm real presence without the Aristotelian apparatus (*metabole*).
