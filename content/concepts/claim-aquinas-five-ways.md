[[thomas-aquinas]]'s five demonstrations of God's existence in *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 2, a. 3, the canonical scaffolding of scholastic and Catholic natural theology: (i) the argument from motion (an unmoved mover is required); (ii) from efficient causation (a first uncaused cause); (iii) from contingency and necessity (a being existing of necessity); (iv) from gradation of perfection (a maximally perfect being); (v) from teleology / governance of natural things (an intelligent orderer). Each Way concludes with the formula "and this everyone calls God." Evangelical classical apologetics (Geisler in particular) reads the Five Ways as recoverable, valid, and a pre-evangelistic foundation; presuppositional apologetics ([[cornelius-van-til]]) rejects natural theology as conceding too much to autonomous reason.
