The Christian "transfer to Sunday" argument relies on apostolic-era texts (Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:2) that describe a *gathering* on the first day, not a *transfer of Sabbath obligation*. The strong identification of Sunday with the Sabbath comes much later — Constantine's civic edict establishing the *dies Solis* as a day of rest in 321 CE, formalized through 4th-5th-century councils. The pagan solar context is not incidental: Christianity absorbed solar imagery (*Sol Invictus*, *Sol Iustitiae*) on the same day.

The argument: this is exactly the kind of post-prophetic alteration [[tahrif]] predicts. Jesus and his disciples kept Saturday; the Christian church gradually shifted, under imperial and assimilationist pressure, to a day whose pagan connotations were politically convenient. The "Lord's Day transfer" is a retroactive theology built on a fait accompli.

That Christian Sabbatarians make a structurally similar historical critique (see `parallels_with` to [[claim-saturday-sabbath-still-binding]]) — without becoming Muslim — shows the historical force of the case, even if its theological conclusion is contested.
