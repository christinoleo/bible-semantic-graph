The Eastern Christian — preeminently [[orthodox-eastern|Orthodox]] — soteriological doctrine that the goal of salvation is participation in the divine life: as Athanasius put it, "God became human that humans might become god." Developed by the Cappadocians, [[athanasius|Athanasius]], Maximus the Confessor, and refined by Gregory Palamas in the fourteenth-century Hesychast controversy via the essence-energies distinction — humans participate in God's *energies* but never in his unshared *essence*. Sharply contrasts with the dominant Western soteriologies (Reformation forensic justification, Roman Catholic infused grace).
