Standard Big Bang cosmology dates the observable universe to a finite past beginning approximately 13.8 billion years ago. The Borde-Guth-Vilenkin theorem (2003) extends this: any universe whose average expansion rate is positive must have a past spacetime boundary — a result that applies to inflationary multiverse models as well as to single-universe cosmologies. Empirical cosmology thus independently supports the second premise of the [[kalam-cosmological-argument|Kalām]] that the [[claim-universe-began-to-exist|universe began to exist]].
