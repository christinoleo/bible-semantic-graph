Some Muslim apologists, recognizing that the Quran appeals to the [[injil|Injil]] and [[torah|Torah]] as authoritative *in Muhammad's own century* (Q 5:47, Q 7:157, Q 10:94), shift the [[tahrif]] thesis forward: the prior scriptures were intact when [[muhammad]] taught the Ahl al-Kitāb to consult them, and were *subsequently* altered — to suppress the [[ahmad-prophecy]], to inflate [[pauline-christology]], to introduce the crucifixion as a literal historical event. This preserves the Quran's coherent appeal to the previous scriptures while still explaining the divergence between the [[bible|current Bible]] and Quranic claims.

The position fits dialogical and Sufi strands more comfortably than the classical-kalam line — but it sets up the second horn of the [[islamic-dilemma]]: see [[claim-pre-muhammad-manuscripts-match-extant-bible]].
