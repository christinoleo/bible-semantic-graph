The argument that everything which begins to exist has a cause, that the universe began to exist, and therefore the universe has a cause — identified by classical theists with [[divine-creation]]. Formulated by [[al-ghazali]] and the [[classical-kalam]] tradition against Aristotelian eternalism and revived in late-20th-century analytic philosophy of religion by [[william-lane-craig]], who pairs the second premise with Big Bang cosmology and Hilbert's-hotel-style objections to an actual infinite.
