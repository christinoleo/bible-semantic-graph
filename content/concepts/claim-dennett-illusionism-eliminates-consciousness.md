Daniel Dennett argues that phenomenal consciousness — qualia, the "hard problem," what-it-is-likeness — is an illusion generated by the brain's introspective mechanisms, not a feature of reality that requires explanation. There is no "Cartesian theater" where qualia are exhibited; there are only cognitive processes that *report* having qualia. Consciousness can be exhaustively analyzed via "heterophenomenology" — taking the reports as data without granting them ontological privilege. The position dissolves the [[claim-hard-problem-of-consciousness-real|hard problem]] by denying its premise but is widely criticized as eliminating exactly what is to be explained.
