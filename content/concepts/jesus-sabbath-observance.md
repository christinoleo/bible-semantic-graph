[[jesus]] observed the Saturday Sabbath as a first-century Jew (Luke 4:16 — *kata to eiōthos*, "as was his custom") and engaged the major Sabbath controversies of his time (healing on the Sabbath, grain-plucking, *Mark 2:27-28*). After the resurrection, the disciples gather on the "first day of the week" (Luke 24:1, Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:2), and patristic sources by the early 2nd century identify Sunday as the *kyriakē hēmera* — the Lord's Day.

This produces a structural divergence: Saturday remains *the Sabbath* in scripture and Jewish practice; most Christians worship on Sunday. The interpretive question — was the Sabbath obligation transferred, fulfilled, abolished, or simply distinguished from a separate Lord's Day — divides Christians among themselves and creates the apologetic opening Islam exploits.

See `has_case` Nodes for the three internal-Christian positions and the Islamic claim. Note: the Christian readings disagree with each other before any of them engage the Islamic argument.
