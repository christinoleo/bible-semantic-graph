A human judge who waives the lawful penalty for a crime — "robbing a bank earns jail time, but I'll let you off with community service" — is not merciful, he is corrupt; he has broken the law he was appointed to uphold. The argument transposes: God's perfect holiness means he cannot simply *pardon* sin without dealing with it, since unilateral pardon-without-penalty is corruption masquerading as mercy. The cross is therefore not an "extra step" but the only structure where justice and mercy both stand.
