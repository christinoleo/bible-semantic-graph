The popular-culture claim — pervasive in post-Da-Vinci-Code religious skepticism — that the emperor Constantine personally commissioned the [[council-of-nicaea]] to produce the Bible by selecting four "approved" Gospels from a pool of eighty-plus competitors, suppressing the rest as heretical. Refuted on every empirical count: Nicaea did not deliberate the canon (see [[claim-council-of-nicaea-did-not-decide-the-canon]]); the four canonical Gospels were already the dominant liturgical and catechetical Gospels by ~150 CE (Irenaeus, *Adversus Haereses* 3.11.8, ~180 CE, treats their fourfold authority as settled); the alleged suppressed alternatives are mostly mid-second-century or later (see [[claim-apocryphal-gospels-are-late-second-fourth-century]]).
