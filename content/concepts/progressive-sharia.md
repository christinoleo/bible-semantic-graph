The Islamic doctrine that successive prophets — [[abraham]], [[moses]], [[jesus]], and finally [[muhammad]] — were each given their own *sharīʿa* (revealed legal code) suited to their community, so that the legal and ritual content of one prophet's message can differ from the next's without any of them being false. *ʿAqīda* (core creed: [[tawhid]], prophets, judgement) is invariant across the prophets; *sharīʿa* is not. Used by Muslim debaters as the standing answer to Christian "[[claim-muhammad-confirms-jesus-so-cannot-contradict-him|how can Muhammad contradict Jesus while confirming him]]?" — confirmation operates at the level of prophetic mission and core monotheism, not at the level of specific legal rulings (the [[hajj]] is not in Jesus's teaching, but that is not a contradiction).
