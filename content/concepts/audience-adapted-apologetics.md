The principle that the Christian apologist should pre-emptively phrase doctrinal claims in the formula least vulnerable to predictable twists by the interlocutor — same answer, different lead-in. The illustrative case from a [[muhammad-hijab]]-style exchange: refusing to say "fully God" of [[jesus]] (because the Muslim interlocutor will treat *fully* as a quantitative modifier and press toward [[partialism]] — "90%? what percent is the Father?") and saying "truly God" instead, which closes the same trap.

The technique is not deception; it is the recognition that theological vocabulary carries different connotations across [[traditions]], and that words like *fully*, *born in sin*, or *Son of God* import meanings in [[islam]] that they do not carry in [[christianity]]. Critics (especially [[presuppositional-apologetics|presuppositionalists]]) reply that re-phrasing concedes the opponent's framework instead of contesting it.
