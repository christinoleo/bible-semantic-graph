The Islamic claim relies on *not finding* a Gospel scene where [[jesus]] explicitly receives προσκυνέω labelled as "worship-of-God." But that test is the wrong one — a first-century Jewish monotheist's acceptance of προσκυνέω is itself the theological act, and the structural comparison inside the New Testament makes the category clear.

**Direct acceptances:** Matt 14:33 (after walking on water, the disciples *προσεκύνησαν αὐτῷ*, confessing "Truly you are the Son of God" — Jesus does not deflect); Matt 28:9 (women grasp his feet and worship — Jesus says only "do not be afraid"); Matt 28:17 (disciples worship on the mountain — Jesus moves directly to the Great Commission); Luke 24:52 (post-ascension worship endorsed by the narrator); John 9:38 (the man born blind says "Lord, I believe" and worships — Jesus accepts); **John 20:28** (Thomas: "*My Lord and my God!*" — Jesus, far from rebuking blasphemy, responds "because you have seen me, you have believed," explicitly endorsing the confession).

**The structural contrast:** in the same New Testament corpus, when proper monotheists are offered proskynesis they immediately refuse. **Acts 10:25-26**: Cornelius falls at Peter's feet and worships; Peter "lifted him up, saying, *I myself am also a man*." **Rev 19:10 and 22:8-9**: John falls at the angel's feet; the angel says "*See that you do not do that! I am your fellow servant... Worship God!*" These NT writers know exactly what the Jewish monotheistic test for divine worship is, and they apply it. Jesus is the only figure who consistently receives proskynesis without deflecting.

The Islamic reading needs to either (i) deny that proskynesis directed at Jesus carries the theological weight it carries when Peter and the angels reject it — which makes the NT incoherent within itself — or (ii) accept [[tahrif]] across all six passages above plus Acts and Revelation, which is a much larger concession than the prostration argument originally needed. The simpler reading: the Gospel writers, and Jesus himself, accept what monotheist Jews would only allow if the recipient is God.
