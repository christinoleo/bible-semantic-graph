The philosophical and theological challenge of reconciling the existence of an omnipotent, omniscient, and omnibenevolent God with the existence of evil and suffering. Distinguished into the *logical* formulation (J. L. Mackie: the propositions are strictly incompatible) and the *evidential* formulation (William Rowe: gratuitous suffering renders theism improbable), with the logical version widely held to have been defused by [[alvin-plantinga]]'s free-will defense while the evidential version remains live. Classical responses partition into Augustinian (evil as privation, traceable to the Fall), Irenaean / soul-making (John Hick: suffering as the precondition of moral and spiritual maturation), free-will theodicies, and *skeptical theism* (we lack the standing to judge which goods justify which evils).
