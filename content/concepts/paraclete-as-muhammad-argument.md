The Islamic apologetic argument — popularized by Ahmed Deedat and standard in modern [[dawah|daʿwa]] — that [[jesus]]'s promise of "another *Paraklētos*" in [[gospel-of-john|John]] 14:16, 14:26, 15:26, 16:7 is in fact a prophecy of [[muhammad|Muhammad]] rather than of the [[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]]. The argument typically claims the original Greek read *Periklytos* ("the praised one" — semantically equivalent to *Aḥmad*, Q61:6) and was corrupted to *Paraklētos*. Refuted on textual-critical grounds: no manuscript witness anywhere attests *Periklytos*; and on contextual grounds: the *Paraklētos* of John 14 is explicitly the *Spirit of truth* who would dwell *in* the disciples (Jn 14:17), an identification incompatible with a future seventh-century human prophet.
