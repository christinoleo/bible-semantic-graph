Second-Temple Jewish norms (Mishnah Ketubot 5:2 and Tosefta parallels) record betrothal beginning around age twelve-to-twelve-and-a-half but consummation deferred to physical maturity, and Luke's narrative depicts [[mary-mother-of-jesus|Mary]] as already betrothed and old enough to engage Gabriel in a sustained theological dialogue (Lk 1:26-38), travel unaccompanied to Elizabeth (Lk 1:39-56), and bear a child to term. Even granting an age in the mid-teens, the parallel to [[aisha|ʿĀʾisha]] (consummation at nine, [[claim-bukhari-6130-aisha-pre-puberty|explicitly pre-puberty per Bukhari 6130]]) fails: post-puberty in betrothal is categorically not pre-puberty in marital consummation, and the central event in Mary's story is conception by the Holy Spirit, not consummation by a fifty-year-old husband.
