The [[quran]]'s narratives about [[jesus]], [[moses]], [[abraham]], [[mary-mother-of-jesus|Mary]], and the Old Testament prophets are recited by [[muhammad|Muhammad]] approximately six hundred years after the New Testament events and well over a thousand years after most Old Testament events — without any documentable chain of textual access to the original sources. Compounding this, the canonical Quranic *muṣḥaf* itself was only standardized under [[uthman|ʿUthmān]] c. 650 CE (after Muhammad's death) and is the product of a curation that destroyed competing codices ([[claim-uthmanic-burning-of-quran-variants|Uthmanic burning]]). The temporal-and-evidentiary distance between Quran and the events it retells is therefore vastly larger than between the New Testament and the events *it* records.
