Gospel narrative in which [[jesus]] is transfigured before [[peter]], [[james-son-of-zebedee]], and [[john-apostle]] on a high mountain (Matt 17:1-8, Mark 9:2-8, Luke 9:28-36) — face and clothes radiating divine light, [[moses]] and [[elijah]] appearing alongside, the Father's voice declaring "this is my beloved son." Pivotal christological event for the patristic and [[eastern-orthodox]] traditions, where the uncreated light seen on Tabor is read as a direct manifestation of divinity to creaturely senses. Foundational for [[theosis]] and for the later doctrine of divine energies articulated under Palamism. Western reception (Catholic, Reformed) typically treats it as a confirmatory theophany prefiguring the resurrection rather than as the locus of the energies/essence distinction.
