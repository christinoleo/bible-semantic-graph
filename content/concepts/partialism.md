The trinitarian heresy that [[trinity|the one God]] is composed of three *parts* — Father, Son ([[jesus]]), and Holy Spirit each constituting a fraction of the divine essence — such that no single person is fully God on their own. Orthodox trinitarianism rejects this: each person possesses the whole divine essence ("the whole Godhead dwells bodily" — Col 2:9), not a third of it. The error is the inverse of [[modalism]] (which collapses the persons) — partialism over-divides them. In contemporary inter-religious debate, Christians wary of [[claim-fully-god-language-invites-partialism-charge|"fully God" being twisted toward partialism]] sometimes prefer the formula "[[claim-truly-god-not-fully-god-avoids-partialism-trap|truly God]]" instead.
