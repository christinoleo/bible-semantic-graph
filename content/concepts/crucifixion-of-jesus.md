The execution of [[jesus]] by Roman crucifixion under [[pontius-pilate]] (~30-33 CE). Asserted by all four canonical Gospels, by [[paul]] in the earliest layer of NT material (the pre-Pauline creed embedded in 1 Cor 15:3-8, datable to within 3-5 years of the event), and by hostile extrabiblical sources (Tacitus *Annales* 15.44; Josephus *Antiquities* 18.63-64, *Testimonium Flavianum* with disputed Christian interpolation).

The historical event has near-universal acceptance across critical scholarship — including non-Christian historians and Jewish scholars (Geza Vermes, Paula Fredriksen). The "criterion of embarrassment" weighs heavily: an early Jewish movement would not have invented a humiliating crucifixion for its claimed messiah unless it had actually occurred. Bart Ehrman: "one of the most secure facts in the history of early Christianity."

The **theological interpretation** of the crucifixion is, by contrast, contested at every level:
- Within Christianity: penal substitution, Christus Victor, moral exemplar, recapitulation — the Atonement is one of the most variously interpreted Christian doctrines.
- Across traditions: Christian (atoning death, vindicated by resurrection), Jewish (a historical execution of one of several messianic claimants; not theologically constructive), Islamic ([[quran]] 4:157 denies the event in fact: "they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them").

This is the only major event of the Gospel narratives that the Quran explicitly contradicts on **historical-factual** grounds rather than merely interpretive ones. That makes the crucifixion the sharpest test case for [[tahrif]] — for the Quran to be right, the entire Gospel tradition plus Paul plus Tacitus plus Josephus must be wrong on the brute fact.

See [[jesus-crucifixion-islamic-reading]] and [[jesus-crucifixion-christian-reading]] for the partisan treatments.
