The opening move of the [[twelve-points-of-christianity-argument-framework|twelve-point]] framework: the claim "there is no objective truth" is itself offered as an objective truth, and "all truth is culturally constructed" is offered as a culture-transcending claim — both self-refute. Relativism cannot be coherently asserted; therefore at least some objective truths are knowable; therefore the question "is theism true?" is a real question with a real answer rather than a private preference. The argument's apologetic role is methodological — it clears the epistemic field on which the subsequent eleven points are argued — rather than positively theistic.
