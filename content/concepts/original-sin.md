The doctrine that the sin of [[adam|Adam]] in the [[fall-of-man|Edenic fall]] (Gen 3) was not merely his own but is inherited by — or imputed to — all his descendants, producing a universal condition of guilt and corruption requiring redemption. Articulated systematically by [[augustine-of-hippo|Augustine]] against Pelagius and refined in scholastic and Reformed federal-headship theology ([[pauline-epistles|Romans]] 5:12-21). Eastern Orthodox theology rejects inherited *guilt* while affirming inherited *mortality* and proclivity to sin (ancestral sin). Pelagians and most modern liberals deny the doctrine altogether. Islam categorically rejects inherited sin: each person is born in *fiṭra* (natural innocence) and accountable only for personal acts.
