The discipline of [[quran]] interpretation. Classical *tafsir* combines lexical analysis of Quranic Arabic, intra-Quranic cross-reference (*tafsīr al-Qurʾān bi-l-Qurʾān*), prophetic and companion-attributed reports on the meaning of verses, *asbāb al-nuzūl* (occasions of revelation drawn from [[sira]]), and juristic application. The foundational Sunni works are al-Ṭabarī's *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* (10th c.) and al-Zamakhsharī's *Al-Kashshāf* (12th c., Muʿtazilī); Ibn Kathīr's *Tafsīr* (14th c.) became the standard *ḥadīth*-centered Sunni commentary; Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī's *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb* (12th–13th c.) is the major philosophical-theological commentary.

The internal pluralism of classical tafsir is itself a load-bearing fact: on contested verses (Q 4:157, Q 61:6, Q 19:33) the major commentators disagree, which is exegetically used to argue both sides of [[islamic-apologetic]] debates — see [[claim-ahmad-not-proper-name-in-classical-tafsir]] and [[claim-q19-33-future-eschatological-death]]. Modern Quranism, Salafi *tafsir bi-l-maʾthūr*, and Western Quranic studies each contest the classical synthesis from different angles.
