Deut 22:25-27 (the rape case) uses Hebrew *ḥāzaq* ("to seize by force") and stipulates an open-country setting, a victim crying for help, and the death of the perpetrator; Deut 22:28-29 uses a different verb, *tāpas* ("to grasp, manipulate, seduce") and pointedly adds *"they are found"* — i.e. discovered together — locating the case in covert consensual intercourse, not violence. The reading is confirmed by the parallel statute in [[exodus]] 22:16, which uses the unambiguous verb *pātāh* ("to seduce") for the same scenario; NIV-style English translations that render *tāpas* as "rape" in 22:28 collapse two distinct Hebrew verbs into one and create a moral problem the text does not pose.
