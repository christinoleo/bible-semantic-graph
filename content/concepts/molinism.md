The theological-philosophical system developed by Luis de Molina, S.J. (1535–1600) and revived in twentieth-century analytic philosophy of religion by [[william-lane-craig|William Lane Craig]] and Thomas Flint, which reconciles divine sovereignty with libertarian creaturely freedom by attributing to God a special category of *middle knowledge* (*scientia media*) — knowledge of *counterfactuals of creaturely freedom*: what every possible free creature would freely choose in every possible circumstance. God uses this knowledge to actualize the world in which His providential ends are best served by creatures' free responses — without coercing those responses. Sharply opposed by [[reformed|Reformed]] theology (compatibilism) and by classical Thomism (premotion).
