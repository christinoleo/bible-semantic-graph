Paul Moser argues that the [[existence-of-god|God of Christian theism]] is *purposively elusive*: He reveals Himself only to those who seek Him with the right moral-spiritual disposition — kenotic openness and willingness to surrender — not on the [[claim-schellenberg-divine-hiddenness-argument|epistemically neutral terms Schellenberg assumes]]. On this analysis the category of [[nonresistant-nonbelief]] is largely chimerical: apparent nonresistance often masks subtler moral resistance to the [[lordship-of-christ|cruciform Lord]] who would be encountered, so divine hiddenness reflects [[divine-love|loving pedagogy]] rather than absence.
