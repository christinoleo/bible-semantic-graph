Sunday and Sabbath are distinct categories that get conflated in both the mainstream Christian and the Islamic-apologetic readings. **Saturday is, and remains, the Sabbath** — the seventh-day commemoration of creation (Gen 2:2-3) and the sign of the Mosaic covenant. **Sunday is the Lord's Day**, the weekly memorial of the resurrection, on which Christians gather for the Eucharist. Going to church on Sunday does not redefine Saturday; the two days do different theological work.

The Christian who holds this view rejects both the Catholic-Reformed "transfer" story (Sunday is not the new Sabbath) *and* the Sabbatarian claim that Saturday rest is binding (the ceremonial Mosaic obligation is fulfilled in Christ — Heb 4:9-10). What remains is the festival logic of the resurrection appearance on the first day (Luke 24:1, John 20:19) as the natural anchor for corporate worship, not a relocation of Sabbath law.

Against the Islamic argument: the claim that "Christians moved the Sabbath" misdescribes Christian practice. Sabbath was not moved; a *different* day was instituted, for a *different* purpose. Both the Islamic critic and the Lord's-Day-transfer Christian agree on a description of practice that careful Christology denies.
