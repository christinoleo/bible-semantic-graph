The Christology — confessed today by the Oriental Orthodox communion (Coptic, Armenian, Syriac, Ethiopian, Eritrean, Indian Malankara) — that the incarnate [[jesus]] is "one *physis* of God the Word made flesh" (*mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē*), [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril of Alexandria]]'s formula, refusing the [[chalcedonian-christology|Chalcedonian]] "in two natures" as splitting Christ and refusing the [[eutychianism|Eutychian]] "out of two natures" as confusing them. Miaphysitism insists that the two are united *henōsis kath' hypostasin* (hypostatic union) into a single, composite, undivided nature in which divinity and humanity remain real and distinguishable in *thought* but not in *being* — "*ek dyo physeōn*, not *en dyo physesin*." Modern ecumenical agreements (notably the 1990 Geneva and 1993 Chambésy statements between Eastern and Oriental Orthodox) judge the Miaphysite and Chalcedonian formulas to be linguistically different but doctrinally equivalent, both rejecting [[nestorianism]] and [[eutychianism]] for the same reasons; the historic schism is increasingly read as terminological rather than substantive.
