[[john-lennox|Lennox]] argues that modern science arose specifically within Western Christendom — Galileo, Kepler, Newton, Boyle, Faraday, Maxwell — because the Judeo-Christian doctrine of creation supplied two preconditions naturalism cannot ground: that nature is *contingent* (so it must be studied empirically, not deduced *a priori* from necessity), and that nature is *rationally intelligible* (because it is the work of a rational Mind, "thinking God's thoughts after Him" — Kepler). Naturalism inherits this assumption that the universe is comprehensible to reason but cannot explain why, on its own ontology, this should be so.
