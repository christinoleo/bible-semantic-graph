The Islamic doctrine that prior revealed scriptures — the [[torah]] given to [[moses]], the [[psalms]] given to [[david]], and the [[injil]] (the original Gospel given to [[jesus]]) — were corrupted in transmission, so apparent contradictions with the [[quran]] reflect Jewish or Christian tampering rather than Quranic error. Anchored in Quranic charges that earlier communities altered the divine word (Q 2:75, Q 4:46, Q 5:13, Q 5:41, Q 3:78), classical [[islamic-apologetic]] scholarship splits between *taḥrīf al-maʿnā* (corruption of meaning / interpretation only) and *taḥrīf al-lafẓ* (corruption of the text itself). The strong textual reading was sharpened by [[ibn-hazm]] in 11th-century al-Andalus and hardened in modern polemic (Rahmatullah Kairanawi's *Iẓhār al-Ḥaqq*, [[ahmed-deedat]], [[zakir-naik]]), where it functions as the global escape valve for any tension between the received Christian and Jewish scriptures and Islamic claims. Christian and historical-critical responses press the manuscript record against the strong reading — see [[claim-nt-textual-stability-vs-tahrif]] and [[claim-patristic-citations-reconstruct-nt-independently]].
