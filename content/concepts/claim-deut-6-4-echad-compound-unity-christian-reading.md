Reads Deut 6:4's *ʾeḥād* ("one") as a compound rather than absolute unity — appealing to Gen 2:24 ("the two shall become *ʾeḥād* flesh"), Num 13:23 ("one *ʾeḥād* cluster of grapes"), and the plural form *ʾĕlōhîm* — so that the [[shema-deut-6-4|Shema]]'s monotheism does not exclude the [[trinity]]'s plurality of persons within the one divine essence. The argument's force is philological, not exegetical of the verse alone: the same root governs structurally plural unities elsewhere in the [[deuteronomy|Deuteronomic]] and Pentateuchal corpus.
