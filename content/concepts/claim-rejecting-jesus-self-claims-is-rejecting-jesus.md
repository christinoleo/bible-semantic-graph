If [[jesus]] himself claims to be the [[gospel-of-john|Son of God]] (Jn 3:16), the agent of the Father's will granting eternal life (Jn 6:38-40), and the final judge of mankind (Jn 5:22-29), then a "Jesus" stripped of these self-claims is not the historical Jesus but a different figure constructed from outside the Gospel record. The Muslim affirmation "we love Jesus" therefore affirms a re-narrated Jesus — the one Muhammad describes six centuries later — not the Jesus on record. The position requires that the Gospels themselves be unreliable on this central point, which loops back into [[islamic-dilemma|the dilemma]] over the [[claim-quran-defers-to-torah-and-gospel|Quran's deference to the Injil]].
