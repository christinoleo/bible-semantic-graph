The title — Greek for "God-bearer," Latin *Dei Genitrix*, English "Mother of God" — predicating of [[mary]] that the one she bore is, without qualification, God the Son. Dogmatized at [[ephesus]] (431) against [[nestorianism|Nestorius]] (who preferred *Christotokos*, "Christ-bearer," to avoid implying the eternal Logos has a human origin), the title functions less as a Marian doctrine than as a Christological one: the rule of predication is that whatever is said of either nature can be said of the one *hypostasis* — the *communicatio idiomatum*. To deny *Theotokos* is therefore to deny the unity of Christ's person, which is why [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril of Alexandria]]'s Twelve Anathemas press the title as the diagnostic test of orthodox [[chalcedonian-christology]]. Confessed in identical terms by Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Oriental Orthodox traditions; cautiously affirmed but rarely invoked by most Protestant confessions.
