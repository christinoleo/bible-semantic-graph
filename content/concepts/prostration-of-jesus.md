The Gospel reports that during Gethsemane [[jesus]] *fell on his face* (Matt 26:39 ἔπεσεν ἐπὶ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ; Mark 14:35 ἔπιπτεν ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς; Luke 22:41 θεὶς τὰ γόνατα — kneels rather than falls prostrate). The same posture appears in [[abraham]] (Gen 17:3), [[moses]] and [[aaron]] (Num 16:22), [[joshua]] (Josh 5:14), [[ezekiel]] (Ezek 1:28), and many ANE-cultic contexts. A culturally well-attested posture of submission, supplication, or theophanic encounter.

The interpretive question that divides traditions: does this posture position [[jesus]] *as a worshipper of God* (his Father, or in Islamic reading, Allah) — and therefore as not-God-himself — or is it consistent with a kenotic, two-natures Christology in which the human nature submits to the Father within the intra-trinitarian life?

[[islamic-apologetics-against-christianity|Islamic apologetic]] readings see the prostration as straightforwardly diagnostic: a god does not prostrate. Trinitarian Christian readings invoke [[chalcedonian-christology]]: Jesus's human nature submits while the divine nature reigns. Mainline biblical scholarship reads more historically — Gethsemane prostration is a Jewish-pious posture continuous with patriarchal and prophetic precedent, neutral on later metaphysical questions.

Each tradition's reading is itself a discrete argument; see the `has_case` Nodes below for the partisan treatments.
