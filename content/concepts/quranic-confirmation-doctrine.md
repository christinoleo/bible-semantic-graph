The repeated Quranic self-description as *muṣaddiqan limā bayna yadayhi* — "confirming what is between its hands," i.e. the previously revealed scriptures (the [[torah]], the [[psalms]], and the [[injil]] of [[jesus]]). The doctrine generates a recurring polemical pressure: if the [[quran]] confirms prior scripture, how can it also contradict it, and how is that contradiction explained without conceding [[tahrif]]? Muslim debaters (e.g. [[hamza-of-hamzas-den]]) typically resolve this by distinguishing the *speaker* (Allah confirms previous prophets through Muhammad's mouth, but [[muhammad]] himself never claims to *follow* Jesus's teaching) and by invoking [[progressive-sharia]] (each prophet had a locally distinct legal code), which together absorb apparent contradictions without invoking textual corruption.
