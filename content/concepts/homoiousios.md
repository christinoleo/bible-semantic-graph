The fourth-century semi-Arian formula — *homoiousios*, "of *like* substance" — distinguished from Nicene [[homoousios]] ("of the *same* substance") by a single iota, but doctrinally a different position: the Son is granted full divine likeness without full identity of essence, preserving a hierarchical relation to the Father. Promoted by figures like Basil of Ancyra and George of Laodicea as a middle path between strict [[arianism|Arian]] subordinationism and what they feared was Nicene modalism, the formula was the conceptual backbone of the homoiousian party in the post-Nicene controversies and the immediate target of Athanasius's *De Synodis*. Athanasius read the homoiousians charitably as nearly-orthodox; the Cappadocian settlement at [[constantinople-i]] (381) absorbed many of them by clarifying that *homoousios* names *identical* essence and the [[hypostasis-vs-ousia|hypostasis distinction]] handles the worry about modalism — making *homoiousios* doctrinally unnecessary. The iota of difference became proverbial in the West for hair-splitting; in fact what it indexed was a substantively different theology of the divine relations.
