The genre of biographical writing on [[muhammad]], assembled from prophetic reports but arranged as continuous narrative rather than legal-topical hadith collections. The foundational work is the lost *Sīrat Rasūl Allāh* of [[ibn-ishaq]] (d. ~767 CE), preserved through the redaction of [[ibn-hisham]] (d. ~833 CE); other early authorities include [[al-waqidi]] (Maghāzī) and [[ibn-saad]] (Ṭabaqāt).

For Western historical-critical scholarship the *sīra* corpus is the principal — and famously problematic — source for any non-confessional reconstruction of Muhammad's life ([[william-montgomery-watt]], [[patricia-crone]]'s revisionist critique in *Hagarism* and *Meccan Trade and the Rise of Islam*, Fred Donner's later corrective work). The relation of *sīra* narrative to the bare Quranic text is a recurring methodological pivot in the field.
