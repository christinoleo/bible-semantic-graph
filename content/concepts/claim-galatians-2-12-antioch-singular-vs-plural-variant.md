In Galatians 2:12 [[paul]] reports that before "*they came / he came* from [[james-brother-of-jesus|James]]," [[peter]] ate with Gentile believers; after their / his arrival he withdrew. The Greek manuscripts split between ἦλθον (plural — *they* came, a delegation) and ἦλθεν (singular — *he* came, a single envoy, by some readings James himself, more naturally an unnamed messenger). The choice affects the historical reconstruction of the Antioch incident — whether Peter capitulated to organised pressure or a single representative — but not Christian doctrine. [[wesley-huff]]'s paper assesses the manuscript evidence and the scribal-tendency arguments and lands a specific reading; the case illustrates the typical scale of meaningful NT variants.
