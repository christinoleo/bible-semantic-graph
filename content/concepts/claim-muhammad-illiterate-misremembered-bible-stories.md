Islamic tradition holds that [[muhammad|Muhammad]] was *ummī* (illiterate, Q7:157-158, Q29:48), receiving biblical content not by reading scripture but by recitation. The [[quran]]'s biblical narratives display the characteristic errors of oral retelling at distance: [[mary-mother-of-jesus|Mary the mother of Jesus]] is addressed as "sister of Aaron" (Q19:28) and "daughter of Imran" (Q3:35), conflating her with Miriam the sister of Moses ~1300 years earlier; Haman appears as Pharaoh's vizier (Q28:6, Q40:24), a thousand-year anachronism since Haman is a Persian-court figure in Esther; Dhul-Qarnayn (Q18:83-98), commonly identified with Alexander the Great, is presented as a monotheist servant of Allah, contrary to all extant historical record. These are precisely the kinds of errors a non-reading recipient would generate from imperfectly transmitted oral material.
