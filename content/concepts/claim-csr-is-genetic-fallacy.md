The cognitive-science-of-religion debunking argument commits the *genetic fallacy*: showing *how* a belief is formed says nothing about *whether* it is true. The same evolutionary-cognitive machinery that produces religious belief also produces mathematical, ethical, and scientific belief — yet no one concludes that mathematics is unreliable because we evolved to detect quantity. Justin Barrett (himself a developmental psychologist *and* a Christian) presses that the natural readiness for belief in God is consistent with — and in [[reformed|Reformed]] epistemology positively predicted by — the existence of a *sensus divinitatis* ([[claim-sensus-divinitatis-calvin|Calvin]], [[alvin-plantinga|Plantinga]]) implanted by the Creator. The very same data supports two opposite interpretations; CSR alone does not adjudicate.
