Sunni doctrine holds that prophets are *maʿṣūm* — protected from sin and major moral defect from birth — yet the [[shaqq-al-sadr|chest-splitting]] narrative explicitly states that a black clot was removed from [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s heart, framed in the *Sīrah* as "the share of Satan" within him. If no defect existed there could be no need to remove one; if a defect existed *ʿiṣma* is false. Islamic apologetics typically reads the clot allegorically rather than ontologically, but the hadith's plain sense binds it as a literal purification — leaving the doctrine of impeccability and the canonical narrative pulling against each other.
