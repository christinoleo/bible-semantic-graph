The argument that finite mental properties (subjective experience, intentionality, qualia) are best explained by a fundamental mind rather than by emergence from physical matter. Developed by J.P. Moreland and Richard Swinburne, the argument leverages the "hard problem of consciousness" — that physicalist accounts struggle to explain how subjective experience arises from neurological structure — to argue that theism, which posits mind as ontologically basic, has greater explanatory power than naturalism on this datum.
