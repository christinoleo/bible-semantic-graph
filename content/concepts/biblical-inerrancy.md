The doctrine that the [[hebrew-bible|Bible]] in its original autographs is without error in all that it affirms — including matters of history, science, geography, and theology — articulated in its dominant modern form by the Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy (1978). Held in stricter form by conservative evangelicals and confessional Reformed traditions; weaker forms (*infallibility* limited to matters of faith and practice, *limited inerrancy* in salvific matters only) are common in mainline Protestant, Roman Catholic, and Eastern Orthodox theology. The doctrine sits at the heart of the [[bible-vs-quran-preservation|comparative-preservation]] debate with Islamic apologetics.
