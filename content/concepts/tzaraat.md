The biblical Hebrew skin-condition (and analogous afflictions of garments and house-walls) regulated extensively in [[leviticus|Leviticus]] 13-14 and [[numbers|Num]] 12. Translated misleadingly as "leprosy" in older English versions but biologically distinct from Hansen's disease (true leprosy); rabbinic tradition treats it as a divinely-imposed *ritual* condition signaling moral fault (often *lashon hara*, slander). The afflicted person was quarantined outside the camp and required priestly inspection and an elaborate two-stage ritual of restoration; the cleansing of "lepers" by [[jesus]] (Mk 1:40-45; Lk 17:11-19) draws its force from this priestly background.
