A criterion in historical-Jesus research holding that a saying or event of [[jesus]] attested independently in multiple early sources (e.g., [[gospel-of-mark|Mark]], Q, M, L, [[gospel-of-john|John]], [[pauline-epistles|Paul]]'s pre-Pauline tradition) is more likely to derive from the historical Jesus than one attested in only a single source. Developed by F.C. Burkitt and refined by John P. Meier (*A Marginal Jew*) and E.P. Sanders. Frequently invoked in defense of the historicity of the [[crucifixion-of-jesus|crucifixion]] (attested across Mark, John, Paul, [[claim-josephus-confirms-execution|Josephus]], [[claim-tacitus-confirms-crucifixion-under-pilate|Tacitus]]) and of the disciples' belief in resurrection appearances ([[claim-empty-tomb-and-appearances|1 Cor 15 creed]] + four Gospels).
