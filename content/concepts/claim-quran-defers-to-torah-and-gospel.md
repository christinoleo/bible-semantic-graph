If [[tahrif]] is true — if the Torah and Gospel were corrupted before the Quran was revealed — then the Quran is incoherent when it tells [[muhammad]] himself that his coming is described "in the Torah and the Gospel that they have with them" (Q 7:157), and when it instructs doubters to ask "those who read the scripture before you" (Q 10:94). The Quran appeals to the *currently extant* Jewish and Christian scriptures as a confirming authority for its own claims. It cannot do that and simultaneously hold that those same scriptures were already corrupted at the moment of revelation.

The Christian (and historical-critical) argument: either (i) the scriptures were intact in the 7th century, in which case our textual record — earlier than the Quran by centuries via Dead Sea Scrolls, Septuagint codices, NT papyri — preserves what the Quran itself certified; or (ii) the scriptures were already corrupted, in which case the Quran's appeal to them is itself based on an error. The classical Tahrif doctrine cannot escape this fork.

Sub-arguments and primary-source treatments live in [[claim-nt-textual-stability-vs-tahrif]] and [[claim-nt-manuscript-stability-on-crucifixion]] — see those Nodes for the manuscript-evidence side. This Node is the *internal* refutation: Tahrif is unstable on the Quran's own terms.
