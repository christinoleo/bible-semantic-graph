If [[claim-tahrif-pre-muhammad|the previous scriptures were corrupted before Muhammad]], then the [[quran]]'s repeated commands to consult them are unintelligible. **Q 5:47** instructs the people of the Injil to "judge by what Allah has revealed in it" — present tense, current copy. **Q 5:68** declares Jews and Christians "stand on nothing" until they observe the Torah and the Injil — again, the books they then possessed. **Q 7:157** tells [[muhammad]]'s audience to find him *written* in the Torah and Injil they have. **Q 10:94** sends doubters to "those who read the scripture before you." **Q 3:3-4** describes the Torah and the Injil as divine guidance freshly affirmed alongside the Quran.

A revelation cannot coherently send its addressees to read a corrupted text as confirmation of itself. The pre-Muhammad-corruption thesis collapses the Quran's internal logic: either the Quran is wrong to recommend the prior scriptures (because they were already corrupted), or the prior scriptures were *not* corrupted at the time the Quran was revealed. The first horn of the [[islamic-dilemma]] is closed.

The Muslim apologist who insists on pre-Muhammad corruption must concede the Quran was misinformed about the state of the texts it endorsed; the Muslim apologist who preserves Quranic authority on this point must move to [[claim-tahrif-post-muhammad]] — and then face the second horn at [[claim-pre-muhammad-manuscripts-match-extant-bible]].
