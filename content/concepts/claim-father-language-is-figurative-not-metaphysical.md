The Christian refutation depends on reading "Father" in [[jesus]]'s prayers (Matt 26:39, Mark 14:36) as a metaphysical claim about Trinitarian paternity. But "Father" in Second Temple Jewish prayer is a *relational* metaphor for nearness, care, and authority — not generative paternity. The Quran itself describes Allah as *closer than the jugular vein* (Q 50:16) without that closeness implying a familial-metaphysical category. The same Jesus tells the disciples (Matt 23:9) that there is one Father in heaven and earthly fathers carry the name only by analogy — collapsing rather than securing a literal-paternal Christology.

The Islamic position is not that Jesus never used paternal language; it is that the language is figurative and the Trinitarian metaphysical inflation imposed on it by [[paul]] and the post-apostolic church is the corruption. Reading the prayer *as a first-century Jew* — addressing the closest possible relational image for God — does not commit Jesus to anything beyond what [[tawhid|Tawḥīd]] already permits. The dilemma assumes its Trinitarian conclusion to make the refutation work.

The argument concedes nothing about the prostration or greeting practice claims: those rest on the *form* of action (sujūd-like prostration, š-l-m greeting) which translates literally; "Father" rests on the *interpretation* of a metaphor which Christians read maximally and Muslims read minimally.
