Concrete cases of biological suboptimality argue against an intelligent designer's craftsmanship: the recurrent laryngeal nerve in mammals travels from the brain *down* through the chest and *back up* to the larynx — wasteful by inches in humans, by meters in giraffes; the vertebrate retina is wired with photoreceptors *behind* the blood vessels and nerve fibers, producing the necessary blind spot; vestigial structures (human appendix, the plantaris tendon, palmar grasp reflex, wisdom teeth, ostrich wings, whale pelvic bones); the panda's "thumb" is a repurposed wrist bone. Each is precisely what *unguided evolution from constrained ancestral material* would produce, and badly fits a designer optimizing from scratch.
