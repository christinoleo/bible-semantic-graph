The Christology — named for the elderly archimandrite Eutyches of Constantinople (d. after 454) — that the human nature of [[jesus]] was so wholly absorbed into the divine that after the incarnation there remains only one nature, "as a drop of vinegar in the ocean of the divinity." It is the misshapen extreme that the [[chalcedonian-christology|Chalcedonian Definition]] explicitly excludes with "*asynchytōs, atreptōs* — without confusion, without change": the humanity must remain real and complete, *homoousios* with us, or the incarnation does not save what it does not assume. Eutychianism was condemned at [[chalcedon]] (451) after being briefly rehabilitated at the so-called *Latrocinium* ("Robber Synod") of [[ephesus]] (449), and is rejected with equal vigor by [[miaphysitism]] — a point routinely overlooked when polemicists call Oriental Orthodox Christians "monophysites" without distinction. The doctrinal stakes are soteriological: a humanity dissolved is a humanity unhealed.
