Strong emergentism holds that consciousness *emerges* from sufficiently complex physical systems as a genuinely novel property — irreducible to but supervenient on the underlying physical substrate. Defended by C.D. Broad, Roger Sperry, more recently by Timothy O'Connor, the position accepts the hard problem's *existence* but denies that it requires theistic explanation: emergence is a natural feature of complexity, not a divine intervention. Theistic critics (Moreland) press that "emergence" here is little more than a label for the unexplained gap, not a mechanism — and that taking mind as fundamental in theism does substantive explanatory work that strong emergentism merely names.
