The deep applicability of abstract [[mathematics|mathematical]] structure to the physical world — [[eugene-wigner|Wigner]]'s "unreasonable effectiveness" — is anomalous on [[naturalism]], which has no principled reason to expect the contingent physical order to track necessary abstract truths, but is exactly what [[theism]] predicts when [[mathematical-realism|mathematical truths]] reflect the intelligible order of a rational [[creator]]. [[william-lane-craig]] and [[james-anderson]] argue that grounding [[abstract-objects]] and the laws of logic in the [[divine-mind]] explains both their necessity and their grip on creation, a fit that [[naturalism]]'s austere ontology cannot deliver.
