The five-letter mnemonic Geisler and Turek use to argue the historical reliability of the New Testament documents in *[[i-dont-have-enough-faith-to-be-an-atheist]]*: **S**ources are early (NT documents authored by eyewitnesses and their associates within decades of the events); **U**naltered transmission (manuscript abundance allows reconstruction of the original wording); **R**eliable archaeological corroboration (Acts in particular survives sustained archaeological scrutiny); **G**ospel writers tell the truth (the literary fingerprint is reportage, not legend); **E**yewitnesses confirm (and would have contradicted false claims). Together the five tests are presented as cumulative: the document survives each independently, and survival across the five is what justifies historical confidence prior to confessional commitment.
