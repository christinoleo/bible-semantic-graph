The Islamic apologetic claim that [[jesus]]'s prostration in Gethsemane (Matt 26:39, Mark 14:35) is direct textual evidence that he practiced Islamic *sujūd* — bodily submission to Allah — and therefore that his identity is that of a Muslim prophet, not the divine Son of orthodox Christology.

Three moves: **(i)** prostration (*pesher prosōpon*, Hebrew *hishtaḥawah*, Arabic *sujūd*) is the paradigmatic Islamic act of submission to Allah; **(ii)** in the Gospels this same act is performed *by Jesus* in his most theologically significant moment, in private prayer to his Father; **(iii)** a divine person does not submit, prostrate, or supplicate — therefore the canonical narrative itself witnesses against the divinity claim later imposed by [[paul]] and the Hellenistic church.

The argument is reinforced by the consistency of the Quranic portrait: [[quran]] 19:30-33 has the infant Jesus declare himself "the servant of Allah"; Q 3:51 has him commanding worship of Allah alone. The Gospel prostration is read as preserving, against Pauline Christological inflation, the original Muslim Jesus.

**Where it depends on contested moves:** the argument needs [[tahrif]] to dismiss the Johannine "I am" sayings, the kenotic logic of [[philippians]] 2:6-11, and the canonical NT's resurrection-glorification narratives. Without Tahrif, the prostration is just one piece of a NT christological picture that the same texts make far more complex than the Islamic reading allows.

The argument is also embedded in a wider apologetic methodology — see [[jesus-was-a-muslim-prophet]] for the parent claim and [[islamic-christology]] for the doctrinal framework.
