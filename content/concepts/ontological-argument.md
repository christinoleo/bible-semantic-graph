The family of *a priori* arguments inferring God's existence from the concept of God alone. Originated by [[anselm-of-canterbury|Anselm]] in the *Proslogion* — God is "that than which nothing greater can be conceived," and such a being must exist in reality and not merely in the understanding — and reformulated by [[alvin-plantinga|Plantinga]] in modal logic using the notion of maximal greatness across possible worlds. Famously challenged by Gaunilo's "perfect island" parody and by Kant's denial that existence is a predicate.
