Modern English Qurʾān translations frequently render *muhaymin* in Q 5:48 as "supreme authority" (or equivalent), but the Arabic — root *h-y-m-n*, cognate with the Aramaic-Syriac *mhayman* ("trustworthy") and sharing the semantic family of *amīn* — means "guardian / trustworthy witness." Substituting "supreme authority" smuggles the [[tahrif]]-licensing *corrector* reading into the text the Qurʾān itself never says, and is the move [[godlogic-apologist|Avery]] presses Muslim interlocutors to defend against the classical *tafsīr* tradition and the unambiguous use of *al-Muhaymin* as a divine name in Q 59:23.
