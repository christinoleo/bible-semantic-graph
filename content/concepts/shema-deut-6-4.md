The opening declaration of Deut 6:4 — "*šəmaʿ Yiśrāʾēl YHWH ʾĕlōhênû YHWH ʾeḥād* — Hear, O Israel: YHWH our God, YHWH is one" — and the daily liturgical core of rabbinic Judaism. The grammatical ambiguity of *ʾeḥād* (numerical "one" vs. unitary "one of") and the plural form *ʾĕlōhîm* are the philological pivots on which both the Jewish unitarian and the Christian Trinitarian readings hang. [[jesus]] himself cites the Shema as the first commandment in [[gospel-of-mark|Mark 12:29-30]], placing the Christian reading of [[trinity|triune monotheism]] inside the same monotheistic frame.
