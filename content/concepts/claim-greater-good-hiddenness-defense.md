Christian responses to [[claim-schellenberg-divine-hiddenness-argument|Schellenberg]]: divine hiddenness serves goods that *overt* divine presence would foreclose — the development of authentic faith (John Hick's "epistemic distance"); the protection of free moral response from coercive divine self-disclosure; the deepening of devotion through earnest seeking (Paul Moser); the cultivation of trust in dark times. Premise (1) of Schellenberg's argument — that perfect love precludes any nonresistant nonbelief — is rejected: perfect love can permit temporary epistemic distance for the sake of greater relational goods, just as human parents sometimes withhold immediate help to foster their children's growth.
