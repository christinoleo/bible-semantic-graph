The classical-kalam and Ibn Hazm-derived strand of [[tahrif]] holds that [[injil|the Gospel]] and [[torah|the Torah]] were corrupted **before** [[muhammad]]'s revelation — which is precisely why they conflict with the [[quran]] on [[jesus]]'s deity, crucifixion, and prophecies. The Quranic accusations against earlier scriptural communities (Q 2:75, Q 4:46, Q 5:13) are read as describing completed corruptive acts. On this reading the Quran's role is restorative: it does not engage the corrupted text as authoritative but offers the uncorrupted divine revelation in its place.

The position fits naturally with Ibn Hazm's *Fiṣal* (11th c. al-Andalus) and the modern Deedat-Naik polemic — but it sets up the first horn of the [[islamic-dilemma]]: see [[claim-quran-commands-following-extant-injil]].
