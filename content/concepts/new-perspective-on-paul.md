The school of [[pauline-epistles|Pauline]] interpretation initiated by E.P. Sanders's *Paul and Palestinian Judaism* (1977), developed by James D.G. Dunn, and popularized by N.T. Wright, which argues that Second-Temple Judaism was a religion of "covenantal nomism" — election by grace, Torah-observance as response — rather than the legalistic merit-religion against which the Reformers read Paul. On this reading, Paul's "works of the law" are primarily the *boundary markers* (circumcision, Sabbath, kosher laws) separating Jew from Gentile, not generic moral effort; and "justification by faith" is principally an ecclesiological claim about Jew-Gentile equality in Christ. Contested by Reformed and Lutheran defenders of the traditional reading (John Piper, Don Carson).
