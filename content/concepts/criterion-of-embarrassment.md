A criterion in historical-Jesus research holding that material the early church would have found doctrinally awkward — and thus had no plausible motive to invent — is more likely to be historical than convenient material. Standard examples: [[jesus]]'s baptism by [[john-the-baptist|John the Baptist]] (implies subordination), the cry of dereliction at the cross (Mk 15:34), the women as first witnesses of the empty tomb (in a culture discounting female testimony), and Jesus's confession of ignorance about the day of judgment (Mk 13:32). Articulated by John P. Meier and widely used across mainstream Historical Jesus scholarship.
