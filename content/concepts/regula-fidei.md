The *kanōn tēs pistēos* / *regula fidei* — the summary content of apostolic teaching transmitted in the church before and alongside the developed [[creed|creeds]] — invoked by the second-century [[patristic|Fathers]] ([[irenaeus]], [[tertullian]], Origen) as the interpretive frame for [[hebrew-bible|Scripture]] and the bright line between Catholic Christianity and [[claim-csr-natural-origin-of-religion|gnostic]] reinterpretation. The Rule is not yet a fixed verbal text but a stable doctrinal substance: one God, creator of all; one Lord Jesus Christ, his incarnate Son, born of the Virgin, crucified under Pilate, raised on the third day, ascended, to come again as judge; one Holy Spirit speaking through the prophets; one Church, one baptism for the forgiveness of sins; resurrection of the flesh and life everlasting. The [[old-roman-symbol]] and [[apostles-creed]] are direct verbal codifications of this material in the West; the [[nicene-creed]] and [[nicene-constantinopolitan-creed]] are its conciliar elaboration in the East. Patristic hermeneutics treats Scripture as readable *secundum regulam* — under the rule — so that the apostolic Trinitarian-Incarnational substance, not the bare letter, controls exegesis.
