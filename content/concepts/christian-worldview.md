The integrated framework of theological, ethical, epistemic, and anthropological commitments presupposed by Christian thought — creator/creature distinction, image of God, fall and redemption, scriptural authority, eschatological telos — treated not as separable doctrines but as a single hermeneutic that conditions every act of knowing. The term is especially loaded in [[reformed]] and *presuppositional* apologetics, where Abraham Kuyper's "every square inch" and [[cornelius-van-til]]'s transcendental argument cast worldviews as totalizing and mutually exclusive systems, each judged by its internal coherence and its capacity to ground the preconditions of intelligibility. [[patristic]] antecedents (Augustine's *credo ut intelligam*) supply the older form of the same intuition; modern critics object that "worldview" smuggles a German idealist framework into biblical theology that the Fathers would not have recognized.
