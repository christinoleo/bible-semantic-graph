The early Muslim biographers [[ibn-ishaq]] (via Ibn Hisham) and [[al-tabari]] report that [[muhammad]], pressured to reconcile with the Quraysh of Mecca, initially recited verses praising the goddesses al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, and Manāt as "high-flying cranes whose intercession is to be hoped for" — verses later retracted as Satanic insertion (Q 22:52 acknowledges that "We did not send before you any messenger but that, when he recited, Satan threw into his recitation"). If the report is historical, the [[quran]]'s claim of verbatim divine preservation is broken at its origin; if rejected, the rejection requires impugning the same Muslim biographical tradition (sīra, ḥadīth) on which the canonical life of Muhammad rests.
