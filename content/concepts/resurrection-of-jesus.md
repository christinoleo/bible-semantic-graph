The claim that [[jesus]] was bodily raised from the dead on the third day after his [[crucifixion-of-jesus|crucifixion]], the load-bearing claim of Christian faith ([[paul]]: "if Christ is not raised, your faith is in vain" — 1 Cor 15:14). Attested in all four canonical Gospels (Matt 28, Mark 16, Luke 24, John 20-21), in the pre-Pauline creed at 1 Cor 15:3-8, and in the public preaching of [[acts]] from Pentecost forward.

Apologetic defense has crystallized in modern evangelicalism around the [[minimal-facts-approach]] developed by [[gary-habermas]] and [[michael-licona]] and popularized for a Muslim-Christian register by [[nabeel-qureshi]] in *[[no-god-but-one]]*. The Islamic counter-position is structurally awkward: Q 4:157 denies the *crucifixion* on which the resurrection rests, so the dispute relocates to whether Jesus died at all (mainstream Sunni: no — Allah raised him bodily; [[ahmadiyya]]: no — he survived the cross and died naturally in Kashmir, see [[claim-ahmadiyya-survival-thesis]]).
