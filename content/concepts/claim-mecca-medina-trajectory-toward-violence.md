[[muhammad]]'s biography reconstructed from the [[sira]] (Ibn Isḥāq via Ibn Hishām) and the *maghāzī* hadith literature shows a chronological transition: in Mecca (610-622 CE) he leads a minority preaching movement and is forbidden retaliation; after the hijra to Medina (622), having achieved political and military power, he authorizes raids, dictates treaties, conducts the Battle of Badr, expels and executes the Jewish tribes of Medina, conquers Mecca, and dies in 632 having unified Arabia under arms. The Quranic text-strata track this transition — Meccan suras are eschatological and minoritarian, Medinan suras are legislative and militant — and on the [[naskh-abrogation|abrogation principle]] the later strata bind.
