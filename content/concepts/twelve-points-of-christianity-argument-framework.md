The chained twelve-step argument [[norman-geisler]] and [[frank-turek]] use in *[[i-dont-have-enough-faith-to-be-an-atheist]]* as the spine of evangelical classical apologetics: (1) truth about reality is knowable; (2) opposites cannot both be true (law of non-contradiction); (3) the theistic God exists, demonstrable from the [[cosmological-argument|cosmological]], [[teleological-argument|teleological]], and [[moral-argument|moral]] arguments; (4) if God exists, miracles are possible; (5) miracles performed in connection with a truth-claim authenticate the messenger; (6) the New Testament is historically reliable (carried by [[surge-new-testament-reliability-framework|SURGE]]); (7) the New Testament reports Jesus claiming to be God; (8) Jesus's claim is confirmed by fulfilled prophecy, sinless miraculous life, and resurrection (carried by [[feat-resurrection-evidence-framework|F.E.A.T.]]); (9) therefore Jesus is God; (10) whatever Jesus teaches is true; (11) Jesus taught the Bible is the Word of God; (12) therefore the Bible is the Word of God.

The framework's apologetic move is sequential dependency: refuting step *n* breaks the chain. The reverse is also its vulnerability — critics target step (5) (miracle-authentication) or step (11) (Jesus's actual scriptural views) as the load-bearing weak links.
