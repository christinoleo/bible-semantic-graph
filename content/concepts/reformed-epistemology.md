The view, developed by [[alvin-plantinga]], Nicholas Wolterstorff, and the Notre Dame school, that belief in God can be *properly basic* — warrant-conferring without inferential support from prior premises — in the same epistemic class as belief in other minds or in the reliability of memory. Drawing on [[reformed]] theology's *sensus divinitatis* (Calvin) and Reid's common-sense epistemology, it inverts the [[evidentialism|evidentialist]] demand that theistic belief produce arguments before counting as rational, and is articulated technically in Plantinga's warrant trilogy culminating in *Warranted Christian Belief*. Critics charge it licenses any belief whatsoever (the "Great Pumpkin objection"); defenders reply that proper basicality is constrained by cognitive faculties functioning properly in their design environment.
