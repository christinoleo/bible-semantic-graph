The six articles of belief required of every Muslim, codified from the [[quran]] and the *Ḥadīth of Gabriel*: belief in (1) [[allah]], (2) His angels, (3) His revealed books ([[torah]], [[psalms|Zabūr]], [[injil]], [[quran|Qurʾān]], and the lost scriptures of earlier prophets), (4) His messengers ([[abraham]], [[moses]], [[jesus]], [[muhammad]], and all prophets in between), (5) the Last Day (resurrection, judgement, paradise, hell), and (6) *qadar* — the divine decree, both its good and its bad. Inclusion of the prior scriptures and prophets as articles of faith is the doctrinal anchor for the Muslim category of [[ahl-al-kitab]] and the standing rebuttal to Christian framings of Islam as wholly hostile to the Bible. The articles are confessional rather than legal — distinct from the five [[pillars-of-islam|Pillars of Islam]] (*arkān al-Islām*) which govern outward practice.
