A motif in Deutero-Isaiah ([[book-of-isaiah]] 40–55) of a righteous figure designated *ʿeved YHWH* ("servant of the LORD") whose suffering is somehow redemptive for "the many." Bernhard Duhm's 1892 commentary isolated four passages — Isa 42:1–9, 49:1–13, 50:4–11, and 52:13–53:12 — as discrete "Servant Songs," and the grouping has structured scholarly discussion since. The identity of the servant is the central interpretive crux. [[jewish-anti-missionary]] and much critical scholarship read the servant as corporate Israel, explicitly named as such at Isa 49:3 ("you are my servant, Israel"). [[christian-apologetic]] tradition reads the servant — especially in the fourth song, [[isaiah-53]] — as an individual messianic figure prefiguring [[jesus]]. Both readings can cite internal Isaianic warrant.
