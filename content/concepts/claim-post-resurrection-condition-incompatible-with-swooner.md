A man who has been scourged near death, crucified, speared in the chest, and entombed for ~36 hours does not — within days — walk seven miles to Emmaus ([[gospel-of-luke|Luke]] 24:13-35), eat broiled fish unaided (Luke 24:42-43), invite finger-inspection of nail-wounds ([[gospel-of-john|John]] 20:27), and commission disciples globally ([[gospel-of-matthew|Matt]] 28:18-20). The Gospel portrayal of the post-resurrection [[jesus]] is not a battered survivor but a transformed agent — passing through closed doors (John 20:19, 20:26), appearing and vanishing (Luke 24:31), and inspiring worship rather than caretaking (Matt 28:9, 17).

The Ahmadiyya [[claim-ahmadiyya-survival-thesis|survival thesis]] would require either: (i) all these post-crucifixion descriptions are fabricated — but then the very Gospel sources the thesis selectively cites collapse as evidence; or (ii) they are accurate descriptions of a recovering swoon victim — which is medically and behaviorally absurd. Either branch leads back to [[islamic-dilemma|the dilemma]].

This extends [[claim-swoon-theory-medical-implausibility|the medical implausibility argument]] with the behavioral evidence: even granting the wildly improbable survival, the *post-crucifixion behavior* recorded in the Gospels does not match swoon-and-flight to Kashmir; it matches resurrection.
