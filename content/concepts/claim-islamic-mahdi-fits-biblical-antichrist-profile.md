Joel Richardson and others argue that the biblical antichrist matches the Islamic [[mahdi|Mahdī]] point-for-point: both arise in the Middle East (Dan 7, Dan 11:40-45); both establish a global government (Rev 13:7 / global caliphate); both demand universal allegiance with execution for refusal (Rev 20:4 beheading / Islamic *ridda* penalty); both reject the divine sonship of [[jesus]] (1 Jn 2:22 / Islamic *tawḥīd*); both are accompanied by a "False Prophet" figure — Rev 13:11-18 paralleling the Islamic [[returning-isa-islamic-eschatology|returning ʿĪsā]] who breaks the cross and forces conversion. The thesis inverts the traditional European-Roman-revival reading of the antichrist (Tim LaHaye, Hal Lindsey) by relocating the prophecies to the Islamic eschatological framework.
