Muslims greet each other with *as-salāmu ʿalaykum* (السلام عليكم), built on the Semitic root س-ل-م — the same root as Hebrew/Aramaic ש-ל-ם behind Jesus's recorded *εἰρήνη ὑμῖν* (Luke 24:36, John 20:19). The argument: Muslims literally still say what Jesus said; Christians replaced his greeting with culturally unrelated formulas ("hello," "God bless," "the peace of Christ"). Therefore on the level of daily liturgical practice, Muslims walk in Jesus's footsteps more faithfully than Christians do.

The argument depends on a stronger background claim — that the *form* of greeting carries the religious identity. Without that, the shared root is just historical Semitic continuity, not proof of Islamic priority over Jesus's tradition.
