The Catholic mendicant religious order founded by Dominic de Guzmán and confirmed by [[innocent-iii|Innocent III]] (1216), dedicated specifically to preaching and the refutation of heresy (originally in response to Catharism in southern France). Produced the central scholastic theological tradition — [[thomas-aquinas|Thomas Aquinas]], Albert the Great, Cajetan, the Salamanca school — and stood in characteristic intellectual rivalry with the [[franciscans|Franciscans]] across debates on the will, divine knowledge, sacramental theology, and the Immaculate Conception. Active in the Inquisition and in early-modern mission (Bartolomé de las Casas).
