The corpus of roughly 900 manuscripts discovered between 1947 and 1956 in eleven caves near [[qumran|Khirbet Qumran]] on the northwest shore of the Dead Sea, dated paleographically and by radiocarbon from c. 250 BCE to 70 CE. The corpus comprises three categories: biblical manuscripts (every book of the [[hebrew-bible|Hebrew Bible]] except Esther — most importantly [[1qisa-a|the Great Isaiah Scroll]]); deuterocanonical and Second-Temple Jewish texts ([[1-enoch|1 Enoch]], [[jubilees|Jubilees]], Tobit, Sirach); and sectarian writings of the [[qumran-community|Qumran community]] ([[1qs|Community Rule]], [[1qm|War Scroll]], Habakkuk Pesher, *Hodayot*, Damascus Document). The corpus revolutionized Second-Temple Judaism scholarship and confirmed the textual stability of the Hebrew Bible across a millennium.
