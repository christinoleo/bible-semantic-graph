The Islamic theological category — Arabic *shirk*, "associating" — for the cardinal sin of attributing partners, peers, or constituent persons to God, the negation that defines [[tawhid|tawḥīd]]. The Qur'an names *shirk* as the one unforgivable sin (Q 4:48, 4:116: "*inna Allāha lā yaghfiru an yushraka bihi* — God does not forgive that partners be associated with him"), and the [[trinity|Trinitarian]] confession of three divine persons is read by classical Islamic theology as a paradigmatic case (Q 5:73, "*la-qad kafara alladhīna qālū inna Allāha thālithu thalāthah* — they have certainly disbelieved who say God is the third of three"). Distinguished in [[classical-kalam|kalām]] into *shirk akbar* ("greater," idolatry proper, exits one from Islam) and *shirk aṣghar* ("lesser," ostentatious piety, ingratitude — major sins but not apostasy). The category structures Islamic apologetic against Christianity at the deepest level: not as a debate over particular passages but as the prior judgment that the very *grammar* of plurality-in-divinity is theologically inadmissible.
