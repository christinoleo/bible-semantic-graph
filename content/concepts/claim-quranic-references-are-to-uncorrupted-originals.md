Quranic references to "the Torah" and "the Gospel" are to the *aṣl* — the original, uncorrupted revelations given to [[moses]] and [[jesus]] — not to the 7th-century textual recensions Jews and Christians physically possessed. The distinction is built into the Quran itself: Q 2:75, Q 4:46, and Q 5:13 explicitly accuse Jewish and Christian communities of distorting the word, while Q 7:157 and Q 10:94 affirm the integrity of the original revelation those communities once received. The two claims are not contradictory — they describe revealed truth that was *given* faithfully and *transmitted* unfaithfully.

The Christian refutation only works if "the Torah/Gospel" in Q 7:157 must refer to extant 7th-century textual artifacts. The Islamic position is that scriptural reference is to *revealed content* (what God said to Moses, what God revealed to Jesus), not to *textual instantiation* (the physical scrolls/codices in circulation). Just as a Christian theologian can speak of "the Gospel" without committing to any particular manuscript family, the Quran speaks of "the Torah" and "the Gospel" as God's revelation — which the Quran itself now corroborates, completes, and corrects.

This is *taḥrīf al-maʿnā* (corruption of meaning) at minimum and *taḥrīf al-lafẓ* (corruption of the letter) in the stronger tradition — both versions consistent with Quranic appeal to the originals. The objection that "the appeal would be useless if the texts were already corrupted" misreads the Quran's audience: the Christians and Jews of 7th-century Arabia were called to recognize, in what remained of their scripture, the prophecies that pointed to Muhammad — see [[ahmad-prophecy-islamic-reading]] and [[claim-paraclete-was-periklytos]].
