The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaa, ca. 125 BCE) and the Masoretic Aleppo / Leningrad codices (~1000 CE) — separated by more than a millennium of independent transmission — show greater than 95% letter-for-letter agreement, with the remaining differences predominantly spelling and word-order variants that do not affect meaning. The New Testament shows the same pattern across the papyri, uncials, and minuscules: textual variation is heavily concentrated in spelling, word order, and synonym choice, with the substantive doctrinal content stable across traditions. The Islamic-apologetic picture of a corrupted, drifting Bible is directly contradicted by the convergent manuscript record.
