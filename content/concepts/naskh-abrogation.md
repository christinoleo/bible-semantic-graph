The classical Islamic legal-hermeneutical doctrine that later Quranic revelations can abrogate earlier ones — established on Q 2:106 and Q 16:101 — with the chronological ordering of revelation reconstructed from the [[sira]] and asbāb al-nuzūl literature. Practical consequence: where Meccan and Medinan verses conflict (paradigmatically on relations with non-Muslims), the later Medinan verses bind. Mainstream Sunni jurisprudence catalogues hundreds of abrogated rulings; reformist and modernist Muslims contest both the inventory and the underlying chronology. The doctrine is central to apologetic disputes about Quranic ethics — see [[claim-q9-5-verse-of-the-sword|Q 9:5 as abrogating tolerance verses]] in [[answering-jihad]].
