The argument, developed in modern form by J.L. Schellenberg (*Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason*, 1993), that if a perfectly loving God existed He would make Himself sufficiently evident to every capable creature open to relationship with Him — but "nonresistant nonbelievers" demonstrably exist (sincere seekers who fail to find), therefore such a God does not exist. The argument's strength is its tighter logical structure than the [[problem-of-evil]]: it does not require evaluating *how much* evil is too much, only the *existence* of sincere atheists. Christian responses turn on the value of [[claim-greater-good-hiddenness-defense|epistemic distance / faith / free response]] in salvation.
