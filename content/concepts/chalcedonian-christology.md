The dogmatic definition that [[jesus]] Christ is one *hypostasis* (person) subsisting in two complete *physeis* (natures) — divine and human — united "*asynchytōs, atreptōs, adiairetōs, achōristōs* — without confusion, without change, without division, without separation" (Council of [[chalcedon]], 451). The formula presupposes the [[trinity|Trinitarian]] grammar of [[hypostasis-vs-ousia|hypostasis and ousia]]: the Son is one of the three divine persons who, in the incarnation, takes a complete human nature into the unity of his single person without compromising the divine essence he shares with the Father and the Spirit. Chalcedon is read as the Christological corollary of [[nicaea-i|Nicene]] *homoousios* — if the Son is fully God by essence, the union with humanity must be at the level of person, not nature — and as the boundary against [[nestorianism]] (two persons), [[miaphysitism]] (one composite nature, [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]]'s formula pressed to its limit), and [[eutychianism]] (humanity absorbed). It remains binding in Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and most Protestant confessions; the non-Chalcedonian (Oriental Orthodox) churches reject the formula in favor of the *mia physis* of Cyril.
