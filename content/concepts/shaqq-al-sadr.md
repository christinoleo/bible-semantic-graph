The episode preserved in *[[sahih-muslim|Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim]]* 162 and Ibn Hisham's *Sīrah*: while [[muhammad|Muhammad]] as a child lived with his wet-nurse [[halima|Halīma]] among the Banū Saʿd Bedouin, two beings (interpreted in Sunni tradition as angels) reportedly threw him to the ground in front of his foster-brother, opened his chest, removed his heart, washed it of a black clot, and returned it. Three substantially different versions of the same event appear in the canonical hadith — in early childhood among Banū Saʿd, in pre-prophetic adolescence, and during the *Miʿrāj* (the Night Journey) — generating long-standing skepticism among Sunni hadith critics. The narrative carries non-trivial theological weight: it presupposes a *need* for [[muhammad]]'s purification, which sits in tension with the doctrine of prophetic *[[claim-shaqq-al-sadr-contradicts-prophetic-isma|ʿiṣma]]* (impeccability from birth).
