The Benedictine monk Gaunilo of Marmoutiers, replying to [[anselm-of-canterbury|Anselm]]'s *Proslogion* in his *In Behalf of the Fool*, argued that the same logical form generates absurdity if applied to any greatest-conceivable object: we can conceive of "the most perfect island" — including its existence in reality — and by parallel reasoning conclude it exists. Since perfect islands manifestly do not exist, the argument-form must be invalid. Anselm responded that the parody fails because only a maximally great *being* (not an island) has *necessary existence* as an intrinsic property — an argument [[claim-plantinga-modal-ontological-argument|Plantinga]] later formalizes in modal terms.
