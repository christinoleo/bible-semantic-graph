Frank Jackson's "Mary's Room" thought-experiment (1982): Mary, a color-vision scientist confined to a black-and-white room, knows every physical fact about color perception. When she leaves the room and sees red for the first time, she learns something new — what red *looks like*. If she knew all the physical facts but learns a new fact, then qualia are not physical facts. The conclusion is reinforced by inverted-qualia thought experiments and Kripke / Chalmers "zombie" arguments — beings physically identical to us but lacking subjective experience are conceivable, suggesting qualia are not entailed by physical states.
