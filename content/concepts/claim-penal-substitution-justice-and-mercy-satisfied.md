God's holiness forbids "sweeping sin under the rug" — the wage of sin is death, and a corrupt judge who waives the penalty satisfies neither justice nor moral law. Yet a willing substitute who absorbs the lawful penalty *does* satisfy justice (cf. the bail-payer analogy: if the law permits proxy execution of the sentence, the penalty is genuinely discharged), so the Son's voluntary death in his assumed humanity (John 10:17-18) lets divine justice and divine mercy meet without either being sacrificed.
