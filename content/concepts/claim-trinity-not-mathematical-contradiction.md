The mathematical objection ("1+1+1≠1") commits a category error: it treats *essence* and *person* as a single quantitative dimension when they are categorically distinct. The Christian formula is "one *what*, three *whos*" — single in essence, plural in personhood, with no claim that the *same* category yields both "one" and "three." Analogically: a single human nature is exemplified by many persons; a single mind can host distinct cognitive subjects in certain pathological cases; a single substance can ground multiple inseparable relations. The Trinitarian formula is *unusual*, not *contradictory*.
