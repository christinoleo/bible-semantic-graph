The cluster of arguments that defends the historicity of Jesus's [[crucifixion-of-jesus|crucifixion]] on critical-historical rather than confessional grounds, converging on it as among the most secure facts in ancient history (so Gerd Lüdemann, John Dominic Crossan, Bart Ehrman — none of them apologists). It marshals the pre-Pauline creed of 1 Cor 15:3–7 dating within years of the event, multiple independent attestation across the [[gospels]] and the hostile sources [[josephus]] and [[tacitus]], and the criterion of embarrassment (a crucified messiah was scandalous for the earliest community, not invented). Methodologically owned by [[historical-critical-scholarship]], the cluster is heavily deployed by [[christian-apologetic]] writers against the Q 4:157 denial — a borrowed weapon, not native to the apologetic tradition.
