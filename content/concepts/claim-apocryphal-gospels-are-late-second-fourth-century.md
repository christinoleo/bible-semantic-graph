The "lost gospels" foregrounded in popular skepticism — the [[gospel-of-thomas|Gospel of Thomas]], the Gospel of Mary, the Gospel of Judas, the Gospel of Philip, and others — are not first-century rivals of the canonical four but mid-second to fourth century compositions, recovered most famously from the Nag Hammadi library (4th c.) and the Tchacos codex (3rd-4th c.). The Gospel of Thomas's earliest stratum is debated (some scholars argue components date to ~120 CE) but its extant Coptic form is fourth century and its content (Gnostic-inflected sayings) places it within developing rather than original Christianity. None were "suppressed" by Constantine or Nicaea; they were sidelined by ecclesial use because their dates and theological profiles disqualified them.
