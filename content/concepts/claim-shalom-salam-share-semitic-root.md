Granting that *šālōm* is Second Temple Jewish — that concedes the point. Muslims keep saying *as-salāmu ʿalaykum* in liturgy and daily life on the same Semitic root that [[jesus]] himself used (š-l-m / س-ل-م). Christians, by their own admission, translated it away into vernacular conventions ("hello," "good morning"). The Islamic claim is not that *salām* is more original than *šālōm*; it is that the lived practice of the greeting carries through into Islam and was *abandoned* by the Christian community. "We greet how Jesus greeted" is a continuity claim about practice, not a priority claim about etymology.

The Christian fallback to "the kiss of peace in the Mass preserves the substance" makes the inverse problem: a ritualized liturgical token replaces the daily-life greeting of [[jesus]] precisely because the daily-life form was abandoned.
