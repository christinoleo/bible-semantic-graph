Hebrew *Māšîaḥ* (anointed one), translated into Greek as *Christos* — the title applied in the [[hebrew-bible|Hebrew Bible]] originally to anointed kings ([[david|David]], [[solomon|Solomon]]) and high priests, and then in late-Second-Temple Jewish expectation to a future ideal Davidic king who would deliver Israel and inaugurate God's reign (e.g. Psalms of Solomon 17-18; the Qumran Messianic Apocalypse, [[4q521|4Q521]]). Christian theology applies the title to [[jesus]] of Nazareth as its eschatological fulfillment ("Jesus Christ"). Jewish messianic expectation continues to await its fulfillment; Islamic tradition affirms [[jesus|Isa]] as *al-Masīḥ* but interprets the title as a designation of his prophetic mission rather than divine sonship.
