[[gospel-of-john|John]] 19:34 reports a Roman soldier driving a spear (*λόγχη*) into [[jesus]]'s side, "and at once there came out blood and water" — followed in 19:35 by an explicit eyewitness affirmation. The detail is medically diagnostic: separation of blood and clear serous fluid (pericardial effusion or pleural-cardiac fluid) is a sign of post-mortem condition or, at minimum, of injury to the heart/pericardium incompatible with survival. The soldier's act is also procedurally final — Roman crucifixion practice required confirmation of death; the spear-thrust *is* that confirmation, applied to [[jesus]] specifically while the leg-breaking applied to the other two crucified.

The Ahmadiyya [[claim-ahmadiyya-survival-thesis|survival thesis]] needs Jesus to be alive after the cross. A spear through the chest of a man already in extremis, with the eyewitness narrator emphasizing the lethal-medical character of the discharge, cannot be reconciled with a swoon-and-recovery narrative on the Bible's own terms. The thesis must either dismiss this verse (triggering [[islamic-dilemma|the dilemma]]) or reinterpret it against its grain.
