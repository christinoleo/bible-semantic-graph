The hundreds of thousands of New Testament textual variants — frequently weaponized in popular skepticism as evidence that "we don't know what the originals said" — are overwhelmingly orthographic (spelling), nonsense errors, or stylistic. The variants that are *both* meaningful *and* viable (i.e., genuinely contestable, affecting the sense) number in the low hundreds, and none requires altering an essential Christian doctrine, a point [[bart-ehrman]] himself has publicly granted in interviews despite his skeptical framing in *Misquoting Jesus*. The argument is a staple of [[wesley-huff]]'s, Daniel Wallace's, and Dan Brown-era evangelical responses to popular skepticism.
