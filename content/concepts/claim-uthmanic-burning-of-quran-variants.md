*[[sahih-al-bukhari|Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī]]* 4987 records that [[uthman|ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān]], c. 650 CE — roughly twenty years after [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s death — gathered the variant codices of the [[quran]] (including the codices of Ibn Masʿūd, Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, and others), produced a single standardized text by the hand of [[zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thābit]], and ordered all competing codices burned. The act presupposes the existence of substantial textual variation prior to standardization — variation significant enough that ʿUthmān felt the unity of the *umma* required it be destroyed. The "Quran has been verbatim-unchanged since revelation" claim is thus contradicted by the foundational act of standardization itself.
