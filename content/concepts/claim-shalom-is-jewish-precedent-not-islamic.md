The *šālōm* greeting is Second Temple Jewish practice, attested in [[psalms|Psalms]] (Ps 122:6-8) and rabbinic tradition centuries before Islam. [[jesus]] greets as a first-century Jew — not as a proto-Muslim. The Arabic *salām* shares the Semitic root because Arabic is a Semitic language cousin to Hebrew/Aramaic, not because Islam preserves Jesus's distinctively religious practice. By the same logic, a Samaritan or a Mizrahi Jew today speaks Jesus's greeting more directly than a 7th-century Arabic speaker.

What Christians do with daily greetings is a question of cultural translation, not Christological betrayal. The liturgical kiss of peace, the pax in the Mass, and the *pax vobis* of clergy preserve the substance; vernacular forms vary.
