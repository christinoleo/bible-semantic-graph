A fixed, public, recitable confession of Christian belief — a verbal symbol (Greek *symbolon*, a token of identity) by which a community confesses what it holds and excludes what it does not. Creeds emerge from three converging settings: baptismal interrogation (a candidate is asked "do you believe...?" and responds), catechetical instruction (the *traditio* and *redditio symboli* — handing over and giving back the creed), and conciliar dogmatic definition against heresy. The earliest extant Christian confessional formulas are the [[claim-1-cor-15-creed-earliest-witness|pre-Pauline kerygma of 1 Cor 15:3-8]] and the [[claim-phil-2-kenotic-hymn-explains-submission|Christ-hymn of Philippians 2]]; the developed Western baptismal symbol crystallizes in the [[old-roman-symbol]] and matures into the [[apostles-creed]], while the Eastern conciliar tradition produces the [[nicene-creed]] (325) and the [[nicene-constantinopolitan-creed]] (381). Later doctrinal expansions — the [[athanasian-creed]] (Quicumque, Latin, 5th-6th c.), the [[chalcedonian-definition]] (451), and the disputed [[filioque]] interpolation — extend the creedal grammar to Trinitarian and Christological precision. Across all forms the creed performs the same triple work: it confesses (worship), catechizes (formation), and demarcates (boundary against heresy).
