Islamic sirah literature (Ibn Hisham; al-Ṭabarī; Ibn Saʿd's *Ṭabaqāt*; cf. *[[sahih-al-bukhari|Bukhārī]]* 3617) reports that ʿAbdullāh ibn Saʿd ibn Abī Sarḥ — one of [[muhammad|Muhammad]]'s revelation scribes — claimed he had altered the wording of revelations as he wrote them down (e.g. changing *ʿalīm ḥakīm* to *samīʿ ʿalīm*), and that Muhammad approved his alterations. ibn Saʿd then apostatized, reasoning that a true prophet would not have approved unauthorized changes. He fled to Mecca, and when later pardoned at the Conquest, the episode survived in the foundational biographical record. The story is reported *by Muslim sources* and directly undermines the doctrine of verbatim Quranic preservation.
