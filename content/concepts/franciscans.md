The Catholic mendicant religious order founded by Francis of Assisi (c. 1209) and approved by Pope Innocent III, characterized by voluntary poverty, itinerant preaching, and care for the poor. Divided historically into Conventuals (more accommodating to property and learning) and Observants/Spirituals (strict poverty), and reunited and re-divided multiple times through Capuchin and other reforms. Produced major scholastic theologians ([[bonaventure|Bonaventure]], Duns Scotus, William of Ockham) and stood in characteristic intellectual rivalry with the [[dominicans|Dominicans]] in the medieval university.
