J.P. Moreland argues that the appearance of irreducibly mental properties (qualia, intentionality, subjective first-person experience) within an otherwise purely physical natural history is anomalous on naturalism but expected on theism, where mind is ontologically basic. Naturalist accounts must either deny that mental properties are real (eliminativism), reduce them to physical properties (a project facing the "hard problem of consciousness"), or accept emergent mental properties without an account of why mind would arise from non-mind. Theism takes mind as the fundamental category and so faces no such explanatory gap.
