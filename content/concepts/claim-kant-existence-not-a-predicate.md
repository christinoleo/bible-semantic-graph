Immanuel Kant argued in the *Critique of Pure Reason* that "existence is not a real predicate" — saying "X exists" adds nothing to the concept of X; it merely asserts that an instance falls under the concept. Therefore "existence" cannot make a being greater (as Anselm's [[claim-existence-in-reality-greater-than-conception|key premise]] requires); existence is a different category from greatness-conferring properties. Formalized later by Frege and Russell in the predicate-calculus distinction between predicates and the existential quantifier. Christian responses (Plantinga) argue that *necessary* existence (not bare existence) is the property at issue, and that *that* is a predicate.
