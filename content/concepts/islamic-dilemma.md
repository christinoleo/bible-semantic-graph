The [[quran]] simultaneously affirms the prior scriptures — Torah, Psalms, [[injil]] — as inspired, divinely preserved, and authoritative (Q 3:3-4, Q 5:47, Q 5:68, Q 18:27, Q 6:115) and commands their consultation (Q 7:157, Q 10:94), yet contradicts them on the crucifixion, deity, and resurrection of [[jesus]]. Either the prior scriptures are preserved — in which case the Quran is false because it contradicts what it endorses — or they were already corrupted before [[muhammad]] — in which case the Quran is false because it endorses a corrupted text and instructs Muslims to consult it. The [[tahrif]] doctrine is the escape route this dilemma is built to close — sharpened in modern Christian apologetics by David Wood and GodLogic against the Deedat-Naik tradition (see [[ahmed-deedat]], [[zakir-naik]]).
