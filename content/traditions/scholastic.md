Medieval Latin Christian intellectual tradition (c. 11th–17th centuries), characterized by systematic synthesis of [[patristic]] theology with recovered Aristotelian metaphysics and logic. Foundational figures include Anselm, Peter Lombard, [[thomas-aquinas]], Duns Scotus, and William of Ockham, with continuation through the Second Scholastic (Suárez, Cajetan, John of St. Thomas).

Method is dialectical: *quaestio*, distinction, syllogism, with strong reliance on natural-theology arguments alongside revealed sources. See `/axis/tradition/scholastic` for Arguments grounded in this tradition.
