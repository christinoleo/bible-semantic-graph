The modern Jewish argumentative tradition — chiefly Orthodox, late-twentieth-century onward — devoted to refuting Christian missionary use of the Hebrew Bible against Jews. The recurring targets are the standard missionary proof-texts: [[isaiah-53]] read as a prediction of the suffering [[jesus]] rather than as Israel-the-servant; [[psalm-22]] read as crucifixion prophecy; the seventy weeks of [[daniel-9]] read as dating the messianic advent; Isaiah 7:14 read through the Septuagint *parthenos* rather than the Hebrew *ʿalmah*. The argumentative apparatus is Tanakh in the Masoretic Hebrew, classical rabbinic commentary, and refusal of christological pre-interpretation — the same apparatus that Messianic-Jewish apologists like [[michael-brown]] turn in the opposite direction.

Medieval roots run through the Jewish-Christian disputations (Nahmanides at Barcelona in 1263, the Tortosa disputation, the *Toledot Yeshu* counter-narrative tradition) but the modern movement is institutional and post-1970s, responding to the rise of evangelical mission organizations (Jews for Jesus, Chosen People Ministries) targeting Jewish communities. The main institutional vehicles are Outreach Judaism ([[tovia-singer]], founded 1994), Jews for Judaism (Bentzion Kravitz, founded 1985), and the older *Why the Jews?* educational network. Cross-cuts denomination but practiced most actively from [[rabbinic]] and Orthodox positions, where the textual stakes are read as continuous with the medieval disputation tradition.
