The Eastern Orthodox communion — the second-largest Christian body (c. 220 million adherents), holding apostolic continuity through the Eastern patriarchates (Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, Jerusalem) and rejecting Roman papal universal jurisdiction since the Great Schism of 1054. Distinguished theologically by *theosis* (deification as the goal of salvation), the essence-energies distinction (Gregory Palamas), the Eastern liturgical tradition, the iconography defended at [[nicaea-ii|Nicaea II]] (787), and rejection of the *Filioque* clause. Rejects [[original-sin|inherited guilt]] while affirming inherited mortality (ancestral sin).
