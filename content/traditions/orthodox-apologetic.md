The Eastern Orthodox strand of [[christian-apologetic]], shaped less by polemical-systematic argument than by claims of unbroken liturgical and doctrinal continuity with the [[patristic]] Fathers and the seven Ecumenical Councils. Its anchoring frame is [[theosis]] — salvation as participation in the divine life — articulated through the Cappadocian Fathers and crystallized in the Palamite [[essence-energies-distinction]] that lets God be genuinely known in His energies while remaining unknowable in His essence.

Methodologically it `contradicts` the [[scholastic]] tradition, rejecting Latin rationalist natural theology and the Thomistic synthesis as a Western departure from patristic mystagogy. The twentieth-century neo-patristic synthesis — Vladimir Lossky's *The Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church*, John Meyendorff on Palamas, [[kallistos-ware]]'s *The Orthodox Way*, Alexander Schmemann's liturgical theology, and Georges Florovsky's "return to the Fathers" — gives this tradition its modern voice.
