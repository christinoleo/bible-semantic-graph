The dominant Jewish argumentative and legal tradition that emerged after the [[destruction-of-the-second-temple]] in 70 CE and crystallized through the [[mishnah]], the [[jerusalem-talmud]] and [[babylonian-talmud]], and the [[midrash|Midrashim]]. It positions itself as both heir to Second-Temple [[pharisaism]] (via [[hillel-the-elder|Hillel]], [[shammai]], [[rabbi-akiva|Akiva]], and the redactor [[judah-ha-nasi]]) and as the live voice of [[oral-torah]], whose hermeneutic norms govern subsequent figures from [[rashi]] to [[maimonides]].

Method is halakhic-aggadic exegesis, midrashic argumentation, and chain-of-tradition authority. It functions historically as the counterpoint to early [[patristic]] readings of the Hebrew Bible — especially on [[messianic-prophecy]], [[christology]], and the status of the Mosaic Law — and later as the orthodox foil against which [[karaite]] scripturalism defines itself. See `/axis/tradition/rabbinic` for Arguments grounded in this tradition.
