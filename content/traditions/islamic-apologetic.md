The Muslim argumentative tradition defending Islamic claims against Christian, Jewish, and secular alternatives. Continuous from medieval *ʿilm al-kalām* ([[ibn-hazm]], [[ibn-taymiyya]], al-Qarafi) through colonial-era responses (Rahmatullah Kairanawi's *Iẓhār al-Ḥaqq*) to the modern televised debate circuit ([[ahmed-deedat]], Zakir Naik). Recurring methods are [[textual-critique]] of the biblical corpus (presupposing [[tahrif]]), [[from-prophecy]] arguments centered on Q 61:6, and *tawḥīd*-grounded critique of [[trinity]] and [[chalcedonian-christology]].

Anchoring Arguments include [[jesus-was-a-muslim-prophet]] and the Q 4:157 denial of the [[crucifixion-of-jesus]]. See `/axis/tradition/islamic-apologetic` for the full corpus.
