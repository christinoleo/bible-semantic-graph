The [[reformed]] apologetic method founded by [[cornelius-van-til]] at Westminster Seminary, arguing that the Christian worldview is the necessary precondition of intelligibility itself — that logic, science, ethics, and meaning are unintelligible apart from the triune God revealed in [[scripture]]. The signature move is the transcendental argument: reasoning "from the impossibility of the contrary," showing that any rival worldview must covertly borrow from Christian metaphysics to function at all.

It `contradicts` classical evidentialism (and so stands in methodological tension with [[evangelical-apologetic]]) by denying that believer and unbeliever share neutral epistemic ground from which evidence can be jointly weighed — fallen reason is itself part of what needs redeeming. The tradition extends through Greg Bahnsen (*Always Ready*, the 1985 Stein debate), John Frame's multi-perspectival refinement, and Scott Oliphint's covenantal reformulation.
