The Christian tradition originating in the sixteenth-century Reformation — Martin Luther's 95 Theses (1517), Ulrich Zwingli at Zurich, John Calvin at Geneva, Thomas Cranmer in England — defined by the five *solas* (*sola scriptura, sola fide, sola gratia, solus Christus, soli Deo gloria*), the rejection of papal authority, and (in varying degrees) the rejection of transubstantiation, purgatory, prayers to saints, and seven-sacrament theology. Encompasses the Lutheran, [[reformed|Reformed]], Anglican, Anabaptist, and (later) Methodist, Baptist, Pentecostal, and evangelical movements.
