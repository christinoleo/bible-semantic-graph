The Calvinist branch of the magisterial [[protestant-reformation]] (16th century onward), shaped by [[john-calvin]] in Geneva and consolidated by [[theodore-beza]], the [[westminster-assembly|Westminster divines]], [[jonathan-edwards]], [[charles-hodge]], and [[herman-bavinck]]. Its doctrinal identity is fixed by the [[heidelberg-catechism]], [[belgic-confession]], [[westminster-confession]], and the broader [[three-forms-of-unity]], with [[tulip]] (the Canons of Dort) as its soteriological shorthand and [[covenant-theology]] as its dominant biblical-theological frame.

Self-consciously [[augustinian-tradition|Augustinian]], it claims direct continuity with the [[patristic]] doctrine of grace while rejecting medieval sacramental accretions. Distinctives include regulative-principle worship, presbyterian polity in many strands, and a strong doctrine of divine sovereignty that puts it in structural opposition to [[arminianism]] and to Tridentine [[roman-catholicism]].
