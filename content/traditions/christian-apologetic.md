The Christian argumentative tradition defending creedal claims against Jewish, Islamic, pagan, and secular alternatives. Begins with the [[patristic]] *apologetai* (Justin Martyr, Tertullian, Origen) replying to Greco-Roman critique, sharpens against [[islamic-apologetic]] from John of Damascus and [[thomas-aquinas]]'s *Summa Contra Gentiles* onward, and continues in evidentialist (William Lane Craig), presuppositional (Cornelius Van Til), and historical-critical (N.T. Wright) modern forms.

Anchoring Arguments include defenses of the [[crucifixion-of-jesus]] via [[criterion-of-multiple-attestation]] and patristic Trinitarianism against Islamic monism. See `/axis/tradition/christian-apologetic` for the full corpus.
