The Sunni kalam school founded by [[al-maturidi]] in 10th-century Samarqand, predominant wherever the Hanafi legal madhhab took root — Central Asia, the Persianate world, the Turkic steppe, and the Ottoman lands. It runs parallel to [[ashariyya]] on most contested questions against the [[mutazila]] (real divine attributes, uncreated Quran, affirmation of the beatific vision) while differing in tone and detail.

Its distinctives are a higher confidence that unaided reason can know the existence of God and the basic obligation to believe prior to revelation, a more compatibilist account of human action (*ikhtiyār*) that gives the human will genuine, though God-created, efficacy, and a finer-grained taxonomy of the [[divine-attributes-debate|divine attributes]] separating essential from active attributes. Together with [[ashariyya]] it forms the twin pillars of post-classical Sunni orthodoxy, transmitted especially through the Ottoman *medrese* curriculum.
