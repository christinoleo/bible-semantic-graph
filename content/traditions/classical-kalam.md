The classical period of Islamic speculative theology (c. 8th–13th centuries), organized around three competing schools: the rationalist [[mutazila]], the mediating [[ashariyya]] of [[al-ashari]], and the [[maturidiyya]] of [[al-maturidi]]. Its method is dialectical defense of Islamic doctrine using Greek-inspired logic and physics, refined by [[al-ghazali]] and [[fakhr-al-din-al-razi]] into a mature scholastic instrument capable of engaging Christian, Jewish, and philosophical interlocutors on their own terms.

Recurring battlegrounds are the [[divine-attributes-debate]], the [[uncreated-quran]] controversy, [[free-will-and-predestination-in-islam|free will and predestination]], and the metaphysics of causation. Kalam's argumentative grammar — particularly its anti-Trinitarian and [[tawhid|*tawḥīd*]]-centered moves — flows directly into the later [[islamic-apologetic]] tradition.
