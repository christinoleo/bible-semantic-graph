The popular-accessible strand of Protestant [[christian-apologetic]] anchored in a high view of [[biblical-inerrancy]], classical-evidentialist methodology, and direct address to the educated lay reader. Marquee works — Josh McDowell's *Evidence That Demands a Verdict*, Lee Strobel's *The Case for Christ*, [[william-lane-craig]]'s *Reasonable Faith*, Norman Geisler's *Christian Apologetics*, and the Frank Turek / Ravi Zacharias public-debate tradition — share a common argumentative spine: cumulative-case reasoning from the [[historical-jesus-quest|historical Jesus]], [[cosmological-argument]], [[fine-tuning-argument]], and [[moral-argument-for-god]] toward the truth of [[christianity|orthodox Christianity]].

Methodologically it `parallels_with` [[presuppositional-apologetics]] in confessional commitment but differs sharply on prolegomena — evangelicals grant the unbeliever common epistemic ground and reason *to* Christianity from shared evidence, where presuppositionalists deny that ground exists. It also `parallels_with` [[catholic-apologetic]] on shared classical theism while differing on Magisterium and Scripture. See `/axis/tradition/evangelical-apologetic` for the Arguments grounded in this tradition.
