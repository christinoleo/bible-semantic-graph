The Roman Catholic strand of [[christian-apologetic]], distinguished from its Protestant siblings by argumentation for the [[magisterium]], [[apostolic-succession]], the [[real-presence|Eucharistic real presence]], and a Marian dogmatic deposit, alongside shared creedal claims. Methodologically it `builds_on` the [[scholastic]] tradition — [[thomas-aquinas]]'s [[five-ways]] of natural theology remain its baseline grammar — while extending into literary-imaginative apology (G.K. Chesterton's *The Everlasting Man*, *Orthodoxy*), historical-developmental apology ([[john-henry-newman]]'s *Apologia Pro Vita Sua* and *Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine*), and contemporary Thomism (Edward Feser, Peter Kreeft).

Other anchoring figures include Joseph Cardinal Ratzinger / Benedict XVI (*Introduction to Christianity*), Karl Adam (*The Spirit of Catholicism*), and Ronald Knox. It `parallels_with` [[evangelical-apologetic]] on classical theism and on defense of the [[historical-jesus-quest|historical Jesus]], but diverges on ecclesiology, sacramentology, and the sufficiency of [[sola-scriptura]]. See `/axis/tradition/catholic-apologetic` for the corpus.
