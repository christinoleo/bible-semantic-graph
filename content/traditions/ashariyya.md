The mainstream Sunni kalam school founded by [[al-ashari]] in 10th-century Baghdad after his break with the [[mutazila]], and elaborated by al-Bāqillānī, al-Juwaynī, [[al-ghazali]], and [[fakhr-al-din-al-razi]] into the dominant theological framework of the medieval Sunni world. It conceded the Muʿtazilī rational method while reversing most of its conclusions: divine attributes are real and distinct from the essence "without asking how" (*bilā kayf*), the Quran is uncreated, and the beatific vision in the hereafter is affirmed.

Its signature metaphysics is occasionalism — God is the direct cause of every event, secondary causes being habitual conjunctions God could break at any moment — paired with the doctrine of *kasb* ("acquisition"), which preserves human moral responsibility without granting genuine causal power. This framework supplied [[al-ghazali]]'s critique of [[falsafa]] in the *Tahāfut* and shaped the [[islamic-apologetic]] grammar through which later Muslims engaged Christian and philosophical interlocutors.
