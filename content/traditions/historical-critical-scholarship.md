The post-Enlightenment academic tradition (18th century onward) treating biblical texts with the same source-critical, form-critical, redaction-critical, and historical methods applied to any other ancient corpus. Bracketed from confessional commitment; conclusions binding regardless of faith stance. Lineage runs through Reimarus, Strauss, Wellhausen, Bultmann, the Jesus Seminar, and contemporary scholars (E.P. Sanders, Bart Ehrman, Dale Allison, John P. Meier).

Cross-cuts confession: a Catholic, Reformed, agnostic, or Jewish scholar can equally practice it. Provides the lingua franca of academic biblical studies even where conclusions are contested. See `/axis/tradition/historical-critical-scholarship` for Arguments grounded in this tradition.
