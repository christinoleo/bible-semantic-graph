A messianic reform movement within Islam founded by [[mirza-ghulam-ahmad]] in Qadian, Punjab, in 1889, whose claim to be the promised Mahdi and returned Messiah places it at sharp odds with mainstream [[sunni-islam]] (and Shi'a authorities), which formally declare Ahmadis non-Muslim. Its defining contribution to interfaith polemic is the [[survival-of-jesus-thesis]]: that [[jesus-of-nazareth]] survived [[the-crucifixion]], recovered, and migrated east to die a natural death in Kashmir — a historical reconstruction designed to reconcile Q 4:157 with the bare fact of Roman execution without conceding either resurrection or substitution.

The movement inherits the methods of [[islamic-apologetic]] (textual critique of the Gospels, [[from-prophecy]] arguments) but couples them with comparative-religion and travel-history evidence drawn from [[buddhist-tradition|Buddhist]] and Hindu sources. See `/axis/tradition/ahmadiyya` for Arguments grounded in this tradition.
