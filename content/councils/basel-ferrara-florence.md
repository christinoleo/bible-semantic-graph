The Council of Basel-Ferrara-Florence (1431-1449 CE) was a single ecumenical council that migrated locations across three cities (with a brief Roman session at the close), embodying the climactic struggle between **conciliarism** and **papal monarchy** in the 15th century. Convoked at Basel in 1431 under Pope Martin V (who died days after) and Pope Eugene IV, it began as a conciliarist assembly executing the [[constance]] decree *Frequens* and ended as the papal vehicle that crushed conciliarism.

**At Basel (1431-1437)**, the assembly adopted radical conciliarist positions, restricting papal financial prerogatives (annates, reservations) and asserting council-over-pope. Eugene IV tried to dissolve it; the Basel fathers refused and (in 1439) deposed him, electing the antipope Felix V (Amadeus VIII of Savoy). The Basel rump collapsed into ineffectiveness during the 1440s, finally dissolving in 1449 when Felix V abdicated.

**At Ferrara (1438) and Florence (1439-1445)**, Eugene IV held the rival, legitimate continuation of the council, which became the negotiation site for **reunion with the Greek East**. Emperor John VIII Palaiologos, facing the imminent Ottoman threat to Constantinople (which fell in 1453), led a large Byzantine delegation including Patriarch Joseph II of Constantinople, the philosopher Gemistos Plethon, and the Greek prelates Bessarion of Nicaea and Mark of Ephesus. After months of dogmatic disputation, the **Bull of Union *Laetentur Caeli*** (6 July 1439) proclaimed reunion, defining four contested points: the [[filioque]] (acknowledged as legitimate doctrinal expression, "as from one principle"), purgatory, unleavened bread in the eucharist, and papal primacy ("the Roman Pontiff is the true vicar of Christ, the head of the entire church, the father and teacher of all Christians").

The union was **rejected in the East almost immediately upon the delegation's return**. Mark of Ephesus refused to sign; popular and monastic resistance was overwhelming. After 1453, Sultan Mehmed II installed an anti-unionist patriarch (Gennadios Scholarios). The Council of Constantinople (1484) formally repudiated the union. The Bull of Union became another well-meant fiasco like [[lyons-ii]].

Subsequent bulls *Exsultate Deo* (1439, with the [[armenian-apostolic-church|Armenians]]) and *Cantate Domino* (1442, with the Copts) extended the unionist effort, including a detailed sacramental theology (definition of the seven sacraments along Thomistic-Aristotelian lines, drawn from [[thomas-aquinas]]'s *De sacramentis*) that became authoritative for later Latin sacramental theology. These unions also largely failed in practice.

The council's enduring legacy is the **defeat of conciliarism** in favor of papal monarchy. Eugene IV's bull *Etsi non dubitemus* (1441) declared that the Roman Pontiff "has full power" to convene and dissolve councils — a frontal rejection of *Sacrosancta*. From Florence forward, the papal-monarchical reading of ecclesiology dominates until challenged again by Gallicanism, Febronianism, and ultimately resolved (in the Catholic line) at [[vatican-i]].
