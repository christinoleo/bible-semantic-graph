The First Council of Lyons (1245 CE) was convoked by Pope Innocent IV in exile at Lyons (then technically imperial territory but politically safer than Rome) to confront Holy Roman Emperor **Frederick II** Hohenstaufen. About 150 bishops attended — far fewer than [[lateran-iv]] — primarily from France, Spain, and Italy, with the church of the Empire mostly absent.

The council's principal act was the deposition of [[frederick-ii]] as emperor and king of Sicily, declaring him a perjurer, peace-breaker, sacrilegious despoiler of the church, suspected of heresy, and absolved subjects of allegiance. The deposition was the climactic moment of the long papal-imperial conflict between the Hohenstaufens and the post-Lateran IV papacy: Frederick had been excommunicated three times, had quarreled over Sicilian succession, had recovered Jerusalem peacefully in 1229 (which only deepened papal suspicion), and had besieged Rome itself in 1241.

Innocent IV's deposition sentence had limited practical effect — Frederick retained imperial power until his death in 1250 — but it became a canonical-political precedent for papal deposition of secular rulers, invoked through subsequent centuries. The Hohenstaufen dynasty collapsed within a generation of Frederick's death; the long interregnum (1250-1273) followed.

The council also called for a new crusade (after the loss of Jerusalem to the Khwarezmians in 1244) and addressed the Mongol threat: the Franciscan friar **John of Plano Carpini** was dispatched as papal envoy to the Mongol Khan, producing one of the first European ethnographic accounts of the Mongol empire. The council further sought aid for the Eastern empire of Constantinople, then under the Latin Empire (1204-1261) installed by the Fourth Crusade.

Procedural canons reformed papal curia practice, particularly the handling of episcopal elections and benefice appointments. The council's chief historical interest, however, lies in its display of high-medieval papal claims to a juridical supremacy over secular rulers — a claim near its political zenith under Innocent IV and his canonist theorists, just before the long decline through the [[avignon-papacy]] and the [[western-schism]].
