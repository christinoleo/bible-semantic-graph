The Council of Hippo (393 CE) was a regional synod of the North African church held at Hippo Regius. Though [[augustine-of-hippo]] was not yet bishop (he was ordained priest in 391 and consecrated bishop ca. 395-396), he was present and preached his treatise *De fide et symbolo* before the assembled bishops.

The council's most consequential act was canon 36, which lists the books accepted as canonical scripture. The list includes the full Old Testament with deuterocanonicals (Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach, 1-2 Maccabees, Baruch) and all 27 books of the New Testament. This is one of the earliest conciliar promulgations of what becomes the Catholic biblical canon and likely the immediate source for [[augustine-of-hippo]]'s own canon list in *De doctrina christiana* 2.8.

Hippo's acta have not survived intact; the canon decisions are preserved indirectly through their ratification at the [[council-of-carthage]] (397) and subsequent African synods. The Breviarium Hipponense, a summary of Hippo's canons read at Carthage 397, is the principal witness.

The council operated under the authority of the African primate (Aurelius of Carthage) rather than papal convocation, illustrating the relative autonomy of the African church in the late 4th century. Its canon decision was regional, not ecumenical, but it became the bedrock of Western canonical consensus and was reaffirmed dogmatically over a millennium later at [[trent]].
