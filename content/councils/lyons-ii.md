The Second Council of Lyons (1274 CE) was convoked by Pope Gregory X with three principal aims: (1) reunion with the Greek church, (2) reform of the Latin clergy, and (3) a new crusade. About 500 bishops, 60 abbots, and 1000 prelates attended; [[thomas-aquinas]] was summoned to attend but **died en route** at Fossanova on 7 March 1274. [[bonaventure]] (general of the Franciscans, cardinal-bishop of Albano) was a leading figure at the council and died during it, on 15 July 1274.

The headline achievement was the **(failed) reunion of East and West**. Byzantine Emperor Michael VIII Palaiologos — restored to Constantinople in 1261 after the Latin Empire — needed Western political support against Charles of Anjou (king of Sicily) and was willing to trade ecclesial reunion for it. A Greek delegation arrived at Lyons; the emperor's representatives swore to a profession of faith including acceptance of the [[filioque]] ("the Holy Spirit proceeds eternally from the Father and the Son ... as from one principle") and acknowledgment of Roman primacy. The reunion was formally proclaimed.

The reunion **collapsed within a few years**. Most of the Byzantine clergy and laity rejected it as a political imposition; Michael's son Andronikos II repudiated it after Michael's death in 1282. The Greek bishops who had accepted the union were anathematized; Michael was denied Christian burial. The episode prefigured the similar collapse of the union concluded at [[basel-ferrara-florence]] (1439).

The council also legislated on clergy reform (limiting plurality of benefices, regulating papal-curia procedure), affirmed the doctrine of the [[filioque]] and the procession of the Holy Spirit, condemned residual joachimite and spiritualist tendencies in the [[franciscans]], and called for a new crusade (which did not materialize). Constitution 1 (*Ubi periculum*) regulated **papal conclaves**: cardinals locked in (*cum clave*) and progressively restricted in food and shelter to force quick election — a response to the three-year vacancy that had preceded Gregory X's own election in 1271.

The council reduced the number of mendicant orders, suppressing several minor groups while preserving the [[dominicans]], [[franciscans]], [[carmelites]], and [[augustinians]] (Hermits). Its central historical significance is the brief, illusory possibility of healing the East-West schism — an attempt repeated at [[basel-ferrara-florence]] with the same political opportunism and the same eventual repudiation in the East.
