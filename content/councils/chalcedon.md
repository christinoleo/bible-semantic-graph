The Council of Chalcedon (451 CE) was convoked by Emperor Marcian at Chalcedon (across the Bosporus from Constantinople), gathering roughly 520 bishops — the largest of the ancient councils. It was called to repudiate the "Robber Council" of [[ephesus]] II (449), at which [[dioscorus-of-alexandria]] had restored the archimandrite Eutyches (who taught that Christ's humanity was absorbed into his divinity, "one nature after the union") and intimidated the bishops into ratifying his miaphysite Christology.

The council deposed Dioscorus and ratified as its doctrinal standard the [[tome-of-leo]] — the letter of Pope [[leo-i]] to Patriarch Flavian of Constantinople (449) — together with the second and third letters of [[cyril-of-alexandria]] to [[nestorius]]. The famous acclamation reportedly greeted Leo's Tome: "Peter has spoken through Leo." The council balanced Cyrillian and Antiochene-Western emphases, condemning both Eutychian monophysitism and any residual Nestorian dualism.

The [[chalcedonian-definition]] confesses [[christ]] as "one and the same Son ... acknowledged in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation (ἀσυγχύτως, ἀτρέπτως, ἀδιαιρέτως, ἀχωρίστως), the distinction of the natures being in no way taken away by the union, but rather the property of each nature being preserved, and concurring in one Person (πρόσωπον) and one Subsistence (ὑπόστασις)." The four adverbs become the structural skeleton of subsequent orthodox Christology.

Canon 28 (heavily disputed and rejected by Rome) granted Constantinople equal privileges with Rome on imperial-political grounds, deepening the East-West friction over the basis of papal primacy. [[leo-i]] accepted the doctrinal decrees but refused canon 28.

Chalcedon precipitated the **first lasting schism** in the church. The non-Chalcedonian "miaphysite" churches — Coptic Orthodox, Syriac Orthodox, Armenian Apostolic, Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo, Eritrean, and the Indian Malankara churches — rejected the council, arguing that "two natures after the union" betrayed [[cyril-of-alexandria]]'s formula *mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē* ("one nature of God the Word incarnate"). Modern ecumenical dialogues (since 1971) have largely concluded the disagreement was substantially terminological, but communion has not been restored. Chalcedon is the **fourth ecumenical council** for Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran, and Reformed traditions.
