Joshua, son of Nun, of the tribe of Ephraim (descendant of [[joseph]] via Ephraim). Originally Hoshea (`hôšēaʿ`, "salvation"); renamed `yĕhôšuaʿ` ("YHWH saves") by Moses (Num 13:16) — same root, Greek transliteration `Iēsoûs`, identical to the name of [[jesus]] (a fact Heb 4:8 plays on).

Servant of [[moses]] (`mĕšārēt mōšeh`) from young manhood (Ex 33:11). Commands Israel's forces against Amalek at Rephidim (Ex 17:9-13) — first military leader of Israel. One of the twelve spies sent into [[canaan]] (Num 13); with [[caleb]] alone brings back a faith-report, against the ten who say the land cannot be taken. For this both are exempted from the generation condemned to die in the wilderness (Num 14:24, 30).

Commissioned as Moses' successor by laying-on-of-hands (Num 27:18-23, Deut 31:7-8, 34:9) — the transfer of authority from prophet to commander, charisma to office. Leads the conquest of Canaan in the book that bears his name: crossing the Jordan on dry ground (Josh 3-4, an Exodus echo), the fall of Jericho (Josh 6), the southern and northern campaigns, allotment of tribal territories. The book climaxes in the covenant renewal at Shechem (Josh 24): "as for me and my house, we will serve YHWH" — and the people choose, by oath.

Conquest historiography is heavily contested. The Joshua account presents a rapid, comprehensive, divinely sanctioned military conquest with the `ḥērem` (devotion to destruction) practiced against Canaanite cities. Archaeology and the more gradual, regionalized picture of [[judges]] complicate this radically: most scholars now propose models of internal Israelite emergence (peasant revolt, gradual infiltration, or indigenous Canaanite stratification) with a smaller military component than Joshua narrates. The ethics of the `ḥērem` against Canaanite populations remain one of the OT's most uncomfortable issues for modern readers — see scholarly literature on biblical "genocide" and theological responses across traditions.

Dies at 110, buried in his Ephraimite inheritance at Timnath-serah (Josh 24:29-30). Heb 4:8-10 reads his entry-into-the-land as preparatory to a deeper "rest" to be entered through [[jesus]].
