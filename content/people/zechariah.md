Zechariah son of Berechiah son of Iddo is a post-exilic prophet active in [[jerusalem]] from the second year of Darius I (520 BCE), contemporary with [[haggai]] in the campaign to motivate Temple rebuilding under [[zerubbabel]] and Joshua the high priest ([[ezra]] 5:1, 6:14). His ministry is dated more precisely than almost any other prophet's — Zech 1:1, 1:7, and 7:1 give specific month, day, and year.

The first eight chapters present eight night visions — the man among the myrtle trees, the four horns and four craftsmen, the man with a measuring line, Joshua and the Satan in heavenly court, the golden lampstand, the flying scroll, the woman in the ephah, the four chariots — interspersed with oracles encouraging Temple completion and reform. The visionary apparatus (angelic interpreter, symbolic objects, cosmic landscape) is a key bridge between classical prophetic vision and full apocalyptic literature; the line from Zechariah's visions to [[book-of-daniel]] 7-12 and to [[revelation]] is direct.

Chapters 9-14, often called Deutero-Zechariah, are critically considered a separate compositional layer — most scholarship treats them as later (4th-3rd c. BCE on common readings), addressed to different historical circumstances, with sharply different style (oracular rather than visionary) and theology (more universal eschatology, the Day of YHWH, a coming king on a donkey, a pierced figure mourned, plagues on Egypt). These chapters are disproportionately cited in NT passion narratives.

NT mobilization of Zech 9-14 is intense. Zech 9:9 ("your king comes to you, righteous and victorious, lowly and riding on a donkey") is cited in the Triumphal Entry ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 21:5, [[gospel-of-john]] 12:15). Zech 11:12-13 — thirty pieces of silver thrown to the potter — frames Judas's betrayal in Matt 27:9 (oddly attributed to [[jeremiah]]). Zech 12:10 ("they shall look on him whom they have pierced") is cited in [[gospel-of-john]] 19:37 at the crucifixion. Zech 13:7 ("strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered") is cited at Gethsemane (Matt 26:31). The book's afterlife in Christian passion theology is among the densest in the canon.

The Zechariah killed "between the sanctuary and the altar" referenced by [[jesus]] in [[gospel-of-matthew]] 23:35 is more naturally identified with the Chronicler's Zechariah son of Jehoiada ([[2-chronicles]] 24:20-22) than with the post-exilic prophet, though the patronymic complicates the question.
