Ḥafṣ ibn Sulaymān al-Asadī al-Kūfī (c. 90–180 AH / 709–796 CE) — the rāwī (transmitter) whose reading from his teacher [[asim-ibn-abi-al-najud|ʿĀṣim ibn Abī al-Najūd]] (the Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim reading) is the recension carried by the overwhelming majority of printed [[quran|Qurʾān]]s today and the standard reading of the Sunni world outside the Maghreb. Classical [[hadith|ḥadīth]] biographical evaluation (*ʿilm al-rijāl*) records sharp negative judgements on Ḥafṣ as a transmitter of *ḥadīth* — *matrūk al-ḥadīth* ("abandoned in ḥadīth"), accused of fabrication by Ibn Maʿīn, Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal, and others — while his Qurʾānic transmission is fully accepted. The asymmetry ([[claim-hafs-trust-inconsistency-undercuts-gospel-author-rejection|trusted for the Qurʾān, untrusted for ḥadīth]]) is regularly invoked by Christian apologists as an *internal* inconsistency when Muslim interlocutors reject Gospel authors on biographical grounds.
