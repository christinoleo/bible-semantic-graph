Sarah, daughter of Raguel of [[ecbatana]], is the female counterpart to [[tobias]] in [[book-of-tobit]]. Distinct from [[sarah]] (wife of [[abraham]]) — same theophoric name ("princess"), unrelated narrative. The demon Asmodeus has killed her seven successive bridegrooms on each wedding night (Tob 3:8), and her own maids accuse her of having strangled them. In despair she prays for death in the same hour [[tobit]] prays the same prayer in [[nineveh]] — the synchronicity is the book's structural pivot.

[[raphael-archangel]] is dispatched in answer to both prayers. By Mosaic endogamy law she is the only eligible match for [[tobias]] as her nearest kinsman (Tob 6:12, citing Num 36); the marriage doubles as halakhic exemplar and demonic exorcism. On the wedding night Tobias burns fish heart and liver as incense; Asmodeus flees to upper Egypt where Raphael binds him (Tob 8:3). She returns with Tobias to Nineveh, bears seven sons (a deliberate inversion of seven dead husbands), and outlives both her father-in-law and her husband.

The Asmodeus episode is the most extensive demonological narrative in the [[catholic]]/[[orthodox-eastern]] biblical canon and a primary source for medieval and Renaissance demonology. Rabbinic tradition develops Asmodeus independently in [[talmud|b. Pesahim 110a]] and the Solomon legends; the Tobit Asmodeus is the earliest extant Jewish appearance.
