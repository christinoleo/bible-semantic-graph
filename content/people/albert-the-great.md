Albert the Great (c. 1200-1280) — Dominican friar; the *Doctor Universalis* of the medieval Latin West and the teacher who set [[thomas-aquinas]] on his Aristotelian course. Born to a Swabian noble family near Lauingen; entered the Dominicans c. 1223 at Padua; lectured at Cologne, Paris (master 1245-1248), then back at Cologne where he founded the *studium generale* and taught Aquinas from 1248 to 1252. Briefly bishop of Regensburg (1260-1262) before returning to scholarship and to the Dominican studia.

The first Latin to read Aristotle whole — paraphrasing and commenting on the entire Aristotelian corpus (logic, natural philosophy, metaphysics, ethics) and integrating Arabic commentators (Avicenna, Averroes) and natural-scientific observation. *De animalibus, De vegetabilibus, De mineralibus* exhibit a working empiricist who dissected, observed, and corrected Aristotle from experience — unusual for the thirteenth century. His Commentary on the *Sentences* of [[peter-lombard]] and *Summa de creaturis* gave Aquinas the architecture his pupil would refine. Defended Aquinas posthumously against the 1277 Paris condemnations; canonized 1931; declared Doctor of the Church and patron of natural scientists by Pius XI.
