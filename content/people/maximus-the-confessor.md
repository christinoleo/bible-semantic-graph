Maximus the Confessor (c. 580-662) — Byzantine monastic theologian; the decisive defender of Chalcedonian dyothelitism — that Christ has two natural wills, divine and human — against the imperial Monothelite compromise. Probably born to a noble Constantinopolitan family (the rival Syriac *Vita* has him a Palestinian villager); served briefly as *protoasekretis* in the imperial chancery under Heraclius before entering the monastery of Chrysopolis c. 614; fled the Persian invasion to North Africa, where he found his decisive interlocutor in the exiled abbot Sophronius (later patriarch of Jerusalem).

*Ambigua* — speculative paraphrases resolving the "difficulties" in [[gregory-of-nazianzus]] and [[pseudo-dionysius]], rejecting [[origen]]ist pre-existence of souls and apokatastasis while preserving Origenist trajectories of deification; the cosmology of logoi-in-the-Logos became seminal for [[gregory-palamas]] and the entire later Byzantine mystical tradition. *Mystagogia* is a contemplative reading of the divine liturgy as cosmic and ecclesial mystery. *Questions to Thalassius* — sixty-five exegetical *quaestiones* on hard Scripture passages.

The *Disputation with Pyrrhus* at Carthage (645) publicly defeated the deposed Monothelite patriarch and turned the African and Roman churches against Constantinople. Maximus organized the Lateran Council of 649 with Pope Martin I, which condemned Monothelitism and the imperial *Typos*. Arrested in 653; tried at Constantinople (655 and 662) on charges of treason and theological obstinacy; refused to commune with the imperial church; in 662 his right hand and tongue were severed and he was exiled to Lazica on the Black Sea, where he died. Vindicated posthumously at [[constantinople-iii]] (680-681), which adopted his dyothelite formula verbatim.
