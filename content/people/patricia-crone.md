Danish-American Islamic studies scholar (1945–2015), Princeton's Institute for Advanced Study, the most influential revisionist historian of early Islam. Co-author with Michael Cook of *Hagarism* (1977), which proposed a radical reconstruction of Islamic origins from non-Muslim contemporary sources, treating the canonical Islamic tradition as later sectarian construction. *Meccan Trade and the Rise of Islam* (1987) demolished the consensus economic-historical reading of Mecca's commercial importance. Her later work moderated some early positions but consistently treated the canonical Sirah and hadith as late narrative constructions rather than historical sources — a stance that informs the [[claim-quran-recounts-bible-events-six-centuries-late|critical-historical]] approach to Islamic textual claims.
