Gad, seventh son of [[jacob]], borne by Zilpah — [[leah]]'s handmaid — and reckoned as Leah's (Gen 30:9-11). Name glossed as `bĕgād` / `bā gād`, "fortune" or "a troop comes." Full brother to [[asher]].

Eponymous ancestor of the Tribe of Gad, which together with [[reuben]] and the half-tribe of Manasseh asked for and received territory east of the Jordan, in Gilead (Num 32) — fertile pastureland suited to their livestock. The Jacob blessing (Gen 49:19) plays on the name with a tight pun: `gād gĕdûd yĕgûdennû wĕhûʾ yāgūd ʿāqēb` — "Gad, raiders shall raid him, but he shall raid at their heels." Moses' blessing (Deut 33:20-21) praises Gad as a lion crouching, tearing the arm and scalp, and notes that "he chose the best of the land for himself." The Mesha Stele (9th c. BCE) confirms Gad as an Israelite tribal presence in the Transjordan during the divided monarchy.
