John of Damascus (~675-749) — last of the Greek fathers; Damascene Christian under Umayyad rule, born Mansur ibn Sarjun into a family of Melkite financial administrators serving the caliphate. Followed his father's office briefly before resigning to enter the monastery of Mar Saba in the Judean desert around 706, where he spent the remainder of his life as monk and presbyter under the Patriarchate of Jerusalem.

The Fount of Knowledge (Pēgē gnōseōs) in three parts: a philosophical primer (Dialectica) of Aristotelian and Neoplatonic terms; a heresiology (De haeresibus) of one hundred sects including a striking and historically important chapter 100/101 on Islam ("the heresy of the Ishmaelites") — the earliest extended Christian theological treatment of Islam from a writer with direct knowledge; and De fide orthodoxa (Expositio fidei), a systematic synthesis of the Greek patristic tradition in one hundred chapters. The latter was translated into Latin in the 12th century and influenced [[thomas-aquinas]] decisively as the entry point to Greek theology.

Three Treatises on the Divine Images (726-730) — composed beyond Byzantine reach under Muslim rule — defend the veneration of icons against the iconoclast edicts of Leo III and Constantine V. Distinguishes proskynēsis (veneration, due to icons and saints) from latreia (worship, due to God alone); argues the incarnation as the warrant for the icon (the invisible God has made himself visible in the flesh); and frees the image-debate from straight Old Testament prohibition by the dispensational logic of the incarnation. Vindicated at [[nicaea-ii]] (787), forty years after his death.

A prolific hymnographer of the Octoechos, the canon liturgy genre, and the Paschal canon ("The day of resurrection") still sung in the Byzantine rite; the Octoechos and the hymnal restructuring at Mar Saba shape Byzantine liturgical music to the present. Declared Doctor of the Church by Leo XIII (1890). The point of contact for medieval Latin theology with the Greek synthesis Cyril, the Cappadocians, and Maximus the Confessor had built.
