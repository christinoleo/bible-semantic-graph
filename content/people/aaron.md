Aaron, elder brother of [[moses]] and [[miriam]], Levite. Eponymous ancestor of the Aaronic priesthood. Three years older than Moses (Ex 7:7). Acts as Moses' mouthpiece (`pe`, lit. "mouth") before Pharaoh because of Moses' speech impediment (Ex 4:14-16); wields the staff that becomes a serpent and triggers several plagues.

Golden calf (Ex 32). While Moses is on Sinai receiving the covenant, the people demand gods to go before them; Aaron collects gold, casts the calf, declares "these are your gods, O Israel, who brought you up from Egypt," builds an altar, proclaims a feast to YHWH. When Moses descends and confronts him, Aaron's excuse is comically evasive: "I threw [the gold] into the fire, and out came this calf." The narrative both indicts him and protects him — he survives, retains the priesthood, but the moment haunts the priestly tradition. [[documentary-hypothesis]] notes the tension: P sources elevate the Aaronic line, but the calf story (composite, with E and Deuteronomistic layers) preserves a strand critical of it — possibly polemic against the northern calf-shrines at Bethel and Dan (cf. 1 Kgs 12:28, [[jeroboam]]'s near-verbatim repetition).

Consecrated as high priest in Lev 8-9 along with his sons Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, Ithamar. Nadab and Abihu die immediately for offering "strange fire" (Lev 10:1-2) — the holiness-system's first lethal demonstration. Aaron, told not to mourn, is silent: `wayyiddōm ʾahărōn`. Eleazar succeeds him.

Joins [[miriam]] in challenging Moses' Cushite wife (Num 12); Miriam alone is struck with leprosy, Aaron is spared — a P-favoritism that scholarship reads structurally. Aaron's budding rod (Num 17) vindicates his line against Korah's challenge. Barred with Moses from [[canaan]] for Meribah; dies on Mt. Hor, vestments transferred to Eleazar (Num 20:22-29).

Reception. Hebrews 5-7 frames the Aaronic priesthood as preparatory and superseded — [[jesus]] as priest "not according to the order of Aaron but according to the order of Melchizedek." Modern Jewish kohanim trace patrilineal descent (claimed, with mixed historical and genetic evidence) to Aaron. The priestly blessing of Num 6:24-26 is recited by kohanim to the present day.
