Northern Irish mathematician (Emeritus Professor of Mathematics, Oxford; b. 1943) and Christian apologist, prominent in the science-faith debates of the 2000s–2020s as a public interlocutor of [[richard-dawkins|Richard Dawkins]] (Birmingham 2007, Oxford 2008), Christopher Hitchens, Peter Singer, and Stephen Hawking. Distinctive emphases: science is the *fruit* of the Judeo-Christian worldview, not its rival; the laws of physics describe but do not create ([[claim-laws-of-physics-do-not-create-lennox|critique of Hawking]]); DNA's specified complexity is information that points to mind ([[claim-dna-as-information-points-to-mind|information argument]]); and "[[claim-scientism-self-refuting|scientism]]" — the claim that only science yields knowledge — is itself an unscientific philosophical claim.

Major works: *God's Undertaker: Has Science Buried God?* (2009); *God and Stephen Hawking: Whose Design Is It Anyway?* (2010); *Gunning for God: Why the New Atheists Are Missing the Target* (2011); *Seven Days That Divide the World* (2011); *2084: AI and the Future of Humanity* (2020); *Cosmic Chemistry* (2021).
