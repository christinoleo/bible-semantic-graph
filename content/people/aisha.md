ʿĀʾisha bint Abī Bakr (c. 614–678 CE), daughter of [[abu-bakr|Abū Bakr]] and wife of the prophet [[muhammad|Muḥammad]]. Married to Muhammad as a child and consummated according to *[[sahih-al-bukhari|Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī]]* and *[[sunan-al-nasai|Sunan an-Nasāʾī]]* at age nine, she became one of Islam's most prolific hadith transmitters and a politically consequential figure during the early caliphate (notably at the Battle of the Camel).
