Herod Antipas — son of Herod the Great and Malthace, tetrarch of Galilee and Perea from 4 BCE to 39 CE. Rules during the entire public ministry of [[jesus]] (Galilee being his jurisdiction) and the execution of [[john-the-baptist]]. Founds Tiberias on the Sea of Galilee c. 19 CE as his new capital, naming it for the emperor — built atop an old cemetery, rendering it ritually unclean and unattractive to Jewish residents at first.

Marries Herodias, formerly wife of his half-brother Herod (Philip in [[gospel-of-mark]] 6:17, conflicted with Josephus), in violation of Levitical law ([[leviticus]] 18:16; 20:21). [[john-the-baptist]] publicly denounces the marriage; Antipas imprisons him at Machaerus and eventually beheads him. The gospels ([[gospel-of-mark]] 6:14-29; [[gospel-of-matthew]] 14:1-12) novelize the execution around Salome's dance and a rash oath; Josephus (Ant. 18.116-119) gives a soberer political-fear motive. The two accounts are independent corroboration of the historical execution.

The original marriage rupture sets up his eventual military disaster: the spurned first wife, daughter of the Nabataean king Aretas IV, returns home, and her father invades and routs Antipas' army (c. 36 CE). Josephus reports the popular Jewish reading: divine punishment for the [[john-the-baptist]] killing.

In [[gospel-of-luke]] 13:31-33 [[jesus]] is warned of Antipas' designs ("Go and tell that fox..."); in [[gospel-of-luke]] 23:6-16, [[pontius-pilate]] sends [[jesus]] to Antipas, who happens to be in Jerusalem for Passover — Antipas mocks him in a gorgeous robe and sends him back, and the previously hostile [[pontius-pilate]] and Antipas become friends "that very day." The Lukan episode is sometimes read as redactional (no parallel in the other gospels), sometimes as an authentic memory of jurisdictional ping-pong. Antipas is finally deposed in 39 CE by Caligula (provoked into requesting royal status by his nephew Herod Agrippa I) and exiled to Gaul.
