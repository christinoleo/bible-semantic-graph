Deborah is a judge and prophetess in pre-monarchic Israel (Judg 4-5), one of the few women holding either office in the Hebrew Bible. She judges from beneath a palm in the hill country of Ephraim and summons [[barak]] to muster northern tribes against Sisera, commander of [[jabin]] of Hazor's army.

The prose account (Judg 4) and the poetic [[song-of-deborah]] (Judg 5) preserve parallel but divergent versions of the same event. The Song is widely held to be among the oldest sustained passages in the Hebrew Bible — its archaic morphology and orthography place it in the 12th-11th c. BCE on linguistic grounds. Critical scholarship treats it as a near-contemporary war hymn; the prose framing is later Deuteronomistic redaction.

Deborah's authority is presented matter-of-factly: she prophesies, adjudicates, and commands without apology. [[barak]] refuses to march without her, and she rebukes him by predicting that the victory's glory will fall to a woman — fulfilled when [[jael]] drives the tent peg through Sisera's temple. The Song relishes the irony.

Reception traditions vary. Rabbinic literature is ambivalent — some sources read her prominence as indictment of male leadership of the era. Feminist and liberation readings recover her as a counterweight to the patriarchal default. She is one of seven prophetesses recognized in the Talmud (Megillah 14a).
