Simon called Peter (Aramaic Cephas, "rock") — Galilean fisherman from Bethsaida, brother of [[andrew]], chief of the Twelve. Called by [[jesus]] from the Sea of Galilee ([[gospel-of-mark]] 1:16-18); renamed at Caesarea Philippi after the confession "you are the Christ" ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 16:13-20, where "on this rock I will build my church" anchors later Roman primacy claims). Inner-circle witness at the [[transfiguration]] and Gethsemane alongside [[james-son-of-zebedee]] and [[john-apostle]].

Threefold denial during the passion ([[gospel-of-mark]] 14:66-72 and parallels) — historically secure under the criterion of embarrassment — followed by threefold restoration in [[gospel-of-john]] 21. First-listed witness of the resurrection in the pre-Pauline creed of [[1-corinthians]] 15:5. In [[acts]] 1-12 he leads the Jerusalem church, preaches at Pentecost ([[acts]] 2), opens the gentile mission with Cornelius ([[acts]] 10), and is imprisoned by Herod Agrippa I before miraculous release.

The Antioch incident ([[galatians]] 2:11-14) shows [[paul]] publicly rebuking him for withdrawing from gentile table fellowship under pressure from "men from James" — a sharp reminder that the early movement was not monolithic on Jewish-gentile relations. Traditionally martyred in Rome under Nero (c. 64-67 CE), crucified upside down per later tradition (Acts of Peter); 1 Clement 5 attests Roman martyrdom without detail. Excavations beneath St. Peter's Basilica claim his bones.

[[1-peter]] and [[2-peter]] are attributed to him. [[1-peter]]'s polished Greek and Pauline echoes lead most critical scholars to read it as written by a secretary or composed in his name from Rome ("Babylon," 5:13); [[2-peter]] is near-universally considered pseudepigraphal — latest book of the NT, dependent on [[jude]]. Papias and Irenaeus link [[gospel-of-mark]] to Peter's preaching: Mark as interpreter of Peter writing down what he remembered.
