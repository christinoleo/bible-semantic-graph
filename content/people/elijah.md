Elijah the Tishbite is the towering 9th-c. BCE prophetic figure of the northern kingdom under [[ahab]] and [[jezebel]]. He appears abruptly in 1 Kings 17 announcing a drought "by my word," is fed by ravens at the Wadi Cherith, multiplies meal and oil for the widow at [[zarephath]], and raises her dead son. The cycle (1 Kings 17 - 2 Kings 2) is structured around miracle and confrontation rather than oracle — Elijah is closer to the "former prophets" (Samuel, Nathan) than to the literary prophets who come after him.

The defining set piece is the contest at Mt. Carmel (1 Kings 18). Elijah confronts 450 prophets of [[baal]] under [[ahab]]'s patronage; both sides build altars; the deity who answers by fire is God. The Baal prophets cut themselves and shout from morning to noon with no response. Elijah pours water on his altar three times, prays briefly, and fire falls. He has the Baal prophets killed at the Wadi Kishon.

The Mt. Horeb sequence that follows (1 Kings 19) is one of the canon's most psychologically interior prophetic narratives. [[jezebel]]'s death threat sends Elijah fleeing south in suicidal despair, lying down under a juniper tree asking to die. An angel feeds him; he travels forty days and forty nights to [[mount-horeb]] (Sinai), where [[yhwh]] passes in wind, earthquake, and fire — but is found in "a still small voice" / "sound of sheer silence." The episode is a deliberate inversion of the [[moses]] Sinai theophany. Three tasks: anoint Hazael over Aram, [[jehu]] over Israel, [[elisha]] in his own place.

He does not die. The chariot of fire takes him up at the Jordan in [[elisha]]'s sight (2 Kings 2:11). This makes him eligible for eschatological return. [[book-of-malachi]] 4:5-6 ("I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and terrible day of YHWH") generates the NT identification of [[john-the-baptist]] as Elijah redivivus ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 11:14, 17:10-13). Elijah also appears with [[moses]] at the [[transfiguration]] (Matt 17, Mark 9, Luke 9), pairing the prophets representing covenant law and prophetic witness.
