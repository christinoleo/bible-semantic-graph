Tenth-century Tiberian Masorete (active c. 920 CE), final and most influential scholar of the Ben Asher dynasty of [[masoretic-text|Masoretes]], to whom is attributed the vocalization, cantillation, and Masorah of the [[aleppo-codex|Aleppo Codex]] — the most authoritative manuscript of the Tiberian Masoretic tradition and the textual basis of modern critical Hebrew Bible editions (the Hebrew University Bible Project, BHQ). The Ben Asher tradition became dominant over the rival Ben Naphtali tradition by Maimonides' explicit endorsement.
