Ahmed Deedat (1918-2005) — South African Muslim apologist and lecturer; the figure who, more than any other, established the modern public-debate format as the dominant register of [[islamic-apologetic]] in the late twentieth century. Born at Tadkeshwar in Gujarat; emigrated to South Africa as a child; began his polemical career responding to Christian missionaries at the Adams Mission station in Natal. Founded the Islamic Propagation Centre International (IPCI) in [[durban]] in 1957.

Built a global apologetic platform out of the televised debate — large-venue engagements against the American Pentecostal evangelist Jimmy Swaggart (Louisiana, 1986), the Arab-American convert apologist Anis Shorrosh, the Spanish ex-Jesuit Jose Branco, the British evangelist Erwin Lutzer, distributed on VHS and later online. *The Choice* and the booklets *Is the Bible God's Word?*, *Crucifixion or Cruci-fiction?*, *What the Bible Says About Muhummed*, and *Muhummed the Greatest* compress his standard arguments — *taḥrīf*, the [[paraclete-as-muhammad-argument]], the [[swoon-theory]] of the crucifixion, the [[isaiah-29-12-argument]] — into pamphlet-length tracts that have been translated into dozens of languages and remain in heavy circulation. Suffered a debilitating stroke in 1996 that ended his lecturing; the IPCI continues distribution of his archive. The Deedat style — confrontational, scripture-quoting, performative — is the inheritance assumed by [[zakir-naik]] and the wider generation of Peace TV preachers.
