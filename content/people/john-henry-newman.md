John Henry Newman (1801-1890) — English Anglican-turned-Catholic theologian; intellectual leader of the Oxford Movement in his Anglican phase and afterwards the most consequential Anglophone Catholic thinker of the nineteenth century. Born to a London banker; precocious classicist and evangelical convert in adolescence; elected fellow of Oriel College, Oxford in 1822, then tutor and from 1828 vicar of the University Church of St. Mary the Virgin, where his Parochial and Plain Sermons drew a generation of undergraduates.

Oxford Movement (1833-1845) — co-led with John Keble, [[edward-bouverie-pusey]] (forward ref), Hurrell Froude. Tracts for the Times argued for the Catholic, apostolic, sacramental, and creedal character of the Church of England against Erastian liberalism and against evangelical pietism. Tract 90 (1841) — Newman's attempt to read the 39 Articles in a Catholic-compatible direction — provoked the storm that began his Anglican retreat to Littlemore. The decisive intellectual hinge was the patristic study of the fourth-century Arian controversy, in which the Eutychian/Monophysite parallel led him to suspect that contemporary Anglicanism stood in the place of historical heresy with respect to Rome.

Received into the Roman Catholic Church 9 October 1845 at Littlemore by the Passionist Dominic Barberi, with several Oxford companions following. Ordained Catholic priest 1847; founded the Birmingham Oratory of [[philip-neri]] (forward ref) 1848. The Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine (1845) — written during the conversion crisis and published just before — defended the legitimacy of doctrinal change through seven "notes" or marks of authentic development (continuity of type, conservative action upon the past, etc.) over against corruption. Foundational text for modern Catholic theology of tradition.

Apologia Pro Vita Sua (1864) — autobiographical narrative composed at speed in reply to Charles Kingsley's accusation of dishonesty; one of the great spiritual autobiographies in English. The Grammar of Assent (1870) develops his epistemology of religious knowledge: real (concrete, particular, image-rich) versus notional (abstract) assent, the illative sense as the cumulative judgment by which probabilities converge to certainty in concrete matters — proto-personalist, attentive to the conscience as "the aboriginal Vicar of Christ in the soul." The Idea of a University (1852, 1858) argues for liberal education as an end in itself within a confessional university structure.

Strained relations with Catholic Ultramontane circles in England (Henry Edward Manning, W. G. Ward); reservations about the timing and scope of the [[vatican-i]] definition of papal infallibility (1870), which he accepted but considered inopportune. Created cardinal by Leo XIII in 1879 — his motto Cor ad cor loquitur ("heart speaks to heart"). Beatified 2010 by Benedict XVI; canonized 2019 by Francis. Influence on [[vatican-ii]] in development of doctrine, role of laity, ecumenism, and theology of conscience is widely acknowledged.
