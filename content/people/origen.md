Origen of Alexandria (~184-253) — most prolific patristic exegete and the most consequential systematic theologian before [[athanasius]]. Born to Christian parents; his father Leonides martyred under Septimius Severus (202). Headed the Alexandrian catechetical school from his late teens (succeeding [[clement-of-alexandria]] de facto), then moved to Caesarea Maritima around 232 after a falling out with bishop Demetrius over his irregular ordination by Palestinian bishops.

Hexapla — the gargantuan six-column comparative edition of the Hebrew Bible (Hebrew, Hebrew transliterated in Greek letters, Aquila, Symmachus, [[septuagint]], Theodotion) — anchors textual criticism for a millennium. Only fragments survive. Commentaries, homilies, and scholia cover most of the Bible; the systematic Peri Archōn (On First Principles) is the first attempt at a Christian systematic theology in four books. Contra Celsum (eight books, ~248) replies to the lost True Doctrine of the pagan philosopher Celsus and is the most substantial early apologetic engagement with Greek philosophical criticism.

Hermeneutics: threefold sense (literal / moral / spiritual, mapped to body / soul / spirit per Prov 22:20-21 LXX), with the spiritual sense the goal. Allegorical reading extended even to apparently historical narratives where the literal seems impossible or unworthy. Foundational for later Christian allegoresis and the medieval quadriga.

Speculative theses condemned at [[constantinople-ii]] (553) and earlier under Justinian: pre-existence of souls; apokatastasis (eventual universal restoration including Satan); subordinationist Trinitarianism (the Son eternally generated but lesser than the Father). The condemnations target sixth-century Origenism (developed by Evagrius and the monks of the New Laura) as much as the historical Origen — the distinction matters and remains scholarly debate. The legend of self-castration on Matt 19:12 (Eusebius HE 6.8) is unreliable and was disputed in antiquity. Tortured under the Decian persecution; died of injuries soon after.
