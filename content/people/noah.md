Noah, tenth from [[adam]] via the Sethite line (Gen 5). Name etymologized in Gen 5:29 from `nāḥam` ("comfort / relief") — his father Lamech: "this one shall give us rest from our work." Introduced as `ṣaddîq tāmîm bĕdōrōtāyw` ("righteous, blameless in his generations") who "walked with God" (Gen 6:9) — a P formula echoing Enoch (Gen 5:24).

Builds the ark on divine command and survives the Flood (Gen 6-9) with wife, three sons — [[shem]], [[ham]], [[japheth]] — and their wives. The Flood narrative is a textbook composite: P and J strands interwoven, with doublets on the number of animals (Gen 6:19-20 vs. 7:2-3), the duration of the flood (40 days vs. 150), and the cause. See [[documentary-hypothesis]].

ANE parallels are extensive and unmistakable: the Sumerian Eridu Genesis (Ziusudra), the Akkadian Atrahasis epic, and the Gilgamesh Epic tablet XI (Utnapishtim) all feature divine flood-decree, righteous survivor, multi-decked boat, bird-release sequence, post-flood sacrifice, and divine smelling/blessing. Scholarly consensus reads Gen 6-9 as Israel's polemical retelling of a shared Mesopotamian tradition — see [[enuma-elish]] for comparable polemic logic.

Post-flood: sacrifice, the Noachic covenant with rainbow-sign (Gen 9:8-17 — universal, unconditional, with all flesh, the first explicit `bĕrît` in the canon), the disturbing vineyard episode (Gen 9:20-27) where a drunk Noah curses Canaan after [[ham]] "sees his nakedness" — a passage gravely misused in later pro-slavery exegesis (the so-called "Curse of Ham"). Dies 950 years old. In rabbinic tradition the Noachide laws (seven universal commandments derived from Gen 9) define righteous-gentile obligation; Christian reception treats Noah as type of [[jesus]] (Heb 11:7) and the flood as type of baptism (1 Pet 3:20-21).
