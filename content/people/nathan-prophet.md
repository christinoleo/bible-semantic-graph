Nathan is the court prophet of [[david]] and a structurally pivotal figure in [[2-samuel]] and the opening of [[1-kings]]. He delivers the oracle of the [[davidic-covenant]] (2 Sam 7) — [[yhwh]]'s promise of an enduring dynasty in exchange for David's son building the Temple — which becomes the constitutional foundation of all subsequent royal and messianic theology in the canon.

His most famous scene is the parable of the rich man and the poor man's lamb (2 Sam 12), confronting David over his appropriation of [[bathsheba]] and murder of Uriah. The trap is rhetorical: David condemns the rich man to death before Nathan reveals "you are the man." The episode establishes the prophetic office as authorized critic of the king — a template the classical prophets pick up.

At David's deathbed (1 Kings 1) Nathan engineers, with [[bathsheba]], the succession of [[solomon]] over [[adonijah]]. The narrative leaves open how much of the appeal to a prior royal oath was historical and how much manufactured for the moment. He survives into Solomon's reign as part of the cabinet; two of his sons hold court office under Solomon (1 Kings 4:5).

The Chronicler attributes prophetic source material to Nathan ("the chronicles of Nathan the prophet" — 1 Chr 29:29, 2 Chr 9:29). Modern critical scholarship is skeptical of his recoverability as a discrete historical source but treats him as an essential narrative-theological device through which the Deuteronomistic History asserts the prophet's authority over the king.
