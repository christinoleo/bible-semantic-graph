Japheth, son of [[noah]], eponym of the "Japhethite" peoples. The Table of Nations (Gen 10:2-5) routes through him the northern and coastland peoples — Gomer (Cimmerians), Magog, Madai (Medes), Javan (Ionians/Greeks), Tubal, Meshech, Tiras — i.e., the Indo-European and Anatolian world from Israel's vantage. "From these the coastland peoples spread."

In the vineyard episode (Gen 9:20-27) Japheth, with [[shem]], walks in backwards to cover [[noah]]'s nakedness. Noah's blessing on him is a Hebrew pun: `yapt ʾĕlōhîm lĕyepet` — "May God enlarge (`yapt`) Japheth" — and "let him dwell in the tents of Shem" (Gen 9:27), an ambiguous line read variously as Japheth sheltered by Shem's covenant, Japheth politically dominant over Shem's territory, or (in patristic exegesis) the Gentiles entering Israel's covenantal space through [[jesus]].

Lighter reception than his brothers. Greek and Latin antiquity occasionally identified Japheth with Iapetos, the Titan father of Prometheus — a doubtful etymological link but a long-lived one. Rabbinic tradition tends to favor [[shem]]; patristic exegesis (esp. with Gen 9:27) makes Japheth a figure of the Gentile mission. 19th-century race theory pressed the three-sons typology into a Shem-Ham-Japheth racial schema (Semitic / Hamitic / Japhetic = "Aryan"), with predictable damage; modern scholarship treats Gen 10 as a stylized ethnogeography rather than a racial taxonomy.
