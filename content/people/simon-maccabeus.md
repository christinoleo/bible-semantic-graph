Simon "Thassi" (1 Macc 2:3 — the epithet of disputed etymology, possibly "the zealous" or "the guide"), second son of [[mattathias]], is the founder of the Hasmonean dynasty proper. He commands alongside [[judas-maccabeus]] and [[jonathan-maccabeus]] through the [[maccabean-revolt]] (1 Macc 5, 9, 11); on Jonathan's capture by Trypho in 143 BCE he assumes leadership of the Judean state.

In 142 BCE Simon negotiates de facto independence from Demetrius II: tribute is remitted, the "yoke of the Gentiles" is lifted, and 1 Macc 13:41-42 declares "in the first year of Simon the great high priest and commander and leader of the Jews" — the inaugural formula of Hasmonean self-dating. The Great Assembly of 140 BCE (1 Macc 14:27-49) formalizes him as ethnarch and high priest "forever, until a trustworthy prophet should arise" — the qualifier acknowledging the [[zadokite]] anomaly while making the office hereditary. He captures the Akra in Jerusalem (141 BCE), removing the last Seleucid garrison.

He is assassinated in 134 BCE at Dok near [[jericho]] by his son-in-law Ptolemy son of Abubus, along with two of his sons (1 Macc 16); the third son, John Hyrcanus I, escapes to succeed him. [[1-maccabees]] closes shortly after — its terminus is essentially the Simon settlement, lending support to the view that it was composed under John Hyrcanus as Hasmonean court history. Distinct from [[simon-peter]], [[simon-the-zealot]], and [[simon-of-cyrene]] in the New Testament — the Hasmonean Simon predates them all by a century and a half.
