Stephen — first Christian martyr (protomartyr). A Hellenist (Greek-speaking diaspora Jew) in the Jerusalem church, one of the seven appointed in [[acts]] 6 to handle food distribution after Hellenist widows are neglected in favor of Hebraist widows — the first recorded ethnic-cultural fault line in the movement, resolved organizationally. Tradition calls the seven "deacons" but [[acts]] never uses the noun.

Performs "great wonders and signs" and disputes with members of the synagogue of the Freedmen (former slaves and their descendants). Arrested on charges of speaking against the temple and the Torah — the same charges later leveled against [[paul]] and a charge that [[gospel-of-mark]] 14:58 reports against [[jesus]] himself. Delivers the longest speech in [[acts]] (chapter 7): a re-narration of Israel's history from [[abraham]] through the temple, ending in a polemic — "you stiff-necked people... as your fathers did, so do you" — that frames [[jesus]] as the Righteous One whom they killed, in the line of all the persecuted prophets.

Stoned outside Jerusalem, sees the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God — the only NT occurrence of "Son of Man" outside [[jesus]]' own usage. Witnesses lay their cloaks at the feet of "a young man named Saul" ([[paul]]), who "approved of their killing him" ([[acts]] 8:1) — the most consequential cameo in the NT, since the persecution that follows scatters the church and the figure approving the lynching will become its principal apostle.

Stephen's speech is a literary set-piece, theologically polemic against temple-centric Judaism in a way that anticipates [[hebrews]] and the wider Christian supersessionist trajectory. Historicity of the event is generally accepted (the cameo by Saul is unlikely to be invented); historicity of the speech as delivered, less so.
