Rehoboam is the son and successor of [[solomon]], the first king of the now-truncated southern kingdom of Judah after the division of the united monarchy (traditional dating c. 931 BCE). His reign opens with the disastrous assembly at Shechem (1 Kings 12), where the northern tribes ask for relief from Solomon's forced labor and taxation. Rejecting his elders' counsel for accommodation and following his contemporaries' advice to escalate — "my little finger is thicker than my father's loins" — he loses ten tribes to [[jeroboam]] within a day.

The Deuteronomistic narrator frames the schism as direct fulfillment of [[yhwh]]'s judgment on [[solomon]]'s apostasy, announced through the prophet Ahijah. Rehoboam's residual Judah retains [[jerusalem]] and the Temple but lacks the demographic and economic base of the north. The Egyptian pharaoh Shishak (Shoshenq I) invades in his fifth year and strips the Temple treasury (1 Kings 14:25-26) — an event corroborated by Shoshenq's campaign list at Karnak, one of the firmer chronological anchors in the period.

[[2-chronicles]] 11-12 supplements with material about fortification of Judahite cities and a more sympathetic spiritual trajectory, though it acknowledges his abandonment of "the law of YHWH." The Chronicler's distinct theological program — focused on the southern kingdom, the Davidic line, and proper Temple worship — colors the divergence from Kings.

He appears in the [[gospel-of-matthew]] genealogy of [[jesus]] (Matt 1:7), maintaining the Davidic line through the divided-kingdom period. Reception traditions are generally unflattering; he is the textbook case of the imprudent heir who squanders an inheritance through arrogance.
