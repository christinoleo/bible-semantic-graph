Andrew — Galilean fisherman from Bethsaida, brother of [[peter]]. In the synoptic call narrative ([[gospel-of-mark]] 1:16-18) he and [[peter]] are summoned together from their nets. [[gospel-of-john]] 1:35-42 gives the alternate, theologically loaded version: Andrew is first a disciple of [[john-the-baptist]], hears the Baptist identify [[jesus]] as Lamb of God, follows, and then brings [[peter]] to [[jesus]] — earning him the Orthodox title Prōtoklētos, "first-called."

Sparse synoptic role beyond the Twelve lists. Johannine portrait gives him three vignettes of bringing others to [[jesus]]: [[peter]] (John 1:42), the boy with loaves at the feeding of the five thousand (John 6:8-9), and the Greeks who wished to see Jesus (John 12:20-22). Last NT mention in [[acts]] 1:13 in the upper room.

Tradition assigns mission fields in Scythia and Achaea; crucified at Patras on an X-shaped cross (the "St. Andrew's cross" or saltire, embedded in the flags of Scotland and Russia). Apocryphal Acts of Andrew (2nd-3rd c.) and a continuing Eastern Orthodox tradition treat him as founder of the see of Byzantium, parallel to [[peter]]'s Rome — a charter for ecclesiastical parity between East and West.
