Dunn, James D. G. (1939-2020) — British New Testament scholar; Lightfoot Professor of Divinity at [[durham]] from 1982 until retirement in 2003. Trained at Cambridge under C. F. D. Moule. The figure who in a 1982 Manson Memorial Lecture christened [[e-p-sanders]]'s post-1977 reading of Paul "the [[new-perspective-on-paul]]" — the label that organized a generation of debate. Methodist by ordination; ecumenically active throughout his career.

Two large bodies of work. On Paul, the new-perspective program is developed across *The Theology of Paul the Apostle* (1998) and collected essays: Paul's polemic against "works of the law" reads as a polemic against Jewish ethnic-boundary markers (circumcision, food laws, Sabbath) rather than against meritorious works-righteousness in the Lutheran sense, and justification language describes God's declaration of covenant membership extended now to gentiles. On [[jesus]], *Christology in the Making* (1980) traces how high christology develops within Second Temple Jewish categories rather than as a Hellenistic apotheosis, and *Jesus Remembered* (2003) — the first volume of his *Christianity in the Making* trilogy — relocates the historical question from "what was said" to "how was it remembered," displacing the criteria-of-authenticity apparatus with an oral-tradition model. Positioned between [[e-p-sanders]] and [[n-t-wright]] in the new-perspective lineage and broadly within mainstream [[historical-critical-scholarship]].
