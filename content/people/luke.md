Luke — gentile companion of [[paul]], named only three times in the NT: "the beloved physician" ([[colossians]] 4:14), "only Luke is with me" ([[2-timothy]] 4:11), and in the greeting of [[philemon]] 24. Traditional author of the largest single-author block in the NT — [[gospel-of-luke]] and [[acts]] together comprise about 27% of NT word count.

Pauline companionship reconstructed primarily from the "we-passages" of [[acts]] (16:10-17; 20:5-21:18; 27:1-28:16), where the narrator shifts to first-person plural — the simplest reading is that the author traveled with [[paul]] from Troas to Philippi, rejoined for the trip to Jerusalem, and accompanied him to Rome. Alternative readings treat the we-passages as a literary convention or a source document incorporated by a later editor.

Authorship of [[gospel-of-luke]] and [[acts]] is traditionally affirmed (Irenaeus, Muratorian Canon) and critically defended by many on the basis of: (a) consistent we-passage authorship, (b) absence of competing tradition, (c) the unflattering portrait of [[paul]] in [[acts]] (no mention of letters, downplayed conflicts) being unlikely for a later forger. Critics counter: [[acts]] disagrees with the Pauline letters on key points (Jerusalem visits, Antioch incident), suggesting the author was not a close Paul companion. Either way Luke-Acts is the work of a single educated Hellenistic author writing c. 80-90 CE.

Distinctive Lukan emphases: the Holy Spirit, prayer, the poor and reversal of fortune (Magnificat, Beatitudes-of-Luke, parable of Lazarus and the rich man), women disciples ([[mary-magdalene]], Joanna, Susanna in 8:1-3), gentile inclusion, parables unique to Luke (Good Samaritan, Prodigal Son). Iconographic symbol: the winged ox/calf (Irenaeus). Tradition: never married, dies at 84 in Boeotia; medieval legend has him painting the first icon of [[mary-mother-of-jesus]] — anachronistic but pious.
