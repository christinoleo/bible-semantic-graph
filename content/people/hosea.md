Hosea son of Beeri is the only 8th-c. BCE classical prophet of clearly northern origin, prophesying during the long reign of Jeroboam II and the chaotic decade leading up to the destruction of [[samaria]] by [[assyria]] in 722 BCE. His name (variant of [[joshua]] / [[jesus]]) and his marriage to Gomer "a wife of whoredom" structure the book's opening chapters (Hos 1-3) as the canon's defining marriage-metaphor for covenant.

Hosea is commanded to marry Gomer and to name three children with prophetically loaded names: Jezreel (recalling [[jehu]]'s bloodshed and announcing its retribution), Lo-Ruhamah ("Not Loved"), Lo-Ammi ("Not My People") — the last directly inverting the [[sinai-covenant]]'s "you shall be my people." The marriage breaks down; in ch. 3 the prophet redeems his wife back from someone, signaling [[yhwh]]'s patient redemption of Israel through a coming disciplinary wilderness. "I will allure her and bring her into the wilderness, and speak tenderly to her" (Hos 2:14) is the iconic image.

The middle and later chapters (4-14) move from biographical metaphor to direct indictment. The charges: priestly corruption, political intriguing with Egypt and Assyria, Baal worship, knowledge-of-God absent ("there is no faithfulness or steadfast love, and no knowledge of God in the land" — Hos 4:1). The poetic register is dense, broken, often grammatically obscure — the Hebrew of Hosea is among the most textually difficult in the prophetic corpus and is widely treated as preserving northern dialect features.

The famous "out of Egypt I called my son" (Hos 11:1) — referring to the Exodus generation — is appropriated in [[gospel-of-matthew]] 2:15 for the holy family's return from Egypt. The Pauline use of "I will call them my people who were not my people" (Hos 1:10, 2:23, in Rom 9:25-26) extends Hosea's reversal to Gentile inclusion. Hosea 6:6 ("I desire mercy, not sacrifice") is cited twice by [[jesus]] in Matthew (9:13, 12:7) as a hermeneutic key.
