Karl Barth (1886-1968) — Swiss Reformed theologian; the dominant Protestant theological voice of the twentieth century. Born in Basel to the theologian Fritz Barth; trained in the German liberal Protestant tradition under Adolf von Harnack and Wilhelm Herrmann; pastor at Safenwil in the Aargau 1911-1921, where wrestling with sermon preparation in the shadow of his teachers' August 1914 manifesto endorsing the Kaiser's war policy precipitated a fundamental break with liberal Protestantism.

The Epistle to the Romans (Der Römerbrief, 1919 first edition, 1922 wholly rewritten second edition) — the manifesto of dialectical theology, landing "like a bombshell on the theologians' playground" (Karl Adam). Recovered the eschatological alterity of God against the liberal immanentist confusion of God with religious experience, culture, or history. "God is in heaven, and you are on earth" (Eccl 5:2) became programmatic. The infinite qualitative distinction (Kierkegaard); the krisis of all human possibility; the no of God to religion that opens the yes of grace.

Career as professor at Göttingen, Münster, Bonn (1921-1935); deportation to Switzerland after refusing the loyalty oath to Hitler in 1935; professor at Basel until retirement 1962. Primary drafter of the Barmen Declaration (May 1934) for the Confessing Church (Bekennende Kirche), the theological response to the Deutsche Christen movement that sought to align Protestant Christianity with Nazi ideology. "Jesus Christ, as he is testified to us in Holy Scripture, is the one Word of God which we have to hear and which we have to trust and obey in life and in death" — first thesis, programmatic christological concentration against any natural-theology bridge for the church's submission to alien lords.

Church Dogmatics (Kirchliche Dogmatik, 1932-1967, over six million words across thirteen part-volumes, left unfinished) — the largest systematic theology of the modern era. I/1 and I/2 on the Word of God; II/1 and II/2 on the doctrine of God (election as the gracious choice of God in Christ — a striking revision of double predestination that has divided Reformed reception); III/1-III/4 on creation, including a substantial doctrine of marriage and humanity; IV/1-IV/4 on reconciliation (Christology and atonement worked out in three concurrent aspects). Eschatology (planned V) never written. Theological method: revelation as God's act in Christ, witnessed in Scripture, attested in proclamation; the analogia fidei rather than analogia entis; Christology as the center from which all loci radiate. Disputes with [[emil-brunner]] (forward ref) on natural theology ("Nein!" 1934), with Bultmann on demythologization, with the Roman Catholic [[erich-przywara]] on analogy of being. Late warmth toward Catholicism after [[vatican-ii]] and theological friendship with [[hans-urs-von-balthasar]] (forward ref).
