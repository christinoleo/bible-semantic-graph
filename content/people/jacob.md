Jacob, son of [[isaac]] and [[rebekah]], twin and rival of [[esau]]. Name puns on `ʿāqēb` ("heel" — grabs Esau's at birth, Gen 25:26) and `ʿāqab` ("to supplant / cheat" — Esau's bitter pun, Gen 27:36). Third patriarch; eponym of Israel through his rename.

Trickster cycle. Buys Esau's birthright for stew (Gen 25:29-34); with Rebekah's orchestration, steals Esau's blessing from a blind Isaac (Gen 27). Flees to Laban in Haran. The Bethel theophany — ladder, ascending and descending angels, divine promise (Gen 28:10-22) — sanctifies the runaway. In Haran he is out-tricked by Laban: tricked into marrying [[leah]] before [[rachel]], works fourteen years, then breeds Laban's flock by selective rod-stripping (Gen 30) to amass wealth.

Returns. Wrestles a `ʾîš` ("man" — angel? God? his own conscience?) at the Jabbok ford all night, refuses to release without blessing, is renamed Israel — `kî-śārîtā ʿim-ʾĕlōhîm wĕʿim-ʾănāšîm wattûkāl`, "for you have striven with God and with men and prevailed" (Gen 32:28). Walks away limping. Meets Esau the next day in a reconciliation that the narrative leaves ambiguous (Gen 33).

Fathers twelve sons by [[leah]], [[rachel]], and their maids Bilhah and Zilpah, plus [[dinah]] — the eponymous ancestors of the tribes of Israel. Loses Rachel in childbirth at Benjamin's birth; favors [[joseph]] and is deceived in turn by his sons in the Joseph cycle (Gen 37). Migrates to [[egypt]] in old age (Gen 46); blesses the twelve in the deathbed oracle of Gen 49 — a major poetic text and a key source for tribal geography. Buried at Machpelah.

Reception. Hosea 12 reframes the trickster as ambiguous-or-condemned. [[paul]] in [[romans]] 9:13 quotes Mal 1:2-3 ("Jacob I loved, Esau I hated") as Exhibit B (with [[rebekah]]) for unconditional election. The Jacob/Esau pair, like [[cain]]/[[abel]] and [[isaac]]/[[ishmael]], works the canon's "younger displaces elder" theme — but Jacob is the most morally complicated of the patriarchs, and the text refuses to clean him up.
