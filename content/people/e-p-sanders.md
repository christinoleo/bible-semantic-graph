Sanders, E. P. (Ed Parish; 1937-2022) — American New Testament scholar; Arts and Sciences Professor of Religion at Duke from 1990; one of the most consequential figures of late-twentieth-century biblical studies on two fronts at once. *Paul and Palestinian Judaism* (1977) dismantled the Lutheran caricature of Second-Temple Judaism as a legalistic works-righteousness religion against which Paul reacted, introducing the category "covenantal nomism" — Israel is *in* the covenant by grace, the law specifies how to *stay* in — and launching what [[james-dunn]] would later name the [[new-perspective-on-paul]].

*Jesus and Judaism* (1985) extended the program to the historical-Jesus question: [[jesus]] read as a Jewish restoration-eschatology prophet, the Temple action read as a symbolic prophecy of Temple destruction and replacement, and the criteriological apparatus of dissimilarity quietly displaced by the criterion of plausibility-within-Judaism. The double move — Paul made Jewish again, [[jesus]] made Jewish again — set the methodological climate of the Third Quest and shaped successors including [[james-dunn]] and [[n-t-wright]]. Mainstream [[historical-critical-scholarship]] from the 1980s onward is largely a series of conversations with or against Sanders.
