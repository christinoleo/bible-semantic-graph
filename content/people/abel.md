Abel (Heb. `Hevel`, "breath / vapor" — the same word that runs through [[ecclesiastes]] as `hebel hăbālîm`, "vanity of vanities"). Second son of [[adam]] and [[eve]]; shepherd. The name foreshadows his fate: a life as brief as a breath.

Offers firstlings of his flock; YHWH "had regard" for his offering but not [[cain]]'s (Gen 4:4-5). The text supplies no causal mechanism — Heb 11:4 retrofits "by faith," patristic readers infer interior disposition, source-critics note a pastoralist/agrarian polemic embedded in the J narrative.

Murdered by Cain in the field; first death in the Bible, and a murder before a natural death — death enters the world as fratricide. The blood "cries out from the ground" (Gen 4:10) — a Hebrew idiom for unavenged blood demanding justice that recurs throughout the OT and into Revelation 6:9-10.

Reception is dense relative to his ~10 verses. He is the prototypical righteous victim: [[jesus]] frames the arc of murdered prophets "from the blood of Abel to the blood of Zechariah" (Matt 23:35; Luke 11:51). Heb 11:4 makes him the first exemplar of faith; Heb 12:24 contrasts his blood (crying for vengeance) with that of Jesus (crying for mercy) — a typological inversion. Augustine pairs Abel/Cain as the City of God / City of Man. Islam preserves the narrative (Q 5:27-31) with Abel as Hābīl.
