William Lane Craig (b. 1949) — American analytic philosopher of religion and Christian apologist; the most public face of evidentialist [[christian-apologetic]] in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries. Doctorates under [[john-hick]] (Birmingham, philosophy) and Wolfhart Pannenberg (Munich, theology); long affiliations with Talbot School of Theology and Houston Christian University.

Revived the medieval [[kalam-cosmological-argument]] within contemporary philosophy of religion, defending a finite past on philosophical (impossibility of an actual infinite) and scientific (Big Bang cosmology) grounds; couples it with a moral argument, a fine-tuning argument, and a defense of the historicity of the [[resurrection-of-jesus]] modeled on minimal-facts evidentialism. Extensive public debate record against [[bart-ehrman]], Sam Harris, Christopher Hitchens, [[lawrence-krauss]], Antony Flew, [[richard-dawkins]] (declined). His Reasonable Faith ministry has shaped a generation of popular evangelical apologetic; his philosophical work on divine eternity, middle knowledge, and the metaphysics of time positions him as a Molinist within the broader theistic-philosophy revival associated with [[alvin-plantinga]].
