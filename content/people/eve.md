Eve (Heb. `Ḥawwāh`) is the first woman in the J creation account (Gen 2:4b-3:24). Named only after the expulsion (Gen 3:20): "the mother of all living" (`ʾēm kol-ḥāy`) — folk etymology linking `ḥawwāh` to `ḥāyâ` ("to live"). Formed from `hāʾādām`'s `ṣēlāʿ` (traditionally "rib," more accurately "side" — the same word names the side-chambers of Solomon's temple).

She is the conversational partner of the serpent (Gen 3), the one who first reasons, eats, and gives. The narrative gives her speech and agency; [[adam]] is passive ("with her," Gen 3:6). Whether the text codes this as her fault, his fault, or shared is a perennial interpretive split — Jewish tradition tends to share blame, Christian tradition (esp. after Tertullian, "you are the devil's gateway") tends to load it on her.

Mother of [[cain]], [[abel]], and [[seth]]; Gen 4-5 names no daughters but assumes them. The "seed of the woman" (Gen 3:15, the `protoevangelium`) is read messianically in patristic and Catholic tradition, more modestly as etiological serpent-enmity in critical scholarship.

Reception splits sharply. 1 Tim 2:13-14 uses Eve's deception to ground a prohibition on women teaching — heavily contested in modern exegesis (see complementarian/egalitarian debates). 2 Cor 11:3 deploys her as a figure for credulous deception. Gnostic and feminist re-readings (e.g., Phyllis Trible) recover Eve as a figure of curiosity, wisdom-seeking, and moral first-mover.
