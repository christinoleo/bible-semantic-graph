British Muslim public speaker, debater, and co-founder of the [[sapience-institute]] (London) — a leading figure in the YouTube-era generation of [[islamic-apologetic]] inheriting the platform-debate format from [[ahmed-deedat]] and [[zakir-naik]]. Best known for confrontational street-corner exchanges and long-form debates with Christian apologists, atheists, and ex-Muslims, in which he characteristically presses interlocutors on precise formulations of the [[trinity]], [[incarnation]], and the divinity of [[jesus]] — looking for openings into [[partialism]] or other trinitarian incoherences.

His debating style — fast-paced, taxonomically aggressive, optimized for clip-length virality — is the foil against which Christian counter-apologists (e.g., [[claim-truly-god-not-fully-god-avoids-partialism-trap|the "truly God, not fully God" recommendation]]) increasingly train their phrasing.
