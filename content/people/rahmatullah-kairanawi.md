Rahmatullah Kairanawi (1818-1891) — Indian Hanafi scholar; the figure who, with the physician Muḥammad Wazīr Khān, produced the formative nineteenth-century Muslim apologetic against Protestant missionary Christianity in colonial South Asia. Born at Kairana in present-day Uttar Pradesh; trained at Delhi in Hanafi fiqh, kalām, and the rational sciences. Drawn into public controversy by the Anglican missionary [[karl-gottlieb-pfander]], whose Persian-language tract *Mīzān al-Ḥaqq* circulated widely in North India and required a serious response.

The Agra Debates of April 1854 — held over two days at the residence of the Anglican bishop, attended by hundreds of observers — pitted Kairanawi and Wazīr Khān against Pfander and Thomas Valpy French. Kairanawi's tactic was to deploy contemporary European biblical scholarship (Eichhorn, Horne, Adam Clarke) against the missionary appeal to scriptural authority — turning the tools of [[historical-critical-scholarship]] back on its missionary employers. Forced into exile after the 1857 Indian Rebellion (in which he participated), he relocated to [[mecca]] under Ottoman protection. There, at the request of the Ottoman sultan, he composed *Iẓhār al-Ḥaqq* (Demonstration of the Truth, 1864) — a four-volume Arabic refutation that became the most widely circulated nineteenth-century Muslim work on Christianity, translated into Urdu, Turkish, English, French. Foundational text for modern [[islamic-apologetic]]; arguments and footnotes pass directly through Rashīd Riḍā to [[ahmed-deedat]] and the twentieth-century debaters.
