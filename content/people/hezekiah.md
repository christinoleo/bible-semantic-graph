Hezekiah is the thirteenth king of Judah (traditional dating c. 715-686 BCE, with substantial chronological debate), one of two kings the Deuteronomistic historian rates without qualification ("he trusted in YHWH the God of Israel; so that after him was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor among those who were before him" — 2 Kings 18:5; the other being [[josiah]]). His reign is presented across [[2-kings]], [[2-chronicles]], and [[book-of-isaiah]] in parallel — the same material appears as Isa 36-39 with mostly minor variants.

His reform program (2 Kings 18:4) removes high places, breaks down sacred pillars, cuts the Asherah, and destroys the bronze serpent of [[moses]] that had become an object of incense offerings ("Nehushtan"). This is the first canonical move toward cult centralization at [[jerusalem]] — the program [[josiah]] will later radicalize. Source-critical scholarship has long debated whether the Hezekian and Josianic reforms are two stages or whether the Hezekian reform is partly retrojected from the later one.

The defining crisis is the [[assyria]]n invasion under Sennacherib in 701 BCE. The biblical account credits miraculous deliverance — 185,000 Assyrian soldiers struck down overnight by [[yhwh]]'s angel (2 Kings 19:35). The Assyrian record (Sennacherib's prism inscriptions) corroborates the siege of Jerusalem but claims Hezekiah was "shut up like a bird in a cage" and paid heavy tribute — a tension scholars resolve variously (plague hypothesis, Egyptian intervention, narrative theology). The [[siloam-tunnel]] inscription, found in Hezekiah's water tunnel under the city, is among the longest pre-exilic Hebrew inscriptions in situ.

He is contemporary of [[isaiah]] (8th c.), and the [[book-of-isaiah]] frames much of First Isaiah around his court. The illness-and-recovery narrative (2 Kings 20 / Isa 38) extends his life fifteen years, including the sign of the sun's shadow moving backward on the dial of Ahaz. He appears in the [[gospel-of-matthew]] genealogy of [[jesus]] (Matt 1:9-10) as link in the Davidic line.
