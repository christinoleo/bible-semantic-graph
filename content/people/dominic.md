Dominic de Guzmán (1170-1221) — Castilian; founder of the Order of Preachers (Ordo Praedicatorum, OP — Dominicans); contemporary of [[francis-of-assisi]] and the architect of an alternative mendicant model centered on doctrinal preaching, scholastic training, and the apostolic refutation of heresy. Born at Caleruega; nephew and protégé of the prior of Osma; canon regular and subprior of Osma cathedral chapter under bishop Diego d'Acebes from the late 1190s.

The founding moment was a 1203 diplomatic journey through Languedoc with bishop Diego on a mission for King Alfonso VIII of Castile. They encountered the Cathar (Albigensian) heresy and the apparent ineffectiveness of Cistercian preaching teams traveling in expensive cavalcades to confront preachers committed to apostolic poverty. Diego and Dominic concluded that effective response required matching the heretics' austerity — barefoot itinerancy, manifest poverty, intellectual preparation in scripture and theology.

Operated as itinerant preacher in Languedoc (1206-1215), most intensively in Toulouse and Prouille, where he founded a community of converted Cathar women (1206 — by chronology, the first Dominican house, though the male order's formal foundation comes later). Did not lead the Albigensian Crusade militarily — the violent prosecution belongs to Simon de Montfort, the papal legates, and after 1209 the crown of France — though the order's later involvement in the medieval inquisition is partly his legacy. Approval of the order by Innocent III informally (1215) and Honorius III formally (1216, under the existing Rule of [[augustine-of-hippo]] with custom constitutions on top).

Constitutional innovations: representative chapter government with elected priors, mobility, dispensation from choir obligation for study, prioritized university foundations at Paris and Bologna. The Dominicans rapidly produced the scholastic generation of Hugh of Saint-Cher, Albert the Great, [[thomas-aquinas]] himself. The rosary — traditionally attributed to a Marian apparition to Dominic — is a fifteenth-century devotion retrojected onto him. Canonized 1234 by Gregory IX.
