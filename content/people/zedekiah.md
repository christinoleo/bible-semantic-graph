Zedekiah is the twenty-first and last king of Judah (traditional dating c. 597-586 BCE), installed as Babylonian puppet by Nebuchadnezzar after the deportation of Jehoiachin in the first wave of exile. His birth name is Mattaniah; Nebuchadnezzar renames him at installation, a standard ANE assertion of suzerainty. He is a son of [[josiah]] and uncle of the king he replaces.

His reign is presented across [[2-kings]], [[2-chronicles]], and especially [[book-of-jeremiah]] (where it dominates the prose narrative material) as a study in indecision. [[jeremiah]] repeatedly counsels submission to Babylon as [[yhwh]]'s decreed instrument; pro-Egyptian court factions counsel rebellion. Zedekiah oscillates, consults Jeremiah secretly, declines to act on the counsel, and finally allies with Egypt — triggering Nebuchadnezzar's second campaign and the eighteen-month siege of [[jerusalem]] (2 Kings 25, Jer 39, 52).

The end is wrenchingly specific. The city falls; the wall is breached; Zedekiah flees by night toward the Jordan valley, is captured on the plains of Jericho, and is brought to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah. There his sons are slaughtered before his eyes; then his eyes are put out; then he is taken in chains to Babylon (2 Kings 25:6-7, Jer 52:9-11). The last image of David's line is a blinded king in chains — narrative inversion of the Davidic covenant of [[2-samuel]] 7.

The Temple is burned, the city walls thrown down, the elite deported. The Deuteronomistic History closes; the so-called "exilic redaction" must explain how the eternal Davidic line ended in catastrophe, and the canonical answer (Manasseh's sin; the cumulative violation of covenant) is consolidated. Jeremiah's intimate access to Zedekiah preserves an unusually detailed portrait of a king in collapse, including private conversations otherwise unique in the Hebrew Bible.
