Shem, eldest son of [[noah]] (Gen 5:32; though birth-order between Shem, [[ham]], [[japheth]] is unstable across listings). Name simply means "name" — possibly hinting at "renown / reputation," fitting his role as eponym of the Semitic peoples.

In the vineyard episode (Gen 9:20-27), Shem and [[japheth]] walk in backwards to cover Noah's nakedness, refusing to look — and receive Noah's blessing: "Blessed be YHWH, God of Shem" (Gen 9:26) — uniquely identifying YHWH with Shem's line, which is theologically loaded: Israel's God is Shem's God.

Genealogical bridge. The Table of Nations (Gen 10:21-31) routes the Semitic peoples — Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud, Aram — through Shem; the toledot of Gen 11:10-26 narrows that to Arphaxad → ... → Terah → [[abraham]], establishing the patriarchal line as Shemitic. [[gospel-of-luke]] 3:36 extends the line to [[jesus]]. Lives 600 years.

Reception is light — Shem rarely appears outside genealogies. "Semite / Semitic" as a linguistic and ethnic category derives from his name (coined by 18th-c. German philologists). Rabbinic and patristic traditions occasionally identify him with Melchizedek of Salem (Gen 14), exploiting chronological overlap, though the canon does not.
