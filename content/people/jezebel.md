Jezebel is the Phoenician princess daughter of Ethbaal of Sidon, given in dynastic marriage to [[ahab]] of Israel (1 Kings 16:31). She becomes the paradigmatic biblical antagonist — the foreign queen who sponsors Baal and Asherah cults in the northern capital at [[samaria]], maintains hundreds of prophets at royal expense, and systematically persecutes the prophets of [[yhwh]].

She is the prime mover in two of the dynasty's defining episodes. After the Mt. Carmel contest, when [[elijah]] slaughters the prophets of Baal at the Kishon, her death-threat sends him fleeing to Horeb in suicidal despair (1 Kings 19:1-4). In the Naboth's vineyard episode (1 Kings 21), with Ahab pouting in bed over a refused land sale, she takes initiative — writes letters in Ahab's name, suborns false witnesses to charge Naboth with blasphemy, has him stoned, and delivers the vineyard to her husband. The episode crystallizes her role as a will-to-power figure transgressing both covenant law and royal restraint.

[[elijah]] pronounces a specific curse: dogs will eat her by the wall of Jezreel (1 Kings 21:23). [[jehu]] fulfills it (2 Kings 9). Painted, hair dressed, looking out from a high window, she addresses him with the loaded greeting "Is it peace, you Zimri, murderer of your master?" — recalling the previous coup. He has her eunuchs throw her down. Her blood spatters the wall. Dogs eat the body before burial; only skull, feet, and palms remain.

The figure becomes archetypal. [[revelation]] 2:20 names a false prophetess in Thyatira "Jezebel," collapsing the woman into a symbolic type for seductive idolatry. Reception across Christian and Jewish tradition has been almost uniformly negative, sometimes reading her as a study of foreign-queen agency under patriarchal regimes — though feminist re-readings have noted that her crimes (judicial murder, idolatry) are also charged to kings like [[manasseh]] without the same vitriol.
