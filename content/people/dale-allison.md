Allison, Dale C., Jr. (born 1955) — American New Testament scholar; Richard J. Dearborn Professor of New Testament at Princeton Theological Seminary from 2013. Trained at Duke under W. D. Davies, with whom he co-authored the three-volume ICC commentary on Matthew. Methodologically the most cautious of the contemporary historical-Jesus scholars: skeptical of the criteria of authenticity as ordinarily deployed, skeptical of the historian's ability to reconstruct individual sayings, but constructive about what large-scale patterns across the tradition can be trusted to indicate about [[jesus]] in the aggregate.

*Jesus of Nazareth: Millenarian Prophet* (1998) and *Constructing Jesus* (2010) develop the apocalyptic-prophet portrait — [[jesus]] as a Jewish eschatological figure announcing imminent divine judgment and restoration — grounded not in any single saying but in the convergent shape of the synoptic tradition read through the lens of memory studies. The argumentative move is to displace the criteria-of-authenticity project with a probabilistic, gist-level reconstruction: individual pericopes may be unreliable, but the recurring themes and types across many independent traditions track real memory of the man. *The Historical Christ and the Theological Jesus* (2009) treats the methodological humility itself as a theological resource. Receives broad consensus-building respect across [[historical-critical-scholarship]] precisely because the conclusions are modest and the procedure is transparent.
