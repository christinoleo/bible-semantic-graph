Jonah son of Amittai is the prophet of the [[book-of-jonah]], a 48-verse narrative quite unlike anything else in the prophetic corpus. The only oracular sentence Jonah delivers in the whole book is eight words in Hebrew ("yet forty days and Nineveh shall be overthrown" — Jonah 3:4); the rest is narrative, structured as a four-act drama of the prophet running from his commission, swallowed by a great fish, vomited up, completing the commission reluctantly, and then sulking when [[yhwh]] forgives the foreign capital.

The historical Jonah is named at [[2-kings]] 14:25 as a prophet of Gath-hepher in Galilee whose oracle of expansion was fulfilled under Jeroboam II — placing him in the 8th c. BCE. The book bearing his name is widely dated by critical scholarship much later (post-exilic, often 5th-4th c. BCE) on the basis of Aramaicisms in the Hebrew, the late form of the Hebrew vocabulary, the casual use of Nineveh as the archetypal great foreign city (long after its actual destruction in 612 BCE), and the book's apparent dialogue with [[ezra]]-[[nehemiah]]'s exclusivism.

The genre is widely disputed. Options include historical narrative, parable, satire, midrash, prophetic legend, or didactic short story. The "satire" reading — taking the book as black comedy directed at narrow prophetic-nationalist sensibility — captures more of its texture than the historical reading does: a prophet who flees the direction commanded, sailors who pray more fervently than the prophet, a city that repents on the first sermon ever delivered there (including cattle in sackcloth), and a prophet sulking that his enemies were not destroyed.

The book's deep argument is the scope of divine compassion. "Should I not pity Nineveh, that great city, in which there are more than 120,000 persons who do not know their right hand from their left, and also much cattle?" (Jonah 4:11) closes the book on an unanswered question. [[jesus]]'s "sign of Jonah" — three days and three nights in the fish corresponding to three days in the tomb (Matt 12:40) — and the comparison "the people of Nineveh will rise up at the judgment with this generation and condemn it" (Matt 12:41) make Jonah one of the most NT-mobilized minor prophets.
