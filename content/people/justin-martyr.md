Justin Martyr (~100-165) — Greek-speaking Samaritan born at Flavia Neapolis (modern Nablus), trained in Stoic, Peripatetic, Pythagorean, and Platonic schools before conversion to Christianity through an encounter with an unnamed old man by the sea (per his own narrative in the Dialogue). Wore the philosopher's cloak after baptism; treats Christianity as the true philosophy, fulfilling and surpassing Greek thought.

Three extant works: the First Apology (addressed to Antoninus Pius, ~155) defends Christians against charges of atheism and immorality and offers the earliest detailed description of [[eucharist]] and baptismal liturgy ([[first-apology]] 65-67); the Second Apology, briefer, responds to specific Roman judicial executions; the Dialogue with Trypho (~160) — longest, debates a Jew named Trypho on the messianic status of [[jesus]], the typological reading of the Hebrew Bible, and the church as true Israel.

Articulates the doctrine of the Logos spermatikos — seeds of the divine Word scattered among Greek philosophers (Socrates, Heraclitus) so that pre-Christian pagans participated partially in Christ. The Logos Christology distinguishes Father and Son numerically while affirming the Son's divinity; precursor to Nicene formulation but with subordinationist edges later sharpened against by [[athanasius]] and the Cappadocians ([[basil-the-great]], [[gregory-of-nazianzus]], [[gregory-of-nyssa]]).

Denounced under Junius Rusticus, urban prefect of Rome under Marcus Aurelius; beheaded c. 165 with six companions (Acts of Justin survive in three recensions). His school in Rome trained Tatian, who later turned encratite. Cited extensively by [[irenaeus]], [[tertullian]], Eusebius — foundational reference for second-century proto-orthodox apologetics.
