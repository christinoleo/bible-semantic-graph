Seleucid king (r. 175–164 BCE) whose attempt to forcibly hellenize Judea — outlawing Torah observance and circumcision, profaning the [[temple|Temple]] by erecting an altar to Olympian Zeus over the altar of burnt offering (the "abomination of desolation," [[book-of-daniel|Dan]] 11:31; cf. 1 Macc 1:54), and inflicting the *gezerot* — provoked the Maccabean Revolt and the founding of the Hasmonean dynasty. The "little horn" of [[book-of-daniel|Daniel]] 8 and the king "doing as he wills" of Dan 11:36 are identified with him by historical-critical scholarship. His career also functions as a typological prefigure of the eschatological [[antichrist]] in [[futurism|futurist]] readings of Daniel 11:36-45.
