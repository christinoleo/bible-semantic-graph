Al-Maturidi (c. 853-944) — Transoxanian Ḥanafī theologian; eponymous founder of the [[maturidiyya]], the second of the two major schools of Sunni [[classical-kalam]] alongside the [[ashariyya]], dominant historically across Central Asia, the Indian subcontinent, the Ottoman lands, and most of the contemporary Ḥanafī world. Almost no biographical detail survives beyond his teaching at Māturīd, a quarter of [[samarkand]], and his discipleship in the Ḥanafī tradition descending from Abū Ḥanīfa through several generations of Samarqandī masters; reputation rests entirely on his written corpus and on the school that consolidated around him in the following centuries.

*Kitāb al-Tawḥīd* (The Book of Divine Oneness) — the earliest extant systematic Sunni kalam treatise, arguing for God's existence from contingency, defending the createdness of human acts within a framework that nonetheless preserves genuine human agency (the doctrine of *kasb* given a more robust formulation than the Ashʿarī version), and treating reason as competent — independently of revelation — to know God's existence and the broad moral law. The school parallels [[al-ashari]] in its anti-Muʿtazilī and anti-philosophical thrust but differs on key questions: the ontological status of human acts, the rational accessibility of moral knowledge, and the interpretation of the divine attributes. The two schools are typically treated as the joint orthodox kalam tradition of Sunni Islam, with Māturīdism historically associated with Ḥanafī jurisprudence and Ashʿarism with [[shafii]] and [[maliki]] jurisprudence.
