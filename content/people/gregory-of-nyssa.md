Gregory of Nyssa (~335-395) — Cappadocian Father, younger brother of [[basil-the-great]] and Macrina the Younger; the most speculatively daring of the three Cappadocians. Made bishop of the small see of Nyssa by Basil around 372; briefly deposed by Arian intrigue under Valens; restored 378, then increasingly influential at the imperial court of Theodosius after Basil's death.

Attended [[constantinople-i]] (381) and was named in Theodosius's edict of July 381 as one of the bishops communion with whom defined Catholic orthodoxy in the eastern provinces — a striking imperial endorsement. Played a leading theological role at the council and successor synods in Constantinople through the 380s.

Against Eunomius — completes the Cappadocian project of refuting Anomoean Arianism by distinguishing ousia from energeiai and asserting the incomprehensibility of the divine essence. On the Making of Man (a sequel to Basil's Hexaemeron) and On the Soul and the Resurrection (a Platonic dialogue between Gregory and the dying Macrina, modeled on the Phaedo) develop a Christian anthropology of double creation (the image in 1:26-27, the gendered creation in 1:27c) and a doctrine of bodily resurrection more philosophically intricate than any patristic counterpart.

Life of Moses — late mystical reading of Exodus and Numbers organized into historia (literal) and theōria (spiritual). Gregory's signature doctrine of epektasis: the soul's never-completed advance into the infinite God, drawn forever further by participating in what cannot be comprehended. The dark luminous cloud of Sinai (Exod 24, 19) as image of mystical knowing — direct influence on Pseudo-Dionysius, [[bernard-of-clairvaux]], and the entire apophatic tradition. Held a form of apokatastasis (universal restoration) — eventually condemned in [[origen]]'s name at [[constantinople-ii]], but his own reputation escaped formal censure.
