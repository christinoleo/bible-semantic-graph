Silas — leading member of the Jerusalem church ([[acts]] 15:22), sent with [[paul]] and [[barnabas]] to Antioch carrying the apostolic letter from the Jerusalem Council. Designated a prophet ([[acts]] 15:32). When [[paul]] and [[barnabas]] split over [[mark]], [[paul]] chooses Silas as companion for the second missionary journey ([[acts]] 15:40).

Travels with [[paul]] through Asia Minor, Macedonia, and Achaea. Imprisoned with [[paul]] at Philippi after exorcising a slave-girl's spirit ([[acts]] 16:16-40); their midnight hymn-singing and the earthquake jail-break is a Lukan set piece, climaxing in the jailer's conversion. [[paul]]'s Roman citizenship — and Silas' implied citizenship — bring an apology from the magistrates. Stays in Beroea while [[paul]] is hurried to Athens; rejoins him in Corinth ([[acts]] 18:5).

Co-sender of [[1-thessalonians]] and [[2-thessalonians]] (under the Hellenized form Silvanus); named in [[2-corinthians]] 1:19 as preaching with [[paul]] and [[timothy]] in Corinth. The "Silvanus" of [[1-peter]] 5:12 ("by Silvanus I have written to you briefly") is plausibly the same figure, perhaps the actual amanuensis of [[1-peter]] — which would help explain the polished Greek of a letter attributed to a Galilean fisherman.
