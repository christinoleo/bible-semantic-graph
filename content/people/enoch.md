Enoch, seventh from [[adam]] in the Sethite line of Gen 5, is the antediluvian patriarch whose biblical notice (Gen 5:21-24) is conspicuously short and conspicuously strange: he "walked with God, and he was not, for God took him" — three hundred sixty-five years of life (a solar number), no death notice, the unique formula וְאֵינֶנּוּ. The gap generated a vast Second-Temple apocalyptic literature around him.

Father of [[methuselah]], grandfather of Lamech, great-grandfather of [[noah]] — the genealogical positioning makes him the antediluvian sage par excellence, instructor in the calendar, the secrets of the heavens, and the meaning of the flood to come. [[1-enoch]] (Ge'ez Mäṣḥäfä Henok, fragments at [[qumran]] in Aramaic) is the principal pseudepigraphon attributed to him — a composite of five booklets (Watchers, Parables/Similitudes, Astronomical Book, Dream Visions, Epistle) covering the third century BCE through the first century CE. [[2-enoch]] (Slavonic Enoch — likely first c. CE, Greek original) recounts his heavenly ascent through seven heavens. [[3-enoch]] (Hekhalot, rabbinic-mystical) transforms him into the angel Metatron.

[[jude]] 14-15 cites [[1-enoch]] 1:9 directly and by name — "Enoch, the seventh from Adam, prophesied" — the unambiguous New Testament citation of a non-canonical text and a recurrent embarrassment to closed-canon theories. [[hebrews]] 11:5 invokes him as exemplar of faith translated without death. Canonical in [[orthodox-ethiopian]] tradition (where [[1-enoch]] is scripture); rabbinic Judaism downplays him after the Tannaitic period, and Christian traditions retain him only as figure, not author. Comparison with the Mesopotamian Enmeduranki — seventh antediluvian king, recipient of celestial revelation — is the standard Assyriological parallel.
