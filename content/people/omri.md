Omri is the sixth king of the northern kingdom (traditional dating c. 885-874 BCE), founder of the Omride dynasty and father of [[ahab]]. The biblical notice is famously brief — fourteen verses in 1 Kings 16, mostly negative, with the Deuteronomistic refrain that he "did evil in the sight of the LORD, and did more evil than all who were before him."

The extra-biblical record reverses the proportions. Assyrian royal inscriptions for a century after his death refer to the northern kingdom as "the house of Omri" (*Bit-Humri*) — long after the dynasty's extinction. The [[mesha-stele]] (c. 840 BCE) records Moabite subjugation under "Omri king of Israel," who "humbled Moab many days." Archaeological work at [[samaria]], the new capital he founded by purchasing the hill from Shemer, shows substantial Iron Age IIA construction phases attributed to his reign and Ahab's. Other sites — Hazor, Megiddo, Jezreel — show comparable monumental construction in the same horizon.

The disparity between biblical neglect and extra-biblical importance is one of the textbook cases for the Deuteronomistic History's selective historiography. The narrator is interested in cultic deviation, not state-building; from that lens, Omri's establishment of [[samaria]] as a long-term political center matters less than the religious policy his son and daughter-in-law import.

Modern reconstructions sometimes credit the Omrides with the consolidation of a coherent northern Israelite state — military, economic, diplomatic — surpassing even the Davidic-Solomonic polity (whose extent is itself contested by minimalist historians). The dynasty ends with [[jehu]]'s coup in 841 BCE, ending Omri's line in two generations.
