James son of Zebedee — Galilean fisherman, brother of [[john-apostle]]. Called with his brother while mending nets ([[gospel-of-mark]] 1:19-20). Nicknamed by [[jesus]] Boanergēs, "sons of thunder" ([[gospel-of-mark]] 3:17) — possibly for the fire-from-heaven request against Samaritans ([[gospel-of-luke]] 9:54). Inner-circle witness at the [[transfiguration]] and Gethsemane along with [[peter]] and [[john-apostle]].

Together with [[john-apostle]] (or with their mother, in [[gospel-of-matthew]] 20) requests seats of honor in the kingdom — provoking the rebuke "the cup that I drink you will drink." That cup is drunk first: he is the first apostle martyred, beheaded by Herod Agrippa I c. 44 CE ([[acts]] 12:1-2), the only Twelve-apostle martyrdom narrated in the NT.

Later Spanish tradition (Santiago de Compostela) translates his relics to Galicia, generating one of medieval Christendom's principal pilgrimage routes (the Camino) — historically dubious but ecclesially decisive. Distinguished from [[james-brother-of-jesus]] (the Just, leader of Jerusalem church) and James son of Alphaeus (the Less); the three Jameses are routinely confused in tradition.
