Judas Iscariot — one of the Twelve, treasurer of the group ([[gospel-of-john]] 12:6 adds: and a thief). Betrays [[jesus]] to the chief priests for thirty pieces of silver ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 26:15, echoing [[book-of-zechariah]] 11:12-13 — the price of a slave). Identifies [[jesus]] in Gethsemane with a kiss ([[gospel-of-mark]] 14:43-46). The byname Iscariot is variously read as "man of Kerioth" (a Judean town, making him the only non-Galilean apostle), as related to sicarius ("dagger-man," a zealot epithet), or as Aramaic for "the false one."

The four gospels escalate his role progressively: in [[gospel-of-mark]] he is barely characterized; in [[gospel-of-matthew]] he is greedy; in [[gospel-of-luke]] and [[gospel-of-john]] Satan enters him ([[gospel-of-luke]] 22:3; [[gospel-of-john]] 13:27). The Johannine portrait is bleakest: he is "a devil" from the start ([[gospel-of-john]] 6:70-71), steals from the common purse, and resents the costly perfume of [[mary-of-bethany]].

Two irreconcilable death accounts: [[gospel-of-matthew]] 27:3-10 has him return the silver in remorse, fling it into the temple, and hang himself; [[acts]] 1:18-19 has him buy a field with the betrayal money, fall headlong, and burst open. Both narrate an Akeldama ("field of blood") aetiology by different routes. Harmonizations (hanged, rope broke, body fell) are forced; critical reading treats them as independent traditions.

Hermeneutic problem: the betrayal is repeatedly described as fulfillment of scripture and "as it is written" — yet "woe to that man by whom the Son of Man is betrayed" ([[gospel-of-mark]] 14:21). The tension between divine foreordination and Judas' culpability anchors theological debate from Origen through Augustine, Aquinas, and Calvin. Modern readings (Schweitzer, Klassen) attempt rehabilitation: Judas as misunderstood political accelerationist trying to force [[jesus]] to act. The Gospel of Judas (Coptic, 2nd c., Nag Hammadi-adjacent) reframes him as [[jesus]]' favored disciple acting on instructions — a gnostic reversal, non-canonical.
