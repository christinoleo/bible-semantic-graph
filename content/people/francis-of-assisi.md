Francis of Assisi (~1181-1226) — founder of the Order of Friars Minor (Franciscans); one of two figures (with [[dominic]]) who reshaped Latin religious life in the thirteenth century around evangelical poverty and itinerant preaching. Born Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone to a wealthy cloth merchant in the Umbrian commune of Assisi; nicknamed Francesco ("the little Frenchman"). Early life of military escapade and chivalric ambition, interrupted by imprisonment in war between Assisi and Perugia and a prolonged convalescent illness.

Conversion sequence (1205-1208): an encounter with a leper that he kissed and embraced; the voice from the crucifix at San Damiano ("Francis, rebuild my church"); the public renunciation of his patrimony before his father and Bishop Guido in the Assisi piazza, returning even his clothes. Lived as a hermit-penitent, then as itinerant preacher; the first companions gathered around him led to a request for papal approval of a basic rule, granted orally by Innocent III in 1209-1210.

Founded a parallel female community with [[clare-of-assisi]] (forward ref) at San Damiano in 1212 (Order of Poor Ladies, later Poor Clares). Visited Sultan al-Kamil during the Fifth Crusade at Damietta (1219) in a famous and historically attested mission — neither converted the other; the meeting became a touchstone of later interfaith reflection. Rejected priestly ordination; remained a deacon by humility. The pivotal internal conflict — whether and how the increasingly numerous order could maintain absolute poverty as it acquired buildings, libraries, and universities — already shadowed his last years and split the order after his death (Spirituals vs Conventuals, eventually Observants and Capuchins).

Received the stigmata on Mount La Verna in September 1224 — the first historically claimed instance and the foundational case for the later phenomenon. Composed the Canticle of the Sun (Cantico delle creature, 1225) in his native Umbrian — among the earliest Italian vernacular poems, praising God through creation as kin ("Brother Sun, Sister Moon"). Died 3 October 1226; canonized by Gregory IX two years later, exceptionally quickly. Patron of ecology since John Paul II's 1979 designation.
