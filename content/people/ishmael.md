Ishmael, firstborn of [[abraham]] (Gen 16). Name announced by the `malʾak yhwh` to [[hagar]]: `yišmaʿ ʾēl`, "God hears" — a name marking divine attention to maternal suffering, not paternal lineage.

Circumcised at thirteen along with Abraham's whole household (Gen 17:23-27) — explicitly included in the covenantal sign even as the line of promise is rerouted to the not-yet-born [[isaac]]. Gen 17:20 records God's separate Ishmaelite blessing: twelve princes, a great nation. The text holds two things in tension: Ishmael is covenantally marked AND not the covenantal heir.

The mocking / playing scene (Gen 21:9, the verb `mĕṣaḥēq` puns on Yiṣḥāq/[[isaac]]) precipitates his and Hagar's expulsion to the wilderness of Beersheba and then Paran. Survives via divine provision; becomes an archer; marries an Egyptian. Returns to bury Abraham alongside Isaac (Gen 25:9), one of the canon's quiet reconciliations. Dies 137 years old (Gen 25:17). His twelve sons (Gen 25:13-16) become the Ishmaelite tribes — biblically associated with the northern Arabian peninsula.

Reception. [[paul]]'s [[galatians]] 4 allegory casts Ishmael as the son "born according to the flesh," persecuting the "son born according to the Spirit" — heavily contested. Islamic tradition makes Ismāʿīl the elder, the patriarch of the Arabs, and (notably) the son taken to be sacrificed at Mecca rather than [[isaac]] at [[moriah]] — a fundamental fork from the Genesis account that maps to the Quranic narrative (Q 37:99-113). Rabbinic readings are mixed: midrash both demonizes and rehabilitates him. The "covenantal ambiguity" of Ishmael — promised, blessed, circumcised, yet displaced — has become a major theme in Jewish-Christian-Muslim comparative theology.
