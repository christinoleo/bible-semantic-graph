Clement of Alexandria (~150-215) — Christian Platonist; head of the Alexandrian catechetical school before [[origen]]. Probably born in Athens; converted as an adult; settled in Alexandria under the teacher Pantaenus. Fled the city during the persecution of Septimius Severus (202) and died in Cappadocia.

Trilogy structured around the stages of Christian formation: Protrepticus (Exhortation) addresses pagans, demolishing Greek myth and ritual while affirming the Logos as the truth toward which philosophy gropes; Paedagogus (Tutor) outlines Christian ethical formation under Christ the divine pedagogue, descending to remarkable detail on dress, food, baths, sexual ethics; Stromata (Miscellanies, "patchwork") presents in eight rambling books the "true gnosis" of the mature Christian — knowledge of God refined by philosophical training and ascetic discipline.

Distinctively positive use of Greek philosophy: philosophy was given to the Greeks as the law was given to the Jews, as a paidagōgos leading to Christ. Plato is read as a covert reader of Moses. The "true Gnostic" (in deliberate appropriation of the term against the heterodox systems Irenaeus catalogued) is the mature Christian sage, contemplative, dispassionate, ethical.

Who Is the Rich Man That Shall Be Saved? — extended homily on Mark 10:17-31 arguing that wealth is not damnatory per se; the disposition matters. Influential on later Christian engagement with property and almsgiving. His use of the term "gnostic" for the orthodox sage caused later embarrassment; never formally canonized in the Roman calendar (Clement VIII removed him in 1586 over doubts). Crucial bridge figure between Hellenistic Judaism (Philo, whose allegoresis he extends) and the systematic theology Origen would attempt.
