Nabeel Asif Qureshi (1983–2017) — American physician and Christian apologist of Pakistani descent, raised in the [[ahmadiyya]] Muslim community and converted to evangelical Christianity in 2005 after a multi-year investigation prompted by debates with his college friend [[david-wood]]. His apologetic register — narrative-memoir interleaved with [[minimal-facts-approach|minimal-facts]] historical argument — made him the most-read Muslim-to-Christian convert author of the 2010s.

His three books map a deliberate sequence: *[[seeking-allah-finding-jesus]]* (2014) is autobiographical and explains *why he changed his mind*; *[[no-god-but-one]]* (2016) is the systematic apologetic, structured around eight comparison questions (Same God? Trinity? Deity of Jesus? Death on the cross? Resurrection? Muhammad? Quran? Salvation?); *[[answering-jihad]]* (2016), written after the San Bernardino attack, addresses the question of Islam and violence. Completed a PhD in New Testament under [[gary-habermas]] at Liberty University and worked with Ravi Zacharias International Ministries until his death from stomach cancer.

Distinctive to Qureshi within the broader [[christian-apologetic]] field: (i) the Ahmadi-Muslim *internal* perspective — the [[ahmadiyya-survival-thesis|Kashmir-grave]] account of Jesus, the role of [[mirza-ghulam-ahmad]] as Promised Messiah, the [[khatam-an-nabiyyin]] dispute — which mainstream Sunni polemicists (e.g. [[ahmed-deedat]], [[zakir-naik]]) do not engage from the inside; (ii) his foregrounding of [[islamic-dilemma]] as a structural critique of Quranic-Gospel relations; (iii) sustained popularization of the Habermas-Licona [[minimal-facts-approach]] in a Muslim-Christian register.
