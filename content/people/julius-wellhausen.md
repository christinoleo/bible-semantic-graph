Julius Wellhausen (1844-1918) — German biblical scholar and Semiticist; the figure who gave the [[documentary-hypothesis]] its canonical four-source form (JEDP) and an evolutionary account of Israelite religion that dominated Old Testament scholarship for a century. Studied at [[gottingen]] under Heinrich Ewald; held chairs at Greifswald, Halle, Marburg, and from 1892 back at Göttingen. Resigned the Greifswald theological faculty in 1882 with the famous letter declaring himself unable to train Lutheran clergy in good conscience — moved to Semitic philology, returning to biblical scholarship from the side rather than within the confessional faculty.

*Prolegomena zur Geschichte Israels* (Prolegomena to the History of Israel, 1883; an expansion of the 1878 *Geschichte Israels I*) — the definitive synthesis of the source-critical work of K. H. Graf, Édouard Reuss, and Abraham Kuenen into the JEDP sequence — Yahwist (J), Elohist (E), Deuteronomist (D), Priestly (P) — and the historical-religious thesis that the Priestly Code (Leviticus 1-16, parts of Exodus and Numbers) is *late*, post-exilic, retrojected onto Moses; not the foundational legislation of early Israel but its late codifying overlay. The cult-centralization narrative — early Israelite religion as decentralized, ethical, prophetic; later as hierocratic, ritualized, priestly — drew on Hegelian developmental categories and gave the hypothesis its polemical edge against post-Tridentine and rabbinic readings of the Pentateuch. *Die Composition des Hexateuchs* (1885) is the technical philological companion. Late career turned to early Islamic and Arabian studies (*Reste arabischen Heidentums*, *Das arabische Reich und sein Sturz*) and to the Synoptic Gospels — his short Gospel commentaries are minor classics. The Wellhausen consensus is now substantially contested but remains the reference against which contemporary Pentateuchal scholarship still defines itself.
