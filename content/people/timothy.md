Timothy — convert and protégé of [[paul]] from Lystra in Lycaonia, recruited at the start of [[paul]]'s second journey ([[acts]] 16:1-3). Son of a Jewish-Christian mother (Eunice) and a Greek father; grandmother Lois ([[2-timothy]] 1:5) credited with his early religious formation. [[paul]] circumcises him "because of the Jews in those places" ([[acts]] 16:3) — a gesture in tension with [[paul]]'s refusal to circumcise [[titus-companion]] ([[galatians]] 2:3), reconciled variously: Timothy was already halakhically Jewish through his mother (the matrilineal rule, though its 1st-c. force is debated); Titus was a test case, Timothy was not.

Co-sender of six Pauline letters ([[2-corinthians]], [[philippians]], [[colossians]], [[1-thessalonians]], [[2-thessalonians]], [[philemon]]) and named in [[romans]] 16, [[1-corinthians]], [[hebrews]] 13:23. [[paul]]'s primary courier and trouble-shooter: dispatched to settle issues in Thessalonica ([[1-thessalonians]] 3), Corinth ([[1-corinthians]] 4:17), Philippi ([[philippians]] 2:19). [[paul]] calls him "my beloved and faithful child in the Lord" and "of like soul with me" ([[philippians]] 2:20).

Addressed_in [[1-timothy]] and [[2-timothy]], the two pastoral epistles bearing his name and addressed to him as overseer of the Ephesian church. Critical majority reads these as pseudepigraphal — composed after [[paul]]'s death by a Pauline disciple to address later church-order problems (succession, presbyters, women's roles, false teachers). Conservative scholarship retains Pauline authorship with secretary involvement. Either way the letters preserve Timothy's reputation as Ephesus' figurehead. Tradition: bishop of Ephesus, martyred under Domitian or Nerva.
