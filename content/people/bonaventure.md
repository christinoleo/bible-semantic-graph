Bonaventure (~1217-1274) — Franciscan friar; minister general of the Order of Friars Minor from 1257; the Seraphic Doctor and the major Augustinian counterweight to [[thomas-aquinas]]'s Aristotelianism in thirteenth-century Paris. Born Giovanni di Fidanza in Bagnoregio in central Italy; studied arts and theology at Paris under Alexander of Hales (the founder of the Franciscan school) and the secular master John of La Rochelle.

Itinerarium mentis in Deum (The Journey of the Mind into God, 1259) — composed during a retreat at La Verna, the site of Francis's stigmatization. Six stages of ascent followed by a seventh of mystical rest, structured on the six wings of the seraph in [[francis-of-assisi]]'s vision. Vestiges of God in the external world, image of God in the soul, contemplation of God in Trinity and in the union of natures in Christ — a contemplative architecture that synthesizes Augustinian inwardness, Dionysian hierarchies, and Franciscan affectivity.

Breviloquium — concise systematic theology compressed into seven brief parts, designed as a pedagogical handbook for the Franciscan studia. Commentary on the Sentences of Peter Lombard (his major early scholastic work, completed during his Parisian regency 1253-1257) is more conservatively Augustinian than the Sentences commentaries of his contemporary Aquinas; rejects the eternity of the world even as a logical possibility (where Aquinas held it merely a revealed contingent fact that the world had a beginning).

Minister general from 1257 — steered the Franciscans through the bitter internal crisis between the Spirituals (rigorist on apostolic poverty) and Conventuals (relaxed common-property arrangement) by writing the Legenda maior (the official Life of Francis) in 1263 and ordering the destruction of earlier biographies — a contested decision that has been read as both pastoral consolidation and historiographical loss. Collationes in Hexaemeron (1273) — late Parisian lectures defending Christian-Augustinian wisdom against radical Aristotelianism, never finished as he was made cardinal-bishop of Albano. Died during the Second Council of Lyons ([[lyons-ii]], 1274) which he had helped organize. Canonized 1482; Doctor of the Church 1588.
