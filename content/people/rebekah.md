Rebekah, second matriarch. Granddaughter of [[abraham]]'s brother Nahor; sister of Laban. Selected as wife for [[isaac]] in the long betrothal narrative of Gen 24 — Abraham's servant prays for a sign at the well, Rebekah waters his camels unprompted, family negotiates, she consents ("I will go"). The most extended courtship narrative in the canon, and a paradigm of providential pairing.

Barren twenty years; conceives twins after Isaac's prayer. The children struggle in her womb; she alone receives the oracle (Gen 25:23): "two nations are in your womb ... and the elder shall serve the younger" — a structural inversion announced before birth. [[esau]] emerges first, then [[jacob]] gripping his heel.

Active maternal partisan for Jacob throughout. The blessing-deception in Gen 27 is her plan, not Jacob's: she overhears Isaac's intent to bless Esau, dresses Jacob in Esau's clothes and goat-skins, cooks the meal, and stage-manages the theft. When Esau's murderous rage threatens, she sends Jacob to Laban in Haran "for a few days" — they never meet again. Her death is recorded only obliquely (Gen 35:8 names her nurse's death; Gen 49:31 confirms her burial at Machpelah).

Reception. Rabbinic tradition tends to read her favorably — prophetic mother executing the divine oracle she alone heard. [[paul]] in [[romans]] 9:10-13 uses Rebekah's case (twins of one father, divine election before birth/works) as Exhibit A for unconditional election. Feminist exegesis highlights her as the rare matriarch who acts decisively and verbally, redirecting the patriarchal line at the cost of her relationship with both husband and elder son.
