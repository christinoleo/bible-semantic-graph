Patriarch of [[antioch]] (512-518), exiled by Justin I in 518, d. 538 in Egypt — the most rigorous theologian of post-Chalcedonian [[miaphysitism]] and the figure to whom the Syriac Orthodox Church traces its dogmatic identity (he is called the "Crown of the Syrians"). Against [[chalcedonian-christology|Chalcedon]] he insisted with [[cyril-of-alexandria|Cyril]]'s formula *mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē* on a *single composite nature* of the incarnate Word — but against [[eutychianism|Eutyches]] he was equally vehement that humanity and divinity remain real, complete, and unmixed within that composite, distinguishable only "in contemplation" (*tē theōria monē*) and not "in division."

His massive polemical output (*Philalethes*, the correspondence with Sergius the Grammarian, the *Liber contra impium Grammaticum* against John of Caesarea) sets the terms of the Severan tradition still confessed by the Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian, Eritrean, and Indian Malankara churches. The 1990 and 1993 ecumenical agreements between Eastern and Oriental Orthodoxy rest on the recognition that Severus's "one composite nature" and Chalcedon's "one *hypostasis* in two natures" mean the same thing in different grammars — the schism is increasingly read as terminological.
