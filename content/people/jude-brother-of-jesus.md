Jude (Judas) — brother of [[jesus]] and of [[james-brother-of-jesus]], named in the [[gospel-of-mark]] 6:3 and [[gospel-of-matthew]] 13:55 lists of Jesus' siblings. No NT narrative role beyond appearing in the sibling list. Like [[james-brother-of-jesus]] (and the rest of the brothers), an unbeliever during the ministry per [[gospel-of-john]] 7:5; presumably converted, since [[1-corinthians]] 9:5 attests Jesus' brothers as travelling Christian missionaries with wives.

Traditional author of [[jude]], where he self-identifies as "Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James" — strategically deflecting his Jesus-kinship onto [[james-brother-of-jesus]]. Critical scholarship is divided: the letter's polished Greek, late references to "the apostles" as a past authority (Jude 17), and dependence on [[2-peter]] (or vice versa) suggest pseudepigraphy by most readings; conservatives retain authenticity. Either way the letter is short, fierce, and polemic, citing 1 Enoch and the Assumption of Moses as authoritative — a major data point in canon debates.

Distinct from [[judas-iscariot]] and from Judas the apostle (also called Thaddaeus). Hegesippus (in Eusebius HE 3.19-20) preserves a memorable anecdote: Domitian summons two grandsons of Jude, suspicious of Davidic claimants, finds them callused peasant farmers, and dismisses them. The story attests a continuing Jewish-Christian "desposyni" (kin of the Lord) tradition into the early 2nd century.
