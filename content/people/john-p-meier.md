Meier, John P. (1942-2022) — American Catholic priest and New Testament scholar; William K. Warren Professor of Theology at the University of [[notre-dame]] from 2002. Ordained for the Archdiocese of New York; doctoral work in Rome at the Pontifical Biblical Institute. The dual identity — practicing Catholic clergyman and rigorous historical critic — exemplifies the post-Vatican-II Catholic embrace of [[historical-critical-scholarship]], operating fully within mainstream academic norms while retaining the confessional standing.

His life work is *A Marginal Jew: Rethinking the Historical Jesus*, a five-volume project (1991-2016) reconstructing [[jesus]] through systematic application of the classical criteria of authenticity — multiple attestation, dissimilarity, embarrassment, coherence, rejection-and-execution. The portrait is unspectacular by design: a Jewish layman from the Galilean periphery, exorcist and prophetic teacher proclaiming the kingdom of God, eventually executed for his Temple action. The methodological signature is exhaustiveness: each pericope receives encyclopedic treatment, sometimes hundreds of pages, weighing every relevant fragment of evidence before a verdict is offered. Often contrasted with [[dale-allison]]'s more skeptical procedure on the criteria themselves — Meier remains the strongest contemporary defender of the criteriological method as the discipline's main instrument.
