Gideon (also Jerubbaal, "let Baal contend") is the fifth major judge in [[judges]] (Judg 6-8). A Manassite from Ophrah, he is called by an angel of [[yhwh]] while threshing wheat in a winepress to hide from [[midianite]] raiders. The narrative leans heavily on his diffidence — he demands repeated signs, including the famous fleece test (Judg 6:36-40), which has become a byword for seeking confirmation of divine guidance.

His military campaign is a study in deliberate weakness. [[yhwh]] reduces his army from 32,000 to 300 lest Israel claim credit (Judg 7), and the rout of Midian comes through trumpets, torches, and confusion rather than combat. Critical scholarship treats the 300 figure as a literary device rather than a head count; the underlying conflict likely reflects sustained tension between settled Israelites and trans-Jordanian raiders in the 12th-11th c. BCE.

Gideon refuses dynastic kingship — "YHWH shall rule over you" (Judg 8:23) — a Deuteronomistic touchstone for the anti-monarchic strand of Israelite political theology. Yet he immediately fashions a golden ephod from Midianite spoil that "all Israel prostituted themselves after," seeding the apostasy refrain that closes his narrative. His son [[abimelech]] makes the kingship bid he declined, with bloody consequences (Judg 9).

The double name Gideon/Jerubbaal has long been read as evidence of source-critical seams. Some scholars posit two originally separate figures fused in transmission; others read the renaming as theological reframing of a man with a Baalist name into a YHWHist hero.
