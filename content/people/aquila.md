Aquila — Pontic Jew (from the Black Sea province of Pontus), tentmaker by trade, husband of [[priscilla]]. The couple is expelled from Rome by the Claudian edict of c. 49 CE; they settle in Corinth, where [[paul]] joins them in the tentmaking workshop ([[acts]] 18:2-3) — a textbook example of [[paul]]'s self-supporting ministry through artisanal labor.

Travels with [[priscilla]] and [[paul]] to Ephesus and hosts the house church there ([[1-corinthians]] 16:19). The couple instruct Apollos ([[acts]] 18:26). Returns to Rome (or remains there a second time) where [[paul]] sends greetings in [[romans]] 16:3-5, with the striking note that they "risked their necks" for him. By [[2-timothy]] 4:19 they are back in Ephesus.

Conspicuously, [[priscilla]] is usually named first in the pair — strongly suggesting she was the more visible Christian leader, with Aquila as supporting figure. The pattern survives editorial pressure (a scribe inclined to "correct" the order would, if anything, foreground the husband). The two share so completely that no NT pericope distinguishes their individual contribution.
