Irenaeus of Lyons (~130-202) — born in Smyrna, disciple of [[polycarp]] (who in turn was disciple of [[john-apostle]]), establishing for him a direct apostolic chain that he weaponizes against gnostic claims to secret tradition. Bishop of Lugdunum (Lyon) in Gaul after the martyrdom of his predecessor Pothinus in the persecution of 177.

Against Heresies (Ἔλεγχος καὶ ἀνατροπὴ τῆς ψευδωνύμου γνώσεως, "Refutation of the Falsely Named Knowledge"), five books — the foundational anti-gnostic text, surviving complete only in Latin translation. Book 1 catalogues Valentinian and other gnostic systems with sufficient detail that the Nag Hammadi finds largely vindicated his accuracy. Books 2-5 refute on philosophical, scriptural, and theological grounds.

Articulates the rule of faith (regula fidei) as the apostolic tradition publicly transmitted through episcopal succession in named sees — a counter-criterion to the gnostic appeal to secret tradition. Establishes the fourfold gospel canon ([[gospel-of-matthew]], [[gospel-of-mark]], [[gospel-of-luke]], [[gospel-of-john]]) as "four pillars" — earliest explicit defense of exactly these four against both gnostic multiplication and Marcionite reduction (cf. [[marcion]]).

Theology of recapitulation (anakephalaiōsis): Christ as second [[adam]] sums up and reverses all of human history, redeeming each life stage. Universal scope; influences later patristic soteriology. Held the eschatological hope of an earthly millennial reign — a chiliasm Origen and later Augustine would marginalize. Traditionally martyred under Septimius Severus though early sources are silent.
