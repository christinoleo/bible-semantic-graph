Raphael ("God heals") is one of the seven archangels who "stand before the glory of the Lord" (Tob 12:15). His name and function are first attested in Second-Temple literature — he has no role in the [[tanakh]] proper. The narrative role in [[book-of-tobit]] is the canonical anchor: dispatched in answer to the simultaneous prayers of [[tobit]] and [[sarah-of-tobit]], he descends in human form as "Azarias son of Hananiah," shepherds [[tobias]] to [[rages]] and [[ecbatana]], binds the demon Asmodeus in upper Egypt, and reveals his true identity only at the journey's close (Tob 12).

In [[1-enoch]] he is one of four (or seven) named archangels — alongside Michael, Gabriel, Uriel (and Sariel/Phanuel) — assigned in 1 En 10:4-8 to bind [[azazel]] in the wilderness of Dudael, a Watcher-tradition motif rather than the Tobit demon-binding. 1 En 20:3 explicitly assigns Raphael authority "over the spirits of men" — the etymological gloss generating the healing role. The two episodes (binding Azazel, healing Tobit) are the twin pillars of Raphael's later angelology.

Patristic and medieval tradition expand him into patron of travellers, healers, and the betrothed; he is canonized in the Roman calendar (29 September with Michael and Gabriel since 1969). [[catholic]], [[orthodox-eastern]], and [[orthodox-ethiopian]] traditions recognize him as archangel; Protestant traditions admit him only as a literary figure in non-canonical [[tobit|Tobit]]. Distinct from canonical Hebrew Bible angels (the [[angel-of-yhwh]], the cherubim), Raphael is a named-personality angel — a Second-Temple development paralleled in the Qumran [[songs-of-the-sabbath-sacrifice]].
