James — brother of [[jesus]] (so [[gospel-of-mark]] 6:3, [[galatians]] 1:19), leader of the Jerusalem church from c. 44 CE (when [[peter]] departs after his prison escape, [[acts]] 12:17) until his death. Not part of the Twelve and apparently not a follower of [[jesus]] during the ministry ([[gospel-of-john]] 7:5: "his brothers did not believe in him"); becomes a leader after a resurrection appearance specifically to him ([[1-corinthians]] 15:7).

[[paul]] visits him on his first post-conversion trip to Jerusalem ([[galatians]] 1:19); at the Jerusalem Council ([[acts]] 15) James pronounces the verdict on gentile inclusion, sketching the minimal halakhic requirements (abstain from idols, blood, things strangled, sexual immorality). In [[galatians]] 2:9 he is listed first among the "pillars" — James, [[peter]], [[john-apostle]]. "Men from James" arrive at Antioch and trigger [[peter]]'s withdrawal from gentile tables ([[galatians]] 2:12) — James appears as the more conservative pole on Jewish-gentile relations.

Traditional author of [[james]]; the letter's polished diasporic Greek and its delayed reception into the canon raise authorship questions, though a Jerusalem Jewish-Christian core is plausible. Theology emphasizes works, the royal law of love, control of the tongue, and condemnation of the rich — its "faith without works is dead" (Jas 2:17) was the basis for Luther's notorious dismissal of [[james]] as an "epistle of straw" relative to [[paul]]'s justification by faith. New Perspective work largely resolves the tension.

Martyred c. 62 CE. Josephus (Ant. 20.200) reports the high priest Ananus II convened a Sanhedrin during the interregnum between Roman procurators and stoned "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ, James by name, and certain others" — the second independent Josephus reference to [[jesus]]. Hegesippus (later, in Eusebius) adds dramatic detail: James thrown from the temple parapet, then clubbed to death by a fuller.

Catholic and Orthodox traditions, holding to the perpetual virginity of [[mary-mother-of-jesus]], read "brother" as cousin (Jerome) or stepbrother by a previous marriage of [[joseph-of-nazareth]] (Epiphanius). Protestant and most critical scholarship read him as a uterine sibling. Distinct from [[james-son-of-zebedee]] (the apostle) and from James son of Alphaeus.
