John Mark — Jerusalem Christian whose mother Mary hosts a house church ([[acts]] 12:12); cousin of [[barnabas]] ([[colossians]] 4:10). The "young man" who flees naked from Gethsemane ([[gospel-of-mark]] 14:51-52) is a popular but unprovable identification with the author himself.

Joins [[paul]] and [[barnabas]] on the first missionary journey but abandons them at Perga in Pamphylia and returns to Jerusalem ([[acts]] 13:13). The desertion is the cause of the [[paul]]-[[barnabas]] rift before the second journey: [[barnabas]] insists on giving Mark another chance, [[paul]] refuses; they split, [[barnabas]] taking Mark to Cyprus ([[acts]] 15:36-39). Reconciliation comes later — by [[colossians]] 4:10, [[philemon]] 24, and [[2-timothy]] 4:11 (where [[paul]] writes "get Mark and bring him with you, for he is very useful to me for ministry") the breach is healed.

Papias (early 2nd c., preserved in Eusebius HE 3.39.15) anchors the traditional authorship of [[gospel-of-mark]]: "Mark became Peter's interpreter and wrote accurately, though not in order, all that he remembered of the things said and done by the Lord." [[1-peter]] 5:13 — "she who is at Babylon, chosen together with you, sends you greetings; and so does my son Mark" — supplies the Peter-Mark Roman connection. Critical scholarship is divided: the Papias tradition is early and plausible; the gospel does have Petrine narrative markers (Peter's prominence, the embarrassment of his denial). But the gospel is not first-hand and its Galilean geography is sometimes confused.

Tradition: founder of the church of Alexandria; martyred there. Iconographic symbol: the winged lion (whence the lion of St. Mark, emblem of Venice, whose merchants stole his relics from Alexandria in 828 CE).
