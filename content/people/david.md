David is the archetypal king of Israel, founder of the dynasty from which the [[messiah]] is expected and through which the [[gospel-of-matthew]] genealogy of [[jesus]] runs. His narrative spans [[1-samuel]] 16 through [[1-kings]] 2 — the longest sustained character arc in the Hebrew Bible — and is recapitulated, theologically scrubbed, in [[1-chronicles]] 11-29. He reigns by tradition c. 1010-970 BCE; the [[tel-dan-stele]]'s reference to the "house of David" (9th c. BCE) is the earliest extra-biblical attestation of his dynasty, though hard archaeological data for his reign is contested.

The narrative architecture is the rise-and-fall arc: anointed by [[samuel]] as a Bethlehemite shepherd boy (1 Sam 16), defeats [[goliath]] (1 Sam 17), serves at [[saul]]'s court, flees, gathers a band of outlaws in the wilderness of Judah, eventually reigns from Hebron over Judah and then over a united kingdom from [[jerusalem]] (2 Sam 5). The [[davidic-covenant]] of 2 Sam 7 — [[yhwh]]'s unconditional promise of an enduring dynasty — becomes the constitutional spine of the rest of the Hebrew Bible's royal theology and of NT messianism.

The Bathsheba affair (2 Sam 11) and its aftermath — [[nathan-prophet]]'s "you are the man" rebuke, the death of the first child, [[absalom]]'s rebellion, the murder of [[amnon]] — comprise the so-called Succession Narrative (2 Sam 9-20 + 1 Kings 1-2), one of the most psychologically realistic extended narratives in the ancient Near East. Critical scholarship has long treated it as among the oldest sustained prose in the Hebrew Bible, plausibly composed close to the events.

David's identification as psalmist is canonical convention rather than historical claim — 73 of the 150 [[psalms]] bear Davidic superscriptions, but critical study assigns most to later contexts. The figure of David-as-singer-with-harp consoling [[saul]] (1 Sam 16) is the seed of the tradition. In Christian theology David functions as the principal [[messiah]]-type — shepherd, anointed, rejected, vindicated — explicitly mobilized by Matthew and Acts.
