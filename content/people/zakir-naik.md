Zakir Naik (born 1965) — Indian Muslim televangelist; founder of the Islamic Research Foundation (Mumbai, 1991) and the satellite network Peace TV (launched 2006). The most-watched English-language Muslim apologist of the twenty-first century and the inheritor of the public-debate genre established by [[ahmed-deedat]], whom he acknowledges as his model — Deedat publicly named him his "son" in apologetic vocation in the 1990s.

Trained as a medical doctor in [[mumbai]] before turning full-time to *daʿwa* (Islamic missionary activity). Built a recognizable stage persona — suit and tie, Quran in hand, near-photographic recall of biblical chapter and verse — and a stock repertoire of arguments stabilized into a fixed catechetical pattern: scientific miracles in the [[quran]], the [[paraclete-as-muhammad-argument]], *taḥrīf* of the [[new-testament]], comparative-religion appeal to the unitarian elements of the Hebrew Bible. Peace TV reaches an estimated 200 million viewers; the Islamic Research Foundation produces dozens of debate VHS / DVD / streaming releases per year. Indian authorities banned the IRF under anti-terrorism laws (2016) and Naik relocated to Malaysia, where he holds permanent residency under royal patronage. His [[islamic-apologetic]] is methodologically continuous with Deedat — confrontational, citation-heavy, oriented to mass conversion — but inflected by an Indian Muslim register where comparative-religion claims against [[hinduism]] sit alongside the dominant Christian-Muslim front.
