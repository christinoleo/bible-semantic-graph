Jehu is the army commander who overthrows the Omride dynasty in a bloody coup (2 Kings 9-10, traditional dating 841 BCE) and founds the longest-lasting dynasty in the northern kingdom (five generations, c. 841-752 BCE). He is anointed by an emissary of [[elisha]] at Ramoth-Gilead during the war with Aram, then drives furiously to Jezreel — the narrator's phrase "he drives like a madman" (2 Kings 9:20) is one of the canon's vivid cinematic moments.

The purge is total. He kills king Joram of Israel with an arrow between the shoulders and dumps the body on Naboth's plot, fulfilling [[elijah]]'s curse on [[ahab]] (2 Kings 9:25-26). He kills Ahaziah of Judah (Joram's nephew and ally) in the same action, taking out both kingdoms' rulers simultaneously. [[jezebel]] is thrown from her window. The seventy sons of Ahab in Samaria are slaughtered and their heads piled in two heaps at the city gate. Forty-two relatives of the Judahite king encountered en route are killed at the pit of Beth-eked. The Baal worshippers are gathered into the Baal temple under pretext of a great sacrifice and massacred wholesale (2 Kings 10:18-27).

The Deuteronomistic narrator approves of the anti-Baal violence in principle ("you have done well in carrying out what is right in my eyes" — 2 Kings 10:30) but notes that Jehu himself retains the calf shrines of [[jeroboam]] at Bethel and Dan. The prophet [[hosea]], generations later, condemns the violence at Jezreel directly (Hos 1:4) — one of the canon's rare internal disagreements between historiographic and prophetic verdicts on the same act.

Extra-biblical attestation is unusually direct. The Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III (c. 825 BCE) depicts a figure labeled "Jehu son of Omri" prostrating himself before the Assyrian king and presenting tribute — the only known depiction of an Israelite king. The "son of Omri" label is dynastic shorthand for "king of Israel"; Jehu is in fact not Omride at all but the destroyer of that line.
