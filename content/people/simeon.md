Simeon, second son of [[jacob]] and [[leah]] (Gen 29:33); name from `šāmaʿ`, "to hear" — Leah's claim that YHWH "heard that I was hated." Paired throughout with [[levi]] as the violent brothers.

With Levi he avenges the rape of [[dinah]] by sacking Shechem — convincing the men of the city to circumcise themselves and then, on the third day "when they were in pain," entering and putting every male to the sword (Gen 34). The two are cursed together in the Jacob blessing (Gen 49:5-7): "instruments of violence are their swords ... cursed be their anger ... I will divide them in Jacob, scatter them in Israel." Joseph singles Simeon out for binding before the brothers in Egypt (Gen 42:24), perhaps as ringleader. The curse of scattering plays out: the Tribe of Simeon receives no contiguous territory but a set of enclaves inside [[judah]] (Josh 19:1-9), and is absorbed into Judah's identity before the monarchic period closes.
