Lydia — dealer in purple cloth from Thyatira (a city in the Lydian region of Asia Minor, famous for its purple-dye trade), living in Philippi when [[paul]] arrives on the second missionary journey. A "worshiper of God" (sebomenē ton theon), the technical Lukan term for a gentile God-fearer attached to a Jewish synagogue without full conversion.

At the riverside Sabbath gathering outside Philippi, [[paul]] preaches and "the Lord opened her heart" ([[acts]] 16:14). She is baptized with her household — the first recorded European convert. Immediately constrains [[paul]] to lodge at her house: "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come to my house and stay." After [[paul]] and [[silas]] are released from the Philippian jail, they return to Lydia's house before departing ([[acts]] 16:40) — the home functions as the nascent Philippian church.

Significant as: a woman of independent means and trade, a gentile God-fearer (the demographic [[paul]] most successfully evangelized), and an early house-church host. The Philippian church she anchors becomes [[paul]]'s favorite — [[philippians]] is his warmest letter, and the Philippians are the only church from which [[paul]] consistently accepts financial support ([[philippians]] 4:15-18). She is plausibly behind Euodia or Syntyche of [[philippians]] 4:2 — undecidable.
