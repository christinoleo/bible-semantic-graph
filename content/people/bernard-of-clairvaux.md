Bernard of Clairvaux (1090-1153) — Cistercian monk, abbot of Clairvaux from 1115, the dominant ecclesiastical voice of the twelfth-century Latin West. Born to Burgundian nobility; entered the recently founded reform monastery of Cîteaux with thirty male relatives in tow at age 22; sent within three years to found the daughter house of Clairvaux. Under his abbacy Clairvaux founded sixty-eight further houses; the Cistercian order spread from a handful to over three hundred during his lifetime.

Sermons on the Song of Songs (eighty-six sermons, on chapters 1-3 only — death interrupted) — mystical-allegorical reading of the Song as the love-dialogue between Christ-bridegroom and the soul-bride, in the Origenian-Augustinian lineage and the high mark of medieval Western nuptial mysticism. On Loving God outlines four stages: love of self for self's sake, love of God for self's sake, love of God for God's sake, love of self for God's sake — the last attained only fleetingly in this life.

Theological controversy with [[peter-abelard]] — the brilliant and abrasive Parisian master — culminated at the Council of Sens (1141), where Bernard's prosecutorial homilies and prearranged condemnations cornered Abelard into appealing to Rome before the council could vote. Innocent II ratified the condemnation. Bernard pursued a parallel condemnation of Gilbert of Poitiers at the Council of Reims (1148) with less success. Modern scholarship is more sympathetic to Abelard's actual theological positions than to Bernard's polemical caricature of them.

Preached the Second Crusade at Vézelay (1146) at the request of Pope Eugene III, his former pupil; the crusade's failure (1148-1149) cast a long shadow. Wrote In Praise of the New Knighthood for the Templars. On Consideration, addressed to Eugene, is a startlingly frank set of warnings against the corruptions of the papacy. Canonized by Alexander III (1174); Doctor of the Church 1830. Pivot figure between the patristic-monastic theological mode and the rising scholastic-university mode he distrusted.
