Cain, firstborn of [[adam]] and [[eve]] (Gen 4:1). The name puns on `qānâ` ("to acquire / produce") — Eve: "I have produced (`qānîtî`) a man with YHWH." Farmer; offers `minḥâ` from the ground that YHWH rejects in favor of [[abel]]'s firstlings (Gen 4:3-5). Text gives no explicit reason — later readers supply many (heart-attitude, quality of offering, blood vs. grain).

Murders [[abel]] in the field (Gen 4:8); confronted by [[yhwh]] ("Where is your brother?"); curses out from the ground that "opened its mouth" to drink the blood. Sentenced to restless wandering (`nāʿ wānād`) — etiology for the [[kenites]]/nomadic peoples. The "mark of Cain" (Gen 4:15) is protective, not punitive: a sign warning would-be avengers off, much misread in later (esp. racist) reception.

Builds the first city (Enoch, after his son), fathers a line that invents tents, metallurgy, music (Gen 4:17-22) — a culture-bringer ambivalence. The Cainite line ends at the Flood; the Sethite line (via [[seth]]) carries through.

Reception: archetypal fratricide and unbeliever. Heb 11:4 contrasts Cain's faithless offering with Abel's; 1 John 3:12 makes him the prototype of murderous hatred "from the evil one"; Jude 11 ("the way of Cain") names a path of envy and ungodly profit. Rabbinic readings often soften him (capable of repentance — Gen 4:13 read as "my sin is too great to bear" or as a confession). The Cain/Abel rivalry recurs as a structural motif: [[isaac]]/[[ishmael]], [[jacob]]/[[esau]], [[joseph]] vs. brothers — younger displaces elder, but Cain inverts that by being the elder who destroys.
