Eusebius of Caesarea (c. 263-339) — Greek bishop of Caesarea Maritima in Palestine from c. 314; the inventor of the genre of Christian church history and the indispensable transmitter of pre-Nicene Christianity to the post-Constantinian church. Pupil and intimate of the martyr-presbyter Pamphilus (executed 310), in whose Caesarean library — built around [[origen]]'s books — Eusebius did most of his work; took the cognomen "Pamphili" in his master's honor.

*Historia Ecclesiastica* (ten books, drafted from c. 290s, final edition c. 325) — the first major narrative history of the church from the apostles to Constantine; preserves verbatim hundreds of citations from authors otherwise lost (Hegesippus, Papias, Dionysius of Corinth, the Letter of the Churches of Lyons, the lost works of [[origen]], Julius Africanus's *Chronography*) and is the foundational source for almost everything we know about ante-Nicene Christianity. *Praeparatio Evangelica* and *Demonstratio Evangelica* (fifteen + twenty books) form a paired apologetic *summa*: the *Praeparatio* argues from Greek philosophy and pagan religion to Hebrew prophecy as their fulfilment; the *Demonstratio* argues from Hebrew Scripture to Christ. *Chronicon* (in Jerome's Latin epitome and the Armenian) synchronized world chronology and gave the West its standard timeline.

Theology: Origenist Logos-subordinationism. Initially aligned with [[arius]]; signed the [[nicaea-i]] creed (325) only with reservations and continued to oppose [[athanasius]] at the synods of Tyre and Jerusalem (335). Court theologian and panegyrist of [[constantine-i]]; the *Vita Constantini* and the *Tricennalian Oration* gave Christian political theology its first imperial-providentialist template. The reliability of his sources is high; the editorial framing is conspicuously triumphalist.
