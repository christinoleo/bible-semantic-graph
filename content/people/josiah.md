Josiah is the sixteenth king of Judah (traditional dating c. 640-609 BCE), enthroned at age eight after his father Amon's assassination. He is the second of the two kings the Deuteronomistic historian rates without qualification ("before him there was no king like him, who turned to YHWH with all his heart and with all his soul and with all his might, according to all the law of Moses" — 2 Kings 23:25; the other being [[hezekiah]]).

The defining event is the discovery of a "book of the law" in the Temple during Josiah's renovations in his 18th regnal year (2 Kings 22). The high priest Hilkiah finds the scroll; the secretary Shaphan reads it to the king; Josiah tears his clothes and consults the prophetess Huldah, who confirms the threatened curses but defers judgment past Josiah's lifetime. Source-critical consensus since W. M. L. de Wette (1805) identifies this scroll with some form of proto-[[deuteronomy]] — the centralizing program in Deut 12 (worship only at "the place YHWH will choose") matches Josiah's program with unusual precision. The hypothesis underwrites the standard model of the [[deuteronomistic-history]].

The reform itself (2 Kings 23) is sweeping: removal of vessels for Baal, Asherah, and the host of heaven from the Temple; defilement of the high places throughout Judah; destruction of [[jeroboam]]'s altar at Bethel; killing of pagan priests on their own altars; a Passover celebration unmatched "from the days of the judges." It is the textbook centralizing cult reform of Iron Age Judah.

He dies at Megiddo (2 Kings 23:29-30, with substantially expanded account in 2 Chr 35:20-25), killed by pharaoh Neco who is marching north to assist [[assyria]] against rising [[babylon]]. The death is theologically jarring — a uniquely righteous king cut down young — and is widely treated as the trigger for the rapid decline of Judah through [[zedekiah]] to Jerusalem's destruction in 586 BCE. [[jeremiah]] composes laments for him (2 Chr 35:25).
