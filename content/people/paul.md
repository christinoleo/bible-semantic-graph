Paul of Tarsus — Hellenistic Jew, Roman citizen, Pharisee, persecutor turned apostle. By his own account ([[philippians]] 3:5-6; [[galatians]] 1:13-14) "circumcised on the eighth day, of the people of Israel, of the tribe of [[benjamin]], a Hebrew of Hebrews, as to the law a Pharisee, as to zeal a persecutor of the church." [[acts]] adds Tarsus birth, Roman citizenship, tentmaking trade, and study under Gamaliel in Jerusalem ([[acts]] 22:3); the Pauline letters never mention citizenship or Gamaliel, prompting suspicion.

Damascus road encounter ([[acts]] 9, retold 22 and 26; alluded in [[galatians]] 1:15-16) — the foundational event that recasts persecutor as apostle to the gentiles. Three missionary journeys per [[acts]] establish churches across Asia Minor and Greece (Galatia, Corinth, Philippi, Thessalonica, Ephesus). Jerusalem Council ([[acts]] 15; cf. [[galatians]] 2) resolves — partially, conflictingly — the question of gentile circumcision in his favor. Final arrest in Jerusalem, two-year imprisonment in Caesarea, appeal to Caesar, voyage to Rome under house arrest ([[acts]] 21-28).

The Pauline corpus is sorted by critical scholarship into three tiers. Undisputed (universally accepted as authentic): [[romans]], [[1-corinthians]], [[2-corinthians]], [[galatians]], [[philippians]], [[1-thessalonians]], [[philemon]]. Deutero-Pauline (disputed, perhaps written by Pauline school): [[ephesians]], [[colossians]], [[2-thessalonians]]. Pastoral Epistles (majority view: pseudepigraphal, late 1st or early 2nd c.): [[1-timothy]], [[2-timothy]], [[titus]]. The tiering matters: theology, ecclesiology, and views on women shift across the layers.

Pauline theology: justification by faith apart from works of the law (programmatic in [[romans]] and [[galatians]]), participation "in Christ," the body of Christ ecclesiology, eschatological imminence ([[1-thessalonians]] 4), the abrogation/relativization of Torah for gentiles. The "New Perspective on Paul" (Sanders, Dunn, Wright) since the 1970s reframes "works of the law" as ethnic boundary markers rather than legalistic merit-earning, against the Lutheran reading. Apocalyptic Paul (Käsemann, Martyn, Beker) emphasizes cosmic invasion over individual soteriology.

Biography reconstruction faces a deep methodological problem: [[acts]] and the letters narrate different careers. The Jerusalem visits, the Antioch incident, the timeline of the missionary journeys — all are reconciled differently by different scholars. Companions/associates documented include [[barnabas]], [[silas]], [[timothy]], [[titus-companion]], [[luke]], [[mark]], [[priscilla]], [[aquila]]. Martyred in Rome under Nero c. 64-67 CE per tradition (1 Clement 5, Eusebius); beheaded as a Roman citizen.
