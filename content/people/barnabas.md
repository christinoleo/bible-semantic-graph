Barnabas — Joseph the Levite, a Cypriot Jew nicknamed by the apostles "son of encouragement" (Barnabas; [[acts]] 4:36). Sells a field and lays the proceeds at the apostles' feet — paradigm case of the early Jerusalem community's resource pooling. Vouches for the newly converted [[paul]] in Jerusalem when the disciples are suspicious ([[acts]] 9:27), the introduction without which Paul's reception arguably stalls.

Sent by Jerusalem to investigate the gentile mission at Antioch ([[acts]] 11:22-26); fetches [[paul]] from Tarsus, and they teach together at Antioch for a year — Antioch becomes the second center of the movement and the launchpad for the gentile mission. Together they undertake the first missionary journey ([[acts]] 13-14) through Cyprus and southern Asia Minor; at Lystra the crowd takes them for Zeus (Barnabas) and Hermes (Paul). Together they argue the gentile case at the Jerusalem Council ([[acts]] 15; cf. [[galatians]] 2).

The partnership ruptures over [[mark]] ([[acts]] 15:36-41): Barnabas wants to take his cousin ([[colossians]] 4:10) on the second journey, [[paul]] refuses because [[mark]] had abandoned them mid-trip earlier. They split — Barnabas sails to Cyprus with [[mark]], [[paul]] takes [[silas]] west. [[galatians]] 2:13 records an earlier strain at Antioch where Barnabas, like [[peter]], withdrew from gentile table fellowship — "even Barnabas was carried away," a wounded note.

Tradition has him martyred in Cyprus. The Epistle of Barnabas (early 2nd c., Apostolic Fathers) is a Greek allegorical anti-Jewish treatise pseudepigraphally attributed to him; circulated as near-canonical in Alexandria before being excluded.
