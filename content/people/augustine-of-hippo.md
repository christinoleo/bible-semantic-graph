Augustine of Hippo (354-430) — the most influential theologian of Latin Christianity; bishop of Hippo Regius in Numidia from 396 to his death. Born at Thagaste to a pagan father (Patricius) and Christian mother ([[monica]]); trained in rhetoric at Carthage; an auditor (outer-tier adherent) of Manichaean dualism for nine years (cf. [[mani]]); briefly a Neoplatonist in Milan; baptized by [[ambrose]] at Easter 387 after a long interior crisis recounted in the Confessions.

The Confessions (~400) — autobiographical theology in thirteen books, address to God in the second person, foundational text of Western introspection. Books 1-9 narrate childhood through conversion; 10 on memory and the search for God within; 11-13 on time, creation, exegesis of Genesis 1. City of God (413-426) — twenty-two books against the pagan accusation that abandonment of the gods had caused the sack of Rome (410); develops the two-cities ecclesiology (civitas Dei, civitas terrena) and a theology of history shaping all subsequent Western political theology.

On the Trinity (15 books, 399-422) — the mature Latin theology of the Trinity, organized around the analogy of God in the imago Dei: mens / notitia / amor and memoria / intellegentia / voluntas in the human soul as vestigia of the divine processions. Decisive on the filioque, on the simplicity of God, and on the inseparability of the divine missions. On Christian Doctrine (De doctrina christiana) lays down a Christian theory of signs and a hermeneutical method for the love of God and neighbor as the rule of every reading.

Three polemical fronts define his bishopric. Against the Manichaeans, an internal repudiation of his former dualism; against the Donatists (the African schismatic church purist about post-lapsarian clergy), defending sacramental validity ex opere operato — the minister's holiness does not constitute the sacrament's efficacy; against Pelagians ([[pelagius]], Caelestius, Julian of Eclanum), formulating original sin, the bondage of the will, the necessity of grace prior to any human turning, and double predestination in its harsher late form. The anti-Pelagian theology becomes the seedbed of medieval and Reformation soteriology — [[thomas-aquinas]], [[martin-luther]], [[john-calvin]] all read him as their authority while disagreeing radically among themselves. Died as Vandals besieged Hippo.
