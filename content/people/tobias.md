Tobias, son of [[tobit]], is the journeying hero of [[book-of-tobit]]. Dispatched by his blind father to recover ten talents of silver deposited at [[rages]] in Media, he is accompanied by [[raphael-archangel]] in disguise — the angel introduces himself as "Azarias son of Hananiah," a fictitious Israelite kinsman. The pairing of mortal and angelic companion has been read as a Jewish refraction of the Greek travel-romance.

At the Tigris a great fish leaps at his foot; on Raphael's instruction Tobias extracts heart, liver, and gall as apotropaic remedies (Tob 6). In [[ecbatana]] he marries [[sarah-of-tobit]] — his cousin under endogamy law — burning the fish heart and liver on incense to drive Asmodeus into exile in upper Egypt (Tob 8). On return to [[nineveh]] he anoints [[tobit]]'s eyes with the fish-gall, restoring sight (Tob 11). The cures are presented neither as magic nor miracle but as natural remedies activated by angelic instruction — a deliberate Hellenistic-Jewish synthesis.

Raphael's self-revelation in Tob 12 ("I am Raphael, one of the seven holy angels") reframes the entire journey retrospectively. Tobias inherits his father's piety, fathers seven sons in Ecbatana, and lives to see the fall of [[nineveh]] — the book's closing eschatological wink. Iconographically, "Tobias and the Angel" (Verrocchio, Botticini, Pollaiuolo) becomes a Renaissance commonplace for guardian-angel devotion.
