Reuben, firstborn of [[jacob]] and [[leah]] (Gen 29:32); name glossed in the text as `rĕʾû bēn`, "see, a son" — Leah's relief that YHWH "has looked upon my affliction." As firstborn he holds the structural birthright, but the narrative steadily strips it from him.

He sleeps with Bilhah, his father's concubine (Gen 35:22) — a brief, glancing notice that Jacob "heard of it," with no immediate reaction. The act surfaces again in the Jacob blessing (Gen 49:3-4): "Unstable as water, you shall not have pre-eminence, because you went up to your father's bed." The birthright passes effectively to [[joseph]] (double portion via Ephraim and Manasseh, 1 Chr 5:1-2) and leadership to [[judah]]. In the Joseph cycle (Gen 37:21-22) Reuben tries — ineffectually — to save Joseph from his brothers, planning to restore him to the father; he returns to the pit to find Joseph gone. Eponymous ancestor of the Tribe of Reuben, which settled east of the Jordan in the Transjordan plateau and faded early from biblical record.
