Cyril of Alexandria (~376-444) — patriarch of Alexandria from 412, succeeding his uncle Theophilus (who had deposed [[john-chrysostom]]); the dominant theological architect of fifth-century Christology and the leading hand against [[nestorius]] at [[ephesus]] (431). Inherited and intensified the assertive Alexandrian style of episcopal politics.

The Nestorian controversy ignited in 428 over the title Theotokos ("God-bearer") for [[mary]]. Cyril held that to deny Theotokos was to deny that the one Christ is fully divine — the subject of the incarnation is the eternal Logos, who in becoming human can be said in truth to be born, suffer, and die "according to the flesh" (the communicatio idiomatum carried through a single subject). His Second and Third Letters to Nestorius — the Third bearing the Twelve Anathemas — formulate the position. Presided over [[ephesus]] (431) which condemned and deposed Nestorius before the Antiochene delegation arrived; the resulting schism was patched by the Formula of Reunion (433) which Cyril accepted with the John of Antioch faction.

His one-nature formula — "one incarnate nature of God the Word" (μία φύσις τοῦ θεοῦ λόγου σεσαρκωμένη), borrowed from Apollinarian forgeries he believed to be by [[athanasius]] — became both the spine of Cyrillian orthodoxy and the slogan of post-Chalcedonian Miaphysite churches (Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian). [[chalcedon]] (451), seven years after his death, dyophysite "in two natures" — claimed Cyrillian fidelity, the Miaphysite churches dispute the reading. Both sides cite him; the historical Cyril fits neither neatly.

Involvement in the death of the philosopher Hypatia (415) is historiographically contested. Socrates Scholasticus blames "some of Cyril's followers" without directly implicating him; later Coptic sources (John of Nikiu) celebrate her death; modern accounts range from direct complicity to a leadership culture he fostered. Pro-Cyrillian biographers minimize, critics maximize. Anti-Jewish violence in Alexandria during his episcopate is better documented and more clearly his.
