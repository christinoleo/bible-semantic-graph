Basil of Caesarea (~330-379) — Cappadocian Father; bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia from 370. Brother of [[gregory-of-nyssa]] and the formidable Macrina the Younger (his elder sister and spiritual mentor); close friend of [[gregory-of-nazianzus]] from student days in Athens. Family lineage of Christian rigor: grandmother Macrina the Elder had been disciple of Gregory Thaumaturgus.

Architect of cenobitic monastic rule in the Greek East. The Asketika (Longer Rules and Shorter Rules) emphasize communal life over solitary asceticism, manual labor, structured prayer, and charitable works. The Basileiados outside Caesarea — hospital, hospice, leprosarium, hostel — was an early purpose-built welfare complex and a template for later Christian charity infrastructure.

On the Holy Spirit (375) — composed against the Pneumatomachoi ("Spirit-fighters") led by Eustathius of Sebaste, his former mentor. Argues the divinity of the Holy Spirit from baptismal formula, liturgical doxology, and Scripture, advancing the formula "one ousia, three hypostases" that the Cappadocians together stabilized as Nicene grammar. Decisive influence on [[constantinople-i]] (381) — which he did not live to attend — and the so-called Niceno-Constantinopolitan creed.

Against Eunomius (three books) demolishes the neo-Arian Eunomius's contention that the Father's essence (agennēsia, "ingeneracy") is fully comprehensible and that the Son's essence — generation — is therefore essentially different. Basil distinguishes ousia (shared) from hypostasis (distinct), grounding apophaticism: the divine essence is incomprehensible; only divine activities (energeiai) are known. Hexaemeron — nine lenten homilies on the six days of creation — blends Genesis exegesis with naturalist observation and became a model for later Christian creation commentary including [[ambrose]]'s.
