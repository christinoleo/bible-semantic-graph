Thomas Cajetan (1469-1534) — Italian Dominican; cardinal from 1517; the most influential commentator on [[thomas-aquinas]]'s *Summa Theologiae* between Aquinas's death and the Council of Trent. Born Giacomo de Vio at Gaeta (hence "Cajetanus"); entered the Dominicans at fifteen against family wishes; studied at Naples, Bologna, and Padua; master general of the Order 1508-1518; cardinal-priest of San Sisto, then bishop of Gaeta.

*Commentaria in Summam Theologiae* (1507-1522) — printed alongside the *Summa* in the Leonine edition and standard until the twentieth century. Distinctive theses: analogy of proper proportionality as Aquinas's primary doctrine of analogy (his *De nominum analogia*, 1498, codified the threefold scheme later disputed by [[francisco-suarez]] and rejected by Cornelius Fabro and the existential Thomists); the soul's natural desire for the beatific vision as conditional, not absolute — opening the modern *natura pura* debates that ran through Henri de Lubac. Sent to Augsburg in 1518 as papal legate to examine [[martin-luther]]; demanded retraction of the indulgence theses and the denial of the Treasury of Merit; Luther refused and fled. Cajetan's *Acta Augustana* records the exchange. Late biblical commentaries (Pentateuch, Psalms, Gospels, Romans, Hebrews) used Hebrew and Greek, doubted the canonicity of the deuterocanonicals and of Hebrews-2 Peter-Jude-Revelation on patristic-historical grounds — positions Trent would shortly close against.
