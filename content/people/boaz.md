Boaz is a wealthy Bethlehemite landowner of the clan of Elimelech who acts as [[goel]] (kinsman-redeemer) for [[ruth]] and [[naomi]] (Ruth 2-4). His acquisition of Elimelech's land and his marriage to Ruth fold a Moabite widow into Israel's covenant economy and produce Obed, grandfather of [[david]] — placing Boaz in the Davidic genealogy and, through it, the [[gospel-of-matthew]] genealogy of [[jesus]].

The legal mechanics matter. Boaz is *a* redeemer but not *the* nearest; a closer relative has first refusal and exercises it on the land until learning marriage to Ruth is part of the package, at which point he cedes the right by the symbolic transfer of a sandal at the city gate (Ruth 4). The procedure overlays the levirate marriage law of [[deuteronomy]] 25 with Leviticus's land-redemption provisions in a way that has generated extensive halakhic and source-critical commentary.

His name appears as one of the two bronze pillars before [[solomon]]'s Temple (1 Kings 7:21) — "Jachin and Boaz" — for reasons the text leaves unexplained. Speculation ranges from a memorial to the Davidic ancestor to a more abstract symbolic pairing ("he establishes" / "in him is strength").

Rabbinic tradition treats Boaz as a model of restrained righteousness — he eats and drinks with the workers, blesses them in [[yhwh]]'s name, and at the threshing floor responds to Ruth's bold approach with chastity and gratitude. He is identified in some midrashim with the judge Ibzan (Judg 12:8-10).
