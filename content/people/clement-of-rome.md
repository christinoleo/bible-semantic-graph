Early bishop of [[rome|Rome]] (traditional dates c. 88–99 CE) and author of the *First Epistle of Clement to the Corinthians* (c. 96 CE) — one of the earliest extant Christian writings outside the [[new-testament|New Testament]], addressing factional strife in the Corinthian church. Counted among the Apostolic Fathers and traditionally regarded as the third or fourth bishop after [[peter|Peter]] in early Roman succession lists. His epistle is significant for early Christology, ecclesiology, and the development of episcopal authority.
