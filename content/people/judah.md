Judah, fourth son of [[jacob]] and [[leah]]; name from `yādâ`, "to praise" (Gen 29:35, Leah: "this time I will praise YHWH"). Eponymous ancestor of the tribe of Judah and the southern kingdom; ancestor of [[david]] and [[jesus]].

In the Joseph cycle (Gen 37, 43-44) Judah emerges from the brothers as the de facto leader — proposes selling [[joseph]] rather than killing him (Gen 37:26-27, morally ambiguous), guarantees [[benjamin]]'s safety personally, and delivers the long speech in Gen 44:18-34 offering to be enslaved in Benjamin's stead — the speech that breaks Joseph open. The textual handoff of leadership from [[reuben]] (disqualified by Bilhah) to Judah is gradual and deliberate.

The Tamar episode (Gen 38) is a freestanding chapter dropped into the Joseph narrative. Judah marries a Canaanite, fathers Er, Onan, Shelah; the first two die under YHWH's hand; Tamar, his daughter-in-law twice widowed, is denied levirate marriage to Shelah. She disguises herself as a prostitute, sleeps with Judah, conceives, is condemned by him to be burned, then produces his signet/cord/staff as evidence. His verdict: `ṣādĕqâ mimmennî`, "she is more righteous than I" (Gen 38:26). Her son Perez is in the messianic line ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 1:3, Ruth 4:18-22).

The Jacob blessing (Gen 49:8-12) elevates Judah as lion's whelp, sceptre-bearer, with the cryptic `ʿad kî-yābōʾ šîlōh` ("until Shiloh comes") — read messianically in rabbinic and Christian traditions, prosaically (toponym, "tribute," "to whom it belongs") in critical scholarship. The Davidic monarchy emerges from this line; Heb 7:14 notes that [[jesus]] "sprang from Judah," making the priesthood-of-Melchizedek argument; Rev 5:5 names him "the Lion of the tribe of Judah."

The name persists: Judah → Judea → Jew (`Yĕhûdî`). The tribe of Judah eventually absorbs [[benjamin]] in the southern kingdom; after the northern exile, "Judah" effectively names the surviving people.
