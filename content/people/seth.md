Seth, third son of [[adam]] and [[eve]], born after the death of [[abel]]. Name etymologized in Gen 4:25 from `šît` ("to set / appoint"): "God has appointed (`šāt`) for me another seed in place of Abel." Functional replacement for the murdered brother and reset of the human line away from [[cain]].

With Seth's son Enosh, "people began to call on the name of YHWH" (Gen 4:26) — the J narrative marks the inception of cultic worship in the Sethite, not Cainite, line. This deliberately competes with later texts (esp. Ex 6:3, P) that reserve YHWH's name for the Mosaic revelation. See [[documentary-hypothesis]].

Genealogical hinge. The toledot of Gen 5 (P) routes from Adam → Seth → Enosh → ... → [[noah]] → [[shem]] → ... → [[abraham]]; [[gospel-of-luke]] 3:38 extends this line all the way to [[jesus]] ("son of Seth, son of Adam, son of God"). The Cainite genealogy of Gen 4 and the Sethite of Gen 5 share several names (Enoch, Lamech, Methushael/Methuselah) — likely two recensions of one tradition that the redactor preserved side by side.

Reception: Sethian Gnosticism made Seth a recipient of secret revelation and progenitor of an elect race; later Christian and Islamic traditions reabsorb him as ordinary righteous progenitor. Less narrative weight than his brothers, more structural weight as the bridge.
