John the Baptist — apocalyptic wilderness prophet operating along the Jordan c. 28-29 CE. Camel-hair ascetic ([[gospel-of-mark]] 1:6) preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins. Baptizes [[jesus]] (the dominical baptism: [[gospel-of-mark]] 1:9-11 and parallels), a historically secure datum precisely because it embarrassed later Christology (why would the sinless Christ submit to a sinner's rite?) — hence the criterion of embarrassment.

[[gospel-of-luke]] 1 narrates a priestly father Zechariah and mother Elizabeth, kinswoman of [[mary-mother-of-jesus]], making John and [[jesus]] relatives — a Lukan harmonization not echoed in other gospels. Identified by [[jesus]] with the eschatological [[elijah]] of [[malachi]] 4:5-6 ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 11:14; 17:12-13); the Fourth Gospel denies this identification ([[gospel-of-john]] 1:21), redirecting the witness onto Christological testimony.

Beheaded by [[herod-antipas]] at Machaerus after publicly denouncing Antipas' marriage to Herodias (his brother's wife) — the gospels ([[gospel-of-mark]] 6:14-29; [[gospel-of-matthew]] 14:1-12) novelize the scene with Salome's dance; Josephus (Ant. 18.116-119) frames the execution as political, driven by Antipas' fear of crowd influence. The two accounts are independent corroboration of the historical event.

Acts records John's baptism still circulating decades later: Apollos ([[acts]] 18:25) and Ephesian disciples ([[acts]] 19:1-7) know only John's baptism until [[paul]] re-baptizes them. The Mandaeans of southern Iraq preserve a continuing John-the-Baptist tradition that rejects [[jesus]]. Critical scholarship treats John as an independent eschatological movement absorbed and reframed by early Christianity.
