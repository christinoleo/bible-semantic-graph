Titus — Pauline companion, gentile-born (the explicit point of [[galatians]] 2:3: "even Titus, who was with me, though he was a Greek, was not compelled to be circumcised"). The test case at the Jerusalem visit of [[galatians]] 2 — his uncircumcised presence in Jerusalem is exhibit A in [[paul]]'s argument that the gentile mission requires no Judaizing. Why he is conspicuously absent from [[acts]] (despite the prominence in [[paul]]'s own account) is a long-standing puzzle, sometimes resolved by speculating that he was actually [[luke]]'s brother or otherwise covered under a Lukan editorial choice.

Central in [[2-corinthians]]: [[paul]]'s emissary to Corinth in the crisis over the "tearful letter," returning to [[paul]] in Macedonia with good news ([[2-corinthians]] 7:5-16). Subsequently dispatched to organize the Jerusalem collection in Corinth ([[2-corinthians]] 8). Demonstrably trusted with delicate pastoral and financial work.

Addressed_in [[titus]], where he is left in Crete to appoint elders city by city. Pastoral epistles tier with [[1-timothy]] and [[2-timothy]] — critical majority reads as pseudepigraphal, conservative as authentic with secretary. [[2-timothy]] 4:10 has Titus departed to Dalmatia. Tradition: first bishop of Crete; dies there of old age.
