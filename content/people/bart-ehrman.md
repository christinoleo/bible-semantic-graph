Ehrman, Bart D. (born 1955) — American New Testament textual critic; James A. Gray Distinguished Professor at the University of North Carolina at [[chapel-hill]] from 1988; the figure who, more than any other in the early twenty-first century, brought academic biblical criticism to a mass readership. Raised evangelical, took an undergraduate degree at Moody Bible Institute and Wheaton, then doctoral work at Princeton Theological Seminary under [[bruce-metzger]] — the era's leading New Testament textual critic — whose method he inherits while disowning his teacher's confessional commitments. Moved through evangelical to liberal Christian to agnostic positions in the 1990s; the problem of evil is his stated chief intellectual reason for leaving Christianity.

*The Orthodox Corruption of Scripture* (1993) — his academic monograph; argues that scribal variants in the New Testament manuscript tradition reflect not merely error but theologically motivated alteration by proto-orthodox copyists harmonizing the text against christological alternatives (adoptionist, separationist, docetic) in the second and third centuries. *Misquoting Jesus* (2005) — the trade-press repackaging of the textual-critical case for a lay audience; a surprise bestseller. *Jesus, Interrupted* (2009) and *How Jesus Became God* (2014) extend the project from textual variants to redactional contradictions among the Gospels and the developmental Christology of early Christian communities. *Did Jesus Exist?* (2012) — a polemic *against* mythicism (the position that no historical Jesus existed), made on the same critical grounds — distinguishes him from the popular-skeptic / Jesus-mythicist register and locates him squarely within mainstream [[historical-critical-scholarship]]. Frequent debate opponent of evangelical apologists and an important conduit through which the Bultmann-Käsemann-Metzger inheritance reaches general readers.
