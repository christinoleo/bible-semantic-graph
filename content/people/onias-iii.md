Onias III, son of Simon II "the Just," is the last [[zadokite]] high priest before the Hellenistic crisis. Presented in [[2-maccabees]] 3-4 as the paradigm of pious legitimacy: "a man of mild manner and reverent disposition" (2 Macc 3:1), under whose tenure even Gentile kings honour the Temple. The episode of Heliodorus (2 Macc 3) — the Seleucid minister dispatched to plunder the treasury, struck down by an angelic horseman and revived through Onias's intercession — has been a fixture of patristic and Renaissance iconography (Raphael's Vatican fresco, 1511-12).

His brother Jason buys the high priesthood from [[antiochus-iv-epiphanes]] in 175 BCE (2 Macc 4:7-10); Onias is deposed and retires to asylum at Daphne near Antioch. Menelaus then outbids Jason, and on Menelaus's procurement Andronicus murders Onias at Daphne under safe-conduct (2 Macc 4:30-38) — c. 171 BCE. The killing is presented as the precipitating sacrilege of the persecution that follows.

A long-running scholarly reading identifies Onias III with the "anointed one cut off" of [[book-of-daniel]] 9:26 — the 70-weeks prophecy's mashiach-yikkaret — locating Daniel's composition in the immediate wake of his assassination (the standard Maccabean-dating of Daniel 7-12). Alternative readings of Dan 9:26 (Joshua ben Jehozadak, [[jesus]], an eschatological anointed) are well-attested but the Maccabean reading fits the chronological scheme of Dan 9 most economically. His son Onias IV later founds the dissident temple at [[leontopolis]] in Egypt — a Zadokite continuation outside the land.
