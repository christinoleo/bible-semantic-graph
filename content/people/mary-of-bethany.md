Mary of Bethany — sister of [[martha]] and [[lazarus]] in the household at Bethany, a village on the eastern slope of the Mount of Olives near Jerusalem. [[gospel-of-luke]] 10:38-42: sits at [[jesus]]' feet to listen to his teaching while [[martha]] serves; [[jesus]] defends her choice of "the better part." Sitting at a teacher's feet is the posture of a disciple — Mary occupies a male-coded learning role.

[[gospel-of-john]] 11: when [[lazarus]] dies, Mary stays in the house until summoned; meets [[jesus]] with the same line [[martha]] had used ("Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died") and her weeping moves [[jesus]] to tears (John 11:33-35). [[gospel-of-john]] 12:1-8: six days before Passover she anoints [[jesus]]' feet with costly nard and wipes them with her hair — [[judas-iscariot]] objects to the waste; [[jesus]] interprets the act as anointing for burial.

The synoptic anointing accounts ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 26:6-13; [[gospel-of-mark]] 14:3-9) place a similar scene in the house of Simon the Leper with an unnamed woman; [[gospel-of-luke]] 7:36-50 places a different anointing earlier in Galilee with a "sinful woman." These three or four anointing scenes are routinely conflated and routinely re-distinguished. Mary of Bethany is also routinely fused with [[mary-magdalene]] in Western medieval tradition; the conflation is unfounded and the two are independent figures.
