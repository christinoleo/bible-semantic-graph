Tobit, righteous Naphtalite exile in [[nineveh]] under Assyrian deportation, is the eponymous protagonist of [[book-of-tobit]]. Pious almsgiver and corpse-burier in defiance of Sennacherib's edicts, he is struck blind after sparrow droppings fall in his eyes while he sleeps in his courtyard (Tob 2). The narrative frames his suffering as a Joban test of Diaspora piety rather than punishment.

Reduced to poverty and despair, he prays for death; the same hour [[sarah-of-tobit]] in [[ecbatana]] prays likewise after the demon Asmodeus kills her seven bridegrooms. The parallel prayers are answered by [[raphael-archangel]], dispatched to bind Asmodeus and heal Tobit. He sends his son [[tobias]] to Media to recover a deposit at [[rages]]; the journey, the marriage to [[sarah-of-tobit]], and the cure via fish-gall form the narrative spine.

Healed of his blindness in Tob 11, Tobit utters the long hymn of Tob 13 — a Second-Temple psalm of restoration anticipating the rebuilt [[jerusalem]]. He dies in Tob 14 after a deathbed prophecy foreseeing the fall of [[nineveh]] and the return from exile. The framing names him as a tribal Naphtalite, anchoring him to the lost northern kingdom rather than the [[judah-southern-kingdom|Judahite]] diaspora.

The book reads as Diaspora wisdom-novella — almsgiving, endogamy, burial of the dead, and angelic providence — and the figure of Tobit has been received as the archetypal pious exile. Historicity is generally denied (anachronisms collapse Shalmaneser, Sennacherib, and Esarhaddon into Tobit's own lifetime); the text is excluded from the [[tanakh]] but included in [[catholic]] and [[orthodox-eastern]] canons.
