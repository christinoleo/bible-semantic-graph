Rachel, younger daughter of Laban, beloved wife of [[jacob]]. Meets him at the well in Haran; he rolls the stone away alone, kisses her, weeps (Gen 29:10-11). Jacob serves Laban seven years for her — "they seemed to him but a few days because of his love for her" (Gen 29:20). Cheated by Laban's substitution of [[leah]], serves another seven.

Barren for years while Leah and the maids bear children — "give me children, or I shall die" (Gen 30:1). Eventually bears [[joseph]] (Gen 30:22-24, "God remembered Rachel") and, much later, [[benjamin]] — dying in childbirth on the road to Ephrath/Bethlehem (Gen 35:16-20). Names him `ben-ʾônî`, "son of my sorrow," with her last breath; Jacob renames him `binyāmîn`, "son of the right hand." Buried on the road, not at Machpelah with the other matriarchs — a striking exclusion.

Steals her father's `tĕrāpîm` (household gods) when Jacob flees Haran (Gen 31:19); hides them in the camel saddle and feigns menstruation when Laban searches (Gen 31:34-35) — the canon's only laugh at female-impurity taboos. The text leaves her motive opaque: ancestral inheritance claim, religious devotion, defiance, theft.

Two sons; two tribes (Joseph splits into Ephraim and Manasseh — three tribes effectively). Rachel's line yields [[joshua]] (Ephraim) and [[saul]] (Benjamin) — the conquest hero and the first king.

Reception is concentrated in one verse. Jer 31:15: "A voice is heard in Ramah, lamentation and bitter weeping — Rachel weeping for her children, refusing to be comforted, because they are no more" — read in context as Israel's exile, picked up by [[gospel-of-matthew]] 2:18 to frame Herod's massacre of the innocents around [[jesus]]'s infancy. Rachel becomes the archetype of the grieving Israelite mother. Her roadside tomb near Bethlehem remains a pilgrimage site across Jewish, Christian, and Muslim traditions.
