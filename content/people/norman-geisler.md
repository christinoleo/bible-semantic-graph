Norman Geisler (1932–2019) — American evangelical philosopher and theologian, founding figure of the late-twentieth-century "classical apologetics" school that fused Thomistic natural theology with [[evangelical-apologetic]] biblical inerrancy. Co-founded Veritas Evangelical Seminary; long teaching career at Trinity Evangelical Divinity School, Dallas Theological Seminary, and Southern Evangelical Seminary.

His distinctive program reads [[thomas-aquinas]]'s Five Ways and the classical proofs as a *pre-evangelistic* foundation: natural theology demonstrates that God exists, then revelation-historical argument shows that *this God* spoke through Scripture. *[[i-dont-have-enough-faith-to-be-an-atheist]]* (2004), co-written with his student [[frank-turek]], compresses that program into a popular-level twelve-step argument. His 1999 *Baker Encyclopedia of Christian Apologetics* and four-volume *Systematic Theology* are the academic backbone behind the popular work. A central public figure in the inerrancy debates of the late 1970s (signatory of the Chicago Statement, 1978) and a sharp internal critic of evangelical drift toward open theism and post-conservative theology.
