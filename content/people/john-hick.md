British philosopher of religion (1922–2012), the leading twentieth-century advocate of religious pluralism — the claim that all major world religions are equally valid culturally-conditioned responses to a single transcendent "Real" (*Real an sich*), and that no single religion (including Christianity) holds privileged epistemic access. Argued for a "Copernican revolution" replacing Christ-centered with God-centered (later Real-centered) interpretation. Also developed an Irenaean "soul-making theodicy" against the dominant Augustinian free-will theodicy. Sharply opposed by exclusivist Christian apologetics (Carson, Plantinga, Hellenistic-style Christology).
