John son of Zebedee — Galilean fisherman, brother of [[james-son-of-zebedee]], with [[peter]] and James in the inner circle of the Twelve. Witness at the [[transfiguration]] ([[gospel-of-mark]] 9:2-8) and Gethsemane. In [[acts]] he partners with [[peter]] at the temple (chapters 3-4) and the Samaritan mission (8:14-25). [[paul]] names him a "pillar" of the Jerusalem church alongside [[peter]] and [[james-brother-of-jesus]] in [[galatians]] 2:9.

Traditional author of the Johannine corpus: [[gospel-of-john]], [[1-john]], [[2-john]], [[3-john]], [[revelation]]. Critical scholarship distinguishes a "Johannine school" or community centered in Ephesus, with the Beloved Disciple ([[gospel-of-john]] 13:23; 19:26-27; 21:20-24) as community founder and the Gospel and letters as products of his disciples. [[revelation]] is stylistically and theologically distinct — different author writing from Patmos, identified only as "John" without apostolic claim. Conservative scholarship retains traditional unitary authorship.

Identification of the unnamed Beloved Disciple with John son of Zebedee is patristic (Irenaeus, citing Polycarp) rather than internal to the text. Alternatives proposed: [[lazarus]], an Ephesian elder John (the "presbyter" of Papias), or a purely literary figure. Tradition exiles him to Patmos under Domitian, then returns him to Ephesus where he dies of old age — the only apostle not martyred.

Personality flashes: the "sons of thunder" rebuke ([[gospel-of-mark]] 3:17), the Samaritan-fire request ([[gospel-of-luke]] 9:54), the seats-of-honor request ([[gospel-of-mark]] 10:35-45). Johannine theology — Logos Christology, realized eschatology, love commandment, dualism of light/darkness — shapes nearly all subsequent Christian mysticism.
