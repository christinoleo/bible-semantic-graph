Jonathan "Apphus" (epithet of uncertain meaning — perhaps "the dissembler" or "the favoured"), youngest son of [[mattathias]], assumes leadership of the Maccabean party on the death of [[judas-maccabeus]] at Elasa in 160 BCE. The first phase of his command (1 Macc 9-10) is fugitive guerrilla warfare against Bacchides from the wilderness of Tekoa.

In 152 BCE Alexander Balas, contesting the Seleucid throne against Demetrius I, courts Jonathan's military support by appointing him high priest of [[jerusalem]] — the inaugural Hasmonean high priesthood, in defiance of the [[zadokite]] line and the deposed [[onias-iii]] succession. The investiture is enacted at Sukkot 152 BCE (1 Macc 10:21). The break with Zadokite legitimacy is plausibly the trigger for the [[teacher-of-righteousness]] schism at [[qumran]], on the standard Essene-origins reading.

Jonathan plays the rival Seleucid claimants (Demetrius I, Alexander Balas, Demetrius II, Antiochus VI under Trypho's regency) against one another to expand Judean autonomy, renews the Roman and Spartan treaties (1 Macc 12), and consolidates territorial gains in the coastal plain. In 143 BCE Trypho — fearing a strong Jewish ally for the boy-king Antiochus VI — lures him to Ptolemais under guarantee of safe conduct, captures him with a thousand picked men, and eventually executes him at Bascama (1 Macc 12:39-13:30). His brother [[simon-maccabeus]] succeeds him. Distinct from [[jonathan-son-of-saul]] (David's covenant companion), whom 1 Maccabees never invokes despite the obvious nominal parallel.
