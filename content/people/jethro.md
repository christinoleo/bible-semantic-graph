Jethro, Midianite priest (`kōhēn midyān`, Ex 2:16, 18:1), father of [[zipporah]], father-in-law of [[moses]]. Named inconsistently across sources: Reuel (Ex 2:18, Num 10:29) and Hobab (Num 10:29, Judg 4:11) coexist with Jethro/Yeter (Ex 3:1, 4:18, 18:1-12) — likely the seam of separate strands ([[documentary-hypothesis]]: J/E layering). The text's tolerance for the inconsistency itself is interesting.

Shelters Moses during his forty-year exile from Egypt; gives him Zipporah; Moses tends his flocks. Moses is at "the far side of the wilderness" with Jethro's flock at Horeb when the burning bush calls him (Ex 3:1) — a foreigner's pasture is the site of the foundational theophany. After the Exodus, Jethro brings Zipporah and Moses' two sons to him in the wilderness (Ex 18). Hears the saga, rejoices, declares `ʿattâ yādaʿtî kî-gādôl YHWH mikkol-hāʾĕlōhîm` ("now I know that YHWH is greater than all gods," Ex 18:11), and offers sacrifice — joined in the meal by [[aaron]] and the elders.

Counsels Moses on delegation (Ex 18:13-27). Moses is judging from morning to evening alone; Jethro: "what you are doing is not good, you will wear yourself out." Recommends a tiered system — capable men over thousands, hundreds, fifties, tens. Moses adopts it. The Torah's first system of judicial administration is a Midianite's idea, accepted by Israel's prophet. A pointed canonical detail.

The "Kenite hypothesis." Jethro is identified in Judg 1:16, 4:11 with the Kenites (`qênî`), a clan or guild associated with metalworking and the Negev/Sinai south. From the late-19th century onward, a body of scholarship (Stade, Budde, Rowley, more recently Joseph Blenkinsopp) has argued that Yahwism originated among the Midianite/Kenite peoples and was mediated to Israel through Moses' priest-father-in-law — pointing to Ex 18 (Jethro's sacrifice in the camp), the southern locations of YHWH's manifestations (Sinai, Seir, Teman, Paran), and the absence of YHWH as proper name in the patriarchal narratives (Ex 6:3, P). Counterarguments: insufficient extrabiblical evidence, internal-Israelite alternatives. The hypothesis remains live but unsettled.
