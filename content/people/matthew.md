Matthew — tax-collector (telōnēs) called from his customs booth at Capernaum ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 9:9). The synoptic parallels in [[gospel-of-mark]] 2:14 and [[gospel-of-luke]] 5:27 call him Levi son of Alphaeus; the Matthean redaction renames him Matthew, almost certainly to identify the apostle with the gospel's putative author. Hosts a banquet for "tax-collectors and sinners" provoking Pharisaic complaint.

Traditional authorship of [[gospel-of-matthew]] rests on Papias (early 2nd c., preserved by Eusebius): "Matthew compiled the logia in the Hebrew dialect, and each interpreted them as best he could." Critical scholarship overwhelmingly doubts this: the canonical [[gospel-of-matthew]] is in Greek, depends literarily on the Greek [[gospel-of-mark]] (an unlikely procedure for an eyewitness), and shows signs of post-70 CE composition. The Papias logia may refer to a lost sayings source, possibly related to Q.

Beyond the call narrative and the Twelve lists, Matthew has no individuated role in the gospels or [[acts]]. Tradition variously sends him to Ethiopia, Persia, or Parthia; martyrdom accounts are late and contradictory. Iconographically associated with the winged man/angel — one of the four living creatures of [[ezekiel]] 1 and [[revelation]] 4, assigned to the gospels by Irenaeus.
