Francisco Suárez (1548-1617) — Spanish Jesuit; the *Doctor Eximius* and the systematic apex of Second Scholasticism. Born in Granada to a converso-descended noble family; studied canon law and theology at Salamanca; entered the Society of Jesus in 1564; taught at Ávila, Segovia, Valladolid, the Roman College, Alcalá, Salamanca, and finally Coimbra, where he held the prime chair of theology from 1597 until shortly before his death.

*Disputationes Metaphysicae* (1597) — fifty-four disputations rearranging metaphysics as an autonomous philosophical discipline rather than as a series of glosses on Aristotle's text. Distinctive theses: a unitary concept of being as analogically common to God and creatures (a more univocalist reading than [[thomas-aquinas]]'s); modes rather than a real distinction between essence and existence in creatures; primacy of *suppositum* over nature; the *concursus simultaneus* of Molinist divine cooperation with free secondary causes. *De legibus ac Deo legislatore* (1612) developed natural-law theory and the sovereignty-of-the-people thesis that influenced Grotius and the early-modern law-of-nations tradition. *Defensio fidei* (1613) attacked James I's oath of allegiance; was publicly burned in London. His *cursus* shaped Catholic seminary metaphysics for three centuries and, through Christian Wolff, the Protestant German rationalist tradition.
