Brown, Michael L. (born 1955) — American Messianic Jewish apologist, broadcaster, and Old Testament scholar; host of the *Line of Fire* radio program. Doctorate in Near Eastern Languages and Literatures from New York University (1985). Ethnically and ancestrally Jewish — raised in a Conservative Jewish family on Long Island — but converted to evangelical Christianity in 1971 and operates argumentatively from a Messianic Jewish (Hebrew-Christian) position. Classified here as a practitioner of [[christian-apologetic]] rather than [[jewish-anti-missionary]]: the polemical direction runs *toward* the missionary case for [[jesus]] as Messiah, not against it, even though the apparatus (Hebrew sources, rabbinic literature, Tanakh in the original) is shared with his counter-missionary opponents.

The five-volume *Answering Jewish Objections to Jesus* (2000-2010) is the direct genre-counterpart and reply-target to Outreach Judaism's program: organized as point-by-point responses to the standard anti-missionary catalogue — Jewish objections from general arguments, theological grounds, messianic prophecies, the New Testament, and traditional Jewish sources, one volume each. The work is the primary written interlocutor for [[tovia-singer]], with whom Brown has held multiple long-form public debates on [[isaiah-53]], [[daniel-9]], the Trinity in the Tanakh, and the messianic status of [[jesus]]. The unusual classification — Jewish by ethnicity, Christian by argument — makes him the structural mirror image of his counter-missionary opponents and the standard worked example of why "Jewish anti-missionary" is a tradition-label about argumentative direction, not about ethnic identity.
