Aged archimandrite (d. after 454) of the large monastery of Job at [[constantinople]] and godfather of the imperial chamberlain Chrysaphius, whose insistence — "I confess that out of two natures my Lord was made, but after the union I confess one nature" — gave the [[chalcedonian-christology|Chalcedonian]] settlement its name for the absorption heresy it had to exclude. Summoned before the Home Synod of Constantinople under Flavian in 448 he was deposed; rehabilitated at the so-called *Latrocinium* of [[ephesus]] (449) under the patronage of Dioscorus of Alexandria; finally condemned at [[chalcedon]] (451) after the public reading of [[leo-i|Leo I]]'s Tome.

His significance is less doctrinal than typological: [[eutychianism]] becomes the *theological scarecrow* against which both Chalcedonian and [[miaphysitism|Miaphysite]] Christologies define themselves — *whatever* one's preferred formula, it must not dissolve the humanity into the divinity. The post-Chalcedon Severan tradition was at pains to repudiate Eutyches even more sharply than the Chalcedonians did.
