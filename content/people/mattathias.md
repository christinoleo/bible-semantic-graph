Mattathias, priest of the Joarib course in [[modein]], is the patriarch of the Hasmonean dynasty and the spark of the [[maccabean-revolt]]. [[1-maccabees]] 2 stages him as a second [[phinehas]]: when a Seleucid officer of [[antiochus-iv-epiphanes]] arrives in Modein to enforce sacrifice to Greek gods, an apostate Jew steps forward to comply; Mattathias kills both the Jew and the officer at the altar, then flees to the hills with his five sons. The act inaugurates armed resistance against the Hellenistic decrees of 167 BCE.

His five sons — [[judas-maccabeus]] (called "Maccabaeus"), [[jonathan-maccabeus]] ("Apphus"), [[simon-maccabeus]] ("Thassi"), Eleazar Avaran, and John Gaddi — are introduced in 1 Macc 2:2-5 with their epithets; the chronicle frames the family as a priestly clan independent of the high-priestly Oniad line. Mattathias's deathbed speech (1 Macc 2:49-68) catalogues the heroes of [[tanakh]] (Abraham, Joseph, Phinehas, Joshua, Caleb, David, Elijah, the three young men, Daniel) as exemplars of zeal — a piece of Hellenistic-Jewish anamnesis legitimating the revolt by patriarchal precedent.

He dies in 166 BCE, transferring military leadership to [[judas-maccabeus]] and counsel to [[simon-maccabeus]]. The Hasmonean priest-kingship (from [[simon-maccabeus]] onward) traces its legitimacy back to him; the dynasty's later assumption of the high priesthood — usurping the [[zadokite]] line — is a recurrent source of internal Jewish dispute and a likely root of the [[qumran]] community's break. Absent from [[2-maccabees]], which begins its narrative with Judas; the omission is one piece of evidence for the two books' divergent historiographies.
