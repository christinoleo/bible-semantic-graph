Samson is the last and most idiosyncratic judge of [[judges]] (Judg 13-16), a Danite strongman whose career is one long Philistine entanglement. His birth is announced by an angel to his barren mother under a [[nazirite]] vow that forbids razor, corpse, and grape product — a vow he violates serially, with eventual catastrophic consequence when [[delilah]] cuts his hair.

The narrative is structurally folkloric. Riddle contests at his wedding, the slaughter of a thousand Philistines with a donkey's jawbone, the tearing apart of a young lion barehanded, the carrying off of Gaza's gates — these are hero-tale topoi more than military history. Comparative scholarship has long noted the solar etymology of his name (Shimshon from shemesh, "sun") and parallels with Mediterranean strongman myths (Heracles is the standard touchpoint), though the direction and depth of any influence is disputed.

Politically, Samson never leads an army. His "judgeship" is purely personal vendetta against Philistine antagonists, and he ends his life by pulling down the temple of Dagon on himself and his captors at Gaza — killing more in death than in life (Judg 16:30). The Deuteronomistic framing presents him as the failure case for individual charismatic leadership, leading naturally into the closing chapters of [[judges]] and the demand for kingship in [[1-samuel]].

Reception runs the gamut from Milton's tragic *Samson Agonistes* to typological readings that prefigure [[jesus]] (announced birth, betrayed for silver, dies with arms outstretched between pillars).
