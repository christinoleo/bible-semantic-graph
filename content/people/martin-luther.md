Martin Luther (1483-1546) — Augustinian Eremite friar; professor of biblical theology at Wittenberg; the catalyst of the Protestant Reformation. Son of a Saxon copper-mining entrepreneur; meant for law but vowed monasticism in a thunderstorm at Stotternheim (1505); ordained priest 1507; transferred to the new University of Wittenberg in 1508; doctorate in theology 1512. The intellectual breakthrough — the so-called Tower Experience, on the meaning of God's righteousness in [[romans]] 1:17 — is dated variously between 1513 and 1519; Luther's own retrospective (1545) places it late.

Indulgence controversy of 1517: the Disputation on the Power of Indulgences (95 Theses), posted at the Castle Church door (the door-posting itself is contested by some scholars but supported by recent finds), challenged the Tetzel-Brandenburg indulgence campaign and the underlying theology of penance. The dispute escalated into questions of papal authority (Leipzig Disputation, 1519, against Johann Eck), then the trio of 1520 treatises — To the Christian Nobility of the German Nation, On the Babylonian Captivity of the Church, On the Freedom of a Christian — articulating the program of sola scriptura, the priesthood of all believers, the reduction of sacraments to two (baptism and eucharist), and justification by faith alone. Excommunicated by Leo X in 1521 (Exsurge Domine, Decet Romanum Pontificem); refused to recant at the Diet of Worms ("hier stehe ich" — the famous formula's authenticity is uncertain); placed under imperial ban; protected by Frederick the Wise at the Wartburg, where he translated the New Testament into German in eleven weeks (September 1522).

Theological architecture: justification sola fide as the article on which the church stands or falls; the radical priority of God's promissory word (Deus loquens); the simul iustus et peccator (simultaneously righteous and sinner) anthropology; the theology of the cross against theologies of glory; the two kingdoms (spiritual / temporal). Eucharistic real presence retained against Zwingli at the Marburg Colloquy (1529) — "Hoc est corpus meum" defended as literal — a position separating Lutherans from Reformed permanently. On the Bondage of the Will (1525) — the unequivocally anti-Erasmian assertion of a wholly bound human will apart from grace, Luther's own choice for his most important book.

Lutherbibel — German translation of complete Bible (NT 1522, OT 1534, revised 1545), arguably the single most consequential text in the standardization of modern German. Pastoral output (Small and Large Catechisms, 1529), hymns ("Ein feste Burg ist unser Gott"), liturgical reforms (Deutsche Messe, 1526) shape Lutheran ecclesial life still. Married former nun Katharina von Bora 1525; six children; Tischreden (Table Talk) collects his domestic conversations as recorded by disciples — including the inflammatory On the Jews and Their Lies (1543), an explicit blueprint for violence and dispossession that was weaponized in twentieth-century antisemitism and has been formally repudiated by major Lutheran bodies. Condemned in absentia at [[trent]] (positions, posthumously) across the doctrinal canons.
