Rudolf Bultmann (1884-1976) — German New Testament scholar; long-time professor at [[marburg]] (1921-1951); the dominant figure in twentieth-century Protestant biblical scholarship and the founder of form criticism's mature program. Studied at Tübingen, Berlin, and Marburg under Wilhelm Herrmann; colleague and dialogue partner of Martin Heidegger during the latter's Marburg years (1923-1928), which shaped the existential-hermeneutical cast of his later theology. Refused to take the loyalty oath to Hitler in form acceptable to the regime, signed the Barmen Declaration with the [[confessing-church]], and continued teaching through the war.

*Die Geschichte der synoptischen Tradition* (History of the Synoptic Tradition, 1921) — the systematic form-critical analysis of the Gospel pericopes as units shaped by the oral *Sitz im Leben* of the early Christian communities, classified by genre (apophthegms, miracle stories, dominical sayings, legends). With Martin Dibelius's *Formgeschichte des Evangeliums*, the founding text of [[form-criticism]]; verdicts on the historical Jesus pulled sharply minimalist, sayings authentic by the criterion of dissimilarity from both Judaism and the post-Easter church. *Neues Testament und Mythologie* (1941, the so-called Demythologization Essay) — the proposal that the mythological apparatus of the [[new-testament]] (three-storey universe, descending and ascending redeemer, demonic powers, imminent eschaton) be *interpreted* (not eliminated) into existentialist terms drawing on Heidegger's *Sein und Zeit*: kerygmatic address to authentic existence under the cross. *Theologie des Neuen Testaments* (1948-1953) is the dogmatic synthesis. The Bultmann school — Käsemann, Bornkamm, Conzelmann, Fuchs, Ebeling — divided sharply over the New Quest for the Historical Jesus; the controversy is the immediate background to [[n-t-wright]]'s and [[bart-ehrman]]'s very different inheritances of [[historical-critical-scholarship]].
