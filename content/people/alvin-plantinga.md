Alvin Plantinga (b. 1932) — American analytic philosopher of religion; principal architect of [[reformed-epistemology]] and a central figure in the late-twentieth-century revival of Christian philosophy in the analytic tradition. Long tenure at Calvin College and then the John A. O'Brien chair at the [[university-of-notre-dame]]; founding spirit of the Society of Christian Philosophers (1978).

Argued that belief in God can be properly basic — warranted without inference from other beliefs — within a cognitive faculties account of warrant (the trilogy Warrant: The Current Debate, Warrant and Proper Function, Warranted Christian Belief). Reformulated the modal [[ontological-argument]] using possible-worlds semantics in The Nature of Necessity (1974); his free-will defense in God, Freedom, and Evil (1974) is widely credited with retiring the logical [[problem-of-evil]] in analytic philosophy of religion. The evolutionary argument against naturalism (mature form, Where the Conflict Really Lies, 2011) contends that naturalism conjoined with evolution is self-defeating with respect to the reliability of cognitive faculties. Stands in the Dutch Reformed tradition of [[abraham-kuyper]] and [[herman-bavinck]] while differing sharply in method from the presuppositionalism of [[cornelius-van-til]]; alongside Nicholas Wolterstorff and [[william-alston]] he shaped the analytic style of apologetic that [[william-lane-craig]] popularized.
