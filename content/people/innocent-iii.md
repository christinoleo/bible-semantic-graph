Roman pope (r. 1198–1216), the most politically powerful pontiff of the medieval papacy, who convened the Fourth Lateran Council (1215) — the council that dogmatized [[transubstantiation]], mandated annual confession, and instituted the legal apparatus that would later define the Inquisition. Approved the new mendicant orders ([[franciscans|Franciscans]] and Dominicans), launched the Fourth and Albigensian Crusades, and intervened decisively in European politics by placing England, France, and other realms under interdict. His pontificate marks the high-water mark of papal monarchy.
