Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (1149-1209) — Persian Ashʿarī polymath; the central figure in the *muta'akhkhirūn* (later) phase of [[classical-kalam]], in which the dialectical theology of [[al-ghazali]] is fused with the technical philosophical apparatus of Avicenna into a single discursive idiom. Born at Rayy, son of a Shāfiʿī preacher; trained in jurisprudence and theology there and at Marāgha; travelled widely in Khwārazm and Transoxania disputing Muʿtazilīs, Karrāmīs, and philosophers; settled in [[herat]] under Ghūrid patronage, where he taught until his death.

*Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb* (Keys to the Unseen), known simply as *al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr* (The Great Commentary) — encyclopedic Quranic commentary that doubles as a compendium of philosophical theology, natural science, and dialectical responses to every available heterodoxy; the most-cited Sunni *tafsīr* of the later medieval period. *al-Maṭālib al-ʿĀliya* and *al-Muḥaṣṣal* systematize the kalam-falsafa synthesis: God's existence is demonstrated by contingency, the divine attributes are reconciled with strict [[tawhid|tawḥīd]] through the language of *ṣifāt maʿānī*, and the soul is treated within the Avicennan psychology. Builds directly on [[al-ghazali]]'s integration of philosophical method into kalam, but goes considerably further in absorbing the technical lexicon of [[falsafa]] — the move that [[ibn-taymiyya]] will later treat as the decisive corruption of Sunni theology.
