Joseph — legal father of [[jesus]], husband of [[mary-mother-of-jesus]], tradesman (tekton, usually rendered "carpenter") of Nazareth. Davidic descent anchors the Matthean and Lukan genealogies that establish [[jesus]] as Messiah from the line of [[david]]. The two lists ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 1; [[gospel-of-luke]] 3) diverge sharply between [[david]] and Joseph — classic harmonizations read Matthew as Joseph's legal/royal line and Luke as biological/Mary's line traced through Joseph by levirate convention; critical readings treat them as independent theological constructions.

In [[gospel-of-matthew]] he receives angelic dream-revelations (1:20; 2:13; 2:19) and protects the family through the flight to Egypt — a typology that aligns with [[joseph]] son of [[jacob]] (also a dreamer, also linked to Egypt). In [[gospel-of-luke]] he travels to Bethlehem for the Quirinius census and presents the child in the temple. Disappears from the gospels after the [[gospel-of-luke]] 2 temple episode; presumed dead by the time of the Galilean ministry, given Mary's solo appearances and [[gospel-of-john]] 19:26-27.

Apocryphal traditions (Protoevangelium of James) elaborate his old age and prior marriage — a narrative device supporting perpetual virginity of [[mary-mother-of-jesus]] by making [[james-brother-of-jesus]] and [[jude-brother-of-jesus]] children of a previous wife. Catholic devotion canonizes him as patron of workers and of the universal church (Pius IX, 1870).
