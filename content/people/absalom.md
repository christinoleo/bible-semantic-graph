Absalom is the third son of [[david]], born at Hebron to Maacah daughter of the king of Geshur (2 Sam 3:3). His narrative within the Succession Narrative (2 Sam 13-19) is the long-form working-out of [[nathan-prophet]]'s curse on David's house — "the sword shall never depart from your house" (2 Sam 12:10).

The arc starts with the rape of his sister Tamar by their half-brother [[amnon]] (2 Sam 13). David fails to act; Absalom waits two years and has Amnon assassinated at a sheepshearing feast, then flees to Geshur for three more years. The reconciliation with David, brokered by Joab via a wise woman of Tekoa, is partial — Absalom returns to Jerusalem but is barred from the king's presence for two further years (2 Sam 14).

His political insurgency (2 Sam 15) is a study in patient cultivation: he stands at the city gate intercepting petitioners, "stealing the hearts of the men of Israel" by performing the justice his father neglects. Once raised to kingship at Hebron, he marches on Jerusalem; David flees barefoot across the Kidron. [[ahithophel]]'s defection to Absalom and his blunt counsel to seize David's concubines on the palace roof publicly (fulfilling Nathan's word — 2 Sam 12:11-12) is one of the canon's hardest passages.

He dies caught by his famous hair in an oak in the forest of Ephraim, run through by Joab against David's explicit order to spare him (2 Sam 18). David's lament — "O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! Would I had died instead of you" (2 Sam 19:1) — is among the rawest expressions of parental grief in the canon. Reception traditions are deeply ambivalent about him; rabbinic literature often treats him as an exemplar of vanity, though the text itself is more tragic than condemnatory.
