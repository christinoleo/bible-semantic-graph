Levi, third son of [[jacob]] and [[leah]]; name from `lāwâ`, "to join / be attached" (Gen 29:34, Leah: "this time my husband will be joined to me"). Eponymous ancestor of the priestly tribe — but a violently ambivalent founder.

The Shechem massacre (Gen 34). After [[dinah]] is raped/abducted by Shechem son of Hamor, the city's prince offers marriage and alliance; Jacob's sons agree on condition that every male of the city be circumcised. On the third day, when the men "were in pain," Levi and [[simeon]] enter the city and put every male to the sword, taking women, children, and livestock as plunder. Jacob's response is pragmatic ("you have brought trouble on me, made me odious"); the brothers' is moral ("should our sister be treated like a whore?"). The text refuses to resolve.

The Jacob blessing (Gen 49:5-7) reverses the typical patriarchal blessing: Simeon and Levi are cursed — "instruments of violence are their swords ... cursed be their anger" — and condemned to be "divided in Jacob, scattered in Israel." For Levi this dispersal is read prospectively as the priestly diaspora across all Israel's territory (no tribal land for the Levites, just cities and tithes — Num 18, Josh 21).

Rehabilitated through [[aaron]] and [[moses]] (his great-grandsons via Kohath, Ex 6:16-25) and through the golden calf episode: when Moses calls "whoever is for YHWH, to me!" the Levites alone rally and execute the idolaters (Ex 32:26-29), and Moses says "you have ordained yourselves today for YHWH" — the priestly office is consecrated through violence against idolatry. Num 3 and 18 institutionalize the Levitical service of the tabernacle; Deut 33:8-11 (Moses' blessing) and Mal 2:4-7 idealize the priesthood as covenant of life and peace.

[[documentary-hypothesis]] sees the Levitical material as P (Priestly source) project — elevating the Aaronic line within Levi and securing priestly prerogative. The descendants of Levi: Kohathites (incl. Aaron, Moses, Korah), Gershonites, Merarites.
