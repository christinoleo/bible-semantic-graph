Eleazar, a "leading scribe" of advanced age (ninety in 2 Macc 6:24), is the first named martyr of the [[antiochus-iv-epiphanes|Antiochene]] persecution in [[2-maccabees]]. Compelled to eat pork in a public sacrifice, he refuses; offered the face-saving compromise of pretending to eat his own kosher meat in lieu of pork, he refuses again on the explicit ground that the young would be scandalized by his apparent apostasy (2 Macc 6:24-28). He is beaten to death.

The narrative is the template for the martyr-genre in Jewish and Christian literature: principled refusal of a token act, calculated thought for the next generation, a final speech, and a death framed as vicarious witness rather than mere defeat. [[4-maccabees]] vastly expands the scene (chs. 5-7) as a Stoic-Jewish treatise on reason mastering the passions, recasting Eleazar as a philosopher-priest. The [[mother-and-seven-sons]] martyrdom that immediately follows (2 Macc 7) is structurally paired with his.

Christian patristic reception (Origen, Gregory of Nazianzus, Augustine) reads Eleazar and the seven brothers as proto-martyrs preceding Christ's passion; the Maccabean martyrs are commemorated in the Roman, Greek, and Syriac calendars on 1 August — among the few pre-Christian Jewish figures to enter the Christian sanctorale. The figure is distinct from (a) Eleazar son of [[aaron]] the high priest, (b) Eleazar Avaran, brother of [[judas-maccabeus]] killed under the war elephant at Beth-Zechariah (1 Macc 6:43-46), and (c) Eleazar the priest who is grandfather of [[mattathias]] in some genealogies.
