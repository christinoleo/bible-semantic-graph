Benjamin, twelfth and last son of [[jacob]], second of [[rachel]]. Born on the road to Ephrath/Bethlehem at the cost of his mother's life (Gen 35:16-20). Rachel names him with her dying breath `ben-ʾônî`, "son of my sorrow"; Jacob renames him `binyāmîn`, "son of the right hand" (or "son of the south") — the only son not born in Paddan-Aram.

The youngest and surviving son of the favored wife after [[joseph]]'s presumed death; the apple of Jacob's eye. The Joseph cycle pivots on him: Joseph demands Benjamin be brought to Egypt, [[judah]] personally guarantees him to Jacob, and Joseph's planted-cup test (Gen 44) targets Benjamin specifically to force the brothers to choose between abandoning a son of Rachel a second time or sacrificing themselves. They choose the second — and Joseph reveals himself.

Smallest tribe with outsized narrative weight. The Jacob blessing (Gen 49:27): "Benjamin is a ravenous wolf — in the morning devouring the prey, at evening dividing the spoil" — fierce, martial. The Benjaminite war (Judg 19-21), triggered by the gang-rape of the Levite's concubine at Gibeah, nearly exterminates the tribe — a horror that the canon places at the very end of [[judges]] as a culmination of "everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Benjamin survives by the desperate device of abducting brides from Shiloh.

The first king of Israel, [[saul]], is a Benjaminite (1 Sam 9:1-2) — chosen from the smallest tribe to forestall jealousy. After the kingdom splits, Benjamin sticks with [[judah]] in the south; the two tribes effectively constitute "Judah" through the exile and after. [[paul]] identifies himself as `ek phylēs Beniamín` (Phil 3:5) — Benjaminite, named for Saul, the apostle who renames himself after a Gentile (Paulus).
