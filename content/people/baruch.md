Baruch son of Neriah is the personal scribe and companion of [[jeremiah]] and one of the most concretely attested non-royal figures of late-monarchic Judah. He records Jeremiah's dictated oracles, reads them publicly in the Temple precincts when Jeremiah is barred from doing so (Jer 36), and survives the king's burning of the scroll by re-dictating a longer version. He acts as legal witness for Jeremiah's symbolic land purchase at Anathoth on the eve of the [[fall-of-jerusalem-586]] (Jer 32). After the fall, he is forced with Jeremiah into the Egyptian diaspora at the Judahite remnant's flight (Jer 43).

Jer 45 is a small personal oracle addressed to him alone — a rare canonical document of pastoral consolation from prophet to disciple ("you said, 'Woe is me, for YHWH has added sorrow to my pain'… your life I will give you as a prize of war").

Extra-biblical attestation is unusually strong. Two bullae (clay seal impressions) reading "Berekhyahu son of Neriyahu the scribe" have been published — though the second was authenticated only out of an uncontrolled market and is now generally treated as a probable forgery, while the first is debated. Even cautious analysis of the better-attested seal supports historicity of a high-ranking late-7th-c. scribe of the right name and patronym.

Critical scholarship treats Baruch as a likely first-stage redactor of the [[book-of-jeremiah]]. The biographical prose material (the "B" source) is often hypothesized as Baruch's composition or that of a Baruch-school. Several deuterocanonical / pseudepigraphal works develop the figure: the deuterocanonical [[book-of-baruch]] (presented as written by him in Babylon), [[2-baruch]] (Syriac apocalyptic), [[3-baruch]] (Greek apocalyptic). All are post-biblical and reflect later interest in extending Baruch into a Jeremiah-equivalent prophetic figure for the exilic-postexilic period.
