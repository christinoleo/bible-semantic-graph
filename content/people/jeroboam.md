Jeroboam son of Nebat is the first king of the breakaway northern kingdom of Israel after the division under [[rehoboam]] (traditional dating c. 931-910 BCE). An Ephraimite official under [[solomon]] in charge of forced labor for the house of Joseph, he is identified by the prophet Ahijah — who tears a new cloak into twelve pieces — as [[yhwh]]'s designate for ten tribes (1 Kings 11). He flees to Egypt under Solomon's pursuit and returns at Solomon's death to lead the northern delegation at Shechem.

His defining policy is the establishment of rival cultic centers at Bethel and Dan (1 Kings 12:25-33), with golden calf images and a new festival calendar — explicitly to keep northern pilgrims from going up to [[jerusalem]]. The narrator deliberately echoes the [[golden-calf]] episode in [[exodus]] 32 ("behold your gods, O Israel, who brought you up out of the land of Egypt"), framing Jeroboam's foundation as repetition of original Israelite apostasy.

This becomes the Deuteronomistic historian's master rubric for the entire northern monarchy. Subsequent northern kings are evaluated by whether they "walked in the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin" — a phrase that recurs nearly twenty times through [[1-kings]] and [[2-kings]]. The schismatic cult is the structural sin for which the north is eventually destroyed by [[assyria]] in 722 BCE.

Critical scholarship debates how much of the calf-cult polemic reflects 10th-c. reality versus later Judahite construction. Some treat the Bethel and Dan sanctuaries as legitimate continuations of pre-monarchic [[yhwh]]-worship using bull iconography (bulls as pedestal mounts of an unseen deity, on analogy with surrounding Levantine iconography) and Jeroboam's "sin" as Judahite spin on a non-aniconic but otherwise YHWHist northern tradition.
