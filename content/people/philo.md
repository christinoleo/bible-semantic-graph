Hellenistic Jewish philosopher of [[alexandria|Alexandria]] (c. 20 BCE–c. 50 CE), the principal synthesizer of Second-Temple Judaism with Middle Platonism. Developed an allegorical method of biblical exegesis and a doctrine of the *Logos* as God's intermediary in creation and revelation — a conceptual matrix later influential on the prologue of [[gospel-of-john|John's Gospel]] and on patristic Logos Christology ([[justin-martyr|Justin Martyr]], [[clement-of-alexandria|Clement of Alexandria]], [[origen]]). His writings, extant in c. 50 treatises, were preserved by Christian rather than Jewish transmission.
