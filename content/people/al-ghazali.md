Al-Ghazali (c. 1058-1111) — Persian Sunni theologian, jurist, and mystic; honored as *Ḥujjat al-Islām* (Proof of Islam) and arguably the most influential figure in post-formative Sunni thought. Trained in [[shafii]] jurisprudence and [[ashariyya]] theology under al-Juwaynī at Nishapur; appointed to the Niẓāmiyya college in [[baghdad]] in 1091; underwent a documented spiritual crisis in 1095, abandoned the chair, and spent roughly a decade in retreat and pilgrimage before returning to teaching at Nishapur. Recounts the trajectory autobiographically in *al-Munqidh min al-Ḍalāl* (Deliverance from Error).

*Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* (The Incoherence of the Philosophers) — twenty-point demolition of the Avicennan Neoplatonic synthesis, targeting in particular the eternity of the world, denial of bodily resurrection, and God's knowledge only of universals; the most consequential single attack on falsafa from within [[classical-kalam]], and the work to which [[ibn-rushd]] devoted his entire *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* in reply. *Iḥyāʾ ʿulūm al-dīn* (Revival of the Religious Sciences) — magnum opus integrating jurisprudence, theology, and Sufi spirituality into a single normative framework; canonized the place of [[sufism]] within Sunni orthodoxy. [[fakhr-al-din-al-razi]] builds directly on his kalam syntheses; [[ibn-taymiyya]] later mounts the great Hanbali reaction against his Ashʿarite-Sufi settlement.
