The second person of the [[trinity|Trinity]] — the eternal Son of God, consubstantial with the [[yhwh|Father]] (*homoousios*, [[nicaea-i]] 325) and the [[holy-spirit|Spirit]], begotten not made, through whom all things were made (Jn 1:1-3). Confessed in [[chalcedonian-christology|Chalcedonian doctrine]] (451) as fully divine and fully human in one person without confusion or separation — incarnate as [[jesus|Jesus of Nazareth]].

Where [[jesus|Jesus of Nazareth]] treats the historical Galilean teacher (gospel sources, third-quest reconstructions, [[josephus|Josephus]] and [[tacitus|Tacitus]]), this node names the divine identity that Nicene and [[chalcedonian-christology|Chalcedonian]] orthodoxy confess behind the incarnation — denied by [[islamic-christology|Islamic christology]] (Q 4:171, 5:75) and by [[arianism]].
