Ahab is the seventh king of the northern kingdom (traditional dating c. 874-853 BCE), son of [[omri]] and head of the Omride dynasty at its peak. His marriage to [[jezebel]], daughter of the Sidonian king Ethbaal, brings Phoenician Baal worship into the capital at [[samaria]] and frames the central conflict of the Elijah-Elisha cycle (1 Kings 17 - 2 Kings 10).

The biblical portrait is hostile — "Ahab did more to provoke the LORD, the God of Israel, to anger than all the kings of Israel who were before him" (1 Kings 16:33) — but archaeology and Assyrian sources tell a more substantial story. The Kurkh Monolith of Shalmaneser III lists "Ahab the Israelite" as contributing 2,000 chariots and 10,000 troops to the anti-Assyrian coalition at the Battle of Qarqar (853 BCE), the largest single chariot force on the field. The [[mesha-stele]] records Moabite tribute to "the house of Omri" through Ahab's reign. The Samaria ostraca and the so-called "House of Ahab" palace complex attest to substantial 9th-c. state organization.

His scriptural arc turns on three confrontations with [[elijah]]: the drought announcement (1 Kings 17), the contest at Carmel (1 Kings 18), and the Naboth's vineyard episode (1 Kings 21), where [[jezebel]] engineers judicial murder to secure a coveted plot and Elijah pronounces the dynastic curse. Ahab's surprising fast and humiliation in response (1 Kings 21:27-29) elicits a partial reprieve, deferring the curse to his son's generation.

He dies at Ramoth-Gilead, struck by a random arrow despite his disguise, fulfilling Micaiah ben Imlah's vision of the lying spirit (1 Kings 22). His blood is licked by dogs as Elijah had announced. The dynastic curse is completed by [[jehu]]'s coup a decade later.
