The personal name of the God of Israel, traditionally rendered with the tetragrammaton יהוה. Scholars distinguish YHWH (the covenantal name revealed to Moses, Ex 3:14) from Elohim (the more generic divine designation), though the [[documentary-hypothesis]] takes this distinction further, attributing different name preferences to different source documents.

In the patriarchal narratives, YHWH calls [[abraham]] out of [[ur]], establishes the [[abrahamic-covenant]], and commands the [[sacrifice-of-isaac]]. The portrayal contrasts sharply with the creator-deities of surrounding mythologies (e.g., Marduk in the [[enuma-elish]]): YHWH creates by word, not by combat, and acts in covenantal history rather than primordial chaos.
