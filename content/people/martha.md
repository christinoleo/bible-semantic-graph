Martha — householder at Bethany, sister of [[mary-of-bethany]] and [[lazarus]]. In [[gospel-of-luke]] 10:38-42 she welcomes [[jesus]] and bustles with serving (diakonia) while her sister sits and listens; rebukes [[jesus]] for not redirecting [[mary-of-bethany]] to help. The pericope has anchored centuries of reflection on action vs. contemplation, often unfairly to Martha — the text criticizes her anxiety, not her service.

[[gospel-of-john]] 11 raises her dramatically. When [[lazarus]] dies, she runs out to meet [[jesus]] first while [[mary-of-bethany]] remains in the house. Their exchange (John 11:21-27) builds to her confession: "Yes, Lord; I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who is coming into the world" — Christologically equivalent to [[peter]]'s confession at Caesarea Philippi ([[gospel-of-matthew]] 16:16), placed on a woman's lips. In [[gospel-of-john]] 12 she again serves at the dinner where [[mary-of-bethany]] anoints [[jesus]].

Eastern Orthodox and feminist scholarship read her as a major Johannine theological voice. The Aramaic name Marta is the feminine of "lord" (mar) — "lady" or "mistress" of the house. Patron of cooks, housekeepers, and hospitality workers in Western tradition.
