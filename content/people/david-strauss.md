Strauss, David Friedrich (1808-1874) — German Protestant theologian and biblical critic; the figure whose *Das Leben Jesu, kritisch bearbeitet* (1835-36) opened the so-called First Quest for the [[historical-jesus]] and effectively defined what it would mean to read the Gospels critically for the rest of the nineteenth century. Trained at Tübingen under Ferdinand Christian Baur; post-Hegelian in philosophical formation. The book cost him his academic career — appointment to a chair at Zürich in 1839 was withdrawn after public outcry — and he spent the rest of his life as an independent man of letters.

The argumentative move of *Leben Jesu* was to displace the older choice between supernaturalist reading (the miracles happened) and rationalist reading (the miracles are misdescribed natural events) with a third option: the Gospel miracle traditions are *myth* — narrative crystallizations of the early Christian community's messianic expectations onto the figure of [[jesus]], shaped by Hebrew Bible templates (Moses, Elijah, the suffering servant) rather than by any rememberable event. The mythological category — neither lie nor fact — set the agenda for [[historical-critical-scholarship]] on the Gospels and precedes the demythologization program of [[rudolf-bultmann]] a century later. His later *Der alte und der neue Glaube* (1872) abandoned Christianity entirely for a Darwin-inflected scientific naturalism.
