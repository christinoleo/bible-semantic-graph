Lazarus of Bethany — brother of [[martha]] and [[mary-of-bethany]], the subject of the seventh and climactic "sign" of [[gospel-of-john]] (John 11). Falls ill, dies, and is in the tomb four days (a number rabbinic tradition links to the irreversibility of death — the soul having departed) when [[jesus]] arrives, prays at the tomb, and calls him out alive. The sign precipitates the chief priests' resolve to kill [[jesus]] (John 11:45-53) and to kill Lazarus too as the living evidence (John 12:9-11).

Absent from the synoptic tradition — a major problem if the event were historical at the scale [[gospel-of-john]] describes, since it functions as the immediate trigger for the passion in the Fourth Gospel. Critical scholarship reads the narrative as a Johannine theological composition unfolding the "I am the resurrection and the life" discourse (John 11:25) in narrative form, possibly inspired by the [[gospel-of-luke]] 16:19-31 parable of the rich man and a (different) Lazarus, which itself ends "neither will they be convinced if someone rises from the dead."

Speculative identification with the unnamed Beloved Disciple of [[gospel-of-john]] (an alternative to the [[john-apostle]] identification) — supported by the parallel "the one whom Jesus loved" applied to Lazarus in John 11:3, 11:36 — has periodic scholarly revival.

Distinct from Lazarus the beggar of [[gospel-of-luke]] 16, the only character in a parable of [[jesus]] to be given a name. Patron saint of lepers in medieval tradition (whence "lazaretto," "lazar house"). Eastern Orthodox tradition has him survive to become bishop of Kition in Cyprus, dying a second death there.
