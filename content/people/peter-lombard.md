Peter Lombard (c. 1100-1160) — Italian theologian; bishop of Paris from 1159; author of the *Sententiarum libri quatuor*, which became the standard theological textbook of the Latin West for four centuries. Born near Novara in Lombardy; educated at Reims and at Paris under [[hugh-of-saint-victor]] and the circle around [[bernard-of-clairvaux]], who recommended him to the cathedral school of Notre-Dame.

Four Books of Sentences (c. 1150-1158) — a topical arrangement of patristic excerpts (overwhelmingly [[augustine-of-hippo]], with [[ambrose]], [[jerome]], [[hilary-of-poitiers]], [[john-of-damascus]], and others) organized into Book I (God and Trinity), Book II (creation, angels, sin), Book III (Incarnation and virtues), Book IV (sacraments and eschatology). The sevenfold sacramental enumeration crystallized here. Lecturing on the Sentences became the required apprentice exercise of every aspiring medieval theologian, and the commentary on the Sentences — by [[thomas-aquinas]], [[bonaventure]], [[duns-scotus]], William of Ockham, and hundreds of others — was the standard genre until displaced by direct lectures on the *Summa Theologiae* in the sixteenth century. Confirmed as orthodox at [[lateran-iv]] (1215) against earlier suspicions of Christological nihilism.
