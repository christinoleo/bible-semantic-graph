Leah, elder daughter of Laban, sister of [[rachel]], first wife of [[jacob]] (Gen 29). Married to Jacob by Laban's bait-and-switch — Jacob worked seven years for Rachel, was given Leah veiled on the wedding night, discovered in the morning, worked another seven for Rachel. Described as `rakkôt ʿênayim`, "weak-eyed" or "tender-eyed" — ambiguous, possibly soft/lovely, possibly unimpressive; in either case set in contrast to Rachel's `yĕpat-tōʾar wîpat marʾeh`, "beautiful in form and beautiful in appearance" (Gen 29:17).

Unloved (`śĕnûʾâ`, lit. "hated" — covenantal-legal idiom, not necessarily emotional). YHWH "opens her womb" because she is so (Gen 29:31). She bears [[reuben]], [[simeon]], [[levi]], [[judah]] in rapid succession, each name a small monument to her ache: "surely YHWH has looked on my affliction; now my husband will love me" (Reuben); "YHWH has heard that I am hated" (Simeon); "this time my husband will be joined to me" (Levi); finally with Judah, "this time I will praise YHWH" — the first name not framed around her marriage.

Later bears Issachar, Zebulun, and [[dinah]]; her maid Zilpah bears Gad and Asher. Six of the twelve tribes — including the priestly tribe ([[levi]]) and the royal/messianic tribe ([[judah]]) — descend from Leah, not from the loved Rachel. The blessing at [[boaz]] and Ruth's marriage (Ruth 4:11) names Rachel and Leah together as "those who built up the house of Israel," with Rachel first — but it is Leah's line that carries [[david]] and [[jesus]].

Dies before Jacob's descent to Egypt; buried at Machpelah with [[abraham]], [[sarah]], [[isaac]], [[rebekah]] (Gen 49:31) — the only matriarch besides Rebekah explicitly placed there. Jewish liturgical tradition names her among the four matriarchs. Rabbinic and modern feminist readings tend to recover Leah as the unloved-but-vindicated wife whose grief shapes her piety.
