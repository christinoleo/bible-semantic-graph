Cornelius Van Til (1895-1987) — Dutch-American Reformed theologian and philosopher; founder of presuppositional [[christian-apologetic]]. Born in the Netherlands, emigrated as a child to Indiana; trained at Calvin College, Princeton Theological Seminary, and Princeton University (PhD, philosophy, 1927). Joined the founding faculty of [[westminster-theological-seminary]] in 1929 alongside [[j-gresham-machen]] in the rupture from a modernizing Princeton, and taught apologetics there for more than four decades.

Argued that all reasoning rests on ultimate commitments and that only the [[christian-worldview]] — the self-attesting triune God of Scripture — supplies the preconditions for intelligibility, predication, science, and ethics; non-Christian thought, however brilliant, is internally incoherent when pressed to its presuppositions. The apologist's task is therefore not to meet the unbeliever on supposedly neutral ground (the evidentialist mistake he saw in [[bishop-butler]] and his Old Princeton heirs) but to expose the borrowed capital and self-refutation of autonomous reason — a transcendental argument for Christianity. Heir of [[abraham-kuyper]] and [[herman-bavinck]]; sharp internal Reformed disputes with Gordon Clark over the knowability of God. Shaped the school of [[greg-bahnsen]], John Frame, and contemporary Reformed apologetics broadly.
