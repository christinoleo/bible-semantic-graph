Wright, N.T. (Nicholas Thomas, "Tom"; born 1948) — British New Testament scholar and Anglican churchman; Bishop of Durham 2003-2010; professor at [[st-andrews]] from 2010 and at Oxford's Wycliffe Hall from 2019. Trained at Merton College, Oxford; doctoral work on Paul under G. B. Caird. The dual identity — academic historian and serving bishop — is itself the methodological signature: confessional commitments inform but, on Wright's own account, do not determine his historical conclusions, and the academy receives him as a serious interlocutor across that line.

Two large programs structure the corpus. The *Third Quest* for the historical Jesus (the term is his) — locates [[jesus]] within Second Temple Jewish eschatology, restoration-of-Israel hopes, and Temple theology; against Bultmann-school existentialization and against the Jesus Seminar's Hellenistic-Cynic Jesus. *Jesus and the Victory of God* (1996) is the major statement; *The Resurrection of the Son of God* (2003) is the historical-argumentative case for bodily resurrection as the best explanation of the rise of Christian faith and the empty-tomb / appearance tradition. The "fresh perspective" on Paul (a friendly inflection of E. P. Sanders's [[new-perspective-on-paul]]) — covenant faithfulness (*ʾemûnâ* / *pistis*), continuation of the Abrahamic story, justification as the declaration of covenant membership rather than the imputation of an alien righteousness — set out across *What Saint Paul Really Said* (1997) and the monumental *Paul and the Faithfulness of God* (2013). His double standing — in mainstream [[historical-critical-scholarship]] and in confessional [[christian-apologetic]] — makes him the standard reply-target for both [[bart-ehrman]] (whom he has debated on resurrection) and Reformed critics who read the new-perspective shift as a soteriological capitulation.
