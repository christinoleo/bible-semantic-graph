Aḥmad ibn ʿAlī ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (773–852 AH / 1372–1449 CE) — Egyptian Shāfiʿī jurist and Ashʿarī theologian; the foremost late-classical Sunni *muḥaddith* and the author of *Fatḥ al-Bārī*, the canonical commentary on [[sahih-al-bukhari|Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī]]. *Lisān al-Mīzān* and *Tahdhīb al-Tahdhīb* are his standard works in *ʿilm al-rijāl* (the biographical evaluation of hadith transmitters). Held judicial office (*qāḍī*) under the Mamluks in Cairo. Regularly invoked in modern [[islamic-apologetic]] (e.g. by [[muhammad-hijab]]) as a heavyweight classical witness for the textual-corruption reading of [[tahrif]] — that the Bible Christians hold today is *muḥarraf lafẓan* (textually altered), not merely misinterpreted.
