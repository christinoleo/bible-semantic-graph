The single God of Islamic theology — Arabic *Allāh*, contraction of *al-ilāh* ("the god") — confessed in the [[shahada]] ("*lā ilāha illā Allāh* — there is no god but Allah") and described in the Qur'an by ninety-nine *al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā* ("most beautiful names") of which *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm* ("the Merciful, the Compassionate") is the most prominent. Islamic theology identifies Allah materially with the God of Abraham, Moses, and Jesus — the Qur'an addresses Jews and Christians as "People of the Book" worshipping the same deity — while differing sharply on the divine self-revelation: Allah does not beget (Q 112), is not [[trinity|three]] (Q 4:171, 5:73), and did not incarnate in [[jesus]] (Q 5:75, 19:35). The Christian theological question of whether Christians and Muslims worship the same God — referentially yes (same Abrahamic deity), descriptively contested (the [[trinity|Trinitarian]] Father vs the strictly unitary [[tawhid|Tawḥīd]]ic Allāh) — has been debated continuously since John of Damascus and remains live in modern interreligious theology.
