Patron deity of Babylon, elevated in the [[enuma-elish]] from regional storm-god to king of the gods after defeating the sea-goddess Tiamat. The Enūma Eliš is essentially Marduk's coronation epic — its theological purpose is to justify Babylon's (and Marduk's) supremacy over older Sumerian-Akkadian traditions.

Comparative readings with [[yhwh]] focus on the structural contrast: Marduk creates by combat (theomachy → cosmos), [[yhwh]] by speech (Gen 1). The contrast is itself a polemical literary move when the Israelite priestly traditions encountered Babylonian cosmology during the Exile.
