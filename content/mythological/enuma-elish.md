The Babylonian creation epic, composed in Akkadian on seven tablets, reciting the rise of [[marduk]] (deferred — node TBD) to chief god and his creation of the world from the corpse of the sea-goddess Tiamat.

Comparative scholarship has long noted parallels with Genesis 1: the watery chaos (תְהוֹם / Tiamat), the act of separation, the ordering of cosmos. The differences are equally significant — Enūma Eliš's combat-myth structure (theomachy → cosmos) is conspicuously absent from Genesis, where [[yhwh]] creates by speech alone. Whether Genesis is responding to or independent of the Babylonian tradition is a perennial scholarly debate.

The Enūma Eliš predates the likely composition of Genesis 1 (P source) by several centuries, making influence — direct or indirect — plausible.
