City in southern Iberia, capital of al-Andalus under the Umayyad emirate (756) and then caliphate (929–1031). At its 10th-century peak Córdoba was one of the largest and most learned cities in the Mediterranean world, with libraries that preserved and transmitted Greek philosophy and science back into Latin Christendom.

A multi-confessional intellectual center where Muslim, Jewish, and Christian scholars worked in close proximity: [[ibn-hazm]] the Zahiri jurist and polemicist, [[ibn-rushd]] (Averroes) the great Aristotelian commentator who reshaped both Islamic and Latin [[scholastic]] thought, and the Jewish philosopher Moses Maimonides were all born here. The Mezquita-Catedral and the surviving medieval synagogue stand as architectural witnesses to this convivencia.
