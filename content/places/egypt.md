The land along the Nile that recurs across the biblical narrative — refuge for Abraham and Sarah, setting for the Joseph cycle, place of bondage from which Moses leads the Hebrews in the [[exodus]], and refuge for the holy family fleeing Herod in [[gospel-of-matthew]] 2. The pharaonic civilization frames much of the Hebrew Bible's polemical contrast between [[yhwh]] and foreign gods.

In the Christian era, Egypt becomes a major intellectual and monastic center via [[alexandria]], producing the Septuagint, [[philo]], and the Catechetical School. [[origen]], [[athanasius]], and [[cyril-of-alexandria]] all worked here, and the deserts of upper Egypt give rise to the desert-monastic tradition under figures like Anthony and Pachomius. Today the home of Coptic Orthodoxy.
