Mesopotamian city on the Euphrates, capital successively of the Old Babylonian and Neo-Babylonian empires. Under Nebuchadnezzar II it conquered Judah, destroyed Solomon's Temple, and deported the population in waves (597 and 586 BCE), inaugurating the Babylonian Exile that lasted until Cyrus the Great's decree of return in 539 BCE.

The exile is a theological pivot of the Hebrew Bible: the prophetic ministries of [[jeremiah]], [[ezekiel]], and [[daniel]] are set against it; [[psalms]] 137 voices its grief; and the post-exilic communities of [[ezra]] and [[nehemiah]] return to rebuild [[jerusalem]] in its shadow. In the New Testament, [[revelation]] 17–18 redeploys "Babylon" as a coded name for imperial [[rome]], establishing a typology of oppressive world-power that recurs throughout Christian apocalyptic.
