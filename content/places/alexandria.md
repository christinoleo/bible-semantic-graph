Founded by Alexander the Great in 332 BCE on the Mediterranean coast of [[egypt]]. Premier center of Hellenistic learning, home of the Great Library and the Mouseion. Its large Jewish diaspora produced the Septuagint translation of the Hebrew Bible and the philosophical-allegorical exegesis of [[philo]], which deeply influenced later Christian readers.

In the Christian era, the Catechetical School of Alexandria — associated with [[clement-of-alexandria]], [[origen]], and later [[athanasius]] and [[cyril-of-alexandria]] — became the leading exponent of allegorical and high-Christological reading of scripture, in productive tension with the more literal [[antioch|Antiochene]] school. Seat of one of the five ancient patriarchates and the historic center of Coptic Christianity.
