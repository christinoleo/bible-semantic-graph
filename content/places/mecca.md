City in the Hijaz region of western Arabia, birthplace of [[muhammad]] around 570 CE and the home of the Kaʿba — the cubical sanctuary that Islamic tradition traces to [[abraham]] and Ishmael. The direction of Muslim prayer (qibla) faces the Kaʿba in Mecca, and the annual Hajj pilgrimage required of every able Muslim has its goal here.

The Prophet's early ministry took place in Mecca under persecution by the Quraysh, leading to the Hijra to Medina in 622 CE — the event that opens the Islamic calendar. After the conquest of Mecca in 630 CE the Kaʿba was rededicated to monotheistic worship. The Meccan suras of the [[quran]], generally earlier and shorter, address Arabian polytheism and call to monotheism, while later Medinan suras shift toward communal legislation.
