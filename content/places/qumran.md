Archaeological site on the northwest shore of the Dead Sea where, between 1947 and 1956, eleven caves yielded the Dead Sea Scrolls — roughly 900 manuscripts dated from c. 250 BCE to 70 CE, including the earliest extant copies of every book of the [[hebrew-bible|Hebrew Bible]] except Esther (notably the Great Isaiah Scroll, [[1qisa-a|1QIsa-a]]). The settlement itself is widely identified with the Essene community, whose distinctive theology — apocalyptic dualism, the [[teacher-of-righteousness|Teacher of Righteousness]], priestly Zadokite identity, communal property — pervades the sectarian scrolls ([[1qs|1QS]], [[1qm|1QM]], the Habakkuk Pesher, the Damascus Document).
