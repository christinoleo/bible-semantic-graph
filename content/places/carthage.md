Phoenician colony on the North African coast (modern Tunisia), founded by Tyre in the late 9th century BCE and rebuilt as a Roman colony after its destruction in 146 BCE ending the Third Punic War. Under Roman rule it became the largest city in the Latin-speaking West after [[rome]] and the cultural capital of Roman Africa.

A formative center of Latin Christianity: [[tertullian]], who coined much of the Latin theological vocabulary (including trinitas), and [[cyprian-of-carthage]], the martyr-bishop, both worked here; [[augustine-of-hippo]] studied here and corresponded with its bishops. Multiple Councils of Carthage in the 3rd–5th centuries addressed rebaptism, the [[donatist-controversy]], and were instrumental in shaping the Western [[biblical-canon]].
