Capital of France and, through its university (founded c. 1150 and chartered c. 1200), the leading center of [[scholastic]] theology in the high Middle Ages. Its faculty of theology became the institutional matrix in which [[peter-lombard]]'s Sentences was canonized as the standard theology textbook and where successive generations of masters — [[albert-the-great]], [[thomas-aquinas]], [[bonaventure]], [[duns-scotus]], and others — produced the commentaries and disputed questions that defined medieval Catholic doctrine.

The city also hosted multiple synods and condemnations, including the 1210 and 1277 Parisian condemnations of Aristotelian and Averroist propositions, which shaped the reception of pagan philosophy in Christian theology and constrained the boundaries of permitted speculation.
