Garrison city founded in 638 CE on the west bank of the Euphrates as one of two Iraqi *amṣār* (alongside [[basra]]) settling Arab Muslim troops after the conquest of Sasanian Persia; capital of the Caliph ʿAlī (656–661) and an early Shīʿī stronghold. Across the 8th–9th centuries Kufa became one of the four classical centres of Qurʾānic recitation (alongside Medina, Mecca, Basra, and Damascus) — the Kufan school produced the readings of [[asim-ibn-abi-al-najud|ʿĀṣim]] (transmitted by [[hafs-ibn-sulayman|Ḥafṣ]] and Shuʿba), Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī, of which Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim became the globally dominant recension. Also the cradle of the Ḥanafī legal school (Abū Ḥanīfa lived and taught here) and a major centre of early [[hadith]] transmission.
