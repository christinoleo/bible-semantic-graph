The central sanctuary of ancient Israel, located on Mount [[moriah|Moriah]] in [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]. Built by [[solomon|Solomon]] (c. 950 BCE), destroyed by Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon in 586 BCE (First Temple); rebuilt under Zerubbabel in 516 BCE and dramatically expanded by Herod the Great from 20 BCE; destroyed again by Titus in 70 CE (Second Temple). The locus of Yahwistic sacrifice, the levitical priesthood, and the [[hebrew-bible|biblical]] festal calendar. Eschatologically central to Jewish messianic expectation, to the Pauline "man of lawlessness" in the temple (2 Thess 2:4), and to Islamic eschatological geography (the same plateau hosts the Dome of the Rock and al-Aqsa).
