Capital of the Roman Empire and, after the apostolic age, the ecclesiastical seat of the Bishop of Rome (the Pope). Tradition holds that [[peter]] and [[paul]] were both martyred here, Peter under the Neronian persecution of 64 CE that followed the Great Fire — the event also tied to the earliest large-scale Roman violence against Christians.

After Constantine's edict of toleration (313 CE) and the conciliar age of [[nicaea-i]] and beyond, the city's bishop progressively claimed primacy over the western churches, a claim formalized through the medieval papacy and contested by [[constantinople]] in the [[great-schism]] of 1054. Rome remains the geographic center of Latin Christianity and the venue or sponsor of councils from [[lateran-i]] through [[vatican-ii]].
