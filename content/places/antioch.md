City on the Orontes river in Syria, founded c. 300 BCE by Seleucus I. According to [[acts]] 11:26, it is where disciples of [[jesus]] were "first called Christians," and it served as the missionary base from which [[paul]] launched his journeys. Site of the confrontation between Paul and [[peter]] over table fellowship with Gentiles ([[galatians]] 2).

Seat of one of the five ancient patriarchates and home to the "School of Antioch," which favored literal and historical exegesis over the allegorical method of [[alexandria]]. Theologians associated with this tradition — [[john-chrysostom]], Theodore of Mopsuestia, [[nestorius]], Diodore of Tarsus — emphasized the distinct humanity of Christ, a christological tendency that became contested in the controversies leading to [[ephesus]] and [[chalcedon]].
