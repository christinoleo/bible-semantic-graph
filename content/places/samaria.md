Capital of the Northern Kingdom of Israel, founded by Omri around 880 BCE on a hill purchased from Shemer ([[1-kings]] 16:24). It gave its name to the surrounding region, which lay between Galilee in the north and Judah in the south. Conquered by Sargon II of Assyria in 722 BCE, with its population deported and replaced by foreign settlers — the population mixture from which later Samaritan identity emerged.

The post-exilic Samaritans maintained their own Pentateuch and worshipped on Mount Gerizim rather than at the [[jerusalem]] temple, a rift that frames the New Testament's Samaritan episodes — the woman at the well in [[gospel-of-john]] 4 and the parable of the Good Samaritan in [[gospel-of-luke]] 10 — both of which exploit the boundary between Jews and Samaritans to make theological points about insider and outsider.
